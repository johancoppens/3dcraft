# Changelog

All notable changes to 3DCraft are listed here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html). Until 1.0, a minor version may change behaviour; files saved by any released version keep opening in every later one.

## [Unreleased]

### Results that changed

What 3DCraft answered before can differ now: a measure, a proof, a check, or a body that was built wrong while it looked right. If you relied on one of these, check it again.

- **A clearance could be reported as proven for parts that overlap.** Where the faces of two bodies passed through each other, but no corner of either lay inside the other, `clearance` could say the bodies were clear and prove a gap between them, and an assertion of a minimum clearance passed, although the bodies shared volume. Such a pair is now an overlap. The distance between faces (`distance`), the proof of a wall thickness and the press-brake check (`bendable`) shared the fault and are fixed with it.
- **A clearance, a distance or a wall thickness could be reported as proven next to a cone that comes to its point, for parts that are closer or even overlap.** Where a body has a cone with its tip (a `feature.cone`, a profile turned about an axis it ends on at a slant, the point of a drilled hole), what `clearance`, `distance` and `thickness` proved next to that cone (`atLeast`) was only the value they had found, however far that was from the truth: a ball sunk 0.0001 mm into the side of a slender cone was answered clear with a proven gap of 0.00076 mm, and an assertion of a minimum clearance passed; a gap of 0.000003 mm was proven as 0.0004 mm; the floor under a drill point was proven 0.00000004 mm thicker than it is. What is proven is now never above the true gap or thickness, and the ball sunk 0.0001 mm into the cone is an overlap. What may now read differently: `atLeast` next to such a cone, which was the value found and is now what is proven (under a drill point that leaves 0.5 mm of floor, 0.4999999999999 mm); the thickness of a body with a sharp cone (its sides less than a right angle apart), which is proven as 0 at the tip, as thin as the tip is; an assertion of a minimum that passed on such a value now fails where the parts are closer than it asks; and one of a gap of only a few tolerances next to such a cone may now be *not proven* where it passed, the proof ending with its work before it reaches so small a gap. Such a check now does the work of a proof (tenths of a second to a few seconds where it took none), and that work is bounded: beside a cone's tip with a curved cut a hair from it, where telling a point in the face from out of it is dear, the proof stops telling after a fixed amount of work and gives what it has proven by then, which may be less, down to nothing. Still owed: parts that overlap by only a few tolerances, one of them thousands of times the size of the other (a ball of 10 m radius sunk 0.00001 mm into the side of a cone 12 mm high, or 0.000003 mm into a cylinder's), may still be answered clear, with nothing proven (`atLeast` 0), and an assertion of a clearance of at least 0 passes on them; this is so with or without a cone's tip, and was before. A cone cut short of its point (a countersink, a chamfer on a hole) is proven as before.
- **A proven distance to a sphere or a torus could be slightly too high.** The allowance for how far a sphere's or a torus's surface can lie from the triangles it is measured on was too small (a quarter for a sphere; for a torus it ignored where its surface turns slowly), so `atLeast` of a distance, clearance or thickness that such a face decided could be a little above the truth. It can now be a little lower than before.
- **A proven distance could be above the truth for faces nearly parallel or very thin.** Where the triangles a distance is measured on were long and nearly parallel, or one was a long thin sliver, rounding could make the proven `atLeast` larger than the real gap (micrometres for faces a fraction of a nanometre apart). The proven value is now never above the gap.
- **A proof near nearly flat freeform faces rested on a bound of their normals that could be too narrow** by up to 1.5e-8 rad, so a proven distance, clearance or thickness could rule out a pair of pieces it should have looked at. The bound is right to rounding now; a proof is never less careful than before.
- **A measured volume could be wrong, by far more than the change it was to show, after a face of a long freeform part was offset or moved.** Offsetting the 0.02 mm² end cap of a 20 m lofted blade outward by 0.055 mm made `document.measure` report the part 10 mm³ *smaller*, where the cap adds 0.0013 mm³; on another copy of the same blade it reported 10 mm³ more. The part itself was right; its measured volume (and centroid) was not, wherever an edge lies within the modelling tolerance of its face but not exactly on it, and most where that face lies far from the middle of the part. The measure now follows the points of such edges as they lie on the face. And offset face, shell, thicken, move face, draft and delete face no longer make an edge between two faces that do not move again as an approximate curve where it lies along the border of one of them: it is that border, exactly, between its new ends (the leading and trailing edges of the blade, when only its end cap moves); lines and circles stay lines and circles. A part whose edges lie on their faces measures as before, to the last digit; a part with approximate edges may measure a little differently, mostly in the last digits (a freeform impeller by 4 parts in ten million, well within the accuracy the measure reports with `bound`).
- **A body with a face whose edge loop goes round its circle twice is refused.** Such a face (a disc whose rim of three arcs goes round twice, a hole taken out twice) passed the validity check and measured as twice its area or volume, or a hole's worth short, while looking right; so did a loop that runs back over itself along a stretch. Every operation and `document.check` now refuse it, naming the face and the loop: each loop of a face must go round what it encloses once, round a closed surface (a cylinder, a torus) at most once, and not run over itself or over another loop of the face. A sheet may still have a cut, whose two sides run over each other opposite ways (a slit or a slot in a folded sheet). No operation is known to make such a face since the plate's corners were fixed (A-019); the check is the net under all of them.
- **A plane whose normal is a hair off vertical sees what lies below it near a body's lowest and highest points.** With a normal within about a hundred-millionth of a radian of up or down but not along it (one `measure.floating` comes to as it settles upright, or a face's normal worked out from its corners), `measure.below`, `measure.section`, `measure.levelFor` and `measure.floating` read how far the bodies reach along z instead, off by that angle times their size (5e-6 mm on a part a metre long, five times the default tolerance), so a plane that far into a body near its lowest point could find nothing below it, and a plane that missed a body could be refused ("nothing … was found below the plane …, yet it may reach … below it"). The reach is now read along the normal itself. Along the axes nothing changes, to the bit; along other normals a level or an equilibrium can move by rounding (a few units in the last place).
- **A sheet-metal part or a plate whose outline has arcs that continue each other is built at every size, and never with the wrong volume.** Where two arcs of one circle meet (the joints of a chained sketch pattern, such as the root circle between a gear sector's teeth, or a hole split into arcs), whether the outline runs on smoothly there was decided by the last bit of a rounding, so a part could fail at one value and build at a value a billionth away ("the boundary crosses itself in parameter space", or "loop … does not lie where the face's other loops leave it room"), and a plate of such an outline could even be built with a hole taken out twice. Such a joint now always counts as running on. What moves: a corner where two edges of a plate run on into each other to rounding, and which was solved as a crease until now, is now placed as every smooth corner is, which moves it by rounding (a few units in the last place: 7e-15 mm on a part 90 mm long); a body that was wrong changes by what was wrong. Every corner placed as a smooth one before stays where it was.
- **A chamfer larger than its faces allow is refused where it is found to pass through a face beside its edge.** Where the line from one cut line to the other runs through a face beside the edge (a chamfer far along a pipe that lies in a plate, along a groove, up a shallow dome, past the overhanging nose of an S-shaped face, near the narrow end of a cone, or where two rings meet), 3DCraft built a valid-looking solid that took material away where the chamfer should only add it, or added it where it should only take it away. Such a chamfer is now refused as too large for that face, saying the largest size that fits along a straight edge next to a cylinder; one whose line enters the face by no more than the document's tolerance still builds. This is a search, not a proof: what it does not find is built as before. It can miss a crossing shorter than a sixty-fourth of the edge, along an edge whose faces change along it; a crossing between the 32 points at which some faces are looked at (a ring cut at a slant, a spline face that is not an extrusion, a cone cut in a hyperbola or a parabola); a crossing where the search cannot decide, or in a place across the edge where it finds no section; a chamfer whose cut line is carried across a smooth edge onto the next face, except along a straight edge between flat and cylindrical faces onto a flat or cylindrical face (below); and a chamfer face that lies inside a neighbouring face while its line keeps clear of it, which is still built wrong. Fillets are checked the same way (the next entry).
- **A fillet is refused where a face beside its edge is found to bulge into its rolling ball.** Where a face beside the edge reaches into the fillet's ball between the edge and the place the ball touches that face (a bead on the wall of a groove next to its rim, or on a ridge next to its foot, filleted with a radius at which the bead comes into the ball), 3DCraft built a valid-looking solid that added material where the fillet should only take it away, or took it where it should only fill. Such a fillet is now refused, naming the edge and the face; one whose ball takes in the face by no more than the document's tolerance still builds, as before, and so does every fillet not found so, the same solid as before. A fillet whose ball would rest on the bulge instead is not built yet: give another radius. This is a search, as the chamfer's is, not a proof: it can miss what the chamfer's search can (a stretch shorter than a sixty-fourth of the edge, along an edge whose faces change along it; some faces looked at in 32 points only, among them revolved, lofted and swept spline faces (a wall of 68° with a bead, revolved a quarter turn: a fillet of radius 7.75 wrong by 1.67e-2 mm is still built, as 0.1.0 built it, where the same wall extruded is refused); a place where it cannot decide or finds no ball; a fillet carried across a smooth edge onto the next face), a face that comes back into the fillet beyond the place the ball touches it, and faces other than the edge's own two. Fillets along straight edges between planes and cylinders and round circular edges between planes, cylinders and cones cannot meet this and are not searched.
- **A chamfer whose cut line runs past the end of its face, onto a flat or cylindrical face that goes on from it smoothly, is built, or refused as too large.** Along a straight edge between flat and cylindrical faces, a chamfer that reached past the edge where its face goes on smoothly into the next one (a boss whose wall leaves a plate as an arc and goes on as the arc's tangent plane, chamfered further up the wall than the arc reaches; a top that runs on into a round) failed with *"the result is not a valid body: edge … is … away from the surface of face …"* when the face it runs past was named first. Named the other way round, it was built as a valid solid that was wrong, and `document.check` said nothing: on such a boss 40 mm long, 1 mm along the plate and 5 mm up the wall changed the volume by +50.68 mm³ where the line between the cut lines runs through the arc, and 0.5 mm and 6 mm by −6.54 mm³, a chamfer of an inside corner that took material away. It is now built: its cut line lies on the next face, at the straight distance across the edge that the distance asked for has on the first face (on a cylinder, the chord of that arc), as wherever a chamfer's cut line is carried onto the next face. Where the line from that cut line to the other would run through a face (the arc bulging past it, or the top), it is refused as too large for that face, whichever face is named first, saying the largest size that fits. The same now holds where a cylinder is two faces that meet along its length, and where the flat face beside a cylinder is two faces of one plane: a chamfer whose line runs through the cylinder there was built wrong and is refused, as where each is one face. Every chamfer whose cut lines stay on their own faces builds as before, to the last digit. Still built wrong, as in 0.1.0: where the face that goes on smoothly is a spline face (even a flat one), a chamfer whose line runs through the face before it is built as a valid solid that is wrong (the same boss with its wall going on as a straight spline, 0.5 mm along the plate and 6 mm up the wall: −6.54 mm³).
- **An assembly solved at other drive values than its parts were placed at says when its pose is not the one solve straight from the placement.** Its rest pose was that one solve, which could land a linkage on its mirrored loop without a word: a walking machine's legs at some crank starts, a four-bar or a slider-crank near the pose where its two circuits pass close. It is now also carried there step by step from the drive values its parts are placed at, in steps that shorten near such a pose. Where the two agree, the pose is exactly what it was, and nothing is said. Where they differ, the carried pose is shown and `assembly.solve`'s `note` says so: that the assembly, carried from the placement, rests elsewhere than the straight solve gives, or, when the parts were not placed where their mates meet, that the branch is not certain. Where the straight solve fails and the carry answers, the note says that too. A linkage very near its change point can still be carried onto its other branch: the note marks that the two methods disagreed, not which one is right. When the carry stops short or runs out of steps, the straight solve is shown and the note says the branch is not checked. Moments of a motion and joints held by hand are solved as before. A rest solve whose drives are far from where the parts are placed costs more: twelve walking legs on one crankshaft (74 parts) 27 to 100 ms instead of 10 to 16, fifty (302 parts) 0.43 to 0.78 s instead of 0.34 to 0.39. An assembly placed again with nothing its solve reads changed takes its last answer.
- **A joint that nothing drives, and that moves on its own, keeps its value when an assembly is solved; such assemblies can rest in another pose than before.** When a part was placed a little off its mate, the solve also turned free joints elsewhere to close the gap (a desk lamp's free swivel ended 6.6° from where it was placed). A free swivel, hinge or slider now stays where the parts were put; joints that move only together (a free loop, a free screw and its nut) are left as before. An assembly put together by hand with such a free joint can rest otherwise than before: the published walking machine's leg, opened on its own at another leg scale, by up to 1.6 mm.

### Added

- **After an update, 3DCraft says what is new, and first which results may differ.** The first time a newer version shows its start screen, a card *New in <version>* stands under New part and Open…: what changes an answer first (a measure, a proof, a check, or a body that was built wrong), then what is new, then the rest folded away; a click on a line unfolds what these notes say about it. In a low window it starts as its title and one line that says how many results may differ, so the first row of examples stays in sight; a click unfolds it. After several versions at once it lists them all, newest first. Closing it is remembered, and **Help › What's new** shows it again. It is never over a document, and never shown on a first start. The notes are this changelog, carried in the program: nothing is fetched. In the web version the card shows once when the page has moved to a newer version (not when 3DCraft is embedded in another page). An agent asks the same as data: `session.whatsNew {"since": "0.1.1"}` returns the versions, their entries by kind, and which of them change an answer.
- **Experimental: the web build can be driven by the page it sits in (Embed).** A page that loads the module itself, with `data-craft3d-embed` on its canvas, keeps its own title, focus, keys, storage and address, and gets a port (`craft3d_connect()`) on which it sends the same requests as the control channel (`engine.execute`, `batch`, `ui.get`, `ui.set`), hears `ready` and `regenerated` (each reply says the state it left, so a page knows whether to wait), gives 3DCraft a document or a STEP file as bytes (`embed.give`) and takes what a command writes (`file.export`, `file.save`) as bytes with its reply, or the document's own file without changing it (`embed.take`). A read is answered on the state it was sent for; what would hold the page's own thread (a `batch` step or a `wait: true` that needs the model built) is refused with what to send instead, and many requests at once wait their turn rather than hold it or have the model built on it. The window can be drawn whole, as the 3D view alone, or as a Jig's form beside the view (`presentation`: `full`, `view`, `form`; the last two without the sketches' lines, which come back as they were in `full`); in the embedded stand a ring shows when 3DCraft has the keys, Escape closes 3DCraft's menu, list or dialog first and then leaves the canvas (Tab too, in the view), and the wheel scrolls the page unless 3DCraft has the focus. Without the attribute the web page is exactly as before; the desktop app refuses a presentation other than the whole window. Experimental: the names and the events may still change (`packaging/web/HOSTING.md` › *Embedding*, `docs/control-protocol.md` › *The page as a transport*).
- **A fillet or a chamfer takes one edge by its own name.** Until now an edge was given as the two faces it lies between, which means every edge between them, so one of two edges between the same faces (where a pipe sunk into a plate leaves its top, say) could not be rounded alone. Give the edge's name instead, as `document.edges` lists it or a click in the window gives it: `"edges": ["F3:between[…]"]`, or `[edge name, face name]` to say which face a chamfer's first distance runs along. An edge is taken by its name only where every part of the name is proven not to move to another edge (`docs/design/names.md`); such a name keeps meaning that edge when earlier features change or are put in before it. Pairs mean what they always meant.
- **`document.check` warns (`pointOffArc`) when a point held on an arc lies on its circle beyond the arc's ends**, so the arc does not pass through it: an arc drawn through a point keeps the way round it was drawn with, and goes the long way round its centre once the point crosses the line through its ends.

- **A sheet through a grid of points** (`feature.surface.grid`): a smooth surface that passes through every point of a grid, for a surface known by its points (measured or computed, a scan's patch, a hull's offsets, a lens, a terrain). Give the points row by row, each coordinate an expression, or read the heights of a regular grid from a table, times a factor that is an expression (`"factor": "vertical_scale"`): the sheet follows the table and the variable. `spacing` sets where it takes its points: `even` (the default for a table: over its regular grid the textbook bicubic spline of the heights, a height field of degree three or less each way exactly, and a height field never folds, however steep), `chord` (the default for points given: it follows how far apart they are) or `greville` (a live spline's default); the feature keeps the one it was made with. It passes through each point within rounding, gives a plane exactly from points on one, and is refused, naming the row and point, where its rows are ragged, two neighbours coincide, a row runs back on itself or the sheet would fold (proven on every span, not sampled; the refusal says whether a row runs back or the proof could not decide), or where the grid lies so far from the origin that no model there holds the tolerance (from 1.126e9 at the default tolerance; nearer, from about 6.5e7, it is built and warned of). Its warnings (a sheet too large for a boolean, heights far steeper than the step, a grid far from the origin) are in its result and in `document.check`. A rough sheet (heights that zigzag several steps between neighbours) is built but may not be measured or meshed: the tessellator's and the mass integrator's limits. Its face, edges and corners are numbered by the grid's borders, so moving the points renames nothing. A block cut by the sheet (`feature.boolean` `intersect`) is the solid under it, for a sheet of up to 32 768 spans (184 × 184 points; the result warns past it). A grid read from a table costs about 20 bytes a point in the saved file, one given point by point about 120 (a live spline's point about 670).

- **A live spline can take its points at chord-length parameters** (`sketch.spline {"live": true, "spacing": "chord"}`), which follow how far apart the points are. A spline through points takes them, by default, at parameters that ignore their spacing and are uneven near the ends, so between the points it strays further from the smooth curve they came from than it needs to: through eight points of an involute flank, 0.0095 mm against 0.00044 mm with chord spacing (J-034). Without `spacing` a spline is exactly what it was, in new documents and old. Not yet: chord spacing on a closed spline, on a spline made once (not live), and `pointOn` or `tangent` on a chord spline; each is refused with the reason.

### Changed

- **Names not proven to stay with their edge are refused by a fillet or a chamfer.** A name with a number at its end (`…](1)`), one that lists a round's or a chamfer's face (`fillet(0,1)`, `chamfer(0)`, numbered by where they lie or among the references that are there), a glyph of a text, a developable's face, a plate's rim, or an edge of a lump a split chose by where it lies: an edit could move such a name to another edge without anything failing. The refusal says what names the edge instead, or that nothing does yet; then give the pair of its faces, which takes every edge between them.
- **Files are format version 72.** A file saved by this version is not opened by 0.1.x, which says that a newer 3DCraft is needed; every file of 0.1.x opens as before.
- **A plate's joint that failed may build now.** A joint whose edge was named by its faces in a body a later boolean took failed with "is gone"; it is followed to the edge the boolean kept and built. A joint that built is unchanged.
- **A `{min, max}` bound of a filter is matched within the tolerance, as a value is.** `radius`, `length`, `area`, `volume` and `thickness` in `select.query`, named sets and the subjects of assertions found a value given alone or as `eq` within a millionth of it plus the document's tolerance, but compared `min` and `max` exactly, so a measure a hair on the wrong side of a bound by rounding was left out: a corner whose radius is given as 1 mm could drop out of `{min: 1 mm}` after some edits and not others. A bound now takes in what `eq` would match at it. A measure farther outside a bound than that tolerance stays out; a filter finds more than before only where a measure lies within the tolerance outside a bound.

### Fixed

- **Recover could refuse a document as belonging to a 3DCraft that is still running, although that one was gone.** A program 3DCraft starts (the watcher of the desktop's light or dark setting, a recording's `ffmpeg`) holds a copy of the lock file of a crashed 3DCraft for the moment before it has started, if it was started while 3DCraft looked through the recovery directory, and the lock stayed held for that moment. The lock is now let go of explicitly, which releases every copy. A person was unlikely to meet it (the moment is well under a millisecond), but tests that start programs met it often.
- **A shell was refused where four or more faces meet in a corner.** A trough or a pocket whose floor falls to a drain, a box with a cylinder whose axis lies in the box's side plane: where four faces meet in one point, the walls of the hollow no longer do, and the shell failed at every thickness with "the faces that meet in a vertex there no longer meet in one point". In the hollow such a corner now becomes two corners joined by a short edge, as the moved walls make it, and the shell builds. Where that is not decided by the faces (a corner of both kinds of edge that could become edges in more than one way), it is refused as before. A shell open at its top round a pocket can leave the pocket's lining as a separate piece of the same body, as it always could round a boss. Offset face, thicken, draft and move face still refuse such a corner.
- **A shell next to rounds that meet in a mitre was refused as too thick.** A box whose bottom edges are rounded (the rounds meeting in mitres at the corners), shelled open at its bottom, was refused from a little below the rounds' radius less the wall on ("the edge shrinks to nothing or turns round", naming the two rounds): the edge where the rounds meet shrinks much more than it moves, and was lost while it was followed. It is now followed from a better guess and the shell builds. A shell, offset, draft or move of faces that built before builds exactly as before; only some refusals now build, and the largest size a refusal names may be larger.
- **A face bounded by a helix of many turns as one edge is checked the way it goes round.** A thread's crest or root on a cylinder (or a cone or a sphere) whose side is one spline edge of more than about four turns, as a file can give it, was refused by the validity check as running the wrong way round, or as going round the cylinder many times, while it was right; and the same face turned the wrong way round could pass. Such an edge is now followed round every turn, so the right face passes and the wrong one is refused; a face whose edges would take more than a few milliseconds to follow so (a single edge of more than about 85 turns) is passed without being judged. On a torus a band between curves that wind about its tube was refused while it was right; it passes.
- **A sketch with an arc or circle through three points held at values builds again after an edit that made it fail**, also when a file saved in that state is opened. An arc drawn through a point (its ends and the point written with expressions) could fail with *"constraint … conflicts"* after several sizes changed at once or after the arc was made very flat, and then kept failing at those values, after saving and opening too, while a new document at the same values built. When the solve from the last result fails, 3DCraft now tries once more with the centre of every such arc or circle placed where its three points put it, and takes that result only if nothing conflicts, every arc still runs through its points between its ends, and nothing else in the sketch turns to another side. A sketch that solved before solves exactly as before. Not recovered: an arc whose point has crossed the line through its ends (it fails as before: draw it again through the point), and a sketch that fails for other reasons.
- **Points held at values are their values exactly after a change of the variables.** Before, a few units in the last place could remain from an earlier solve, so the same values did not always give the same held points, and a corner held at a radius of 1 mm could come out a hair under 1 mm and drop out of a `radius: {min: 1 mm}` query. Now, whenever a sketch solves, a coordinate given by an `x`, `y` or `fixed` constraint is exactly its value, unless other constraints already decide it (the constraint is then redundant and holds within the tolerance, as before). On its first solve, a sketch from an older file may move its held points by that much. This also mends a part that failed after `param.set` gave it values at which a new document builds (a sheet-metal arm with a gear sector on its flanges, every point of its sketches held at values): the edit now gives the new document's sketches, to the last bit, and builds.
- **A flat face has a plate thickness when the face opposite it is parallel only to rounding.** `thickness` (in `select.query`, named sets and assertions) asked the face a line leaves through to face exactly the other way, to the last bit, so two faces whose normals differ by rounding alone (each worked out from its own corners, as in a block turned off the axes) had no thickness and never fit. They have one now; every face that had a thickness keeps the same one.
- **`normal` and `axis` filters with `tol: 0` find what is parallel or square to the axis to rounding.** At 0, and at any `tol` below about 6e-7 degrees, a face whose normal was the axis only to rounding was left out (a block turned on a skew axis showed one of its four sides as `perpendicular`). Such a `tol` is now read as an angle, never as less than rounding (about 2e-13 degrees). At the default half degree, and at any tolerance from a thousandth of a degree up, every selection is what it was.
- **A straight edge along a cone of a very small half-angle (under about 0.002°) is recognised as lying on it** in a boolean; before, rounding in the angle could hide it and leave the edge to a general method that may refuse it. The booleans of the reference set (3996 pairs of solids) give the same results to the bit.
- **A shaft turned only by its coupling follows it over any number of turns.** A lead screw that nothing drives but its screw coupling to a nut stopped a motion after about 15 turns ("did not converge in 200 iterations"), and the assembly could not be solved at rest that far out. It now turns with its coupling in the motion and at rest. A moment of a motion that worked before gives the same answer, bit for bit; only what failed is solved again, and only where a coupling's turns are the matter.

## [0.1.2] - 2026-11-13

Fixes for the first things a new user meets: on Windows no console window opens beside 3DCraft any more, the web version's page says what it is, and the MCP server says more about itself to the tools that connect to it. Files of 0.1.0 and 0.1.1 open unchanged.

### Fixed

- **Windows: no console window beside 3DCraft any more.** The Start menu entry and a double-clicked `.3dcraft`, `.3dcraftjig` or `.3dcraftasm` file start 3DCraft through a small launcher, `3DCraft.exe`, which opens no console window, so there is none to close 3DCraft by. If the window cannot start, a message box says why. In the zip, double-click `3DCraft.exe`. `craft3d.exe` and `craft3d-cli.exe` are unchanged for terminals and AI agents.
- **Linux: a 3DCraft window that is killed or crashes no longer leaves a `gsettings monitor` process behind.** The window follows the desktop's light or dark setting through that program, which now ends with the window however the window ends.
- **The web version's page says what it is**: its title, *3DCraft in your browser: parametric 3D CAD*, stays while no document is open, and it has a description for search engines.

### Changed

- **The MCP server says more about itself.** It gives its display name, *3DCraft*, to clients that speak protocol version 2025-06-18 or later; its tools carry annotations, so a client knows that `command_list`, `doc_inspect` and `doc_render_preview` only read and that none of the tools reaches beyond your computer; and `command_run`'s description says that opening, saving, importing and exporting files are commands. The tools, their names and their inputs are unchanged.

### Known limitations

- Windows: the running window is `craft3d.exe`, while the Start menu entry starts `3DCraft.exe`, so the taskbar may show two buttons, and *Pin to taskbar* on the running window pins `craft3d.exe`, which opens a console window beside 3DCraft when started from that pin.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-11-11

The first release for people outside the project. 3DCraft is a parametric 3D CAD modeller built with and for AI: you describe a part, an AI agent builds it through 3DCraft's commands, and you watch it appear in the window, point at faces and check the result. Ready-made designs called Jigs need no AI at all: set their sizes in a form and export. Everything can also be done from the command line or a script, and 3DCraft runs in the browser too.

### Modelling

- **A history that rebuilds.** Variables with units and formulas, and an ordered list of features: change a value and the model rebuilds, only from the feature that changed. Undo and redo, roll back to any step, suppress, edit, move, rename and delete features; insert a feature in the middle and the ones after it follow. A feature that fails says why (often with the largest size that fits) and can be repaired; features made from it name it as the cause.
- **Features.** Blocks, cylinders, cones, spheres and tori; extrude, revolve, sweep (along a path, a helix, or two rails that carry and scale the profile, also closed into a ring) and loft from sketches; patterns, mirror and move; shell, draft, offset, move and delete faces, thicken; ISO metric threads (cosmetic or modelled); datum planes, axes, points and helices; sheet bodies; several bodies in one document.
- **Fillets and chamfers** on edges, chosen by name, by description or by pointing at them.
- **Names that stay put.** Faces and edges are named by how they were made, so a fillet does not jump to another edge when an earlier feature changes, and a face that is gone is reported instead of silently replaced.
- **Units.** Millimetres, inches and the other length units per document, angles in degrees or radians; the length unit can be changed later while keeping the sizes. Mass, time, force, pressure and stress, moment and energy in expressions (`1000 N / (100 * (1 mm)^2)` is 10 MPa), so a design rule can say `"max": "235 MPa"`; an output can carry a label and the unit it is shown in. Named sets can pick flat faces by their plate thickness (`thickness`). Litres (`L`, `mL`) for volumes. A document's tolerance can be set (`document.setTolerance`).
- **Colours and materials** per body and per face, with metallic and roughness, following the face through later edits and kept in 3MF, OBJ and STEP exports. A face's own colour wins over its body's; the button on a body's card that clears its face colours (`face.appearance` with `clear`) takes them all away in one step.

### Sketches

- **Sketches with a constraint solver.** Lines, arcs, circles, rectangles, paths, splines (also through computed points, and closed splines that stay smooth where they close) and patterns, held by dimensions and geometric constraints and solved by 3DCraft's own solver; coordinates can be formulas.
- **Clear diagnostics.** A sketch that cannot be solved names the constraint at fault and the ones it contradicts; a line squeezed to nothing and a sketch with freedom left are reported.
- **Curves from formulas** (`sketch.curve`): `x(t)` and `y(t)` in the variables, fitted again whenever they change, as in the involute gear example; points on them and tangents to them can be constrained.
- Sketches on a face, with the x axis turned to a direction you choose.

### Text

- **Text in sketches** (`sketch.text`), straight or along a curve (`sketch.textOnCurve`): a template that can hold formulas, cap height or em size, anchor, alignment, rotation, line spacing and wrapping to a width, as closed profiles of exact curves with kerning, ready to extrude, emboss or engrave. Text and any sketch wrap onto a cylinder or a cone (`feature.extrude` with `wrap`), embossed or engraved along the surface at true size. Letters are named by glyph, so a feature on one fails by name instead of jumping to another.
- **Fonts.** Noto Sans, JetBrains Mono and a single-stroke font drawn for 3DCraft are built in and carried by the installers (fetched when first needed on the web); `font.add` keeps another font in the document when its licence allows it. The measure `textExtent` gives a text's size.

### Booleans

- **Union, subtract and intersect** between bodies of any shape, including curved and freeform faces, and cuts by sheets and between sheets.
- **Sew** (`feature.boolean` with `op: sew`): sheets that meet along their edges, even tangent or in curvature, joined into one sheet or a closed solid without cutting them.
- When an operation cannot be done exactly it refuses with a clear message instead of producing a broken solid.

### Sheets, plates, sheet metal and origami

- **Plates.** Give faces (flat, developable or freeform) a thickness, inside, outside or in the middle, with square, bevel, mitre, touch and over/under joints between neighbouring plates.
- **Developable surfaces and unfolding.** A developable surface between two curves, unfolded flat at true size with a map between the pattern and the part, and a measure of how developable a face is. Rails with corners (a fan from the corner, a crease where both rails turn) and closed rails (a tube), such as a sea kayak's sheer panels.
- **Sheet metal.** A base with flanges, bent by inner radius and K-factor, with rectangular, round and tear corner reliefs; corners closed by overlap, butt or mitre; holes and cut-outs on any flat; a bend table; checks for bend radius, flange length, hole-to-bend distance and relief; the flat pattern exported with its bend lines. Holes across a bend, and a later through cut carried back onto the flat pattern. Flanges inset on an edge (several tabs), on a flange's side, on a hole's edge or with an outline of their own, with bend reliefs at their ends. A press-brake check (`sheetMetal.bendSequence`) that searches an order of bends and proves the punch and die clear the part, or checks an order you give, also for parts with holes across their bends. Louvres and tabs: a flange on the edge of a cut-out in any flat. The measures `flatExtent` and `flatArea` for a flat pattern's size. Sharp bends (an inner radius of 0); curved flanges along arcs and circles of the base (a round pan, a tray with rounded corners), with the stretch of their approximated flat pattern proven (`bendStrain`); jogs that step a flat; formed louvres pressed by a tool (`louvreStretch`); and hems (flanges bent 180 degrees), which the press-brake check presses flat in two strokes.
- **Cutting.** Plates and sheet-metal parts recognised for cutting: a cut list grouped by thickness and material, identical parts counted and sizes given to the document's tolerance (`cut.list`, JSON or CSV), and every part's outline to DXF or SVG, per part or on one sheet, with a kerf (`cut.export`).
- **Origami.** A crease pattern of mountains, valleys and cuts folded by angles that follow variables, or folded flat with the order of the layers worked out; Kawasaki and Maekawa checks; the crease pattern exported to SVG and DXF. Thick panels on a membrane, slits and slots, a flat-folded stack folded on, and patterns of thousands of creases.

### Freeform surfacing

- **Analysis.** Zebra stripes, highlight lines, a curvature map and a draft check drawn on the exact surfaces, curvature combs on edges and iso-curves, and the measures `draft` and `curvature` at a point (the Analysis toggle in the window).
- **Surfaces from curve networks.** Gordon surfaces through crossing u and v curves (`feature.surface.network`), Coons patches (`feature.surface.coons`) and lofts with guide curves, as exact B-splines.
- **Continuity across seams.** An edge held in position, tangent (G1) or in curvature (G2) to a plane, cylinder, cone, sphere or a spline face, and `measure.continuity`, which proves G0, G1 or G2 with bounded gaps (also as an assertion). A curvature seam spreads its change over the sheet with the least variation of curvature, so the surface stays fair beside the seam.
- **Trimmed faces and N-sided fills.** A face cut out of an existing surface by curves on it, drawn on it or projected onto it, such as an opening or a label area (`feature.surface.trim`), and a loop of 3 to 6 curves filled with a smooth sheet that meets its neighbours tangent or in curvature (`feature.surface.nsided`).
- Zebra stripes and highlight lines stay clean where they are finer than a pixel: they fade to grey instead of breaking into moiré.

### Assemblies and motion

- **Assemblies** as their own documents (`.3dcraftasm`): instances of other `.3dcraft` files by reference (the assembly follows the part when you edit it) or packed into one file, configurations by a part's public parameters, one body picked from a multi-body part, and rigid or flexible subassemblies. A part whose feature fails marks its instance with the reason and fails the assembly's check.
- **Mates, joints and couplings**, solved by 3DCraft's own solver: fixed, revolute and the other mates between named faces of instances (a face that is gone fails its mate by name), free joints set by value or dragged, gears, racks and screws coupling two mates (a screw can also tie a cylindrical mate's slide to its own turn, as a thread), and linear couplings of any number of motions (a planetary stage). A joint's solved value and an instance's pose as measures, usable in checks and formulas (`measure.joint`, `measure.pose`). A part drawn in another length unit keeps its size. Measures and checks name an instance's bodies by path (`Instance/Body`, also into subassemblies); clearance, overlap and mass per instance; STEP export with the assembly's structure.
- **Motion over a timeline `t`.** Play a mechanism in the window, see collisions in red, draw the trace of a point (and export it to SVG or DXF), and check any assertion over the whole motion (`motion.check`). Dead points are reported with their `t`.

### Measuring and design checks

- **Measures.** Volume, area, mass and inertia, distances, clearance, wall thickness, holes that go through, rays and symmetry; what lies below a plane, the section by a plane and its moments, and the level that holds a given volume. The floating position (level and tilt) of a hull for a given weight and centre of gravity, with its stability. A section's torsion constant, shear centre and warping constant, for any section with holes.
- **Assertions** kept in the document ("this wall is at least 2 mm"), checked at every rebuild and shown in the window, optionally only when a condition holds. A bound is met within the document's tolerance (an angle within its angular tolerance), and a bound finer than that is warned about; the line over the feature list counts the checks that pass, fail or are not proven. Read-only **outputs** computed from measures and formulas.
- **Selections.** Queries ("every 8 mm hole"), a selection shared between you and the agent, and named sets.

### Formulas and Jigs

- **Formulas that decide.** Comparisons, `and`, `or` and `not`, `if`, `choose` and `clamp`, and the usual functions in any expression; tables of values looked up by row or key (`belt["GT2"].pitch`); features switched on and off by a condition, so a yes-or-no parameter adds or removes a lid. A part switched off is left out of the exports, cut lists and measures that name it, so one list serves every option, and a check on it alone is inactive.
- **Jigs: designs anyone can set without AI.** Publish the variables that drive a design as parameters (number, whole number, yes or no, a choice; with a label, group, range and step) and export it as a `.3dcraftjig` (File › Export as Jig…). Opening a Jig shows its **Parameters** tab: drag or type values and the model follows; values are kept in range and the form says so; a value that breaks the model is marked, with *Back to last good*. A Jig is never overwritten: Save saves the result as a model, and *Open for editing* makes an editable copy.
- `craft3d-cli check-range` proves a Jig over its whole parameter range. The separate 3DCraft Jigs collection has Jigs to try, from a laser-cut box and a GT2 pulley to a planetary gearbox and a sundial for your place.

### Files and export

- **The native `.3dcraft` format**, with a promise: every file a released version saves opens in every later version and rebuilds to the same bodies.
- **Import** STEP: solids, sheets and assemblies, with colours; FreeCAD documents (`.FCStd`) and BRep files: the visible final objects' solids, with their names, placements and colours.
- **Export** STEP (the exact geometry), STL, 3MF and OBJ (meshes at Coarse, Normal or Fine quality, with colours), and DXF and SVG: the flat outline of a sketch or of faces at true size, a whole cut sheet of a named set laid out to your laser's width, flat and crease patterns, or a drawing of the model as seen (orthographic to scale or perspective, hidden lines left out or dashed), its edges written as the lines, arcs, circles, ellipses and splines they are.
- An export dialog that first asks what goes into the file and offers only the formats that hold it. Bodies hidden in the window are left out of a 3D export of the model and of the cut list, and the dialog says which.
- Saving from the window never replaces a file you were not asked about, and an assembly is saved as `.3dcraftasm`, on the desktop and in the browser.

### The window and installing

- **The window.** A tab per document with its own view; a feature list with an eye per feature and a double click (or F) that flies the camera to it; a history player like a video player; a view cube, see-through bodies and a section plane; File, Edit, View and Help menus with keyboard shortcuts (F1), and light or dark panels; a status bar that says what was done. It draws with your graphics card, or on the processor when there is none. The interface is set in Inter, with JetBrains Mono for code.
- **A start screen** with New part, New assembly and Open, your recent files, the panel that connects an AI agent, and twenty examples built in, so they open offline and in the browser, under three tabs: ten finished parts (a steel bookend and a T-slot profile marked *Start here*, then a moka pot, a ship propeller, a snap-fit enclosure, a hydraulic cylinder, a spiral staircase, a solitaire ring, a flat-pack bookcase and a mailer box), six Jigs that open on their form (a laser-cut box, a Gridfinity bin, a dovetail drawer, a sheet-metal enclosure, a planetary gearbox and a sundial for your place) and four assemblies that open with their player, ready to play (a toggle clamp, a Jansen walker, a scissor jack and a radial engine). An example opens in a tab named after it and counts as unchanged until you edit it; Save asks where. *More examples* opens the tab's part of the galleries on the website. The examples are MIT, the Jigs and assemblies CC BY 4.0 (`EXAMPLES-LICENSE.md`).
- **Check for updates**: Help › Check for updates…, a link beside the version on the start screen, and the About dialog open the website in your browser, which says whether there is a newer version; 3DCraft itself connects nowhere. The AppImage carries update information, so a tool such as AppImageLauncher can update it. The guide says how to install a newer version of each package.
- **Autosave and crash recovery.** Unsaved work is saved in the background, and the status bar says when for the document in front; after a crash the window offers to recover it. Closing the window or a tab asks before unsaved changes are lost, naming the document.
- **Clear errors and bug reports.** A failed file action shows a dialog with what went wrong; after any failed command the status bar offers *Copy details*, a short report to paste into a bug report.
- **Installers** for Linux (an AppImage, and a tarball whose `install.sh` installs 3DCraft into `~/.local` and whose `uninstall.sh` removes exactly what it installed), macOS (a `.dmg` for Apple Silicon) and Windows (a `.msi` installer, and a zip), each with the fonts 3DCraft draws with. The `.msi`, the tarball and the macOS app register the `.3dcraft`, `.3dcraftjig` and `.3dcraftasm` file types; the AppImage brings its menu entry and file types through a tool such as AppImageLauncher.
- **A user guide** for first-time users, in 22 chapters and six parts, with pictures of this version's window: installing (and installing a newer version) and connecting an agent, your first five minutes, working with an AI agent with five tasks to try, the window, Jigs, assemblies and files; how 3DCraft thinks, a first part, formulas, features, sheet metal, surfaces, origami and selection; measuring and engineering checks; making Jigs, the command line and programs on 3DCraft; troubleshooting and a reference. Seven lessons, from an L-bracket to an involute gear, check their own volume. Help › User guide opens the guide of your version on 3dcraft.dev.

### Rendering

- **A studio look**: image-based lighting from three bundled environments, soft shadows, ambient occlusion, a ground that catches shadows and reflections, contact shadows, depth of field and bloom, refined while the camera stands still; a view setting that never changes the document.
- **Materials** kept in the document: anisotropic metal, clear coat paint, glass with refraction and subsurface wrap, with six presets (`material.presets`); solid textures (wood, marble, granite, speckle, noise, brushed metal, carbon) evaluated in the model's space; exports carry what their formats hold.
- Both looks are drawn by the graphics card and by the processor alike, the processor's picture being the reference the graphics card is tested against. Pictures without a window (`view.render`, `view.replay`, `craft3d-cli render`) use the graphics card when there is one (`renderer: auto|cpu|gpu`).
- **Animations** (`view.replay`): variables moved along tracks with easing, the motion timeline, a camera path with orbit and dolly, the look, frames per second and duration, written as PNG frames or, through an installed ffmpeg, as MP4, WebM or GIF. The window records itself too (`ui.record`), choosing an encoder your ffmpeg actually has.
- **The same model on every computer.** 3DCraft computes its sines, cosines and other functions itself instead of using the system's maths library, so a document rebuilds to the same bodies whatever the platform.

### Web

- **3DCraft in the browser**: the same program as a web page (WebGPU, or WebGL2, see-through and sections included), with nothing to install. Open, save, import and export with your system's file dialogs in Chrome, Edge and on Chromebooks, as downloads elsewhere; use Jigs (a Jig on the same site opens with `?open=`), play assemblies and open the start screen's examples, which are built in. The model is rebuilt, measured and exported in the background, so the page stays responsive.

### AI agents and the command line

- **Drive it with an AI agent.** A built-in MCP server (`craft3d-cli mcp`) that any MCP client can start: headless, or bridged to the open window so you see every step as the agent works. The **Connect an AI agent** panel on the start screen gives the command for Claude Code and the configuration for Claude Desktop, Cursor, VS Code or any other MCP client, ready to copy.
- **Allow AI agents.** One switch, on the start screen and behind the agents pill in the status bar, lets agents connect to the window with no flags and nothing secret to copy. They read and write files in a work folder you choose; the window asks you before an agent reaches any other folder, and never offers your home folder itself or the folders of keys and settings. The **Agent** tab of the side panel shows what agents did to the document, newest first, and a feature an agent made carries a mark (✦); `agent.log` gives an agent the same.
- **Everything is a command**, with a name and plain JSON parameters; the same commands run from the command line, a script or the JSON control channel, and the agent can light up faces, fly the camera, play the history, take pictures of the window, record it as a video (`ui.record`) and get the bug report (`session.report`).
- **The command line.** `craft3d-cli` runs commands and scripts, prints a document's state, renders pictures, converts files, draws a model as seen to DXF or SVG, makes a video of how a design is built, scores a design against a task, proves a Jig over its range, and serves MCP and a JSON-lines protocol.

### Known limitations

- Tested on Linux. The macOS and Windows packages are new and less tested: each is built and started on its system, but the full test suite has not passed on those systems yet.
- The installers are not signed, so Windows and macOS warn the first time (the guide shows how to open them).
- macOS: Apple Silicon only (M1 or later), and double-clicking a `.3dcraft` file there starts 3DCraft without opening the file (use File › Open).
- Windows: `craft3d.exe` and `craft3d-cli.exe` show a generic icon in Explorer; the Start menu entry, the file types and the window have 3DCraft's.
- Linux: the packages need glibc 2.35 or newer, as on Ubuntu 22.04 and later.
- 3DCraft does not update itself: Check for updates opens the website, and you install a newer version as the guide says.
- The window has no toolbar of modelling buttons: you model through commands, sent by an AI agent, a Jig's form or a script of your own, and the window is for watching, pointing, checking and exporting.
- An AI agent cannot drive the web version yet.
- Some modelling cases are refused rather than built (the message says which): among them some booleans and freeform fillets, curved sheet-metal bends along a spline and some sharp ones, curved origami creases, and text wrapped onto a surface other than a cylinder or a cone. Text is laid out glyph by glyph with kerning; shaping for complex scripts (such as Arabic or the Indic scripts) is for a later version. Every refusal reported helps.
