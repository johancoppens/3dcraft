# Changelog

All notable changes to 3DCraft are listed here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html). Until 1.0, a minor version may change behaviour; files saved by any released version keep opening in every later one.

## [Unreleased]

### Results that changed

What 3DCraft answered before can differ now: a measure, a proof, a check, a body that was built wrong while it looked right, or a body that was built and is now refused. If you relied on one of these, check it again.

- **Two parts that touch where a curve of the one leaves the border of a face of the other are told to touch.** A wheel on a keyed shaft, its bore on the shaft's seat and its own keyway as wide as the shaft's: the rim of the keyway's rounded end in the shaft is tangent to the side of the wheel's keyway, and stays within the tolerance of it for a while. Where the shaft had a vertex on that rim between one and four tolerances off the side (3DCraft leaves one where its trace of the rim began; an end drawn as two arcs has one too), the clearance could not be measured (*"an imprinted curve that does not fit the edges it should coincide with"*, or *"a curve that is partly on and partly off a face boundary"*), `document.interference` answered `unknown`, and a union, a subtract or an intersect of the two parts was refused with the same words; 0.1.x refused the same bodies. Such a curve is now read as leaving the face (or as running into it, where it leaves the border inwards: a boss on a plate that reaches the plate's edge), so the clearance is a touch, and a boolean of the two parts is built, or refused only where its result would be too thin (a corner within twice the tolerance of an edge it is not on). The gear reducer Jig met this at one setting of its range, with two wheels on their keyed shafts (0.2.0's known limitations). Every body that was built before is built the same.
- **Two parts that touch in one point, where an edge of the one meets the other and no corner of either lies there, are told to touch.** A cylinder standing beside a plate at the level of its top, its rim touching the plate's top edge in one point; a ball against the rim of a cylinder; a cylinder standing rim to rim on another; a cylinder sunk beside a wall that is a spline surface: `document.interference` and `measure.clearance` answered `clear`, at a distance of 0, because no corner of either part lies at that place. They answer `touch` now, also where the two are less than a tolerance apart at such a place, or into each other by less than half a tolerance. The clearance itself was 0 and is 0; whatever reads the word `clear`, or counts the pairs that touch, gets another answer. Two parts that touched already somewhere else have such a place among the places of their contact as well. A union of such a pair was already refused as two parts that touch in a point; an assertion of a clearance of at least 0 passes as it did.
- **A flat face that all but lies in a lofted or extruded spline surface is answered, and curves where two surfaces cross at a very shallow angle are where the surfaces cross.** A spar in the notch of a rib, its flat bottom level with the wing's skin the notch was cut through and following the skin's lowest line along the span: the bottom's plane and the skin's surface cross at a few thousandths of a degree, in a curve that turns round within a few hundredths of a millimetre. There `measure.clearance` could not measure and `document.interference` answered `unknown` (*"marching an intersection curve stalled"*), and a union, a subtract or an intersect of the two parts was refused with the same words; whether it happened depended on the last digits of the design, and 0.1.x refused the same booleans. Such a pair is now answered. Where it was answered before, the curve in which the two surfaces cross could lie up to about two tolerances beside the true one near such a turn, while on both surfaces; it is now within about a tolerance of it (within one in what was tested, 1.1 at the shallowest crossings measured), so a body cut along such a curve can differ there by that much. The same change corrects a wrong answer: a very large ball sunk a few tolerances into the side of a small cone or cylinder (a ball of 10 m against a part of a few millimetres, 0.003 to 0.01 thousandths of a millimetre deep at the default tolerance) was told to be clear of it, and a check of a clearance of at least 0, or of no overlap, passed; it is told to overlap now, and those checks fail. That holds for larger balls too: over a sweep of balls of 1 m to 1 km against the same small parts, from just outside to deep inside, 179 of the 182 that overlap are told to overlap and none is told to be clear (before, 108 were told and 13 were told to be clear), and every one that only touches is told to touch. A very large cylinder, cone, ring, flat face or spline surface (thousands of times the part and more) that crosses a small part at a shallow angle is not answered so yet: where the curve cannot be placed, the boolean or the measure is refused and says so (*"an intersection curve cannot be placed within the tolerance where the surfaces cross this shallowly: one of them is so much larger than the part…"*); against a cylinder of 1 km radius that is a crossing shallower than about a quarter to half a degree, against one of 30 m shallower than a fiftieth of a degree. Apart from that, where surfaces cross at more than a tenth of a degree no answer changes: parts far from the origin, and a small part that crosses a very large surface squarely, are answered as before. One thing does differ in its last digits: a body in which a cone meets a cone (a countersink that runs into a chamfer, two tapers that cross). The curve where the two cones cross is worked out through other points, so such a body's volume, area, centre of gravity and inertia can differ from about their 15th digit on, a clearance next to it in its 16th, a file exported from it in its bytes, and where two faces are exactly equally near, which of the two a clearance names; every verdict (clear, touching, overlapping, an assertion passed or failed) stays what it was. Still refused, in other words (*"an intersection curve could not be fitted within the tolerance"*): a flat face level with a spline skin whose section rises and dips again beside its lowest line, so that the plane crosses it in several curves a few millimetres apart.
- **A plane that lies in the floor of a pocket or a notch, in a wall of a hollow, or in a face of a mirrored body is measured.** `measure.below`, `measure.section`, `measure.levelFor` and `measure.floating`, and the checks `volumeBelow`, `centroidBelow`, `sectionArea`, `sectionCentroid`, `sectionInertia`, `sectionTorsion`, `sectionShearCentre` and `levelFor`, refused such a plane with *"the section of body 0: a face in the plane whose loops enclose no area"*, where a plane cut by the same plane was built: the volume under the floor of a pocket or a notch cut into a block, the part of a shelled box beside the plane of its inner wall or floor, the part of a closed hollow over the plane of its ceiling, a level that falls exactly on such a face (the level for the volume under a pocket's floor), each face that faces away from what is below. They answer now, by the same rule as for a step on the outside of a part (a block with a shoulder): the volume on that side, and a section that takes in the face lying in the plane together with the cut through the walls around it. A level search that landed on such a face, and a floating body whose waterline falls on one, answer where they were refused. The faces that a subtraction turns over, a shell's inner faces, a mirror's faces and the faces of an imported part that its file stores turned over (a STEP face whose sense is reversed) were the ones refused; a plane in a face of a part built by joining blocks was measured before and is measured the same. Every answer given before is given the same; only refusals become answers, also where a refusal was one part of a larger answer: a search from an unstable equilibrium that `measure.floating` listed under `unfinished` with these words now goes on to the equilibrium it reaches (which is then listed, and `floats` may name it), and an assertion that a check or a sample reported as an error with them now passes or fails.

### Added

- **A body cut by planes in one feature** (`feature.planeCut`): a corner taken off a block, a roof, a part trimmed to a datum, a model opened up to show its inside. Give the body and the planes, each a base plane, a datum plane, a flat face of an earlier body, or a point and a normal as numbers or expressions, and which side of each to keep (`side`: `below`, the side the normal points away from, or `above`); several planes keep what lies on the kept side of every one of them. Nothing is drawn or sized for the cut: it reaches as far as the body does. Until now each plane took a datum plane, a sketch, a rectangle, an extrusion and a boolean, so a prism trimmed by 24 planes carried a history of about a hundred entries; it has three now. The face a plane cuts is named by the plane's own number (`F9:cut(3)`), so a fillet, a sketch or a mate on it stays on it when planes are added, deleted or put in another order. The planes are cut exactly as given and nothing is snapped: planes that nearly meet in a point leave the small faces between them, and where faces or points come within the document's tolerance of each other the feature fails and says which plane and which faces (planes meant to meet in one point: give them the same point; planes worked out by a program: give the numbers unrounded). The result says for each plane whether it took anything away and how many faces it left, and warns of a plane that changes nothing. A sheet cannot be cut this way yet. Asked for by the first person to build on 3DCraft as a library ([issue 3](https://github.com/johancoppens/3dcraft/issues/3)). Documents are saved as format version 78 from now on, which 0.2.x does not open (it says that a newer 3DCraft is needed); every older file opens unchanged.
- **glTF export (`.glb`), for showing a model in a web page, a game engine or a renderer.** `file.export` with a `.glb` name (or `"format": "glb"`, and `craft3d-cli convert part.3dcraft part.glb`) writes glTF 2.0 binary: the triangles the other mesh exports make at the same quality, with the surfaces' normals (a cylinder shades smooth, a box keeps its edges), in metres with +Y up as glTF wants (3DCraft's +Z turned to +Y in the coordinates, whatever the document's unit); a node per body named after its feature; in each body's mesh a primitive per face name and look, which names the face in its `extras` as `document.inspect` does, so a program can tell which triangles are which face, or, with `"primitives": "looks"` (`--primitives looks`), a primitive per look of each body, for a page shown on phones (a body of a thousand faces in a few draw calls, the same triangles, no face names; a smaller file, though often a larger one once a web server compresses it); a material per look, with the colour, metallic and roughness, and a clear coat, transmission and index of refraction in their standard extensions. The file says the document's unit and how to turn its axes back (`asset.extras`), and the result counts the points a model far from the origin loses in the file's 32-bit numbers (`mergedPoints`), as for binary STL. What glTF cannot hold (anisotropy, subsurface scattering, solid textures, the refraction through thick glass) is left out and listed in the result's `leftOut`. Checked with Khronos's glTF-Validator. In the window and in the web version, **File › Export…** offers it as **glTF**, for all bodies or the selected ones, with the quality of the other meshes and a **Grouping**: *By face* (each face apart with its name, the default) or *By colour* (quicker on a web page); the status line after the export names what glTF cannot show. Asked for in [issue 3](https://github.com/johancoppens/3dcraft/issues/3).
- **An MCP bundle in every release.** The release carries one more file, `3dcraft-<version>.mcpb`: an MCP bundle with the command-line program for Linux, macOS (Apple Silicon) and Windows, made for the MCP clients that install such bundles, such as Claude Desktop. It starts 3DCraft without a window, `craft3d-cli mcp`, reading and writing only the work folder you choose when you install it (a folder of its own, such as `3DCraft` in your documents; it is made when it does not exist yet); your AI assistant then models there, and shows you pictures it renders. To watch it build in the window instead, install 3DCraft and connect it as the guide says. `SHA256SUMS.txt` lists the bundle with the other files. It was installed and used in a Linux build of Claude Desktop on 2026-11-20; in Claude Desktop on Windows and macOS it has not been tried. On a Mac with an Intel processor its server does not start: 3DCraft for macOS is built for Apple Silicon only.

### Changed

- **`craft3d-cli serve` and `mcp` make a write folder that does not exist yet.** When the folder given with `--automation-write-root` is missing and its parent folder exists, the server makes that one folder as it starts, then works as before; until now it refused to start. A folder given with `--automation-read-root` must still exist (the same folder given for both, as the MCP bundle gives it, is made and then found). A folder that starts with a variable no one filled in, such as `${DOCUMENTS}/3DCraft`, is refused with a message that says to choose a folder. What the commands answer does not change.

### Fixed

- **The window no longer says an assembly is *Solved*, or its parameters *Valid*, while an instance, a mate, a coupling or the solve fails.** Since 0.2.1 the check of an assembly is not ok while an instance is not placed, a feature of an instance's part failed, a mate or a coupling takes no part, the solve failed, or any of these inside a subassembly, but the window did not follow it: the Assembly tab's heading said *Solved* whenever the parts that were placed could be solved, a coupling that takes no part and what was wrong with a subassembly's own mates showed nowhere, a flexible subassembly's instance that was not placed was not listed, and the form of an assembly's public parameters said *Valid* unless a value left an instance unplaced. Now the heading says *Solved* only while the check finds none of these, and otherwise *Not solved*, *Not solved · 2 failed* when other problems stand beside it, or how many problems the check lists (*2 failed*); point at it for each (the first six, then how many more), and the headers *Instances* and *Mates* say how many are theirs (*1 failed*), also when folded. Each is on the row it names, written plainly (names without the check's backticks, numbers as the window writes them): an instance that is not placed is red with why; a part whose feature failed has a red dot after its name and why under it; a subassembly with something wrong in its own mates or solve has the dot and what is wrong under it (*its mate Ground: its instance Plate is not placed*), and so does every row above a problem; a coupling that takes no part is a red row after the mates, with why; a flexible subassembly's instance that is not placed is a red row under it, as in a rigid one. The ⚠ after a part's file name now means only that it is held to a version its file no longer has. Beside the heading stand the assembly's checks, as on the Features tab (*1 of 3 checks fails*), on a line of their own where the panel is too narrow for both. The form says what the heading would say at rest in the place of *Valid*, with the first problem under it and how many more the Assembly tab lists, and *Breaks the model* for a value that makes any of these fail; it weighs the assembly at rest, as the check does, also while a moment of its motion is shown; and in the web version it says *Regenerating…* until the worker has said, also when a value is set while a moment is shown, and *Not checked* where the worker could not say it (it stopped), never *Valid*. Over an assembly none of whose instances is placed, the view says *Nothing is placed.* An agent reads the same with `ui.get` (`assembly` with `instancesFailed` and `matesFailed`, a row's `inside` and `marked`, the form's `status` and `why`).
- **A chamfer or a fillet that failed with an internal error now says why it cannot be built.** Where the edge ends against a face at a slant to it (the rim of a pocket that ends in a ramp, the rim of a pit with sloping sides), a chamfer or a fillet so large that it would lie wholly beyond that face failed with *"internal error … the chamfer lost track of an entity"* (or *"… the fillet lost track of an entity"*); on the pocket's rim, 10 mm long above a wall 3 mm high, a chamfer of 10 mm or more, or a fillet of about 17 mm or more. 0.2.1 and earlier did the same. It is refused now as any blend that is too large is: too large, naming the edge and the face where it stops fitting and the size the largest that fits lies below (on the pocket the wall below the rim, *"the largest distance that fits there is below 3 mm"*), and a size below that builds; or, where no smaller size builds either, *"the chamfer face came out in pieces or not at all: the distance is too large for what is near the edge"*. A chamfer or fillet that was built before is built the same.

## [0.2.1] - 2026-11-20

Three fixes. In the web version, what you select is measured, as it is in the installed 3DCraft. *Keep as a check* no longer says it kept a check that was refused. And the check of an assembly no longer says ok while an instance is not placed, or a mate, a coupling or the solve failed. Files of 0.2.0 open unchanged.

### Results that changed

What 3DCraft answered before can differ now: a measure, a proof, a check, a body that was built wrong while it looked right, or a body that was built and is now refused. If you relied on one of these, check it again.

- **The check of an assembly is no longer ok while an instance is not placed, or a mate, a coupling or the solve failed.** `document.check` asked an assembly only whether a feature of an instance's part had failed. An instance that is not placed (a value outside its part's range, a source file that is gone or is not a 3DCraft document, a body it picks that is gone), a mate or a coupling that takes no part, mates or drivers that cannot all be met, and any of these inside a subassembly left the check `ok` with no problem, while `assembly.inspect` and `assembly.solve` said they failed (for what is inside a subassembly, the subassembly's own; in 0.1.3 and 0.2.0 alike). Each is now a problem, with the reason those commands give: an instance (*instance `Plate` is not placed: …*), a mate or a coupling by its name, the solve (`"solve": "failed"`), and what is wrong inside a subassembly under its instance. So `document.sample` and `craft3d-cli check-range` fail such a point of an assembly too, and `document.inspect`, `craft3d-cli info` and the MCP tool `doc_inspect` answer `ok: false` for such an assembly as well: their `ok` says that nothing failed to build or, in an assembly, to be placed (whether the checks pass is still `document.check`'s to say). Only assemblies that were wrong answer differently: one that is right answers as before, to the byte, as the published assemblies do. An assembly that reads not ok now was not what it showed before either: mend what each problem names (values its part takes, its file back, a mate on a face the instance shows) and it reads ok again. A check that measures an instance that is not placed (a clearance on it) now says that it is not placed, not that there is no feature of its name.

### Fixed

- **In the web version, what is selected is measured.** One thing selected showed only what its name says (an edge the two faces it lies between, and its name), with no number in the card or in the view, and two things selected were not measured at all, although the status bar's hint said to shift-click another to measure between; *Wall thickness* and *Keep as a check* were not offered. The web version builds the model beside the page, and these measures were taken only where the page had built the model itself. They are now taken beside the page too: the card says *Measuring…* until they come, then shows what the installed 3DCraft shows (the number with what it is and the facts under it, the dimension in the view, the relation of two things with *Keep as a check*), and *Not measured*, with why when you point at it, where none came. The installed 3DCraft is unchanged.
- **Keep as a check no longer says it kept a check it did not keep.** On a Jig in use (opened from its .3dcraftjig file, so only its parameters change), *Keep as a check* was offered, and pressed, the status bar said *Kept as the check …* while the check was refused, the refusal shown only on the Features tab. There the button is now greyed, with the reason when you point at it (also on a document that holds as many checks as one may, 1000), and the status bar says a check was kept only once it was made, and *Not kept as a check* with why where it was refused. In the installed 3DCraft and in the web version alike.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-11-19

Notes pinned to the model, on which you and an AI agent answer each other; two things selected measured by themselves; a section you drag, lay on a face or through a hole's axis; fillets that fade to a point, rounded tips and sheets through grids of points. Results that changed come first: in most designs only last digits move, but if you checked a design with 0.1, check it again. Files saved by 0.2.0 do not open in 0.1.x.

### Results that changed

What 3DCraft answered before can differ now: a measure, a proof, a check, a body that was built wrong while it looked right, or a body that was built and is now refused. If you relied on one of these, check it again.

- **Most designs measure a little differently, in their last digits.** Measured against 0.1.3 on 112 documents (the 65 published examples, Jigs and assemblies, and 47 showcase and example models of 3DCraft's own): every feature builds as it did and every body has the same shape, to rounding, but in 46 of them the volume, area or centre of mass of a part made by a boolean, a sweep, a loft, a revolve or a fillet moved, by up to 13 parts in a million (a sailing yacht's hull of 0.6 mm laminate: 156 199.10 mm³, now 156 201.14), always within the bound `document.measure` gives with `"bound": true`, and the largest move held to a closed form went towards it (a welded truss's coped diagonals: 12.60 mm³ over it before, 1.31 now). The masses, centres of mass, outputs and assertion values worked out from them move with them. Proven values (`atLeast`) are now never above what they bound, which lowers many of them in their last digits (an assertion's message then gives the proven value beside the one found), and some are proven tighter (a clock's shaft hole clear of its bushing by 0.2197 mm, where 0.1.3 proved 0). A STEP file of a part may describe the same surfaces differently (a cylinder's origin slid along its axis, a circle's reference direction turned). Of the 65 published documents, 6 give every number as 0.1.3 did. The entries below say why.
- **An edge that passes close by the point of a cone or the pole of a ball is proven to lie on its face, where it does.** An edge that runs past the tip of a cone, or past the pole of a sphere or of a surface turned about an axis it closes on (a hyperbola or a parabola where a plane cuts a cone near its point, written as one long curve; an arc that passes over a ball's top a little to one side), could not be proven on its face although it lay on it: a STEP file with such an edge was refused, or read with the edge counted as unproven (`unprovenEdges`). Such edges are proven now, and one a little off its face gets its own tolerance, as any other does. An edge that is off its face by more than the tolerance is still never taken as on it, and nothing proven before is proven differently.
- **An imported surface of revolution whose profile crosses the axis it is turned about is read on the part of the profile its face lies on, or refused.** A STEP or FreeCAD file whose turned profile (a B-spline, an ellipse, a trimmed curve) runs from one side of its axis to the other, such as a dish whose profile is written across its centre, was imported as a surface swept twice: the part looked right and could not be measured. Each face is now read on the part its edges lie on, with the right volume; where the two parts mirror each other, so that the edges lie on both, which one the face means is not in what is read, and the file is refused saying so. A profile that lies out of its axis's plane by no more than the file's uncertainty (rounding, a turned placement, a part far from the origin) is read so too; one farther out is turned whole and its face read only where that turn can carry it (a cup that would run through the axis onto the other side is refused, saying so). A profile that only starts or ends on its axis reads as before, bit for bit.
- **A clearance could be reported as proven for parts that overlap.** Where the faces of two bodies passed through each other, but no corner of either lay inside the other, `clearance` could say the bodies were clear and prove a gap between them, and an assertion of a minimum clearance passed, although the bodies shared volume. Such a pair is now an overlap. The distance between faces (`distance`), the proof of a wall thickness and the press-brake check (`bendable`) shared the fault and are fixed with it. A pair whose gap is not proven above twice the tolerance is now told by the kernel, as a pair found that close always was; where the kernel cannot tell, the clearance cannot be measured and says so, where 0.1.x answered from the gap it found (a wing's spar found 0.05 mm from its ribs, at a tolerance of 0.01 mm with nothing proven, at 2 of the 53 settings of the published RC wing Jig tried).
- **A clearance, a distance or a wall thickness could be reported as proven next to a cone that comes to its point, for parts that are closer or even overlap.** Where a body has a cone with its tip (a `feature.cone`, a profile turned about an axis it ends on at a slant, the point of a drilled hole), what `clearance`, `distance` and `thickness` proved next to that cone (`atLeast`) was only the value they had found, however far that was from the truth: a ball sunk 0.0001 mm into the side of a slender cone was answered clear with a proven gap of 0.00076 mm, and an assertion of a minimum clearance passed; a gap of 0.000003 mm was proven as 0.0004 mm; the floor under a drill point was proven 0.00000004 mm thicker than it is. What is proven is now never above the true gap or thickness, and the ball sunk 0.0001 mm into the cone is an overlap. What may now read differently: `atLeast` next to such a cone, which was the value found and is now what is proven (under a drill point that leaves 0.5 mm of floor, 0.4999999999999 mm); the thickness of a body with a sharp cone (its sides less than a right angle apart), which is proven as 0 at the tip, as thin as the tip is; an assertion of a minimum that passed on such a value now fails where the parts are closer than it asks; and one of a gap of only a few tolerances next to such a cone may now be *not proven* where it passed, the proof ending with its work before it reaches so small a gap. Such a check now does the work of a proof (tenths of a second to a few seconds where it took none), and that work is bounded: beside a cone's tip with a curved cut a hair from it, where telling a point in the face from out of it is dear, the proof stops telling after a fixed amount of work and gives what it has proven by then, which may be less, down to nothing. Still owed: parts that overlap by only a few tolerances, one of them thousands of times the size of the other (a ball of 10 m radius sunk 0.00001 mm into the side of a cone 12 mm high, or 0.000003 mm into a cylinder's), may still be answered clear, with nothing proven (`atLeast` 0), and an assertion of a clearance of at least 0 passes on them; this is so with or without a cone's tip, and was before. A cone cut short of its point (a countersink, a chamfer on a hole) is proven as before.
- **A proven distance to a sphere or a torus could be slightly too high.** The allowance for how far a sphere's or a torus's surface can lie from the triangles it is measured on was too small (a quarter for a sphere; for a torus it ignored where its surface turns slowly), so `atLeast` of a distance, clearance or thickness that such a face decided could be a little above the truth. It can now be a little lower than before.
- **A proven distance could be above the truth for faces nearly parallel or very thin.** Where the triangles a distance is measured on were long and nearly parallel, or one was a long thin sliver, rounding could make the proven `atLeast` larger than the real gap (micrometres for faces a fraction of a nanometre apart). The proven value is now never above the gap.
- **A proof near nearly flat freeform faces rested on a bound of their normals that could be too narrow** by up to 1.5e-8 rad, so a proven distance, clearance or thickness could rule out a pair of pieces it should have looked at. The bound is right to rounding now; a proof is never less careful than before.
- **A measured volume could be wrong, by far more than the change it was to show, after a face of a long freeform part was offset or moved.** Offsetting the 0.02 mm² end cap of a 20 m lofted blade outward by 0.055 mm made `document.measure` report the part 10 mm³ *smaller*, where the cap adds 0.0013 mm³; on another copy of the same blade it reported 10 mm³ more. The part itself was right; its measured volume (and centroid) was not, wherever an edge lies within the modelling tolerance of its face but not exactly on it, and most where that face lies far from the middle of the part. The measure now follows the points of such edges as they lie on the face. And offset face, shell, thicken, move face, draft and delete face no longer make an edge between two faces that do not move again as an approximate curve where it lies along the border of one of them: it is that border, exactly, between its new ends (the leading and trailing edges of the blade, when only its end cap moves); lines and circles stay lines and circles. A part whose edges lie on their faces measures as before, to the last digit; a part with approximate edges may measure a little differently, mostly in the last digits (a freeform impeller by 4 parts in ten million, well within the accuracy the measure reports with `bound`).
- **A face that closes in a point, as a STEP file can hold one, is measured right where its edges into that point run along a line of its surface.** A face with one border in a single point (the end of a loft to a point in other modellers) whose point stands near a corner of its rim, or over a thin section, was measured a few millionths to a few thousandths short by `document.measure`, the assertions and everything else that measures: a blade of 1000 × 1 mm with a rounded end 1 mm long, read from a STEP file, measured 666.48 mm³ for 666.67. For every such file 0.1.x reads, the bound `document.measure` gives when asked (`"bound": true`) was at least as wide as the error, and the number given without it said nothing; on blunter ends, which 0.1.x does not read, the measure's own bound was too narrow, up to three and a half times in the volume and nineteen in the area. Such a face is now measured to rounding where its edges into the point are borders of its surface, lie beside them within the body's tolerance, or are lines of constant parameter of a surface that runs on past them (as other modellers trim); its bound is shown to hold by summing the face again with finer inner integrals until they converge, which two sums that agree do not show on their own (not done for a face that closes in two points; on a surface with more than eight knot spans across the face it is not done either, and the bound of the area is then as wide as an edge may be off its face). An edge that runs into the point across the face is measured as before, inside a bound that says so. Bodies that 3DCraft builds with such a face (a spline turned onto its axis, a boolean that cuts one) move by up to 7e-11 of their volume, towards their true value where it is known, most with a narrower bound (up to 40,000 times). Every file that was read is read, and every mesh that was made is made as before, to the bit; some faces that could not be meshed ("its boundary is not closed in parameter space") are meshed now, outside the import, whose look for crossings meshes them as before. A file with a long pointed end, or an end very near a corner, is still refused on import, as before ("the surface turns away from the chords of edge …").
- **A plane whose normal is a hair off vertical sees what lies below it near a body's lowest and highest points.** With a normal within about a hundred-millionth of a radian of up or down but not along it (one `measure.floating` comes to as it settles upright, or a face's normal worked out from its corners), `measure.below`, `measure.section`, `measure.levelFor` and `measure.floating` read how far the bodies reach along z instead, off by that angle times their size (5e-6 mm on a part a metre long, five times the default tolerance), so a plane that far into a body near its lowest point could find nothing below it, and a plane that missed a body could be refused ("nothing … was found below the plane …, yet it may reach … below it"). The reach is now read along the normal itself. Along the axes nothing changes, to the bit; along other normals a level or an equilibrium can move by rounding (a few units in the last place).
- **`document.check` warns of a face that folds over itself.** A face whose surface turns back so that two of its layers face each other through air nearer than the document's tolerance, or whose surface has no normal at a point (a spline face read from a file that is pulled through itself by a hair, a blend that turns back within the tolerance), was built, measured, meshed and passed by every check. `document.check` now names each such face in a warning of kind `fold`, with the place where its layers come nearest and how far apart they are there, and lists under `foldsNotProven` the faces it could not settle (layers that face each other only at an edge of the face, loops it cannot follow, a face too large to examine). It warns and refuses nothing: `ok` stays as it was, and every feature, import and export does what it did before; but a mesh, an offset, a shell or a boolean near such a place may go wrong. The rule searches a face only where some part of it bends tighter than half the tolerance, so it can stay silent on a fold that closes in smoothly (a hem, a neck above a wider hollow): no warning is no proof that no face folds.
- **`document.check` warns (`pointOffArc`) when a point held on an arc lies on its circle beyond the arc's ends**, so the arc does not pass through it: an arc drawn through a point keeps the way round it was drawn with, and goes the long way round its centre once the point crosses the line through its ends.
- **A `{min, max}` bound of a filter takes in what rounding leaves beyond it.** `radius`, `length`, `area`, `volume` and `thickness` in `select.query`, named sets and the subjects of assertions compared `min` and `max` exactly in 0.1.3, so a measure a hair on the wrong side of a bound by rounding was left out: a corner whose radius is given as 1 mm could drop out of `{min: 1 mm}` after some edits and not others. A bound now takes in a millionth of itself beyond it, and no more: not the document's tolerance, which a value given alone or as `eq` is still matched within. So a margin of more than a millionth of its bound holds (a corner of exactly 6.35 mm stays out of `{max: 6.35 mm - 0.001 mm}` at a tolerance of 0.01 mm), a smaller one does not (`{max: 2000 mm - 0.001 mm}` takes in 2000 mm), and `{eq: 1 mm}` can find a radius that `{min: 1 mm}` leaves out. A filter finds more than in 0.1.3 only where a measure lies within a millionth of a bound, outside it.
- **A flat face has a plate thickness when the face opposite it is parallel only to rounding.** `thickness` (in `select.query`, named sets and assertions) asked the face a line leaves through to face exactly the other way, to the last bit, so two faces whose normals differ by rounding alone (each worked out from its own corners, as in a block turned off the axes) had no thickness and never fit. They have one now; every face that had a thickness keeps the same one.
- **`normal` and `axis` filters with `tol: 0` find what is parallel or square to the axis to rounding.** At 0, and at any `tol` below about 6e-7 degrees, a face whose normal was the axis only to rounding was left out (a block turned on a skew axis showed one of its four sides as `perpendicular`). Such a `tol` is now read as an angle, never as less than rounding (about 2e-13 degrees). At the default half degree, and at any tolerance from a thousandth of a degree up, every selection is what it was.
- **Points held at values are their values exactly after a change of the variables.** Before, a few units in the last place could remain from an earlier solve, so the same values did not always give the same held points, and a corner held at a radius of 1 mm could come out a hair under 1 mm and drop out of a `radius: {min: 1 mm}` query. Now, whenever a sketch solves, a coordinate given by an `x`, `y` or `fixed` constraint is exactly its value, unless other constraints already decide it (the constraint is then redundant and holds within the tolerance, as before). On its first solve, a sketch from an older file may move its held points by that much. This also mends a part that failed after `param.set` gave it values at which a new document builds (a sheet-metal arm with a gear sector on its flanges, every point of its sketches held at values): the edit now gives the new document's sketches, to the last bit, and builds. Where a held point now lies exactly on its value, a clearance 0.1.3 measured can be one that cannot be measured (the gear reducer Jig at one of its settings, under Known limitations).
- **A body with a face whose edge loop goes round its circle twice is refused.** Such a face (a disc whose rim of three arcs goes round twice, a hole taken out twice) passed the validity check and measured as twice its area or volume, or a hole's worth short, while looking right; so did a loop that runs back over itself along a stretch. Every operation and `document.check` now refuse it, naming the face and the loop: each loop of a face must go round what it encloses once, round a closed surface (a cylinder, a torus) at most once, and not run over itself or over another loop of the face. A sheet may still have a cut, whose two sides run over each other opposite ways (a slit or a slot in a folded sheet). No operation is known to make such a face since the fix to a plate's corners where arcs continue each other (below); the check is the net under all of them.
- **A face bounded by a helix of many turns as one edge is checked the way it goes round.** A thread's crest or root on a cylinder (or a cone or a sphere) whose side is one spline edge of more than about four turns, as a file can give it, was refused by the validity check as running the wrong way round while it was right; and the same face turned the wrong way round could pass. Such an edge is now followed round every turn, so the right face passes and the wrong one is refused; a face whose edges would take more than a few milliseconds to follow so (a single edge of more than about 85 turns) is passed without being judged.
- **A boolean could keep or drop the wrong piece, or fail, on a body with a spline face whose two directions run at very different speeds.** Where one parameter of a spline face runs along it about two million times faster than the other, or more, 3DCraft found the nearest point of that face hundreds of tolerances from where it is, so an edge or a ray that passes through the face was not seen to cross it. Such a face comes at any size in a file from another modeller, as a B-spline whose knots are stretched by millions, and from 3DCraft itself as the straight rim of a plate millions of model units thick where it is kept as a ruled spline (far from the origin on a turned plane, in a document sized in the millions, as one in micrometres or in map coordinates is). A boolean with such a body then mostly failed (*"imprint lost: a curve in which two faces cross does not divide them"*, *"the result would have an edge shared by faces that do not pair up around it"*, *"boolean produced an invalid body"*, *"the result passes through itself"*), and could return a valid-looking solid that was not the result: a pin 11.3412 mm in radius and 32.2413 mm high, imported from a STEP file whose wall is the exact B-spline of the cylinder on knots stretched 3e7 times, with a block through its side, gave the pin untouched for the subtraction (13028.08 mm³ for 12060.03) and refused the union and the intersection. Whether a point lies inside such a body, which several operations and the deep `document.check` ask, could be answered the wrong way round thousands of tolerances from its surface. The nearest point is now found, and where a crossing cannot be read it is reported as not known instead of left out. A body whose spline faces run at even speeds is built exactly as before. Still owed: on a long spline face whose weights differ a millionfold, or one whose two directions are a millionth of a radian apart, the nearest point can still be missed; the booleans tried on such bodies were refused or built with the right faces and corners, none found wrong, but the volume of those could not be measured.
- **A chamfer larger than its faces allow is refused where it is found to pass through a face beside its edge.** Where the line from one cut line to the other runs through a face beside the edge (a chamfer far along a pipe that lies in a plate, along a groove, up a shallow dome, past the overhanging nose of an S-shaped face, near the narrow end of a cone, or where two rings meet), 3DCraft built a valid-looking solid that took material away where the chamfer should only add it, or added it where it should only take it away. Such a chamfer is now refused as too large for that face, saying the largest size that fits along a straight edge next to a cylinder; one whose line enters the face by no more than the document's tolerance still builds. This is a search, not a proof: what it does not find is built as before. It can miss a crossing shorter than a sixty-fourth of the edge, along an edge whose faces change along it; a crossing between the 32 points at which some faces are looked at (a ring cut at a slant, a spline face that is not an extrusion, a cone cut in a hyperbola or a parabola); a crossing where the search cannot decide, or in a place across the edge where it finds no section; a chamfer whose cut line is carried across a smooth edge onto the next face, except along a straight edge between flat and cylindrical faces onto a flat or cylindrical face (below); and a chamfer face that lies inside a neighbouring face while its line keeps clear of it, which is still built wrong. Fillets are checked the same way (the next entry).
- **A fillet is refused where a face beside its edge is found to bulge into its rolling ball.** Where a face beside the edge reaches into the fillet's ball between the edge and the place the ball touches that face (a bead on the wall of a groove next to its rim, or on a ridge next to its foot, filleted with a radius at which the bead comes into the ball), 3DCraft built a valid-looking solid that added material where the fillet should only take it away, or took it where it should only fill. Such a fillet is now refused, naming the edge and the face; one whose ball takes in the face by no more than the document's tolerance still builds, as before, and so does every fillet not found so, the same solid as before. A fillet whose ball would rest on the bulge instead is not built yet: give another radius. This is a search, as the chamfer's is, not a proof: it can miss what the chamfer's search can (a stretch shorter than a sixty-fourth of the edge, along an edge whose faces change along it; some faces looked at in 32 points only, among them revolved, lofted and swept spline faces (a wall of 68° with a bead, revolved a quarter turn: a fillet of radius 7.75 wrong by 1.67e-2 mm is still built, as 0.1.0 built it, where the same wall extruded is refused); a place where it cannot decide or finds no ball; a fillet carried across a smooth edge onto the next face), a face that comes back into the fillet beyond the place the ball touches it, and faces other than the edge's own two. Fillets along straight edges between planes and cylinders and round circular edges between planes, cylinders and cones cannot meet this and are not searched.
- **A chamfer whose cut line runs past the end of its face, onto a flat or cylindrical face that goes on from it smoothly, is built, or refused as too large.** Along a straight edge between flat and cylindrical faces, a chamfer that reached past the edge where its face goes on smoothly into the next one (a boss whose wall leaves a plate as an arc and goes on as the arc's tangent plane, chamfered further up the wall than the arc reaches; a top that runs on into a round) failed with *"the result is not a valid body: edge … is … away from the surface of face …"* when the face it runs past was named first. Named the other way round, it was built as a valid solid that was wrong, and `document.check` said nothing: on such a boss 40 mm long, 1 mm along the plate and 5 mm up the wall changed the volume by +50.68 mm³ where the line between the cut lines runs through the arc, and 0.5 mm and 6 mm by −6.54 mm³, a chamfer of an inside corner that took material away. It is now built: its cut line lies on the next face, at the straight distance across the edge that the distance asked for has on the first face (on a cylinder, the chord of that arc), as wherever a chamfer's cut line is carried onto the next face. Where the line from that cut line to the other would run through a face (the arc bulging past it, or the top), it is refused as too large for that face, whichever face is named first, saying the largest size that fits. The same now holds where a cylinder is two faces that meet along its length, and where the flat face beside a cylinder is two faces of one plane: a chamfer whose line runs through the cylinder there was built wrong and is refused, as where each is one face. Every chamfer whose cut lines stay on their own faces builds as before, to the last digit. Still built wrong, as in 0.1.0: where the face that goes on smoothly is a spline face (even a flat one), a chamfer whose line runs through the face before it is built as a valid solid that is wrong (the same boss with its wall going on as a straight spline, 0.5 mm along the plate and 6 mm up the wall: −6.54 mm³).
- **Rounds and chamfers that use up a face exactly take it away.** A round or a chamfer whose borders meet exactly on a face, so that nothing of it is left (both upright edges of a box 6 mm wide rounded 3 mm, both long edges of a face 5 mm high chamfered 2.5 mm, the inside bend of an L rounded as far as its faces reach), was refused (*"… cannot be meshed to see whether it passes through itself … they enclose no area"*); a chamfer that takes a whole wall (a step chamfered as high as its wall) was built with the wall left in the body as a face of no area, which some placements of the part measured and others refused. The face now goes, and what met it meets along one edge: such a step has one face fewer than in 0.1.x and measures the same however it lies. Still refused: the same design round circular edges (both rims of a pipe's end rounded half its width), a size larger than the one that uses the face up, and a face on a sphere or a torus.
- **A fillet is refused where the two faces of its edge become tangent at a place inside the edge.** Where an edge turns from convex to concave and back (the ridge of a lofted roof that comes down to its eaves, dips just below them and rises again), `feature.fillet` could answer `ok` with a body whose fillet face had no width at those two places and its borders crossed between them: `document.inspect` called it valid, `document.check` could not measure it (*"a boundary integral that does not settle"*), and it could fail to export. Edges of that kind were also refused with reasons that were not true: *"the two faces are tangent along this edge"* for an edge whose faces are tangent at its middle only, for a fillet and for a chamfer, and for one whose faces are within 0.003° of tangent at its middle; and an internal error for an edge convex at its middle and concave further on. Such a fillet is now refused before anything is built, naming the edge, the place (its coordinates) and what the faces do there; no radius rounds such an edge yet. The other refusals now say what is true. Two faces count as tangent where the sine of the angle between them is at most a millionth, the measure 3DCraft already used at the middle of an edge. A fillet that narrows towards the end of its edge, or inside it while its faces keep an angle, however narrow it is there, is built exactly as before, and so are chamfers.
- **A face moved through the flat floor of a hole or a pocket, or far enough for one of its edges to run out through a flat face, a cylinder, a cone, a ball, a ring or a spline face, is refused as too far.** The bottom of a block offset upward past the flat floor of a blind hole in its top, the floor of an undercut pocket moved down until its rim leaves the block: the feature failed because the result passes through itself, without a distance. It now says *"the face would pass through another part of the body, or lie in another face of it; the largest that fits is below 11.4174 mm"*. The distance is said only where it is known: where something that is not flat towards the moved face lies in its way (the point of a drilled hole, which the bottom reaches before it reaches the hole's rim), the feature fails for the same reason and says that how far the face can go was not found. Where a face is moved right through a part and turns it inside out with no corner or edge in its way (the bottom moved up through a groove of the top), the feature is refused as that, without a distance. Spline faces are asked too, where an edge of the moved face comes near one of their spans and where a moved spline face passes a corner: the floor of a round pocket under a spline wall raised 0.02 mm past the wall's lowest point over it, which gave a feature with status ok that `document.check` passed although the pocket's rim had run out through the wall, now fails for that reason, saying how far the floor can go (6.3838 mm); so do rims run out through the side of a turned spline or the round of a spline edge, the floor of a rectangular slot whose straight edges leave through the wall between their ends, also under two or three spline faces in a row and however the part is turned or placed (from 0.0005 mm past; in 0.1.x, 0.0005 and 0.002 mm past built), and a spline face moved up past the corners of an arm that hangs over it (without a distance). Where the search for how far a face can go is stopped by its work bound before it closes in (each try dear: a slot under a spline wall of hundreds of spans), the feature says that the distance it gives does fit and that more may (5.96 mm of a floor that fits to 6.79 mm). **Still built wrong, as in 0.1.x** (owed): *a face moved a little way into the inside of a smooth or spline face far from it, with no corner and no edge in its way.* The bottom of a block moved 0.0005 to 0.01 mm past the pole of a turned bowl in its top, and a spline face moved up 0.0005 to 0.002 mm into a flat arm over it or 0.0005 to 0.005 mm into a spline arm, give a feature with status ok that `document.check` with `deep` passes (a little further the feature fails because the result passes through itself; a program that calls the kernel directly can get such a body further in too); so does the bottom of a block 20 mm thick moved 0.002 mm into a ball's dimple in its top, 0.017 mm into a ring's or a rod's groove, or 0.002 mm past the point of a drilled hole, although the bottom has gone through the dimple; a little deeper (0.017 mm in the dimple, 0.1 mm in the ring's groove, 0.5 mm in the rod's, 0.009 mm past the drill point) the feature fails because the result passes through itself.
- **A sheet-metal part or a plate whose outline has arcs that continue each other is built at every size, and never with the wrong volume.** Where two arcs of one circle meet (the joints of a chained sketch pattern, such as the root circle between a gear sector's teeth, or a hole split into arcs), whether the outline runs on smoothly there was decided by the last bit of a rounding, so a part could fail at one value and build at a value a billionth away ("the boundary crosses itself in parameter space", or "loop … does not lie where the face's other loops leave it room"), and a plate of such an outline could even be built with a hole taken out twice. Such a joint now always counts as running on. What moves: a corner where two edges of a plate run on into each other to rounding, and which was solved as a crease until now, is now placed as every smooth corner is, which moves it by rounding (a few units in the last place: 7e-15 mm on a part 90 mm long); a body that was wrong changes by what was wrong. Every corner placed as a smooth one before stays where it was.
- **A plate along a tight arc, and a sheet-metal bend down tighter than about its thickness, are built to their closed forms.** A plate whose outline has an arc of a radius below about the plate's thickness (a small hole drawn as arcs, a slot's end, a rounded corner), and a sheet-metal part with a bend down whose inner radius is below about the thickness, were at some radii a body that looked right and passed the validity check but could not be measured or meshed, or measured wrong (12 % too much for a flange bent down 140° at an inner radius of 0.176 of the thickness), and were refused at radii next to those; bends down tighter than about a fiftieth of the thickness were refused ("the rim could not be traced …"). By command those bodies, the one 12 % over among them, made the feature fail ("… cannot be meshed to see whether it passes through itself"), at values a billionth from values that built. A plate's edge is traced once round its circle at most now, and these parts are built right; a part that has such an arc and built right before is built right again, its numbers moved within the tolerance (toward the exact circle where a fitted one was off by up to a hundred-thousandth).
- **A bevelled round hole or disc drawn as arcs of one circle has its corners where they belong.** Where two arcs of one circle meet and both are bevelled, the plate's corners slid round the circle, up to several thicknesses (the body's volume right, its vertices and the edges across its corners wrong, with the status ok). The corners are now where the bevel puts them. Where two edges that run on smoothly end differently (one bevelled, the other square, or two bevels unlike), the plate is refused saying so and that one end for both builds it; where a bevel moves an arc's rim past its centre inside the plate (a hole that would close, a disc that would turn inside out), it is refused saying so.
- **A sheet-metal bend of a tiny angle has flat sides.** A flange bent by a few thousandths of a degree could have one side face along its bend that was a freeform surface reaching round most of the bend's circle, so the part's bounding box (as the view's fit and the booleans use it, and as it bounds what an assertion of `extent`, `boxMin` or `boxMax` can prove) reached below the part by almost twice the part's height. That side is the plane it lies in now; the part's volume is as it was.
- **A mesh that was not the closed surface of a solid could be exported without a word.** A face whose surface folds round a seam far more tightly than the tolerance (a dome over a thin ellipse closed across a seam into its tip, as a STEP file can hold one; no command builds such a face) was meshed with points of the two sides of the seam a few billionths of a millimetre apart or on one place, so that triangles there had no area, four triangles shared a side or two fans met in a corner. `file.export` wrote such a mesh to STL, 3MF and OBJ, its result counting it as closed or not, and nothing else said so: on 0.1.x, 15 of 70 STL exports of twelve such domes (at two tolerances and the three qualities, `normal` and `fine` among them) were no closed surface as a slicer reads them, the triangles of no area dropped. Such a face is now meshed as a closed surface, and a mesh in which a point that a face's triangulation added lands on another of its points, or within the rounding of their coordinates, is refused, naming the face and the place, never written. Every other mesh is made as before, to the last bit. And `file.export` now counts `closed` as the file holds the mesh: a binary STL file keeps single precision, where points the mesh keeps apart can fall on one another (a part far from the origin, or a surface that turns more sharply than the angle of the `coarse`, `normal` and `fine` qualities can follow with points that far apart); its answer then counts that body closed only where the file's other triangles are a closed surface once those folded to a line (of no area, which a reader's repair drops) are left out, and gives `mergedPoints`, `foldedTriangles` and a warning naming the body that says which (before, it counted the mesh closed and wrote the file without a word). Such a file is still written, as before; `stl-ascii`, `3mf` and `obj` write every point as the mesh has it. Still owed: such a dome in a turned position, at those qualities, asks for points so close to its seam (about a ten-millionth of a millimetre) that an STL file puts two of them on one place, as on 0.1.x (now said), or its export is refused where its mesh had such points in double precision as well (4 of 15 exports of five such domes, each of which was written folded before); at a tolerance without an angle (`"tolerance"`) its file is a closed surface.
- **An assembly solved at other drive values than its parts were placed at says when its pose is not the one solve straight from the placement.** Its rest pose was that one solve, which could land a linkage on its mirrored loop without a word: a walking machine's legs at some crank starts, a four-bar or a slider-crank near the pose where its two circuits pass close. It is now also carried there step by step from the drive values its parts are placed at, in steps that shorten near such a pose. Where the two agree, the pose is exactly what it was, and nothing is said. Where they differ, the carried pose is shown and `assembly.solve`'s `note` says so: that the assembly, carried from the placement, rests elsewhere than the straight solve gives, or, when the parts were not placed where their mates meet, that the branch is not certain. Where the straight solve fails and the carry answers, the note says that too. A linkage very near its change point can still be carried onto its other branch: the note marks that the two methods disagreed, not which one is right. When the carry stops short or runs out of steps, the straight solve is shown and the note says the branch is not checked. Moments of a motion and joints held by hand are solved as before. A rest solve whose drives are far from where the parts are placed costs more: twelve walking legs on one crankshaft (74 parts) 27 to 100 ms instead of 10 to 16, fifty (302 parts) 0.43 to 0.78 s instead of 0.34 to 0.39. An assembly placed again with nothing its solve reads changed takes its last answer.
- **A joint that nothing drives, and that moves on its own, keeps its value when an assembly is solved; such assemblies can rest in another pose than before.** When a part was placed a little off its mate, the solve also turned free joints elsewhere to close the gap (a desk lamp's free swivel ended 6.6° from where it was placed). A free swivel, hinge or slider now stays where the parts were put; joints that move only together (a free loop, a free screw and its nut) are left as before. An assembly put together by hand with such a free joint can rest otherwise than before: the published walking machine's leg, opened on its own at another leg scale, by up to 1.6 mm.

### Added

- **Notes pinned to the model, for working with an agent** (`note.add`, `note.list`, `note.resolve`, `note.edit`, `note.delete`). A note is one sentence at a place of the model, on a face, an edge, a vertex or a body ("2 mm thicker here", "this hole can go"); an agent reads the open ones, does what each asks and closes each with a sentence of reply, and leaves its own question as a note at the place it is about. A note stays on its face through edits that keep the face, and stays at its point, detached, when the face is gone. Each note says how it came in (typed at the window, from an agent by name, or unknown) and is text from whoever had the file: an agent takes it as a request for a change to the model at that place, and reports anything else to you. Notes are saved in the document and are one undo step each; they change no body. A Jig exported with `jig.export` holds none, nor does any export.
- **You answer an agent in the window, and the Notes tab says what waits for you.** A note is a thread: choose its row in the Notes tab and type in its reply field, and the agent answers the same way (`note.reply`). The tab groups the notes under *For you* (the agent's questions and answers), *For the agent* (with the sentence to give your agent, and a button that copies it) and *Done*, and the agent pill starts with how many notes wait for you. In the view each note is a pin with its number: indigo for a note that waits for you, dark for one that waits for the agent, ringed in amber when chosen; a click on one opens its row. Right-click a face, an edge or a vertex and choose **Note here…**, or select one thing and press **N**, to write a note there; where a note cannot be written (nothing or more than one thing selected, an assembly, a Jig in use) the window says why. Pins in the half a section cuts away are drawn faint.
- **The section has a handle in the view, and its plane can come from what you select.** While the section is on, the view shows its plane: a dashed outline, an arrow at the cut's edge to drag (it snaps lightly to flat faces, to the middle of holes and to round distances; hold Alt to move it freely; a double-click keeps the other half), and beside it a small group: the distance (click it to type one, such as `width / 2`; one past the model is brought to its end, and the group says so), **X**, **Y**, **Z**, **⇅**, and a button that says in a word what it will do with the selection. **On face** lays the plane on a selected flat face, **Midway** puts it midway between two parallel faces, **Bisect** halves the angle between two faces (then **Other bisector** gives the one square to it), and **Through axis** lays it through a hole's or a shaft's axis, turned to face you, so you look into the hole. Greyed, it says why the selection gives no plane. A plane made from the selection stays where it is when the model changes, and when its face is gone the section goes off and says so. On a phone the bar keeps the buttons and a slider for the distance. An agent sets the same with `ui.set {"section": …}`, and `ui.get` says how the plane was made (`section.made`).
- **Two things selected are measured by themselves, and one thing shows its size in the view.** Select a hole and the view writes its diameter at its rim (*Ø 8 mm*), an edge its length, an arc its radius. Shift-click a second thing and the foot of the side panel shows their relation as one large number, what it is in a few words and, for a distance, its parts along X, Y and Z, and the view draws a thin dimension line between them: two holes their centre distance, two parallel faces the distance between them, two faces at an angle the angle, a vertex and anything the least distance, two bodies their clearance. Click a number to copy it with its unit. **Keep as a check** keeps the relation as a check of the document (an assertion `between` or `angleBetween`), measured again after every change and one step to undo. A least distance that is not proven within the tolerance is shown as a range, or as *about* a number, and kept as what is proven. An agent asks the same with `measure.between`.
- **After an update, 3DCraft says what is new, and first which results may differ.** The first time a newer version shows its start screen, a card *New in <version>* stands under New part and Open…: what changes an answer first (a measure, a proof, a check, or a body that was built wrong), then what is new, then the rest folded away; a click on a line unfolds what these notes say about it. In a low window it starts as its title and one line that says how many results may differ, so the first row of examples stays in sight; a click unfolds it. After several versions at once it lists them all, newest first. Closing it is remembered, and **Help › What's new** shows it again. It is never over a document, and never shown on a first start. The notes are this changelog, carried in the program: nothing is fetched. In the web version the card shows once when the page has moved to a newer version (not when 3DCraft is embedded in another page). An agent asks the same as data: `session.whatsNew {"since": "0.1.1"}` returns the versions, their entries by kind, and which of them change an answer.
- **Experimental: the web build can be driven by the page it sits in (Embed).** A page that loads the module itself, with `data-craft3d-embed` on its canvas, keeps its own title, focus, keys, storage and address, and gets a port (`craft3d_connect()`) on which it sends the same requests as the control channel (`engine.execute`, `batch`, `ui.get`, `ui.set`), hears `ready` and `regenerated` (each reply says the state it left, so a page knows whether to wait), gives 3DCraft a document or a STEP file as bytes (`embed.give`) and takes what a command writes (`file.export`, `file.save`) as bytes with its reply, or the document's own file without changing it (`embed.take`). A read is answered on the state it was sent for; what would hold the page's own thread (a `batch` step or a `wait: true` that needs the model built) is refused with what to send instead, and many requests at once wait their turn rather than hold it or have the model built on it. The window can be drawn whole, as the 3D view alone, or as a Jig's form beside the view (`presentation`: `full`, `view`, `form`; the last two without the sketches' lines, which come back as they were in `full`); in the embedded stand a ring shows when 3DCraft has the keys, Escape closes 3DCraft's menu, list or dialog first and then leaves the canvas (Tab too, in the view), and the wheel scrolls the page unless 3DCraft has the focus. Without the attribute the web page is exactly as before; the desktop app refuses a presentation other than the whole window. Experimental: the names and the events may still change (`packaging/web/HOSTING.md` › *Embedding*, `docs/control-protocol.md` › *The page as a transport*).
- **A fillet or a chamfer takes one edge by its own name.** Until now an edge was given as the two faces it lies between, which means every edge between them, so one of two edges between the same faces (where a pipe sunk into a plate leaves its top, say) could not be rounded alone. Give the edge's name instead, as `document.edges` lists it or a click in the window gives it: `"edges": ["F3:between[…]"]`, or `[edge name, face name]` to say which face a chamfer's first distance runs along. An edge is taken by its name only where every part of the name is proven not to move to another edge (`docs/design/names.md`); such a name keeps meaning that edge when earlier features change or are put in before it. Other names are refused, saying what names the edge instead or that nothing does yet: a name with a number at its end (`…](1)`), one that lists a round's or a chamfer's face (`fillet(0,1)`, `chamfer(0)`), a glyph of a text, a developable's face, a plate's rim, or an edge of a lump a split chose by where it lies; give the pair of its faces then, which takes every edge between them. Pairs mean what they always meant.
- **A body can end in a rounded point** (`feature.tip`): a flat end face is replaced by faces that run on from the faces round it without a crease and close the body in one point, with one tangent plane there: the nose of a hull, the end of a slender blade. Give the end face; the length beyond the end, where the point stands and how full the tip is are chosen when not given (a rod tipped as long as its radius ends in a half ball). Taken for now over ends whose neighbours are spline faces (a loft's, a swept or extruded spline's); a tipped body cannot be hollowed or offset yet.
- **A fillet can fade out to a point on its edge** (`feature.fillet` with `fade`): at an end of an edge its radius falls smoothly to nothing over a length along the edge (`over`), and the rounded face closes in a point there, or a little short of the vertex (`back`), tangent to both faces all the way: a hull's stem rounded from the deck down and dying out above the forefoot. The result says where each fade ends (`fades`: the point, its name, `over`, `back`, the edge's length).
- **A sheet through a grid of points** (`feature.surface.grid`): a smooth surface that passes through every point of a grid, for a surface known by its points (measured or computed, a scan's patch, a hull's offsets, a lens, a terrain). Give the points row by row, each coordinate an expression, or read the heights of a regular grid from a table, times a factor that is an expression (`"factor": "vertical_scale"`): the sheet follows the table and the variable. `spacing` sets where it takes its points: `even` (the default for a table: over its regular grid the textbook bicubic spline of the heights, a height field of degree three or less each way exactly, and a height field never folds, however steep), `chord` (the default for points given: it follows how far apart they are) or `greville` (a live spline's default); the feature keeps the one it was made with. It passes through each point within rounding, gives a plane exactly from points on one, and is refused, naming the row and point, where its rows are ragged, two neighbours coincide, a row runs back on itself or the sheet would fold (proven on every span, not sampled; the refusal says whether a row runs back or the proof could not decide), or where the grid lies so far from the origin that no model there holds the tolerance (from 1.126e9 at the default tolerance; nearer, from about 6.5e7, it is built and warned of). Its warnings (a sheet too large for a boolean, heights far steeper than the step, a grid far from the origin) are in its result and in `document.check`. A rough sheet (heights that zigzag several steps between neighbours) is built but may not be measured or meshed: the tessellator's and the mass integrator's limits. Its face, edges and corners are numbered by the grid's borders, so moving the points renames nothing. A block cut by the sheet (`feature.boolean` `intersect`) is the solid under it, for a sheet of any grid in the desktop program (up to 262 144 points, 1 024 a side) and of up to 184 × 184 points in a browser (the result warns past it). The grid a sheet was made through is drawn with the sketches, its rows and columns on the sheet and its points, every k-th line on a grid of more than 65 points a side, and cut where a section cuts the bodies; `view.render` and `craft3d-cli render` draw it with `sketches` on. A grid read from a table costs about 20 bytes a point in the saved file, one given point by point about 120 (a live spline's point about 670).
- **A live spline can take its points at chord-length parameters** (`sketch.spline {"live": true, "spacing": "chord"}`), which follow how far apart the points are. A spline through points takes them, by default, at parameters that ignore their spacing and are uneven near the ends, so between the points it strays further from the smooth curve they came from than it needs to: through eight points of an involute flank, 0.0095 mm against 0.00044 mm with chord spacing. Without `spacing` a spline is exactly what it was, in new documents and old. Not yet: chord spacing on a closed spline, on a spline made once (not live), and `pointOn` or `tangent` on a chord spline; each is refused with the reason.

### Changed

- **A cut is drawn as hatched paper, each part in its own direction, and what a click would select is outlined under the pointer.** Where a section cuts through a part, the cut is light paper with thin dark lines, which stay on the part as you turn the view and keep between 4.5 and 9 points apart as you zoom; it was orange, the colour a selection has. On a single body the lines run at 45°; in an assembly each part's cut has its own angle (45°, 135°, 75° or 165°), so parts that touch read apart, as on a drawing: parts next to each other in the document always differ, parts four places apart share one, where the thicker outline of the cut tells them apart, and a part keeps its direction when others are hidden or the assembly moves. Where two parts overlap (an interference) the cut is cross-hatched; where the two share a direction it shows no cut there, as before. Resting the mouse on the model outlines in amber the face, edge or corner a click there would select, without selecting it. Pictures with a section (`view.render`, `ui.screenshot`) look different; pictures without one are as before. An agent can place the pointer without a click (`ui.pick` with `hover`) and read what lies under it (`ui.get` › `hovered`).
- **The window speaks plainly: the status bar says what a click will do, the card opens with the number, and numbers are written one way.** After what is selected the status bar says, dim, what a click will do now (*Click a face, an edge or a hole to see its size*; *Shift-click another to measure between · N writes a note*; *Esc clears*), and a key that cannot work says why there for a few seconds; it names the file instead of its path, and never cuts the selection short. The selected thing's card opens with the number the view shows (a hole's *Ø 8 mm*, a face's area, a vertex's position, a body's volume). **Measure** is now **Wall thickness**: its answer heads the card and is drawn in the view (the distance between two things needs no button now, above). Numbers are written one way everywhere: at most three decimals in millimetres, no trailing zeros; point at one for its whole value, click it to copy it. The first section cuts across the axis you look along most, through the middle of the model, and every cut a button makes takes away the half on your side, so the cut faces you. Escape steps back one thing at a time, wherever the pointer is: a field, a list, a joint held by hand, the chosen note, then the selection.
- **A boolean takes a freeform face of up to 262 144 Bézier spans in the desktop program**, where it took 32 768: the sheet through any grid of points, or a large spline face from a file, cuts and is cut. A face of more, or one whose spans and their index would take more than 1 GiB (a high degree), is refused naming the face and its spans. Every boolean that was built before is built as before. In a browser the limit stays 32 768.
- **A table holds up to 1 025 columns**, where it held 64, so a grid surface reads a row of up to 1 024 heights from one table; the tables of a document still hold a million cells in all.
- **Files are format version 77, which 0.1.x does not open.** A document, a published Jig (`.3dcraftjig`) or an assembly (`.3dcraftasm`) saved by this version is refused by 0.1.0 to 0.1.3, which say that the file has format version 77 and that a newer 3DCraft is needed to open it: nothing in it is misread or dropped. Keep a copy saved by 0.1.x of a design you still open there. Every file of 0.1.x opens in this version, read as it was saved, and is saved as version 77. The new versions hold one edge of a fillet or a chamfer by its own name (70), a live spline's chord spacing (71), sheets through grids of points (72), tips (73, 74), notes and their replies (75, 77) and a fillet's fade (76).

### Fixed

- **An assembly saved under a full path keeps finding its parts.** An assembly whose parts were added by paths relative to the working folder (the folder an agent works in at the window), saved under a full path (`/home/…/work/clamp.3dcraftasm`), was written with paths to its parts that led nowhere, and the save said it went well: every part then failed, in the open assembly and each time the file was opened again. Wherever an assembly is saved, its paths now lead to the same part files, whether its own path and the new one are written in full or from the working folder, and also when you save, with Save As at the window, an assembly an agent made and never saved into a folder of your own, when the agent was given another folder between two parts, when 3DCraft reads from one folder and writes into another (`craft3d-cli serve --read-root … --write-root …`); the open assembly keeps showing its parts after the save. Saving into a folder reached through a symbolic link, where the paths written would not lead to the parts from the folder on disk, is refused, saying the folder's own path to save it by. Where it can no longer follow a part after such a save (the part lies outside the folder it reads from), the save says so with a warning and lists the part under `notFollowed`; open the assembly by its full path to follow it again. Saving an assembly no longer reads its own parts again: a part changed on disk since it was read is taken in when the window gets the focus back, by `assembly.follow` or `assembly.reload`, not by the save (it was, as a side effect, before). Where no path can lead from the new folder to a part (a part on another drive, or a path that led to no file already), the save is refused, naming the part and what to do, and nothing is written. A file saved wrongly by 0.1.0 to 0.1.3 is mended by pointing each part at its file again (`instance.edit` with its `source`) and saving it. A part file that is not there is now said to be missing (*there is no such file*), not in the system's words.
- **A plate's joint that failed may build now.** A joint whose edge was named by its faces in a body a later boolean took failed with "is gone"; it is followed to the edge the boolean kept and built. A joint that built is unchanged.
- **A STEP file with a surface whose weights are far apart is imported, and its part can be shown, exported and cut.** A rational surface with very uneven weights (a flat or gently curved face whose weights differ a thousandfold and more, as another program can write one) is drawn at a very uneven pace in its own parameters, and 3DCraft sized such a face's mesh by that pace, not by its shape: it asked for thousands to millions of millions of points a side, and `file.import` read the file and failed its part (*"the imported body cannot be meshed to see whether it passes through itself (patch … needs about … segments per side for this tolerance …)"*), so nothing of it could be drawn, exported or built on. Where that sizing is refused the face's mesh now follows its shape (a flat face with any weights is a few triangles, a cylinder's face has the triangles of the same face drawn evenly): the part is imported, drawn, exported as STL, 3MF and STEP, and a boolean with it builds (with weights up to a thousand apart; further apart the boolean itself still refuses). Not yet: a flat face whose weights are ten thousand and more apart is not measured (`document.inspect` shows such a part without a volume); a face whose weights are moderately apart (around thirty times) is meshed as before, with far more triangles than it needs; and a cut that leaves a thin sliver beside such a face can still be refused (*"share a side that is on no edge"*). Faces that were meshed before are meshed as before.
- **A boolean could run for most of an hour.** A boolean, an interference check or a placement of points in a body had no bound on its work: with spline faces whose weights run over many orders it could run for most of an hour (a block whose flat sides are such faces, intersected with a block through three of them), and other freeform faces drawn very unevenly could keep one busy for minutes. Each of these operations now has a bound, far above what any model of the reference set, the examples and the published model repositories needs, and past it the operation is refused, saying that it needs more work than one operation is allowed and naming the faces it was working on; nothing that was built before is refused or built differently. The block above is built in seconds now: the pieces its faces are split into were made again and again, which they are not anymore.
- **A bevel that folds an elliptic plate's rim, and a lens whose bevelled rims part inside the plate, are refused saying so.** Where a bevel moves the rim of an elliptic edge toward its middle by as much as the ellipse's sharpest bend (the radius of curvature at the ends of its long axis, b²/a for semi-axes a and b), the rim would fold back on itself inside the plate; the feature failed saying that the plate could not be meshed, passed through itself or was not valid, and it now says that the bevel folds the rim, where and by how much. A plate whose outline (or hole) is a lens of two arcs bevelled so that the arcs part between the skins said that a corner lay further round a curve than it was traced; it now says that the rims do not meet at that skin and how far apart they are. A bevel that stops short of the fold builds as before.
- **A plate of a whole sphere or torus is a hollow ball or ring.** `feature.plate` of a face that closes on itself (a sphere's, a torus's, or faces that together close and meet smoothly, such as a capsule's) failed with *"plate produced an invalid body … fall into 2 pieces that share no edge"*; it now builds the hollow: an outer skin and a cavity inside it, of the thickness on the side asked. A thickness that reaches the sphere's centre or the tube's axis, or comes within the tolerance of it, fails saying the offset vanishes. A cylinder's side and every face with an edge of its own plate as before.
- **The development of a cylinder's or a cone's side no longer depends on the size of the part.** `feature.unfold` of the side of a cylinder of radius 31 m in a frame turned off the axes failed with *"face … has a boundary that does not close in the surface's parameters"*, while the same cylinder 1000 times smaller, or not turned, unrolled; and every cylinder of radius 1000 km or more failed so (its two rims taken for points). Both unroll now, their areas the closed form; a development that unrolled before is the same.
- **A valid body with a spline face whose weights differ a millionfold could be refused as invalid.** Where the weights of a spline face run over many orders (from a thousandth to a thousand, as a file from another modeller can bring), its parameters race over parts of the face and run nearly the same way over others, and 3DCraft's search for the nearest point of the face could stop short of it, by up to a few percent of the face's size. The validity check, which asks that search for the points of each edge, then read an edge as off its face: a block 30 by 900 by 20 mm whose three sides are such faces was refused (*"edge 2 is 1.65 away from the surface of face …"*), and with it every operation on it. That search now goes on where the point it stopped at could still slide along the face, and such a block passes the check and its booleans are built. It cannot be used much further yet: such a body can be neither meshed (its faces would need millions of segments a side) nor measured (its weights are more than ten thousand times apart within a span), and a boolean with it can take long: most take seconds (since the store of the pieces its faces are split into for the booleans holds what they look into; it let them go and made them again, and an intersection ran for most of an hour), and some still run for minutes. Elsewhere the change was measured, not assumed: in the tests of the kernel and of the engine, on the reference set of booleans and on the meshes of the examples, every body, mesh and measure is the same, bit for bit. Faces without weights are not untouched, though: in the tessellator's own tests the search now finds the right point of a face wound like a spiral band many turns round (934 points, before up to a turn of the band off, not by rounding), so such a face in a document of yours can be meshed differently than before, although none of the measured ones was.
- **Recover could refuse a document as belonging to a 3DCraft that is still running, although that one was gone.** A program 3DCraft starts (the watcher of the desktop's light or dark setting, a recording's `ffmpeg`) holds a copy of the lock file of a crashed 3DCraft for the moment before it has started, if it was started while 3DCraft looked through the recovery directory, and the lock stayed held for that moment. The lock is now let go of explicitly, which releases every copy. A person was unlikely to meet it (the moment is well under a millisecond), but tests that start programs met it often.
- **A shell was refused where four or more faces meet in a corner.** A trough or a pocket whose floor falls to a drain, a box with a cylinder whose axis lies in the box's side plane: where four faces meet in one point, the walls of the hollow no longer do, and the shell failed at every thickness with "the faces that meet in a vertex there no longer meet in one point". In the hollow such a corner now becomes two corners joined by a short edge, as the moved walls make it, and the shell builds. Where that is not decided by the faces (a corner of both kinds of edge that could become edges in more than one way), it is refused as before. A shell open at its top round a pocket can leave the pocket's lining as a separate piece of the same body, as it always could round a boss. Offset face, thicken, draft and move face still refuse such a corner.
- **A shell next to rounds that meet in a mitre was refused as too thick.** A box whose bottom edges are rounded (the rounds meeting in mitres at the corners), shelled open at its bottom, was refused from a little below the rounds' radius less the wall on ("the edge shrinks to nothing or turns round", naming the two rounds): the edge where the rounds meet shrinks much more than it moves, and was lost while it was followed. It is now followed from a better guess and the shell builds. A shell, offset, draft or move of faces that built before builds exactly as before; only some refusals now build, and the largest size a refusal names may be larger.
- **A face with a wavy spline side is no longer refused while it is right, where the check misread the side between the places it looked at.** A thin strip between two waves, one side a single spline and the other in shorter pieces (the edge strip of a rippled panel, the floor of a wave-shaped groove), on a plane, a cylinder, a cone or a sphere, could be refused with "… its loop 0 crosses itself" (or "… every loop runs clockwise …", "… its hole … lies outside …"), in 0.1.x as well: the validity check followed the wavy side in a few straight steps whose ends and middles all fell on the wave's middle line, and took the side for straight. A face the check is about to refuse is now looked at again with a proven bound on how far each edge lies from those steps, and its fault is dropped only where that shows the whole face is right: every loop a closed curve that does not meet itself or another, also not across the seam of a surface that closes, one outer loop running the right way round, its holes inside it; of the 310 such strips of eight waves in the check's own sweeps that were refused, 258 now build. A face the check passes is checked exactly as before, and a face it refuses for a fault that holds is still refused, now and then for the fault the second look shows rather than the one the check first read ("… its hole … lies outside …" where it said "… crosses itself"). Most strips of sixteen waves (294 of 322 in those sweeps), where showing them right would take more than a few milliseconds, are still refused, and so are such strips on a torus or a spline surface, where the check is unchanged.
- **A flat spiral spring is no longer refused while it is right.** A thin strip that winds about a point of its face (the end of a flat spiral spring or of a scroll, a spiral groove's floor) with one long side drawn as a single spline of about 15 turns or more and the other in shorter pieces could not be extruded, nor made by any other operation: "extrude produced an invalid body: the loops of face … do not bound a region: every loop runs clockwise about the outward normal" (in 0.1.x as well). The validity check followed that spline in eight steps of two whole turns each, which read as a straight line. A spline edge is now followed in as many steps as it takes to show that it runs one way over each half of every step, on planes, cylinders, cones and spheres, so the spring builds, whatever its number of turns; an edge that turns less than three eighths of a turn over a sixteenth of its length, which nearly every edge does, is followed exactly as before. Such a face is passed without being judged: turned the wrong way round it passes too, as it did between two single splines, and so does one whose outer side is in pieces, which the check refused before. On a torus or a spline surface, a face with a spline edge that winds about a point is passed unjudged as well.
- **A face moved up to or past the face that ends its neighbours is refused as too far, with the distance that fits where it is found.** A rod that stands out of a block, its end cap offset inward past the block's face; a vase's top offset past its bottom; a block's end moved to where a slanted hole ends: the feature failed with *"… cannot be meshed to see whether it passes through itself"*, which says neither what is wrong nor how far the face can go. It now fails saying why and, where that is found, the largest distance that fits: *"two edges of one face would cross or touch: a face that moves would reach or pass the face that ends its neighbour there; the largest that fits is below 1 mm"*, or *"the edge shrinks to nothing or turns round"*. The distance is found by trying smaller ones; where that search ends on a distance that fails by itself while larger ones fit, or is stopped by its work bound (the cap of a turned spline moved past the cap across from it: *"below 8.75"* of a bowl 10 mm high, where 9.99 builds), the failure says that the distance it names fits and that more may. A program that calls the kernel directly (`offset_faces`, `move_faces`, `draft`, `shell`, `thicken`) got an invalid body there that passed the validity check, and gets the refusal now. Every offset, move, draft, shell and thicken that built a right body before builds the same body, bit for bit; what is refused now and was built before is a body that passed through itself or whose faces' edges crossed.
- **A shell left open at a round alone, with walls as thick as the round's radius or thicker, failed with an internal error.** A block with one edge rounded 2 mm, shelled 2.5 mm with only the round left open: *"internal error … the shell lost track of a face of the hollow"*, which asked for a bug report. There is no such shell (a face that a wall runs into smoothly is opened square to itself, which a round cannot be), and the feature now fails saying so, as it did for thinner walls: *"an open face with a blend along it has to be flat to be opened, and this one is a round whose radius the thickness reaches … leave the faces it joins open with it, or open a flat face"*.
- **Offset face, move face, draft, shell and thicken were refused at an edge sharper than about 14 degrees.** Where two faces meet at a sharper angle than that (the thin end of a wedge, a groove that leaves a face at a slant), any of these that moved the edge failed with *"the face would pass through another part of the body, or lie in another face of it"*, although nothing passed through anything: the check behind that message looked at a point that lay outside so thin a wedge. It looks further from the edge there now, and such features build. Among them a face moved within its own plane, which changes nothing and now succeeds beside such an edge as it does everywhere else. Only features that failed with that message can change; every one that built builds the same body.
- **A sketch with an arc or circle through three points held at values builds again after an edit that made it fail**, also when a file saved in that state is opened. An arc drawn through a point (its ends and the point written with expressions) could fail with *"constraint … conflicts"* after several sizes changed at once or after the arc was made very flat, and then kept failing at those values, after saving and opening too, while a new document at the same values built. When the solve from the last result fails, 3DCraft now tries once more with the centre of every such arc or circle placed where its three points put it, and takes that result only if nothing conflicts, every arc still runs through its points between its ends, and nothing else in the sketch turns to another side. A sketch that solved before solves exactly as before. Not recovered: an arc whose point has crossed the line through its ends (it fails as before: draw it again through the point), and a sketch that fails for other reasons.
- **A straight edge along a cone of a very small half-angle (under about 0.002°) is recognised as lying on it** in a boolean; before, rounding in the angle could hide it and leave the edge to a general method that may refuse it. And where a straight edge nearly along a narrow cone's axis crosses it, crosses a cone far from its point, or passes through a cone's point, the boolean finds where: such a crossing could be lost or taken for a touch (in the tests, 158 of 405 lines against cones of half-angles from a millionth of a radian to 23°). The booleans of the reference set (3996 pairs of solids) give the same results to the bit.
- **A shaft turned only by its coupling follows it over any number of turns.** A lead screw that nothing drives but its screw coupling to a nut stopped a motion after about 15 turns ("did not converge in 200 iterations"), and the assembly could not be solved at rest that far out. It now turns with its coupling in the motion and at rest. A moment of a motion that worked before gives the same answer, bit for bit; only what failed is solved again, and only where a coupling's turns are the matter.
- **A STEP file with hyperbolas or parabolas imports.** Where a plane cuts a cone parallel to its axis the cut edge is a hyperbola, parallel to its side a parabola (a countersunk hole cut by a flat, a chamfered hole meeting a wall), and other modellers write it so; such a file was refused ("#30 HYPERBOLA: this kind of curve is not read"). Each such edge now reads as the exact curve it is, however the file gives it: the conic itself, on a surface, trimmed (by parameter or by point, either way round) or as part of a composite curve; so do faces on a hyperbola or parabola extruded or turned. Trimmed and composite curves of every other kind read too. Still refused, saying why: a parabola or hyperbola turned, untrimmed, about an axis in its plane that it crosses (a dish written that way: which half of the doubly swept surface a face is on is not in what is read).
- **A STEP surface that is a cone offset towards its axis imports.** An offset surface of a cone (or of a line turned about an axis it meets) towards the axis was refused as passing "through the axis" whenever the offset was larger than the cone's radius where the file places it, which is every such offset when the file places it at the tip; it is the same cone with its tip moved along the axis, and reads as that.
- **A STEP cone given as a line turned about its axis imports at every size.** Where the line ran from a point of the cone up towards its tip, a small part (a millimetre across) was refused ("cannot tell which way the swept surface's normal points"): the way its surface faces was looked at a fixed distance along the line, past the tip.
- **An imported part refused because an edge could not be proven on its faces says what would read it.** Where the edge lies within the cap on an entity's tolerance at the 1025 places it is measured at, the message now gives that distance and says that the document's own linear tolerance set to at least that (and how many times its present one that is, `document.setTolerance`) accepts the edge on those places, counted as unproven. A composite curve whose segments do not meet is refused naming the segment, and an edge on a hyperbola whose vertices lie on its other branch is refused saying so.
- **The status bar no longer says 0 triangles beside a model.** After a file was opened, or when the window drew one frame after a pause (hidden, driven by an agent, or pictured with `ui.screenshot`), the renderer's numbers in the status bar could stay those of the empty document.

- In the web version one thing selected shows what it is, not its size, and two things selected are not measured, although the status bar's hint says to shift-click another to measure between: measuring by selecting works in the installed 3DCraft (found on 2026-11-19, after the release; this line was added to these notes then, and 0.2.0's own copy of the notes does not have it; mended in 0.2.1).

Over the 898 settings of the published Jigs tried, where 0.1.3 passed every assertion, this happens at 2 settings of the RC wing (its spars and ribs, found 0.05 mm apart).
- Two parts that touch where a curve of the one lies exactly on the border of a face of the other may not be told from parts that overlap: the clearance cannot be measured ("an imprinted curve that does not fit the edges it should coincide with", or "a curve that is partly on and partly off a face boundary"). 0.1.x refuses the same bodies, but met them less often: a point held at a value was a rounding beside it, and is exactly on it now (above). Over those 898 settings this happens at 1, of the gear reducer: two wheels on their shafts, each shaft's keyway now centred exactly on its axis, which 0.1.3 answered as touching.
- A thin shell of a freeform body can take about twice as long, and nearly three times as long on a computer with few cores: the check that no shell has turned inside out measures the result (a freeform tank shelled to a wall of 1.2 mm, on a laptop: 83 s where it took 44 s; on two of its cores, 146 s where it took 52 s).
- The known limitations of [0.1.3](https://github.com/johancoppens/3dcraft/releases/tag/v0.1.3), [0.1.2](https://github.com/johancoppens/3dcraft/releases/tag/v0.1.2) and [0.1.0](https://github.com/johancoppens/3dcraft/releases/tag/v0.1.0) still hold, where an entry above does not say otherwise.

## [0.1.2] - 2026-11-16

Fixes for the first things a new user meets: on many Windows computers the window no longer closes at once (measured: Intel and AMD graphics), no console window opens beside 3DCraft any more, the web version's page says what it is, and the MCP server says more about itself to the tools that connect to it. Files of 0.1.0 and 0.1.1 open unchanged.

### Fixed

- **Windows: the window no longer closes at once on many computers** (measured: Intel and AMD graphics). On them 3DCraft 0.1.0 and 0.1.1 opened and closed again without a word. There were two causes. For the way it draws see-through bodies and cut-open models, 3DCraft handed the graphics driver a shader that Vulkan's rules forbid: lenient drivers took it, but the Vulkan drivers of Intel and of AMD on Windows closed the program while they prepared it. Through Vulkan 3DCraft now draws those pictures in another way, which Vulkan's rules allow, on every system and whatever the graphics card. On Linux the picture stays the same on the graphics it was measured with; a graphics card whose Vulkan driver cannot shade each sample by itself now draws the model with one sample per pixel (edges a little less smooth), and says so in a terminal. Through OpenGL, DirectX 12 and Metal, and in the browser, they are drawn as before. On older Intel graphics with an older driver, the Vulkan driver itself crashed while 3DCraft started, before anything was drawn: 3DCraft now notices that and starts through OpenGL instead (below).
- **Windows: no console window beside 3DCraft any more.** The Start menu entry and a double-clicked `.3dcraft`, `.3dcraftjig` or `.3dcraftasm` file start 3DCraft through a small launcher, `3DCraft.exe`, which opens no console window, so there is none to close 3DCraft by. If the window cannot start, a message box says why. In the zip, double-click `3DCraft.exe`. `craft3d.exe` and `craft3d-cli.exe` are unchanged for terminals and AI agents.
- **Linux: a 3DCraft window that is killed or crashes no longer leaves a `gsettings monitor` process behind.** The window follows the desktop's light or dark setting through that program, which now ends with the window however the window ends.
- **The web version's page says what it is**: its title, *3DCraft in your browser: parametric 3D CAD*, stays while no document is open, and it has a description for search engines.

### Changed

- **Windows: when a graphics interface fails at start, 3DCraft tries the next and says so.** The window draws through Vulkan, OpenGL or DirectX 12. When one of them ends a start (a driver that crashes), the next start passes over it and takes the next in that order, and `3DCraft.exe` starts the window again by itself, at most three times in a row, so you need not do anything. A bar under the menu says which interface 3DCraft draws through and why, with **Details…**, a button to try the other interface again next time, and **Dismiss**. An interface is tried again after seven days or when your graphics driver or 3DCraft is updated; `craft3d.exe --reset-graphics` tries every interface again at once. Several windows can start at the same time: one that is still starting is never taken for one that failed. When no Vulkan driver is there, the window now prefers OpenGL to DirectX 12, which can take minutes to show the studio look. The guide's [*The window does not start or closes at once on Windows*](https://github.com/johancoppens/3dcraft/blob/v0.1.2/docs/guide/troubleshooting.md#the-window-does-not-start-or-closes-at-once-on-windows) says how to choose an interface yourself and how to make a trace of what the graphics card was asked to do, to send with a report (`CRAFT3D_GPU_TRACE`, `CRAFT3D_GPU_TOUR`).
- **The MCP server says more about itself.** It gives its display name, *3DCraft*, to clients that speak protocol version 2025-06-18 or later; its tools carry annotations, so a client knows that `command_list`, `doc_inspect` and `doc_render_preview` only read and that none of the tools reaches beyond your computer; and `command_run`'s description says that opening, saving, importing and exporting files are commands. The tools, their names and their inputs are unchanged.

### Known limitations

## [0.1.0] - 2026-11-11

The first release for people outside the project. 3DCraft is a parametric 3D CAD modeller built with and for AI: you describe a part, an AI agent builds it through 3DCraft's commands, and you watch it appear in the window, point at faces and check the result. Ready-made designs called Jigs need no AI at all: set their sizes in a form and export. Everything can also be done from the command line or a script, and 3DCraft runs in the browser too.

### Modelling

- **A history that rebuilds.** Variables with units and formulas, and an ordered list of features: change a value and the model rebuilds, only from the feature that changed. Undo and redo, roll back to any step, suppress, edit, move, rename and delete features; insert a feature in the middle and the ones after it follow. A feature that fails says why (often with the largest size that fits) and can be repaired; features made from it name it as the cause.
- **Features.** Blocks, cylinders, cones, spheres and tori; extrude, revolve, sweep (along a path, a helix, or two rails that carry and scale the profile, also closed into a ring) and loft from sketches; patterns, mirror and move; shell, draft, offset, move and delete faces, thicken; ISO metric threads (cosmetic or modelled); datum planes, axes, points and helices; sheet bodies; several bodies in one document.
- **Fillets and chamfers** on edges, chosen by name, by description or by pointing at them.
- **Names that stay put.** Faces and edges are named by how they were made, so a fillet does not jump to another edge when an earlier feature changes, and a face that is gone is reported instead of silently replaced.
- **Units.** Millimetres, inches and the other length units per document, angles in degrees or radians; the length unit can be changed later while keeping the sizes. Mass, time, force, pressure and stress, moment and energy in expressions (`1000 N / (100 * (1 mm)^2)` is 10 MPa), so a design rule can say `"max": "235 MPa"`; an output can carry a label and the unit it is shown in. Named sets can pick flat faces by their plate thickness (`thickness`). Litres (`L`, `mL`) for volumes. A document's tolerance can be set (`document.setTolerance`).
- **Colours and materials** per body and per face, with metallic and roughness, following the face through later edits and kept in 3MF, OBJ and STEP exports. A face's own colour wins over its body's; the button on a body's card that clears its face colours (`face.appearance` with `clear`) takes them all away in one step.

### Sketches

- **Sketches with a constraint solver.** Lines, arcs, circles, rectangles, paths, splines (also through computed points, and closed splines that stay smooth where they close) and patterns, held by dimensions and geometric constraints and solved by 3DCraft's own solver; coordinates can be formulas.
- **Clear diagnostics.** A sketch that cannot be solved names the constraint at fault and the ones it contradicts; a line squeezed to nothing and a sketch with freedom left are reported.
- **Curves from formulas** (`sketch.curve`): `x(t)` and `y(t)` in the variables, fitted again whenever they change, as in the involute gear example; points on them and tangents to them can be constrained.
- Sketches on a face, with the x axis turned to a direction you choose.

### Text

- **Text in sketches** (`sketch.text`), straight or along a curve (`sketch.textOnCurve`): a template that can hold formulas, cap height or em size, anchor, alignment, rotation, line spacing and wrapping to a width, as closed profiles of exact curves with kerning, ready to extrude, emboss or engrave. Text and any sketch wrap onto a cylinder or a cone (`feature.extrude` with `wrap`), embossed or engraved along the surface at true size. Letters are named by glyph, so a feature on one fails by name instead of jumping to another.
- **Fonts.** Noto Sans, JetBrains Mono and a single-stroke font drawn for 3DCraft are built in and carried by the installers (fetched when first needed on the web); `font.add` keeps another font in the document when its licence allows it. The measure `textExtent` gives a text's size.

### Booleans

- **Union, subtract and intersect** between bodies of any shape, including curved and freeform faces, and cuts by sheets and between sheets.
- **Sew** (`feature.boolean` with `op: sew`): sheets that meet along their edges, even tangent or in curvature, joined into one sheet or a closed solid without cutting them.
- When an operation cannot be done exactly it refuses with a clear message instead of producing a broken solid.

### Sheets, plates, sheet metal and origami

- **Plates.** Give faces (flat, developable or freeform) a thickness, inside, outside or in the middle, with square, bevel, mitre, touch and over/under joints between neighbouring plates.
- **Developable surfaces and unfolding.** A developable surface between two curves, unfolded flat at true size with a map between the pattern and the part, and a measure of how developable a face is. Rails with corners (a fan from the corner, a crease where both rails turn) and closed rails (a tube), such as a sea kayak's sheer panels.
- **Sheet metal.** A base with flanges, bent by inner radius and K-factor, with rectangular, round and tear corner reliefs; corners closed by overlap, butt or mitre; holes and cut-outs on any flat; a bend table; checks for bend radius, flange length, hole-to-bend distance and relief; the flat pattern exported with its bend lines. Holes across a bend, and a later through cut carried back onto the flat pattern. Flanges inset on an edge (several tabs), on a flange's side, on a hole's edge or with an outline of their own, with bend reliefs at their ends. A press-brake check (`sheetMetal.bendSequence`) that searches an order of bends and proves the punch and die clear the part, or checks an order you give, also for parts with holes across their bends. Louvres and tabs: a flange on the edge of a cut-out in any flat. The measures `flatExtent` and `flatArea` for a flat pattern's size. Sharp bends (an inner radius of 0); curved flanges along arcs and circles of the base (a round pan, a tray with rounded corners), with the stretch of their approximated flat pattern proven (`bendStrain`); jogs that step a flat; formed louvres pressed by a tool (`louvreStretch`); and hems (flanges bent 180 degrees), which the press-brake check presses flat in two strokes.
- **Cutting.** Plates and sheet-metal parts recognised for cutting: a cut list grouped by thickness and material, identical parts counted and sizes given to the document's tolerance (`cut.list`, JSON or CSV), and every part's outline to DXF or SVG, per part or on one sheet, with a kerf (`cut.export`).
- **Origami.** A crease pattern of mountains, valleys and cuts folded by angles that follow variables, or folded flat with the order of the layers worked out; Kawasaki and Maekawa checks; the crease pattern exported to SVG and DXF. Thick panels on a membrane, slits and slots, a flat-folded stack folded on, and patterns of thousands of creases.

### Freeform surfacing

- **Analysis.** Zebra stripes, highlight lines, a curvature map and a draft check drawn on the exact surfaces, curvature combs on edges and iso-curves, and the measures `draft` and `curvature` at a point (the Analysis toggle in the window).
- **Surfaces from curve networks.** Gordon surfaces through crossing u and v curves (`feature.surface.network`), Coons patches (`feature.surface.coons`) and lofts with guide curves, as exact B-splines.
- **Continuity across seams.** An edge held in position, tangent (G1) or in curvature (G2) to a plane, cylinder, cone, sphere or a spline face, and `measure.continuity`, which proves G0, G1 or G2 with bounded gaps (also as an assertion). A curvature seam spreads its change over the sheet with the least variation of curvature, so the surface stays fair beside the seam.
- **Trimmed faces and N-sided fills.** A face cut out of an existing surface by curves on it, drawn on it or projected onto it, such as an opening or a label area (`feature.surface.trim`), and a loop of 3 to 6 curves filled with a smooth sheet that meets its neighbours tangent or in curvature (`feature.surface.nsided`).
- Zebra stripes and highlight lines stay clean where they are finer than a pixel: they fade to grey instead of breaking into moiré.

### Assemblies and motion

- **Assemblies** as their own documents (`.3dcraftasm`): instances of other `.3dcraft` files by reference (the assembly follows the part when you edit it) or packed into one file, configurations by a part's public parameters, one body picked from a multi-body part, and rigid or flexible subassemblies. A part whose feature fails marks its instance with the reason and fails the assembly's check.
- **Mates, joints and couplings**, solved by 3DCraft's own solver: fixed, revolute and the other mates between named faces of instances (a face that is gone fails its mate by name), free joints set by value or dragged, gears, racks and screws coupling two mates (a screw can also tie a cylindrical mate's slide to its own turn, as a thread), and linear couplings of any number of motions (a planetary stage). A joint's solved value and an instance's pose as measures, usable in checks and formulas (`measure.joint`, `measure.pose`). A part drawn in another length unit keeps its size. Measures and checks name an instance's bodies by path (`Instance/Body`, also into subassemblies); clearance, overlap and mass per instance; STEP export with the assembly's structure.
- **Motion over a timeline `t`.** Play a mechanism in the window, see collisions in red, draw the trace of a point (and export it to SVG or DXF), and check any assertion over the whole motion (`motion.check`). Dead points are reported with their `t`.

### Measuring and design checks

- **Measures.** Volume, area, mass and inertia, distances, clearance, wall thickness, holes that go through, rays and symmetry; what lies below a plane, the section by a plane and its moments, and the level that holds a given volume. The floating position (level and tilt) of a hull for a given weight and centre of gravity, with its stability. A section's torsion constant, shear centre and warping constant, for any section with holes.
- **Assertions** kept in the document ("this wall is at least 2 mm"), checked at every rebuild and shown in the window, optionally only when a condition holds. A bound is met within the document's tolerance (an angle within its angular tolerance), and a bound finer than that is warned about; the line over the feature list counts the checks that pass, fail or are not proven. Read-only **outputs** computed from measures and formulas.
- **Selections.** Queries ("every 8 mm hole"), a selection shared between you and the agent, and named sets.

### Formulas and Jigs

- **Formulas that decide.** Comparisons, `and`, `or` and `not`, `if`, `choose` and `clamp`, and the usual functions in any expression; tables of values looked up by row or key (`belt["GT2"].pitch`); features switched on and off by a condition, so a yes-or-no parameter adds or removes a lid. A part switched off is left out of the exports, cut lists and measures that name it, so one list serves every option, and a check on it alone is inactive.
- **Jigs: designs anyone can set without AI.** Publish the variables that drive a design as parameters (number, whole number, yes or no, a choice; with a label, group, range and step) and export it as a `.3dcraftjig` (File › Export as Jig…). Opening a Jig shows its **Parameters** tab: drag or type values and the model follows; values are kept in range and the form says so; a value that breaks the model is marked, with *Back to last good*. A Jig is never overwritten: Save saves the result as a model, and *Open for editing* makes an editable copy.
- `craft3d-cli check-range` proves a Jig over its whole parameter range. The separate 3DCraft Jigs collection has Jigs to try, from a laser-cut box and a GT2 pulley to a planetary gearbox and a sundial for your place.

### Files and export

- **The native `.3dcraft` format**, with a promise: every file a released version saves opens in every later version and rebuilds to the same bodies.
- **Import** STEP: solids, sheets and assemblies, with colours; FreeCAD documents (`.FCStd`) and BRep files: the visible final objects' solids, with their names, placements and colours.
- **Export** STEP (the exact geometry), STL, 3MF and OBJ (meshes at Coarse, Normal or Fine quality, with colours), and DXF and SVG: the flat outline of a sketch or of faces at true size, a whole cut sheet of a named set laid out to your laser's width, flat and crease patterns, or a drawing of the model as seen (orthographic to scale or perspective, hidden lines left out or dashed), its edges written as the lines, arcs, circles, ellipses and splines they are.
- An export dialog that first asks what goes into the file and offers only the formats that hold it. Bodies hidden in the window are left out of a 3D export of the model and of the cut list, and the dialog says which.
- Saving from the window never replaces a file you were not asked about, and an assembly is saved as `.3dcraftasm`, on the desktop and in the browser.

### The window and installing

- **The window.** A tab per document with its own view; a feature list with an eye per feature and a double click (or F) that flies the camera to it; a history player like a video player; a view cube, see-through bodies and a section plane; File, Edit, View and Help menus with keyboard shortcuts (F1), and light or dark panels; a status bar that says what was done. It draws with your graphics card, or on the processor when there is none. The interface is set in Inter, with JetBrains Mono for code.
- **A start screen** with New part, New assembly and Open, your recent files, the panel that connects an AI agent, and twenty examples built in, so they open offline and in the browser, under three tabs: ten finished parts (a steel bookend and a T-slot profile marked *Start here*, then a moka pot, a ship propeller, a snap-fit enclosure, a hydraulic cylinder, a spiral staircase, a solitaire ring, a flat-pack bookcase and a mailer box), six Jigs that open on their form (a laser-cut box, a Gridfinity bin, a dovetail drawer, a sheet-metal enclosure, a planetary gearbox and a sundial for your place) and four assemblies that open with their player, ready to play (a toggle clamp, a Jansen walker, a scissor jack and a radial engine). An example opens in a tab named after it and counts as unchanged until you edit it; Save asks where. *More examples* opens the tab's part of the galleries on the website. The examples are MIT, the Jigs and assemblies CC BY 4.0 (`EXAMPLES-LICENSE.md`).
- **Check for updates**: Help › Check for updates…, a link beside the version on the start screen, and the About dialog open the website in your browser, which says whether there is a newer version; 3DCraft itself connects nowhere. The AppImage carries update information, so a tool such as AppImageLauncher can update it. The guide says how to install a newer version of each package.
- **Autosave and crash recovery.** Unsaved work is saved in the background, and the status bar says when for the document in front; after a crash the window offers to recover it. Closing the window or a tab asks before unsaved changes are lost, naming the document.
- **Clear errors and bug reports.** A failed file action shows a dialog with what went wrong; after any failed command the status bar offers *Copy details*, a short report to paste into a bug report.
- **Installers** for Linux (an AppImage, and a tarball whose `install.sh` installs 3DCraft into `~/.local` and whose `uninstall.sh` removes exactly what it installed), macOS (a `.dmg` for Apple Silicon) and Windows (a `.msi` installer, and a zip), each with the fonts 3DCraft draws with. The `.msi`, the tarball and the macOS app register the `.3dcraft`, `.3dcraftjig` and `.3dcraftasm` file types; the AppImage brings its menu entry and file types through a tool such as AppImageLauncher.
- **A user guide** for first-time users, in 22 chapters and six parts, with pictures of this version's window: installing (and installing a newer version) and connecting an agent, your first five minutes, working with an AI agent with five tasks to try, the window, Jigs, assemblies and files; how 3DCraft thinks, a first part, formulas, features, sheet metal, surfaces, origami and selection; measuring and engineering checks; making Jigs, the command line and programs on 3DCraft; troubleshooting and a reference. Seven lessons, from an L-bracket to an involute gear, check their own volume. Help › User guide opens the guide of your version on 3dcraft.dev.

### Rendering

- **A studio look**: image-based lighting from three bundled environments, soft shadows, ambient occlusion, a ground that catches shadows and reflections, contact shadows, depth of field and bloom, refined while the camera stands still; a view setting that never changes the document.
- **Materials** kept in the document: anisotropic metal, clear coat paint, glass with refraction and subsurface wrap, with six presets (`material.presets`); solid textures (wood, marble, granite, speckle, noise, brushed metal, carbon) evaluated in the model's space; exports carry what their formats hold.
- Both looks are drawn by the graphics card and by the processor alike, the processor's picture being the reference the graphics card is tested against. Pictures without a window (`view.render`, `view.replay`, `craft3d-cli render`) use the graphics card when there is one (`renderer: auto|cpu|gpu`).
- **Animations** (`view.replay`): variables moved along tracks with easing, the motion timeline, a camera path with orbit and dolly, the look, frames per second and duration, written as PNG frames or, through an installed ffmpeg, as MP4, WebM or GIF. The window records itself too (`ui.record`), choosing an encoder your ffmpeg actually has.
- **The same model on every computer.** 3DCraft computes its sines, cosines and other functions itself instead of using the system's maths library, so a document rebuilds to the same bodies whatever the platform.

### Web

- **3DCraft in the browser**: the same program as a web page (WebGPU, or WebGL2, see-through and sections included), with nothing to install. Open, save, import and export with your system's file dialogs in Chrome, Edge and on Chromebooks, as downloads elsewhere; use Jigs (a Jig on the same site opens with `?open=`), play assemblies and open the start screen's examples, which are built in. The model is rebuilt, measured and exported in the background, so the page stays responsive.

### AI agents and the command line

- **Drive it with an AI agent.** A built-in MCP server (`craft3d-cli mcp`) that any MCP client can start: headless, or bridged to the open window so you see every step as the agent works. The **Connect an AI agent** panel on the start screen gives the command for Claude Code and the configuration for Claude Desktop, Cursor, VS Code or any other MCP client, ready to copy.
- **Allow AI agents.** One switch, on the start screen and behind the agents pill in the status bar, lets agents connect to the window with no flags and nothing secret to copy. They read and write files in a work folder you choose; the window asks you before an agent reaches any other folder, and never offers your home folder itself or the folders of keys and settings. The **Agent** tab of the side panel shows what agents did to the document, newest first, and a feature an agent made carries a mark (✦); `agent.log` gives an agent the same.
- **Everything is a command**, with a name and plain JSON parameters; the same commands run from the command line, a script or the JSON control channel, and the agent can light up faces, fly the camera, play the history, take pictures of the window, record it as a video (`ui.record`) and get the bug report (`session.report`).
- **The command line.** `craft3d-cli` runs commands and scripts, prints a document's state, renders pictures, converts files, draws a model as seen to DXF or SVG, makes a video of how a design is built, scores a design against a task, proves a Jig over its range, and serves MCP and a JSON-lines protocol.

### Known limitations

- Tested on Linux. The macOS and Windows packages are new and less tested: each is built and started on its system, but the full test suite has not passed on those systems yet.
- The installers are not signed, so Windows and macOS warn the first time (the guide shows how to open them).
- macOS: Apple Silicon only (M1 or later), and double-clicking a `.3dcraft` file there starts 3DCraft without opening the file (use File › Open).
- Windows: `craft3d.exe` and `craft3d-cli.exe` show a generic icon in Explorer; the Start menu entry, the file types and the window have 3DCraft's.
- Linux: the packages need glibc 2.35 or newer, as on Ubuntu 22.04 and later.
- 3DCraft does not update itself: Check for updates opens the website, and you install a newer version as the guide says.
- The window has no toolbar of modelling buttons: you model through commands, sent by an AI agent, a Jig's form or a script of your own, and the window is for watching, pointing, checking and exporting.
- An AI agent cannot drive the web version yet.
- Some modelling cases are refused rather than built (the message says which): among them some booleans and freeform fillets, curved sheet-metal bends along a spline and some sharp ones, curved origami creases, and text wrapped onto a surface other than a cylinder or a cone. Text is laid out glyph by glyph with kerning; shaping for complex scripts (such as Arabic or the Indic scripts) is for a later version. Every refusal reported helps.
