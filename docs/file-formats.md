## Native format: `.3dcraft`

**Implemented today (format version 40, which adds the `plate` feature kind, `{"type": "plate", "faces": [names], "thickness": expression, "side": "in|out|mid", "joints": [{"edge": name, "kind": "square|bevel|mitre|touch|over|under|onto", "with": name, "thickness": expression, "side": …, "angle": expression, "onto": plane}]}`, and the name role `rim` ([`design/plates.md`](design/plates.md)); 39 adds the `developable` and `unfold` feature kinds: `{"type": "developable", "a": {"sketch": 7}, "b": {"edges": [names]}}` and `{"type": "unfold", "faces": [names], "at": [three expressions], "angle": expression}` ([`design/developable.md`](design/developable.md)); 38 adds the `fold` feature kind, below; 37 adds `asinh`, `acosh`, `atanh`, `log2`, `hypot` and `sign` to expressions and lets a body's colour and density be expressions, below; 36 renames the assertion measure `interference` to `overlap` with the opposite `is`, below; 35 adds curves from formulas and live splines in sketches, below; 34 adds a feature's condition, `when`, below; 33 adds tables and choices from a table, below; 32 adds public parameters, document info and `thumb.png`, below; 31 adds conditions to expressions and reserves the names of their functions, below; 30 adds `hidden`, the features hidden in the view, below; 29 adds the sketch constraint kinds `x` and `y`, below; 28 adds `assertions`, below; 27 adds `sets`, named sets, below; 26 adds `faces`, a colour per face, below; 25 adds the `import` feature kind and the `blobs/` entries that hold imported STEP files, below; 24 adds the `split` feature kind, below; 23 adds the `step` of a circular pattern (a screw), below; 22 adds the `fill` feature kind, below; 21 adds nested sketch patterns and skipped instances, below; 20 stores where a sketch's image points lay in its stored solution, below; 19 adds the `offsetProfile` feature kind and the name forms of its curves, below; 18 adds patterns inside a sketch and their image points and name forms, below; 17 reserves the names of the whole-number functions of expressions, below; 16 adds a colour and a material per body and the `moveFace`, `thicken` and `draft` feature kinds, below; 15 writes a copy of a copy in its short form, below; version 1 to 12 files open unchanged, version 13 files open with one change, below; 14 let a thread's size and pitch follow the model, below; 13 added the `datumHelix` and `thread` feature kinds and the name role `thread`, below; 12 added the `deleteFace` feature kind, below; 11 added the `offsetFace` and `shell` feature kinds, below; 10 added the `sweep` feature kind, written `{"type": "sweep", "sketch": 7, "path": 9}` with the feature ids of the sketch that holds the profile and of the sketch whose curves are the path; 9 added the `fillet` feature kind and the name roles `fillet` and `filletCorner`, below; 8 added the `loft` feature kind, written `{"type": "loft", "sections": [{"sketch": 7}, {"sketch": 9, "start": 3}]}` with the feature id of each section's sketch in the order the loft passes through them, the entity id of the curve a section starts with when one was given, and `"ruled": true` and `"closed": true` only when set; 7 added the `chamfer` feature kind and the name roles `chamfer` and `chamferCorner`, below; 5 added the `transform`, `mirror` and `pattern` feature kinds and names that are copies of names; 6 added the `spline` kind of sketch entity, written `{"type": "spline", "degree": 3, "control": [1, 2, 3, 4]}` with the ids of its control points, its knots being clamped and uniform by definition):** a ZIP of stored entries: `manifest.json` (below), and since version 25 one `blobs/<sha256>.step` per imported STEP file. Unknown extra entries, and blobs no feature names, are ignored on load and not preserved on save. A document whose file would exceed the 256 MiB that are read is not saved (and an import that would make it so is refused). Since version 32 a Jig is saved with `thumb.png`, a picture for a gallery (below). The directory layout is the plan, not built; autosave and crash recovery are below.

A `.3dcraft` file is a ZIP of STORE (uncompressed) entries (*planned, not built:* a directory with the same layout, for version control and debugging):

```text
manifest.json            the document, versioned
blobs/<sha256>.step      the bytes of each imported STEP file, once per content (format 25)
blobs/<blake3>.zst       (planned) content-addressed, compressed caches of regeneration results
thumb.png                preview of a Jig, 400 x 300 (CPU renderer, on the viewport's light background; format 32)
```

**`manifest.json`** holds `format_version` and the whole `craft3d-doc` document as JSON: readable, diffable and writable by an agent in an emergency.

- **What is stored is what the user wrote.** Expressions are stored as source text (`"width / 2 + 3 mm"`), not as evaluated numbers. Values are recomputed on load.
- **Order is stored explicitly:** the feature list in application order, the variable table in evaluation order, each sketch's constraints in the order they were added, and each sketch's last solved state (so a reopened sketch does not flip to another solution branch).
- **Ids are stable.** Feature and sketch-entity ids are never reused within a document, because persistent names are built from them.
- **Caches are optional.** A cached body is keyed by the hash of everything it was derived from. Loading verifies the key; a missing, stale or damaged cache is regenerated silently. Deleting `blobs/` of a document without imports must not change the model.
- **Imported files are data** (format 25, 2026-10-21). `file.import` adds one `import` feature per solid of a STEP file, written `{"type": "import", "file_name": "part.step", "solid": 0, "sha256": "…"}`: the solid's position among the file's solids and sheets, and the SHA-256 of the file's bytes, which the archive holds as the stored entry `blobs/<sha256>.step`, once however many features read it. The body is regenerated from those bytes, never from the file on disk, so it is not a cache: a `.3dcraft` file whose manifest names a blob it does not hold, or whose blob does not hash to its name, is refused (`MissingBlob`, `DamagedBlob`), and a blob no feature names is not read. The manifest alone (`from_manifest_json`) cannot hold an import. In `document.inspect` the bytes show as `{"bytes": n, "sha256": "…"}`.
- **Version history.** 1 (2026-10-12): variables; block, cylinder, sphere. 2 (2026-10-12): adds cone and torus, and an optional `orientation` (`axis`, `angle`) on block, cylinder, cone and torus. 3 (2026-10-12): adds the `sketch` feature kind. 4 (2026-10-12): adds the `extrude` feature kind (`sketch`: the id of the sketch feature, `distance`: an expression, `symmetric`) and a second kind of sketch plane, `{"type": "face", "face": {"feature": 2, "role": "endCap", "sources": [7, 8, 9]}, "offset": expr}`: a flat face of an earlier body by its persistent name. Nothing in an older file changed meaning in any step; the number was raised so that an older build reports "a newer 3DCraft is needed" instead of an unknown field or kind. 25 (2026-10-21): adds the `import` feature kind and the `blobs/<sha256>.step` entries (above).
- **The ends of an extrusion (2026-10-13, no new version).** An `extrude` has two more optional fields: `end`, one of `{"type": "distance"}`, `{"type": "throughAll"}` and `{"type": "upToFace", "face": {…a persistent name…}}`, and `reversed` (against the sketch's normal). An extrusion that ends at its `distance` and is not reversed is written without them, byte for byte as before, so a file that uses neither is one an earlier build of version 6 reads; a file that does use them is refused by such a build as an unknown field, not as "a newer 3DCraft is needed" (the number was not raised because no existing data changed meaning; raising it is the developer's call, see [`decisions.md`](decisions.md)). `distance` is still written for the other ends: it is the distance the extrusion goes back to. What "through all" goes through and where an extrusion up to a face ends are not in the file: they are derived at regeneration from the features before it ([`design/sweeps.md`](design/sweeps.md) › *Extrude ends*).
- **A sketch in the file** is a feature of type `sketch` with a `plane` (`{"type": "world", "plane": "xy|yz|zx", "offset": expr}`), `entities` in the order they were drawn (`{"id", "construction", "geometry"}`, where geometry is a `point` with `x` and `y`, a `line` with `start` and `end`, a `circle` with `center` and `radius`, or an `arc` with `center`, `start` and `end`, all referring to point entities by id), `constraints` in the order they were added (`{"id", "kind": {"type": …}}` with entity ids and, for dimensions, a `value` expression), and the next free entity and constraint ids. The point coordinates and circle radii are plain numbers in the document's length unit: they are the last solution, and the solver starts from them.
- **Names of pieces (2026-10-14, no new version).** A persistent name has one more role, `piece`, written like a `copy`: `{"feature": 2, "role": "piece", "sources": [1], "of": {"feature": 2, "role": "side", "sources": [7]}}` is the second of the faces that sketch entity 7 sweeps in feature 2 (as text `F2:piece(1)/F2:side(e7)`); the first keeps the plain name. Regeneration gives such names to what a spline sweeps when it is cut into pieces (a closed spline, a spline with corners) and to the second half of a full revolution of a spline. A file never held one before, so nothing changed meaning; a build from before this date refuses the role as unknown.
- **Names of edges and vertices by their faces (2026-10-14, no new version).** A persistent name has two more roles, `between` (an edge) and `corner` (a vertex), and one more optional field that only those roles have: `faces`, an object with `on`, the names of the faces of that feature's body the entity lies on, and `near`, the names of the faces next to it that tell it from others on the same faces; `sources` is then empty or holds the one number that tells it apart when the faces do not. `{"feature": 7, "role": "corner", "sources": [], "faces": {"on": [{"feature": 7, "role": "copy", "sources": [0], "of": {"feature": 1, "role": "face", "sources": [1]}}, …]}}` is what a datum point at a vertex of a boolean stores. The listed names are faces and list none themselves; a file in which they do is refused. Their order in the file does not matter: they are put in the one order on reading. As text, in commands and messages, the same name is `F7:corner[F7:copy(0)/F1:face(1),…|…](1)`. A build from before this change does not know the roles and refuses such a file as damaged.
- **Chamfers (2026-10-14, version 7).** One feature kind: `{"type": "chamfer", "edges": [[name, name], …], "size": …, "chain": false}`. Each edge is the two faces it lies between, as names (`{"feature": 1, "role": "face", "sources": [1]}`), the first distance being measured along the first; the body chamfered is that of the feature the first name starts with, and nothing else says so. `size` is `{"type": "equal", "distance": expr}`, `{"type": "twoDistances", "distance": expr, "second": expr}` or `{"type": "distanceAngle", "distance": expr, "angle": expr}`. `chain` may be left out (false). Two name roles for the faces a chamfer makes, written like every other role: `chamfer` (`sources`: the place of the edge's reference in `edges`, and when that reference gave several edges, which of them) and `chamferCorner` (`sources`: the places of the three references whose chamfers meet there); as text `F9:chamfer(0)`, `F9:chamfer(0,2)`, `F9:chamferCorner(0,1,2)`, with plain numbers. The version was raised because a build that knows version 6 could not read the new kind or the new roles: it now reports "a newer 3DCraft is needed". Nothing a version-6 file holds changed meaning (`v6_to_v7` is the identity; a version-6 fixture is pinned in `crates/format/tests/format.rs`). *For the developer to confirm:* three features of 2026-10-13/14 (datums, extrude ends, the roles `between` and `corner`) were added as optional fields and kinds without a new version, which leaves version-6 files that an earlier version-6 build cannot read; whether those should have been a version each is open, and this version 7 does not settle it.
- **Fillets (2026-10-14, version 9).** One feature kind: `{"type": "fillet", "edges": [[name, name], …], "radius": expr, "chain": false}`. Each edge is the two faces it lies between, as a chamfer's (their order says nothing here); the body filleted is that of the feature the first name starts with. `chain` may be left out (false); `radius` may not. Two name roles for the faces a fillet makes: `fillet` (`sources` as for `chamfer`) and `filletCorner` (`sources` as for `chamferCorner`); as text `F9:fillet(0)`, `F9:fillet(0,2)`, `F9:filletCorner(0,1,2)`. The version was raised for the reason versions 7 and 8 were: a build that knows version 8 could not read the new kind or the new roles, and now reports "a newer 3DCraft is needed". Nothing a version-8 file holds changed meaning (`v8_to_v9` is the identity; a version-8 fixture with a chamfer and a loft is pinned in `crates/format/tests/format.rs`). The fillet was made as version 8 beside the loft, which took 8 first; it became 9 when the two were merged. *For the developer to confirm,* with versions 7 and 8: whether a new feature kind is a version each time.
- **Reference geometry (2026-10-13, no new version).** Three feature kinds that make no body: `{"type": "datumPlane", "by": …}`, `{"type": "datumAxis", "by": …}` and `{"type": "datumPoint", "at": …}`. A plane's `by` is `{"type": "offset", "from": plane, "distance": expr}`, `{"type": "angle", "from": plane, "about": axis, "angle": expr}` or `{"type": "threePoints", "points": [point, point, point]}`; an axis's `by` is `{"type": "twoPoints", "points": [point, point]}`, `{"type": "edge", "between": [name, name]}`, `{"type": "face", "face": name}` or `{"type": "twoPlanes", "planes": [plane, plane]}`. A *plane* is `{"type": "world", "plane": "xy"}`, `{"type": "face", "face": name}` or `{"type": "datum", "feature": id}`; an *axis* is `{"type": "world", "axis": "x"}`, `{"type": "edge", "between": [name, name]}` (the two faces the edge lies between) or `{"type": "datum", "feature": id}`; a *point* is `{"type": "at", "position": [expr, expr, expr]}`, `{"type": "vertex", "vertex": name}`, `{"type": "center", "between": [name, name]}` or `{"type": "datum", "feature": id}`; a *name* is a persistent name as elsewhere in the file. Existing kinds got four optional places for a datum's feature id: a third sketch plane `{"type": "datum", "plane": id, "offset": expr}`, `plane` on a `mirror`, a fourth revolve axis `{"type": "datum", "axis": id}`, and `about` on a circular pattern layout. `plane` and `about` are written only when set, so a file that uses no datum is byte for byte what a build before datums wrote, and such a build refuses a file that does use them as an unknown kind or field (the number was not raised, for the same reason as for the ends of an extrusion: no existing data changed meaning; raising it is the developer's call). Where a datum lies is not in the file: it is derived at regeneration.
- **Face operations (2026-10-14, version 11).** Two feature kinds: `{"type": "offsetFace", "faces": [name, …], "distance": expr}` (the faces of one body, the one of the feature the first name starts with; outward positive) and `{"type": "shell", "source": 3, "thickness": expr, "open": [name, …]}` (`open` may be left out: a closed hollow; its names are faces of `source`). No name role was added: their faces are copies (`copy(0)`; the faces of a shell's hollow `copy(1)`). The version was raised for the reason versions 7 to 10 were; nothing a version-10 file holds changed meaning (`v10_to_v11` is the identity; a version-10 fixture with a fillet, a datum on its face and a sweep is pinned in `crates/format/tests/format.rs`). Made as version 10 beside the sweep, which took 10 first; it became 11 when `work` was merged into its branch.
- **Delete face (2026-10-14, version 12).** One feature kind: `{"type": "deleteFace", "faces": [name, …]}` (the faces of one body, the one of the feature the first name starts with). No name role was added: the faces that stay are copies (`copy(0)`), edges and vertices are named by their faces. `v11_to_v12` is the identity; a version-11 fixture with an offset and a shell is pinned in `crates/format/tests/format.rs`. The same build changed which way a sketch or a datum plane on a face of a subtraction's tool faces (a pocket's floor, a hole's wall: now out of the material, as on every other face). That is how regeneration reads the face, not what the file holds, and it was not migrated: a version-11 file with a sketch on such a face opens with the sketch turned over (its normal and its y axis the other way), so a pin that went down into the material now stands up into the pocket ([`decisions.md`](decisions.md), 2026-10-14).
- **Move face (2026-10-15, version 16).** One feature kind: `{"type": "moveFace", "faces": [name, …], "translate": [expr, expr, expr], "pivot": [expr, expr, expr], "axis": [expr, expr, expr], "angle": expr}` (the faces of one body, the one of the feature the first name starts with; the motion as a `transform`'s; every field but `faces` may be left out: no shift, the origin, z, no turn). No name role was added: every face, edge and vertex is a copy (`copy(0)`). It came in version 16 with the bodies' appearance and the kinds `thicken` and `draft` (one version for all four changes, decided when the branches were merged); nothing an earlier file holds changed meaning, and a version-15 build reports that a newer 3DCraft is needed.
- **Thicken (2026-10-15, version 16).** One feature kind: `{"type": "thicken", "faces": [name, …], "thickness": expr, "side": "outside"}` (`side` is `outside`, `inside` or `both`, always written; a file without it reads `outside`). The faces are of one body, which keeps its own: the layer is a body of its own. No name role: the faces are copies, `copy(0)` on or along a face of the source, `copy(1)` under one. Version 16, as for move face.
- **Draft (2026-10-15, version 16).** One feature kind: `{"type": "draft", "faces": [name, …], "neutral": plane, "angle": expr, "reversed": true}` (a *plane* as for the datums: `{"type": "world", "plane": "xy"}`, `{"type": "face", "face": name}` or `{"type": "datum", "feature": id}`; `reversed` written only when set). No name role: every face, edge and vertex is a copy (`copy(0)`). Version 16, as for move face.
- **Helix and thread (2026-10-14, version 13).** Two feature kinds and a name role. `{"type": "datumHelix", "axis": axis, "start": {"type": "radius", "radius": expr} or {"type": "point", "point": point}, "pitch": expr, "extent": {"type": "turns", "turns": expr} or {"type": "height", "height": expr}, "left_handed": true}` (an axis and a point as for the other datums; `left_handed` written only when set) makes no body. A `sweep` whose `path` is the id of a datum helix is swept along it; the field is unchanged. `{"type": "thread", "face": name, "start": name, "size": "M10", "pitch": expr, "length": expr, "left_handed": true, "cosmetic": true}` (version 13; the two flags written only when set; version 14 writes `modeled` instead, below) cuts a thread into the body of the feature `face` is a face of. The role `thread` names a face of the groove, with one plain number: `{"feature": 9, "role": "thread", "sources": [1]}` is `F9:thread(1)`, a flank. `v12_to_v13` is the identity; a version-12 fixture (a block with a face deleted) is pinned in `crates/format/tests/format.rs`.
- **A thread's size and pitch that follow (2026-10-14, version 14).** In a `thread`, `size` is the designation (`"M10"`), or `{"diameter": expr}` (the nominal diameter as an expression of a length, `{"diameter": "d"}`), or absent: the size of ISO 261 whose nominal diameter the face has, found at every regeneration. `pitch` is absent for the coarse pitch of the size, looked up at every regeneration, so that it follows the size; written, it is the pitch given, which stays. Version 13 always wrote `size` as a designation and always wrote `pitch`: when the command was given none it wrote the size's coarse pitch as text (`"1.5 mm"` for `M10`, `format!("{pitch} mm")` of the table), and that pitch stayed when the size was changed later. **`v13_to_v14` changes the meaning of such a file:** a thread's `pitch` that is exactly that text for its `size` is taken out (against a frozen copy of the version-13 table in `migrate.rs`), so it follows the size from then on; any other text stays (`"1.25 mm"` on M10, `"1.5"` without a unit, `"0.45 mm * 1"`). A pitch a user typed as exactly the default text cannot be told from it and follows the size too; it is the same pitch until the size changes. **Cosmetic by default (the same version):** a thread is written `"modeled": true` when its groove is cut into the body, and nothing when it is cosmetic; `cosmetic` is no longer a field. Version 13 wrote `"cosmetic": true` for a cosmetic thread and nothing for a cut one, so `v13_to_v14` takes `"cosmetic": true` out and writes `"modeled": true` into every other thread: each version-13 thread keeps what it was. A version-13 fixture with five threads is pinned in `crates/format/tests/format.rs`.
- **Copies of copies in the short form (2026-10-14, version 15).** A `copy` name's `sources` are the instances along the way: the one number 0 for a run of instance 0 (the way goes on through instance 0 until the feature of the name in `of`), or numbers none of which is 0, one per step. Version 14 and earlier wrote one link per feature (`{"feature": 9, "role": "copy", "sources": [0], "of": {"feature": 7, "role": "copy", "sources": [0], "of": …}}`); such a chain means the same entity and is read into the short form whatever the version (links that go the same way are merged), so nothing in the JSON is migrated. The version was raised so an older build says a newer 3DCraft is needed instead of refusing a copy with several numbers ([`design/names.md`](design/names.md), *Copies of copies*).
- **A colour per face (2026-10-21, version 26).** The document has one more field, `faces`, written only when it holds something: `[{"feature": 3, "face": {"feature": 3, "role": "copy", "sources": [1], "of": {"feature": 2, "role": "face", "sources": [0]}}, "appearance": {"color": [20, 160, 60], "roughness": 0.3}}, …]`, one entry per face given a colour (`face.appearance`): the feature whose body has the face, the face's persistent name there (written as every name is) and an appearance as a body's. In ascending order of feature and then name, without repeats; loading checks the order, that the ids were given out, that the name is a face's of a feature no later than the body's, and the ranges. An entry of a face that is gone is kept and does nothing. `v25_to_v26` is the identity: a version-25 file has no `faces`; the version was raised so an older build says a newer 3DCraft is needed instead of refusing an unknown field. The fixture `v26.3dcraft` has a face coloured before a cut (both pieces), one in the cut's body, and one in one instance of a pattern; `v26.expected.json` pins the entries (`faceAppearances`), none lost.
- **Named sets (2026-10-22, version 27).** The document has one more field, `sets`, written only when it holds something: `[{"name": "boltholes", "query": "{\"kind\":\"face\",\"where\":{\"hole\":true,\"radius\":\"3 mm\"}}"}, …]`, one entry per set in the order they were defined (`set.define`): the name (as a feature's: not empty, no space at either end, no control characters, at most 256 bytes; no two alike) and the query of `select.query` as JSON text (at most 64 KiB), with the features of `in`, `madeBy` and `copyOf` written as ids (`"F3"`). The file keeps the query, not what it found: it is computed again against every regeneration. Loading checks names, sizes and repeats; what the query means is read when it is used, so a set that names a feature since deleted opens and says why it does not read (`set.list`). `v26_to_v27` is the identity: a version-26 file has no `sets`; the version was raised so an older build says a newer 3DCraft is needed instead of refusing an unknown field. The fixture `v27.3dcraft` has a drilled plate and three sets (one using another); `v27.expected.json` pins what each holds now (`set.list`).
- **A colour and a material per body (2026-10-15, version 16).** The document has one more field, `bodies`, written only when it holds something: `[{"feature": 1, "appearance": {"color": [200, 16, 46], "metallic": 0.25, "roughness": 0.4}, "material": {"name": "Cast iron", "density": 7200.0}}, …]`, one entry per feature whose body was given an appearance or a material (`body.appearance`, `body.material`), in ascending order of `feature`; each entry has at least one of the two, and within them `metallic` and `roughness` (0 to 1) are written only when set. `color` is sRGB, three bytes; `density` is in kg/m³ whatever the document's length unit. An entry belongs to the feature, not to a body: it is not part of the history and does not change what regeneration builds; how it reaches the bodies made from that body (a boolean, a fillet, a pattern) is the rule in [`design/document.md`](design/document.md). - **Autosave and crash recovery** (2026-10-13; `crates/engine/src/recovery.rs` holds the design) reuse the writer, from a snapshot on a background thread, into a recovery directory keyed per launch. See *The recovery directory* below.

### The recovery directory

A session that was given a recovery directory (`craft3d --recovery-dir DIR`, by default `3dcraft/recovery` in the per-user state directory: `$XDG_STATE_HOME` or `~/.local/state` on Linux, `~/Library/Application Support` on macOS, `%LOCALAPPDATA%` on Windows; `craft3d-cli serve|mcp --recovery-dir DIR`, off by default) keeps there, per launch, a lock file and, per open document with unsaved changes, a document and its record. A launch is one running process with one session; its key is 16 random hexadecimal digits.

```text
<key>.lock          empty; the launch holds an operating-system lock on it while it lives
<key>-<n>.3dcraft   document n of that launch: exactly the bytes file.save would write
<key>-<n>.json      its record
.<name>.<pid>.tmp   a write in progress (removed by the next scan when its launch is gone)
```

The record is what varies between runs, so the `.3dcraft` bytes stay deterministic:

```json
{
  "recovery_version": 1,
  "launch": "9f0c2a6b41d7e835",
  "pid": 48213,
  "document": 3,
  "name": "bracket.3dcraft",
  "path": "/home/ann/parts/bracket.3dcraft",
  "original": { "size": 18234, "mtime": [1791907200, 512000000] },
  "revision": 41,
  "features": 12,
  "time": 1791907260,
  "recovered": false
}
```

`path` is the path the document was opened from or last saved to, as the request gave it (null for a document that never had one) and `name` its last part or `untitled`; `original` is the size and modification time (seconds and nanoseconds since the Unix epoch) that file had when the document was opened or last saved, null when there was none; `revision` counts the changes made to the document in that launch; `time` is when it was written, in seconds since the Unix epoch; `pid` is for a person to read and is never used to decide anything. A record of a newer `recovery_version`, one that is not JSON, or one larger than 64 KiB is not read: its document is still listed and can be opened, as `untitled`.

- **Written** when a document has unsaved changes and the autosave interval (30 s by default; `--autosave-interval SECONDS`) has passed since that document's last write, or since its first change when it was never written: a burst of commands gives one write, and at most one interval of work is lost. After a caught panic every document with unwritten changes is written at once. The document is written first and its record second, each to a temporary file that is synced and renamed over the target, so a crash in mid-write leaves the previous recovery file. An empty document that was never saved is not worth a file.
- **Removed** when the document is saved or closed, and when the launch ends on purpose (the window is closed, `ui.quit`, the server's input ends): a launch that ends cleanly leaves nothing, also of documents it did not save. Since 2026-10-21 the window asks before it closes with unsaved changes (Save, Don't Save or Cancel per document; [`control-protocol.md`](control-protocol.md) › *Closing the window*), so what it removes was saved or dropped on purpose; a document still unsaved at the end that nobody was asked about keeps its file.
- **Dead or alive.** A launch is alive exactly while it holds the lock on its `.lock` file; the operating system lets go of it when the process ends, however it ends. A scan (at start, and by `recovery.list`) tries each other launch's lock: got it, or no lock file, means that launch is gone and its documents are offered; "held" means it is running and its files are left alone, by `recovery.open` and `recovery.discard` as well. No process id is looked up, so none can be mistaken for a reused one. Limits: on a file system without working locks (some network mounts) every other launch looks dead, so do not share one recovery directory between machines; a lock file deleted by hand under a running launch makes it look dead.
- **Recovered** by `recovery.open`: the file is loaded, opened as an unsaved document that remembers `path`, and renamed to the recovering launch's key, so it is that launch's autosave from then on and goes away when the document is saved or closed. Nothing is recovered or removed without being asked. A recovered document keeps everything a saved file keeps and regenerates to the same bodies; it loses the undo history, which no file holds.
- **Never over a newer original.** `file.save` of a recovered document to its original path is refused when the file there no longer has the recorded size and modification time (or the record had none and a file is there now), and names `"overwrite": true`. Any other path needs no confirmation.
- **Damaged files** are listed by `recovery.list` with `openable: false` and the reason (a truncated or corrupt document, a record without its document); a document without a readable record is openable and says what is unknown. Nothing in the directory can make a scan panic, and files whose names are not the ones above are never touched.
- **A directory that cannot be written** (missing and not creatable, read-only, a full disk) is a problem that `recovery.list`, `ui.get` (`recovery.problem`) and the status bar show until a write succeeds; it is retried once per interval and never fails or delays a command.

Tests: serde round trip of every feature type; save → load → regenerate gives identical bodies; cache-stripped files; migration fixtures; truncation at every offset and fuzzing never panic.

*(planned)* No B-rep is stored today: the caches (`blobs/<blake3>.zst`) are not built, and every body is regenerated when a file opens. A body encoding, versioned inside its blob header, comes with them.

## STEP

**As built (2026-10-13): export.** `file.export` with a `.step` or `.stp` name (or `"format": "step"`) writes every body of the document as an exact B-rep, with the document's length unit, its angle unit, its linear tolerance as the file's uncertainty, and each body under its feature's name. With `bodies` (2026-10-22; also for STL, 3MF and OBJ) only the bodies named, each by the id or name of the feature that made it, in history order with their names and colours; a name that is not a body now is refused, listing the bodies there are. `craft3d-cli convert part.3dcraft part.step` does the same. **Import (2026-10-21, first slice):** `file.import`, below (*Import*).

**Structure.** `craft3d-step` is the Part 21 layer and nothing else: a file is a header (records) and one data section (entity instances, simple or complex), and a record is a keyword with parameters (integer, real, string, enumeration, binary, reference, list, typed parameter, omitted `$`, derived `*`). It reads and writes that model and knows no entity. Reals are written as the shortest text that reads back as the same `f64` (`1.`, `0.25`, `1.E-7`); strings as printable ASCII with `''`, `\\` and the `\X2\`, `\X4\` directives for everything else (read: also `\X\`, `\S\`, `\P`, raw UTF-8 and raw ISO 8859-1). Unknown entities survive a read → write round trip as the instances they are; comments and white space do not. A reader gets every reference resolved (an unresolved one is an error) and can ask for a reference cycle before it follows references. Not read: a second data section, the `ANCHOR`/`REFERENCE`/`SIGNATURE` sections of edition 3, `&SCOPE` of edition 1; each is an error that says so. Hostile input is an error with byte offset, line and column; nesting (64), instances, parameters and string length are capped (`ReadLimits`). Which entities to write, and what their attributes mean, is `craft3d_io::step`: there is no typed entity layer in `craft3d-step`, the mapping builds records directly.

**Entities written.** The schema declared is `AUTOMOTIVE_DESIGN { 1 0 10303 214 1 1 1 1 }` (AP214; [`decisions.md`](decisions.md) D-open-8).

| 3DCraft | STEP |
|---|---|

| solid body | `ADVANCED_BREP_SHAPE_REPRESENTATION` with one item per lump |
| lump | `MANIFOLD_SOLID_BREP` of a `CLOSED_SHELL`; with cavities `BREP_WITH_VOIDS`, each cavity an `ORIENTED_CLOSED_SHELL` (`.F.`) of a `CLOSED_SHELL` |
| sheet body | `MANIFOLD_SURFACE_SHAPE_REPRESENTATION` of a `SHELL_BASED_SURFACE_MODEL` of `OPEN_SHELL`s |
| face, loop, coedge, edge, vertex | `ADVANCED_FACE` with `FACE_OUTER_BOUND` / `FACE_BOUND`; `EDGE_LOOP`; `ORIENTED_EDGE`; `EDGE_CURVE`; `VERTEX_POINT` on a `CARTESIAN_POINT` |
| a whole sphere or torus; a cone's apex inside a face | a `FACE_BOUND` of a `VERTEX_LOOP` |
| plane, cylinder, cone, sphere, torus | `PLANE`, `CYLINDRICAL_SURFACE`, `CONICAL_SURFACE`, `SPHERICAL_SURFACE`, `TOROIDAL_SURFACE` on an `AXIS2_PLACEMENT_3D` |

| colour of a body, of a face (2026-10-15) | `STYLED_ITEM('color', (style), item)` on each `MANIFOLD_SOLID_BREP` (or `BREP_WITH_VOIDS`, `SHELL_BASED_SURFACE_MODEL`) in the body's colour and on each `ADVANCED_FACE` whose own colour differs from it (the faces a coloured tool brought into it by a union or an intersection, and faces given a colour of their own, `face.appearance`); a style is `PRESENTATION_STYLE_ASSIGNMENT` → `SURFACE_STYLE_USAGE(.BOTH., …)` → `SURFACE_SIDE_STYLE` → `SURFACE_STYLE_FILL_AREA` → `FILL_AREA_STYLE` → `FILL_AREA_STYLE_COLOUR` → `COLOUR_RGB` (0 to 1), one per colour and body; all of a body's styled items in one `MECHANICAL_DESIGN_GEOMETRIC_PRESENTATION_REPRESENTATION` in the body's context (the AP214 presentation resources as other modellers write them, from memory: *fairly sure*). A body without a colour is written as before, byte for byte. Read back through the chain by `crates/io/tests/step.rs`; FreeCAD 1.1 imports such a file with the same solids, but its command-line import keeps no colours (they reach only the GUI's view), so no independent reader has checked them yet |
| units, tolerance | one context: `GEOMETRIC_REPRESENTATION_CONTEXT(3)`, `GLOBAL_UNIT_ASSIGNED_CONTEXT` (length: `SI_UNIT` with a prefix, or `CONVERSION_BASED_UNIT` `'INCH'` 25.4 mm / `'FOOT'` 304.8 mm; plane angle: radian or `'DEGREE'`; steradian), `GLOBAL_UNCERTAINTY_ASSIGNED_CONTEXT` with one `UNCERTAINTY_MEASURE_WITH_UNIT` |

Instances are numbered from 1 in the order they are made and refer only to earlier ones; the same document gives the same bytes (`FILE_NAME.time_stamp` is empty for that reason). Geometry is not shared between entities: every placement has its own point and directions.

**Conventions.** The module documentation of `crates/io/src/step.rs` has each one with how sure it is; in short:

- Surfaces and curves are parametrised by ISO 10303-42 exactly as `craft3d-geom` does (normal `∂u × ∂v`: away from the axis or centre), so frames and radii go over unchanged. A cone gets a positive semi-angle: one that narrows along its frame's z is written on the frame seen from the other side. An ellipse gets its longer semi-axis first, on a placement turned a quarter if need be. Angles are in the document's angle unit.
- An edge runs from its start to its end vertex with the curve's parameter (`same_sense` `.T.`, always) and is the part of its curve between its vertices; an `ORIENTED_EDGE` is `.T.` unless the coedge is reversed; `ADVANCED_FACE.same_sense` is `.T.` unless the face is reversed; bounds are `.T.` (our loops already run with the face on their left seen from outside).
- `FACE_OUTER_BOUND` is written first, and only where a loop is the outer one: the one loop of a face; on a plane the loop that runs counter-clockwise; elsewhere the one loop that does not go around the surface, when none does. A cylinder wall between two circles has two plain `FACE_BOUND`s.
- The knots of a B-spline curve are written on the interval from 0 to 1, whatever they are in the body (2026-10-14). The kernel's intersection curves have knots that are lengths, so they grow with the model; a B-spline is the same point set after an affine change of its knots, and no parameter of a curve is written anywhere else (an edge is the part of its curve between its vertices), so nothing else in the file changes, and a curve whose knots run from 0 to 1 already is written bit for bit. The reason is a reader, found by experiment (below, *What a reader made of the knots*). The knots of a B-spline surface are written as they are.
- Faces on closed surfaces are written without seam edges, as they are in the kernel. Part 42 does not ask for seams; readers add their own. FreeCAD's own seam fails on a cylinder with a radius above some 4.5e8 mm (a cylinder of radius 4e8 mm is read right at any height, one of 5.4e8 mm comes back as two invalid faces), and the same file with a seam edge written by hand is read as a valid solid of our volume: a limit far outside the sizes 3DCraft is tested at, and the one thing a seam of ours would buy.
- A cavity's `CLOSED_SHELL` has its faces turned over (so its normals point away from the volume it encloses, as a closed shell's must), and `ORIENTED_CLOSED_SHELL` `.F.` turns them back. A shell is a cavity when it encloses a negative volume, and belongs to the innermost lump that contains a point of it.

**Refused**, with an error that names the body: a body that fails `topo::check` at the document's tolerance; a face without loops on anything but a sphere or a torus; a planar face without exactly one counter-clockwise loop; a solid of several shells where one cannot be measured, encloses nothing, or is a cavity outside every lump; geometry that is not finite. Not written at all: assemblies (every body is a part of its own in the world frame), PMI, face and edge names, parameter-space curves.

**What a reader made of the knots (2026-10-14).** FreeCAD 1.1 (OpenCascade 7.8) called the showcase's bearing housing invalid at the scale 1e6 ("self-intersecting wire" on the sleeve's wall) and valid at 1e-3 and 1, with the same measures at every scale. What changes its answer was found by changing one thing at a time in the text of the file:

| changed in the file at 1e6 | FreeCAD's verdict |
|---|---|
| the uncertainty, from 1e-7 to 1000 | invalid, the same faces |
| the length unit declared (the same numbers as nanometres: a body of the size of the scale 1) | valid |
| the file of the scale 1 with the kilometre as its unit (a body of the same size in FreeCAD as the one at 1e6) | valid |
| the file of the scale 1 with every point and radius multiplied by 1e6 in the text | valid |
| the same, and the knots of the spline curves multiplied by 1e6 (what the kernel writes at 1e6) | invalid |
| the file at 1e6 with only the knots of its spline curves divided by 1e6 | **valid** |
| the same for the four rims of the oil holes alone | valid; for the pedestal's two edges alone, invalid |
| the knots of the spline surfaces, times or divided by 1e6 | no change |
| on the smallest body that shows it (below): parameter-space curves written by hand for the rim of the hole (`SURFACE_CURVE` with a `PCURVE` on either cylinder or on both) | invalid; FreeCAD's parameter-space curves are its own either way (311 poles where ours had 154) |

So the size of the body in the reader is not it, nor the uncertainty, nor the missing parameter-space curves: it is the knots of a closed spline edge (the rim of a hole drilled into a cylinder: one edge, one vertex), which the kernel parametrises by length. FreeCAD makes such an edge periodic, cuts it in two where it crosses the seam it adds to the hole's wall, and its check of the two halves on the *other* cylinder then depends on the knots: with knots up to some 5e3 every file tried is valid; from 1.5e4 up the verdict goes either way once the model is 1e3 or larger (the same body with its knots up to 1.6e4, 1.6e5, 1.6e6 and 1.6e7: valid, invalid, valid, invalid), which is why it looked like a matter of size; and at the scale 1 every file is valid whatever its knots (up to 1e13). The smallest body that shows it is a cylinder with one blind hole along a radius (5 faces). On 312 sleeves with an oil hole (eight directions, three radii, three depths, two places; the scales 1, 1e3 and 1e6) FreeCAD called 58 invalid as written (57 of the 144 at 1e6, 1 of the 144 at 1e3) and none with the knots from 0 to 1, with its volume and area unchanged to 2e-14; of the 144 at 1e6 enlarged to 1e7, 70 and none. What the reader does inside was not looked up (clean-room); the mechanism is inferred, the verdicts are measured.

**Specs.** The exchange structure is ISO 10303-21; the entity definitions are in ISO 10303-42 (geometry and topology) and the application protocols (ISO 10303-214, -242). ISO documents are paid publications: work from copies the project legitimately has, from the freely published EXPRESS schemas and recommended practices of the CAx Implementor Forum, and from the files other systems write. Never paste text from a standard into the repository; cite the clause. The mapping as built was written from memory of the standard and checked against what FreeCAD makes of the files; the clause numbers in the code are marked as approximate.

**Tests.** In `craft3d-step`: every real reads back bit for bit (20 000 random bit patterns); a file with one of everything reads back as the same model and its text is pinned; every string directive; hostile input (some 40 malformed files, each with the expected error and line), truncation at every offset, 20 000 random damages, limits, cycles. In `craft3d-io` (`tests/step.rs`): 26 bodies at three scales read back as the same instance graph and pass a small independent interpreter (closed loops, each edge of a closed shell used once each way, every vertex on its edge's curve and on its face's surface); each convention above (a spline edge with knots that are lengths is written from 0 to 1 at every scale, with its control points untouched); golden text for a block and a cylinder (`tests/golden/`, `CRAFT3D_UPDATE_GOLDEN=1` rewrites them); what is refused. In `craft3d-engine`: `tests/step.rs` and the acceptance scenario. And the external check ([`testing.md`](testing.md) oracle 8): FreeCAD imports the files and reports the same solids. That last test is the one that matters: a STEP file is only correct if other CAD systems accept it.

**Tolerance on export.** The document's modelling tolerance is written as the file's uncertainty measure; the body is checked with that tolerance before it is written, so no vertex is further from its edge, and no edge from its face, than the file declares. (There are no per-entity tolerances in `topo` yet to tighten or report.)

### Import

**As built (2026-10-21): the first slice; the second the same day** (assemblies, colours, swept and offset surfaces, implied-knot B-splines, every edge proven). `file.import {path, names?}` reads a STEP file into the active document as one `import` feature per placed solid or sheet (each solid once where no assembly places it, else once per placement; the solids in the order of their instances in the file at their first placement, then the further placements), as one undo step; the document keeps the file's bytes (above, format 25) and regenerates the bodies from them (`craft3d-stepread`). The design, the oracles and what is open: [`design/step-import.md`](design/step-import.md).

**Read.** `MANIFOLD_SOLID_BREP`, `BREP_WITH_VOIDS` (its `ORIENTED_CLOSED_SHELL`s as cavities), `SHELL_BASED_SURFACE_MODEL` (as a sheet); `CLOSED_SHELL`, `OPEN_SHELL`, `ORIENTED_CLOSED_SHELL`, `ORIENTED_OPEN_SHELL`, `ADVANCED_FACE` (`FACE_SURFACE`), `ORIENTED_FACE`, `FACE_OUTER_BOUND`, `FACE_BOUND`, `EDGE_LOOP`, `VERTEX_LOOP` (dropped: a whole sphere or torus, a cone's apex), `ORIENTED_EDGE`, `EDGE_CURVE` (also with `same_sense` `.F.`), `VERTEX_POINT`; `PLANE`, `CYLINDRICAL_SURFACE`, `CONICAL_SURFACE`, `SPHERICAL_SURFACE`, `TOROIDAL_SURFACE`, `B_SPLINE_SURFACE_WITH_KNOTS` (polynomial or rational), `UNIFORM_SURFACE`, `QUASI_UNIFORM_SURFACE`, `BEZIER_SURFACE` (the knots their type implies), `SURFACE_OF_LINEAR_EXTRUSION` of a line or of a circle along its axis, `SURFACE_OF_REVOLUTION` of a line (cylinder, cone, plane) or of a circle in a plane through the axis (sphere, ring torus), and both of any other curve read, as the exact B-spline surface (an extrusion over as much of its direction as the face's edges reach); `OFFSET_SURFACE` of a plane, cylinder, cone, sphere or torus (that kind again); `LINE`, `CIRCLE`, `ELLIPSE`, `POLYLINE`, `B_SPLINE_CURVE_WITH_KNOTS` (polynomial or rational), `UNIFORM_CURVE`, `QUASI_UNIFORM_CURVE`, `BEZIER_CURVE`, `SURFACE_CURVE`, `SEAM_CURVE`, `INTERSECTION_CURVE` (their 3D curve); `AXIS2_PLACEMENT_3D`, `AXIS1_PLACEMENT`, `VECTOR`, `DIRECTION`, `CARTESIAN_POINT`; the length and plane-angle units of the item's representation context (an `SI_UNIT` with any prefix, a `CONVERSION_BASED_UNIT` of one), converted to the document's; the context's `UNCERTAINTY_MEASURE_WITH_UNIT`, reported. **Assemblies:** `NEXT_ASSEMBLY_USAGE_OCCURRENCE` with `CONTEXT_DEPENDENT_SHAPE_REPRESENTATION` and a `REPRESENTATION_RELATIONSHIP_WITH_TRANSFORMATION` (`ITEM_DEFINED_TRANSFORMATION` of two `AXIS2_PLACEMENT_3D`s, either order, or a `CARTESIAN_TRANSFORMATION_OPERATOR_3D` without scale or mirror), `MAPPED_ITEM` with `REPRESENTATION_MAP`, nested to 32 levels, each frame in its own representation's units: each placement a feature, its body moved exactly, named after its place (`Peg#2`, the whole path `Rack/Peg#2` in the result's `instance`; the same leaf in two places numbered on, `Peg#2-2`); the part's bytes are one blob. **Colours:** `STYLED_ITEM` → … → `COLOUR_RGB` (as the export writes them, also through `SURFACE_STYLE_RENDERING` and `DRAUGHTING_PRE_DEFINED_COLOUR`) on the solid or its shell, else the most common of its faces': the body's appearance (`body.appearance`), set in the same undo step; an unreadable colour is left out. Faces with their seams as edges (a cylinder, a cone to its apex, a torus with two seams), as other modellers write them, are read as they are. Faces, edges and vertices are named by their position in the file (`F5:face(0)`): the same bytes always give the same names, but a file written again by another program after an edit may number them differently, and a reference to an imported face may then land on another face.

**Sewing, topology only.** Shared `EDGE_CURVE`s and `VERTEX_POINT`s are shared edges and vertices. A `VERTEX_POINT` within the file's uncertainty (at least the document's tolerance, at most the document's cap on an entity's tolerance, `loosest`) of an earlier one is that vertex, and two edges used once each between the same vertices whose midpoints lie that close are one edge; the largest distance joined is reported (`vertexGap`, `edgeGap`), and nothing is moved (docs/decisions.md, 2026-10-18). Then every edge is *proven* on the surfaces of its faces over its whole length (`topo::record_edge`, not samples) and every vertex measured against the ends of its edges: an entity off by more than the document's tolerance gets what was proven (an eighth more) as its own tolerance, up to `loosest` (`looseEdges`, `looseVertices`, `largestTolerance`). Edges from a sphere's pole or a cone's apex and on a closed spline's seam are proven (slice 2); should no bound be found within the cap, 1025 places along the edge on every face within the document's tolerance accept it, counted (`unprovenEdges`, 0 on the tests and the showcase). A cavity listed as a plain `CLOSED_SHELL`, or any shell whose enclosed volume has the wrong sign, is turned over and counted (`turnedShells`).

**Refused**, with an error that names the entity (`#30 HYPERBOLA: this kind of curve is not read`) and changes nothing: every other entity kind where a solid needs it (`HYPERBOLA`, `PARABOLA`, `DEGENERATE_TOROIDAL_SURFACE`, `TRIMMED_CURVE`, an offset of a spline or swept surface, a line turned about an axis it does not meet, `POLY_LOOP`, …); placements that scale or mirror; an assembly that contains itself (naming the relationship), is nested deeper than 32 or places more than 10 000 solids in all, or puts a part so far away that rounding there exceeds the tolerance; a context without a length unit; references to the wrong kind of entity; chains deeper than four (curves of curves, units of units, an oriented face of an oriented face); degrees above 32 and multiplicities that do not add up (checked before anything is allocated); numbers that are not finite once converted; files over 128 MiB; a file whose faces and edges would hold more than four million control points together (a surface shared by many faces is a copy in each), counted from the references before anything is read; a face without an edge loop on a surface that does not close all around; a document that would no longer save; and **a body with an edge or vertex off its faces or edges by more than `loosest`**, or an edge that can neither be proven within it nor sampled within the tolerance, naming it by its instance id (`edge #17 could not be proven within the cap …; the body is refused rather than moved`). A body that passes the check but passes through itself fails its feature, as a loft does.

## Meshes: STL, 3MF, OBJ

All three are written from the tessellator's output at a caller-chosen chordal tolerance and, since 2026-10-23, an angle criterion: `file.export {quality}` is `coarse`, `normal` (the default) or `fine`, a chordal tolerance of 2, 1 or ¼ times the automatic one (a thousandth of the side of a square with the bodies' area) and 20°, 10° or 5° per chord and per triangle side (`tess::TessParams::max_angle`, the viewport's rule); `tolerance` and `angle` set either one, and a `tolerance` alone keeps the mesh it made before (no angle). Normal against the old default, release, triangles: turbofan 32 002 → 144 934, hose 34 430 → 59 890, spring 38 564 → 168 736 (8.4 MB STL), pipe 1 650 → 3 196, housing 1 836 → 2 696. `io` merges vertices at bit-identical positions (the tessellator makes shared edges exact, so no distance tolerance is involved) and marks the object a solid only when the merged mesh is closed and consistently oriented; an open mesh is exported as a surface (3MF `type="surface"`). *Planned with bodies:* a body that claims to be a solid but tessellates open is an error rather than a leaky file.

Today: one object per body; in 3MF and OBJ the colours of the bodies and faces (below); OBJ without normals (`vn`) or groups per face.

**Colours in 3MF (2026-10-15).** One `<basematerials>` group (its id follows the objects') with a `<base name="#c8102e" displaycolor="#C8102E"/>` per colour used, named after it; an object with a colour gets `pid` and `pindex` (its body's colour, or, for a body without one whose faces have colours, since 2026-10-21 the grey the pictures draw it in, so its other triangles are not shown in a face's colour), and a triangle whose face has a colour other than the object's gets `pid` and `p1` (the core specification's base materials, chapter *Material resources*; colour groups are the materials extension and not used). Triangles of a face follow the face's colour by the rule of [`design/document.md`](design/document.md), so the faces a coloured tool brought in by a union keep the tool's colour and the wall a tool cut has the target's. Read back by the tests (`crates/meshio`: the groups, the objects' and the triangles' properties; `crates/engine/tests/appearance.rs`: an export of a document). FreeCAD 1.1 reads such a file as the same closed solid (`cargo xtask freecad`); its command-line reader hands no colour on (a mesh object has none without the GUI), so it does not check the colours themselves. STL has no colours.

**Colours in OBJ (2026-10-22).** An OBJ file of a document with colours gets a material library beside it: `file.export` of `parts.obj` writes `parts.mtl` in the same directory (under the write root) and says it in its result (`mtl`, null when there are no colours: then the OBJ is as before, without `mtllib`, no library is written and any name will do). *The library's name is the OBJ's file name with `.mtl` for its extension, never altered*, so two OBJ files with different names never share a library and no file the person did not name is replaced: a name the `mtllib` statement cannot hold (white space or `#`: it takes a list of names, and `#` starts a comment) is refused with one that can (`a b.obj` → "e.g. `a_b.obj`") rather than renamed, and so is an OBJ that would be its own library (`x.mtl` written as OBJ, compared without case for file systems that ignore it). *Writing:* both places are checked first (neither a directory, the directory there), the library is written before the OBJ, so an OBJ never names one that is not there, and when the OBJ still cannot be written the library is put back as it was (removed, or its former content restored), so a failed export leaves nothing behind (`crates/engine/tests/colour_export.rs`). The OBJ names it with `mtllib` and switches material with `usemtl` before the first triangle and wherever the material changes (a `usemtl` holds until the next, across objects). The library has one `newmtl` per appearance (colour, metallic, roughness) with `Kd` the colour as sRGB over 255 with six decimals (a byte reads back exactly) and, where the appearance has them, `Pr` its roughness and `Pm` its metallic factor (the PBR extension of MTL that Blender and others read). Material names are the 3MF ones made OBJ names (`#c8102e` → `c8102e`; a name met twice gets `_2`). Since a material holds until the next `usemtl`, every body without a colour gets the pictures' grey when any body has one; a triangle without any material (which the exporter does not make) would get `default`, a light grey (`craft3d_meshio::write_obj_with_mtl`). 3MF base materials carry the colour only. *The 3MF output changes only for documents with finishes:* since 2026-10-22 two appearances of one colour with another metallic or roughness are two base materials (named `#c8102e metallic 1 roughness 0.3`), where they were one before; a document whose appearances have no metallic or roughness writes the same 3MF as before.

**An independent reader checks the colours (2026-10-22).** `craft3d_testkit::readers` reads exported files from the specifications alone, sharing no code with the writers or with the STEP reader: a stored ZIP by its central directory (PKWARE APPNOTE), the 3MF model part found through the root relationship with base materials and colour groups by object `pid`/`pindex` and triangle `pid`/`p1` (3MF core and materials extension), a STEP file's instances (ISO 10303-21, complex instances included) with each `STYLED_ITEM`'s colour followed through its style assignment to the `COLOUR_RGB`, solids with their faces (a plane's point and outward normal), and OBJ with its MTL (`usemtl` per triangle, `Kd`, `Pr`, `Pm`). `crates/engine/tests/colour_export.rs` exports one document (a red plate with a blue top drilled by a yellow drill, a green metal pin with a roughness, a block without a colour) to 3MF, STEP and OBJ and checks every triangle and every face against the colour the document gives it, told from where it lies (the top blue, the hole's wall the plate's red); the OBJ's triangles are the 3MF's, colour for colour.

| Format | Notes | Spec |
|---|---|---|
| STL | Binary by default (80-byte header, triangle count, 50 bytes per triangle, little-endian f32), ASCII on request. No units in the format: export in the document's length unit and say so in the result. Vertices are not shared in STL; closedness is checked on our indexed mesh before writing | The original 3D Systems StereoLithography interface description (1989); de facto conventions |
| 3MF | An OPC (ZIP) package with `[Content_Types].xml`, `_rels/.rels` and `3D/3dmodel.model` (XML): indexed vertices and triangles per object, explicit unit, object names, base-material colours (above). Preferred mesh format: it has units and guarantees an indexed mesh | 3MF Core Specification (3MF Consortium, freely available) |
| OBJ | `v` and `f` with one `o` per body; colours in a material library beside it (above). No units | Wavefront OBJ and MTL format descriptions (public); the PBR extension of MTL (`Pr`, `Pm`) |

The ZIP and XML writing needed for 3MF is part of `meshio`: a stored (uncompressed) ZIP writer with CRC-32, no dependency. Files are larger than they need to be (the teapot at tolerance 0.002 is 11.9 MB as 3MF, 6.5 MB as STL); DEFLATE is a later improvement.

Tests (in place): exact expected text for ASCII STL, OBJ and the 3MF model part; binary STL and the ZIP container parsed back by minimal readers in the tests; FreeCAD reading a box (solid, volume 24) and the teapot in all four encodings. The FreeCAD test also covers 32-bit rounding in STL: FreeCAD merges the rounded points to exactly the vertex count of our welded mesh.

## Flat outlines: DXF and SVG

**As built (2026-10-13): export.** `file.export` with a `.dxf` or `.svg` name (or `"format": "dxf"` / `"svg"`) writes the outline of a **sketch** (`"sketch"`: id or name) or of a **flat face** of a body (`"face"`: a persistent name from `document.inspect`, `faceNames`, or an earlier name of it, followed; `"feature"` says which body when several have a face of that name) or of a **sheet** of flat faces (`"faces"`, since 2026-10-17), in the document's length unit at true size, for laser cutters and drawings. `craft3d-cli convert IN OUT.dxf --sketch S` (or `--face F [--feature B]`, or `--set S [--width W] [--gap G]`) does the same from a file; without one of them it draws the projected view (below), with `--view V`, `--projection P` and `--hidden H` (since 2026-10-23; before, it refused). Every curve stays what it is: a line a line, a circular arc an arc with its centre and radius, a circle a circle, an ellipse (a cylinder cut on a slant) an ellipse, a NURBS edge a spline. Nothing is turned into a polyline.

Written from the DXF Reference (Autodesk publishes one per release: the group codes of `HEADER`, `TABLES`, `BLOCKS`, `OBJECTS` and of `LINE`, `ARC`, `CIRCLE`, `ELLIPSE`, `SPLINE`), the W3C recommendation *Scalable Vector Graphics (SVG) 1.1 (Second Edition)* (chapters 7 and 8, and the arc implementation notes of appendix F.6), and *The NURBS Book* (Piegl and Tiller, §5.2–5.3) for knot insertion. No DXF or SVG library is used, in the code or in the tests.

**Where the code lives.** `crates/drawing` (`craft3d-drawing`, L0, standalone like `step` and `meshio`: no workspace dependency) has its own 2D model and the two writers: a `Drawing` is a unit and layers (a name and one of seven colours) of paths; a path is a `Chain` (a start point and `Line`, `Arc`, `EllipticArc` and `Spline` segments head to tail, open or closed), a whole `Circle` or a whole `Ellipse`; a `Spline` is a B-spline of degree 1 to 15 on up to 4096 control points, polynomial or rational, which can be cut between two parameters, reversed and split into Bézier pieces. `crates/io/src/drawing.rs` maps a sketch (`sketch_outline`) and the faces of a body (`face_outline`) onto it; `crates/engine/src/drawing_cmds.rs` is the command side.

**What is drawn.**

- *A sketch:* its lines, arcs, circles and splines at their solved positions, in the sketch's own axes. Curves that meet in a point (the same point entity, or two points within the document's tolerance) are joined into one chain as long as exactly two meet there; a chain that comes back to its start is closed. Chains come in the order their first entity was drawn. Construction geometry is left out, or (`"construction": true`) drawn on a layer of its own (`construction`, blue; the rest is on `outline`, black). Lines and arcs without length are left out and listed (`skipped`). A sketch that failed to solve is refused.
- *A face:* every loop a closed chain, in the face's *drawing frame*, **seen from outside the body** (z is the outward normal and the axes are right-handed), so the outline is what one sees looking at the face and never its mirror image: outer boundaries run counter-clockwise, holes clockwise (a hole that is one whole circle is a `CIRCLE`, which has no direction). The bottom of a plate comes out turned over. The result gives the origin and axes in the model (`origin`, `xAxis`, `yAxis`). A name that several faces answer to (the pieces a boolean cut one face into) draws them all, provided they lie in one plane. A face that is not flat is refused (`the face is a cylinder face: only a flat face has an outline to export`).
- *The drawing frame (2026-10-17).* By default **y runs up the face**: the model's +z projected onto the face's plane, so a wall stands upright as seen from outside it (the front, back, left and right of a box all come out with their top edge up). On a face that **lies flat** (its normal along z within the document's angular tolerance), where up has no direction, **x is the model's +x**, and y follows from x and the outward normal: the top of a plate has x along +x and y along +y, its bottom x along +x and y along −y. The **origin** is the model's origin dropped onto the face's plane, so faces in one plane share their coordinates; `"origin": "corner"` puts the lower left corner of the outline's box at (0, 0) instead (the result's `origin` is then that corner in the model). `"xAxis": [x, y, z]` gives x another direction, projected onto the face's plane (refused when it lies along the normal or is zero); it never mirrors. The frame does not depend on how the operation that made the face oriented its plane. A face only nearly flat (its tilt just past the angular tolerance) is drawn by the slope rule, so its drawing may be turned against that of a flat face by up to 180°; `xAxis` pins it.
- *Names are followed* (2026-10-17): `face` may be the name the face had in an earlier body, such as a blank's `F1:face(1)` after cuts and booleans; it is followed through the features that took that body to its newest form, by exactly the rule a fillet or an offset face follows its references by ([`design/names.md`](design/names.md) › *References that follow a body*). `followed` in the result is the full name it led to. A face the way took away fails with the feature that did; a face that became several (the instances of a pattern) fails and lists their full names; a copy that keeps its source (a mirror or a transform with `keep`) is a body of its own and is named by its own names. `feature` is followed the same way: the blank's feature means the body its body went into.
- *A sheet* (2026-10-17): `"faces": [...]` puts several flat faces, of one body or of several, on one drawing. An entry is a face name or `{"face", "feature", "xAxis", "layer"}`; a top-level `xAxis` applies to every entry without its own. Each face is drawn in its own frame (above) on a layer of its own, named `layer` or after the face name as given (every run of other characters than letters, digits and `-` one `_`, at most 31 characters, `-2`, `-3`, … where a name is taken; in SVG the `<path id="layer-…">`). The faces are placed in the order given, by their outlines' bounding boxes: left to right from (0, 0), `gap` apart (default 5 mm in the document's unit), and, when `width` is given, a new row above the last one when the next face would reach past it (a face wider than the sheet is refused). It is a row (shelf) layout, deterministic and never overlapping, not nesting. The result lists per face its `layer`, `followed` name, frame (`origin`, `xAxis`, `yAxis` in the model), `at` (where the frame's origin lies on the sheet) and `bbox` on the sheet, and `rows`. A face that is not flat, a name that does not resolve, an empty list, a negative gap or one that would put coordinates beyond what a double holds to the document's tolerance are refused, naming the entry (`faces.3`).

- *A sheet by named set* (2026-10-23, Jigs gap G7): `"set": "<name>"` instead of `faces` draws every flat face the named set (`set.define`, [`design/selection.md`](design/selection.md)) holds **now**, in document order (bodies in history order, faces in body order), the faces of one body on one layer named after it (both lumps of a pattern on `Sides`; faces given the same `layer` in `faces` share it too), laid out as a sheet with `gap`, `width` and `xAxis`. Since a set is a query computed again at every regeneration, one set (`{"kind": "face", "where": {"any": [{"in": "Front", "normal": {"along": "-y"}}, …]}}`) is the cut sheet of a laser-cut design at every value of its parameters: the laser box gives 6 panels (7 closed paths with the lid's hole), the welding table at 48 × 36 in 15 parts (570 closed paths: plate, 391 holes, 164 slots, 14 ribs). Faces of the set that are not flat are listed under `notFlat` and items of other kinds counted kind by kind under `others` (`{"body": 1, "edge": 3}`; a kind too large to look through is left out rather than refused); neither is drawn. A body hidden in the view (`feature.visible`) is drawn too: visibility is view data, the set says what is cut; a set with no flat face, an unknown set (the error lists the sets) and `set` with `faces`, `face`, `feature` or a STEP or mesh name are refused. The same faces given one by one as `faces` (with their bodies and layers) write the same bytes.

**DXF: decisions.**

- *AutoCAD 2000 (`AC1015`), ASCII.* The oldest release whose format has every entity needed as itself: `ELLIPSE` and `SPLINE` came with R13, and R13/R14 files are rarer than those of 2000, which every reader of the last twenty years takes. R12, the usual "safe" choice, has neither and would force ellipses and splines into polylines. The file has the sections a 2000 file is expected to have (`HEADER`, `CLASSES`, `TABLES` with all nine tables, `BLOCKS` with model and paper space, `ENTITIES`, `OBJECTS` with the root dictionary), handles on everything, and the subclass markers; it has no layout objects and no plot styles.
- *Units.* `$INSUNITS` from the document's unit (1 in, 2 ft, 4 mm, 5 cm, 6 m, 13 µm) and `$MEASUREMENT` (metric or imperial); `$EXTMIN`/`$EXTMAX` are the outline's exact box.
- *Separate entities, not `LWPOLYLINE`.* A loop is written as its own `LINE`, `ARC`, `ELLIPSE` and `SPLINE` entities head to tail. A polyline holds lines and circular arcs only (as bulges, from which a reader has to work the centre and radius back out), so a loop with an ellipse or a spline in it would fall apart anyway; entities that meet share their end points, and laser and CAM software joins them (FreeCAD does: see the tests).
- *Arcs.* An `ARC` runs counter-clockwise from its start angle to its end angle, in degrees; a clockwise piece of a loop is the same arc from its other end. An `ELLIPSE` has its centre, the end of its longer axis, the ratio and two parameters, counter-clockwise likewise. A `SPLINE` has its degree, knots, control points and, when rational, weights (flags 8, planar, and 4, rational); it is always clamped and never flagged closed or periodic.
- *Layers and colours.* Each layer of the drawing is a DXF layer with one of the colour indices 1 to 7; entities take colour and line type from their layer. Layer `0` always exists. A layer name is 1 to 31 letters, digits, `_` or `-`.

**SVG: decisions.**

- *True size.* `width` and `height` in `mm`, `cm` or `in` (micrometres and metres are written in `mm`, feet in `in`: SVG has no other units) and a `viewBox` of the same extent in document units, so one user unit is one document unit.
- *y points up.* SVG's y axis points down, so every y is written negated: the picture is the drawing, not its mirror image, and there is no `transform` for a reader to forget.
- *One `<path>` per layer*, with a subpath per loop (closed with `Z`), `fill="none"`, `fill-rule="evenodd"` and a hairline stroke (0.01 mm, the width laser software takes for a cut rather than an engraving). A browser draws the hairline at its true width, so the outline looks faint or empty there (a known limit); a screen-only CSS rule was dropped because Inkscape applies it and then exports 1 mm lines. In one path with the even-odd rule a loop inside a loop is a hole whichever way the loops run, for a program that fills; software that only follows strokes sees the same loops either way.
- *Arcs are `A` commands* (end points, radii, flags), in parts of a quarter turn at most. An SVG arc is given by its end points and radii, from which a reader works out the centre; for a half turn that is as badly conditioned as it gets (an error of 1e-16 in a radius moves the centre by 1e-8), for a quarter turn not at all. A path has no circle of its own: a whole circle is four quarter arcs. Elliptic arcs are `A` commands with two radii and the axis angle.
- *Splines.* A polynomial B-spline of degree 1, 2 or 3 is written exactly as `L`, `Q` or `C` Bézier pieces (knot insertion). SVG has no curve of a higher degree and no rational one: those are written as cubic Béziers that lie within `tolerance` (default: the document's linear tolerance) of the curve, and the result says how many there were and how far off they are at most (`approximated`, `deviation`). DXF holds them exactly.

**Numbers and determinism.** A number is written as the shortest decimal text that reads back as the same `f64` (`0.30000000000000004`, `1E-7` outside 1e-5 to 1e17; in DXF always with a decimal point or an exponent, since some readers take a bare `10` for an integer; never `-0`), so a reader gets our coordinates bit for bit and nothing is lost to a fixed number of decimals. The same document gives the same bytes: handles count up from a fixed start, entities come in the document's order, and there is no date, time or program version in either file.

**Tests (in place).** In `crates/drawing`: hand-written expected files for a tiny drawing; every other shape (arcs of either direction, across the angle 0 and ±π, over more than a half and more than three quarters of a turn; a whole circle; a slot; a rounded rectangle with a round hole and a D hole at five scales from 1e-3 to 1e6; ellipses; splines) written and read back by two small readers that live in the tests (DXF group codes; the SVG path grammar with the recommendation's end-point-to-centre conversion) and compared to 1e-12 relative: end points bit for bit, centres, radii, angles, directions, lengths. In `crates/io`: faces with a spline edge (a piece of a longer curve, in both directions, on a face seen from either side) and a rational circle, built by hand. In `crates/engine` (`tests/drawing_export.rs`): a plate drawn with `sketch.path` and the top face of its extrusion give the same outline, whose length is the closed form to 1e-12 and whose area is the solid's volume over its height; six units; scales 1e-3 to 1e3; the cut of a cylinder on a slant is an ellipse (whole, and a quarter with two lines) of the closed-form length; the document is unchanged by an export; everything that is refused.

**FreeCAD reads both** (`cargo xtask freecad`: `crates/drawing/tests/freecad.rs` and `crates/engine/tests/drawing_freecad.rs`, one script, `crates/drawing/tests/drawing_report.py`). It imports each file, and reports the edges it got (how many, on which kind of curve, total length, bounding box), the closed wires they sort into and the area inside. Measured (FreeCAD 1.1rc2): for the plates, length, area and box agree with the closed forms to 1e-14 in DXF and SVG in millimetres, at the scales 1e-3, 1 and 1e3, and in DXF in cm and inches (which FreeCAD scales to millimetres: the proof that the unit is in the file); in SVG in cm and inches to 4e-11, because FreeCAD turns what it scales into B-splines; the bound asked is 1e-9; there are as many closed wires as we have loops; DXF lines arrive as lines and arcs as circles, a whole ellipse as an ellipse, a spline as a B-spline curve, SVG cubics as Bézier curves. Three things FreeCAD cannot tell, each pinned in the test with what was measured: (1) its DXF reader draws every `ELLIPSE` whole and ignores the start and end parameters, so an elliptic *arc* in DXF is checked by our own reader only (the same arc in SVG it reads right); (2) its area of a face with a B-spline in its boundary is rough (20.89947 where the exact area is 20.9, 1.4e-4 off for a half disc), while its length of the same edges agrees to 15 digits, so those areas are held to 1e-3; (3) its older Python DXF reader needs the "DXF Library" addon, which it would download, so only its built-in (C++) reader is used. Not checked by anything: AutoCAD itself, and laser software (LightBurn, RDWorks); the file follows the DXF Reference, but no AutoCAD has read it.

**Not built.** Import of either format. Text, dimensions, hatches.

### Projected views

**As built (2026-10-23).** `file.export` to `.dxf` or `.svg` **without** `sketch`, `face` or `faces` is a drawing of the model as seen: `{view: "iso|front|back|left|right|top|bottom|current" = iso, projection: "orthographic|perspective" = orthographic, hidden: "omit|dashed" = omit, tolerance}`. `current` is the camera of the window's viewport: the window tells the session its camera at every sync, for the document it shows (`Session::set_window_camera`); for another document (one made active since, in a batch) or without a window `current` is the iso view, and the result says so in `note`. The window's Export… with nothing selected shows the export dialog with the drawing's options (`crates/ui-egui/src/files.rs` › `DrawingPlan`: view, *As in the viewport* by default, orthographic or perspective, hidden lines left out or dashed; the last choice offered again), on the desktop and in the browser. Code: `crates/engine/src/view_export.rs`; the writers' dashed layers in `crates/drawing` (`Layer::dash`).

- *What is drawn.* Every edge of the B-rep of the bodies that are not hidden (`feature.visible`), except seams (an edge both of whose sides are the same face); smooth (tangent) edges are drawn, as the viewport draws them. And the **silhouettes** of curved faces: the edges of the mesh, inside one face, between a triangle that faces the eye and one that faces away (the contour of the meshed surface, within the tolerance of the surface's own; a triangle seen exactly edge on counts as facing the eye, so a cylinder seen square on still has its contour; a side of the mesh with other than two triangles, or a triangle without area, makes none). Flat faces have none. Layers: `visible` (edges), `silhouette`, and with `hidden: "dashed"` `hidden`, drawn dashed (DXF line type `DASHED` defined in the file's `LTYPE` table, dash a sixtieth of the drawing's diagonal and a gap of half that; SVG `stroke-dasharray`). All black.
- *Visibility* is decided per point, exactly for the mesh at the export's chordal tolerance (`tolerance`, automatic by default as for meshes, never finer than 1e-5 of the bodies' diagonal): a point is hidden when a triangle of any body covers it in the drawing, strictly inside or on a side inside its face that goes on unfolded, and is nearer the eye there by more than 1.25 tolerances (the mesh lies within a tolerance of the surface, so two surfaces that touch, a pin in its hole, do not hide each other). Triangles seen (nearly) edge on hide nothing. The triangles are found through a quadtree over the drawing, each in the cells it overlaps (a separating-axis test, not its box), a cell split above 24 triangles down to 14 levels. Lines are sampled every 2 × tolerance (at least a two-thousandth of the model's diagonal) and split where the verdict changes, the change placed by bisection; a run shorter than 2.25 tolerances between runs of the other kind, or at the end of a line next to one, takes their kind. **Limit:** a line that goes behind something for less than the sample spacing may stay drawn as seen there, and a line seen within the depth margin of a face in front of it counts as seen. A line on the boundary of what would hide it counts as seen (a back edge in line with a front one is drawn once).
- *Curves are polylines* in this first version: the edges' polylines and the mesh's contour, straight pieces within the tolerance. Pieces in line are merged; a piece that lies on one drawn before it (visible, then silhouettes, then hidden) is left out, found through the cells of a grid its line crosses; what follows on joins into one path. The same document gives the same bytes.
- *Scale.* Orthographic is to scale: one drawing unit is one model unit, in the document's unit (`$INSUNITS`, the SVG's `mm`/`cm`/`in`), the model's origin projected to the drawing's (x along the view's right, y up). Perspective (for illustration) is true size only at the depth of the camera's target; the result says so (`scale`). A perspective eye inside the model, or nearer to any point of it than a twentieth of its distance to the target, is refused.
- *Not applied.* The window's section (the bodies are drawn whole; a drawing of a cut is a separate matter), a scrubbed timeline (the document as it is), datums and sketches.
- *Result.* `view`, `note`, `camera` (`towardEye`, `up`, and a perspective's `eye`), `projection`, `scale`, `hidden`, `bodies`, `tolerance`, the pieces per kind (`visible`, `silhouette`, `hiddenLines`), `paths`, `lines`, `length`, `bbox`, `layers`.
- *Limits (bounded work, never an endless or out-of-memory export).* At most 1 million triangles for all bodies (the tessellator stops at the budget), 12 million triangle entries in the quadtree, 4 million samples, and 1e9 triangle and overlap tests, bisection included (in the browser 250,000 triangles, 3 million entries and 2e8 tests): past them the export is refused with "use a coarser `tolerance` or a simpler view". Refused too: an unknown view, projection or `hidden`; the outline-only keys (`feature`, `construction`, `xAxis`, `origin`, `gap`, `width`); `view`, `projection` or `hidden` with `sketch`/`face`/`faces` or with another format.
- *Tests.* `crates/engine/tests/view_export.rs`: a cube from the front is 4 lines of 10 mm, in iso 9 visible and 3 hidden edges, in perspective from the front 4 visible and 8 hidden; a cylinder from the side has 2 silhouette lines 10 mm apart and 20 mm long and its circles as 2 lines, from the top no silhouette; a tube in iso its outer 2 silhouettes (the bore's hidden); a pin through its hole (two bodies whose surfaces touch): its silhouettes seen for 40 mm outside the plate and hidden for 20 inside, no flicker; a tolerance below the floor is the floor; the same bytes twice; a hidden body is left out; the window's camera for `current` only for its document, iso by default, an eye inside the model refused; bad parameters refused. Unit tests of the module: silhouettes of non-manifold sides and degenerate triangles, the triangle–box test, the walk through grid cells, the work budget. FreeCAD reads them (`drawing_freecad.rs`, `freecad_reads_a_projected_view`: a drilled block in iso, front and top, length and box to 1e-9). The guide's examples drawn in `target/work/view-export/` while it was built.
