# Sheet metal

A part bent from one sheet: a flat base with flanges bent along straight edges, each bend with an inner radius `R` and a K-factor `K`, corner reliefs where two bends meet, the flat pattern with its bend allowance, the bend table, and the manufacturability measures as assertion measures. Roadmap: [`../roadmap.md`](../roadmap.md) › *R1* (the sheets block, item 3 of `plan/r1-sheets.md`, built 2026-10-27). Code: `crates/kernel/src/sheetmetal.rs` (the pattern, the fold, the solid, the measures), `crates/kernel/src/origami/solve.rs` (`Joint` with a radius), `crates/regen/src/sheetmetal.rs` (the feature in regeneration), `crates/engine/src/sheetmetal_cmds.rs` (`feature.sheetMetal`, `sheetMetal.edit`, `sheetMetal.bends`, the measures `bendRadius`, `flangeLength`, `holeToBend`, `relief`, `cornerGap`, the flat pattern's export). Guide: [`../guide/09-features.md`](../guide/09-features.md) › *Sheet metal*.

**Laid out in closed form, measured by the unfold.** The flat pattern is laid out by the closed form `BA = θ (R + K T)` (exact) and folded from it; the unfold (`unfold::develop` on each bend's neutral band) only measures it, in the bend table and the tests, and must agree within a few tolerances. It never lays the pattern out (*The flat pattern measured* below).

**One fold, one unfold, one offset.** Nothing here folds, lays flat or offsets by a method of its own: the bends are joints of origami's linkage ([`origami.md`](origami.md) › *One fold mechanism*), the solid is the plate of the folded bottom skin ([`plates.md`](plates.md)), and the bend allowance is measured by laying each bend's neutral band flat with the unfold ([`developable.md`](developable.md)). This follows the design written in [`plates.md`](plates.md) › *Sheet metal* before it was built, with the changes listed under *Findings and history*.

## Method

### The flat pattern first

The part is defined by its flat pattern, in the plane of the base's sketch (its x and y; `z` along its normal, the side a bend `up` turns to; the bottom skin at `z = 0`, the top at `z = T`). The base is the sketch's one closed profile (lines, arcs, circles, splines; holes allowed). A flange sits on a straight edge `s → e` of a flat, with the flat on its left (the outer loop runs counter-clockwise, so this is the loop's own direction), `û` the unit vector to its right (away from the flat): one of the base's outer edges, by its line's entity id, or the *tip* (far edge) of an earlier flange. The pattern grows across the edge by

- the bend's **strip**, `BA = θ (R + K T)` wide: the bend allowance, the length of the neutral fibre over the angle θ (Machinery's Handbook, *Sheet metal work* › *Bend allowance*), the neutral fibre lying `K T` from the inner surface;
- the flange's **flat**, a rectangle as wide as the edge and `L` long when the length is measured from the bend's tangent line, or `L − (R + T) tan(θ/2)` when it is measured from the virtual sharp of the outside surfaces (the outside mould line; the outside setback `(R + T) tan(θ/2)` holds for any θ below 180 degrees: the two outside surfaces' planes meet that far from each tangent line). Its tip, `b0 → b1`, has the flange on its left, so a flange on it grows the same way.

So the flat length of a chain of flats and bends is the sum of the flats and `Σ θᵢ (Rᵢ + Kᵢ T)`, in closed form: the oracle of the L, the U, the Z and the box. The bend deduction (DIN 6935's *Ausgleichswert* with its sign turned) is `2 (R + T) tan(θ/2) − BA`, how much shorter the flat is than the two flanges measured to the virtual sharp; both are reported in the bend table.

### Inset flanges, outlines, sides and holes

A flange sits on a straight edge `s → e` of a flat with the flat on its left: an edge of the base's outer loop or of a **hole** of the base (a hole runs clockwise, so the base is on its left there too and the flange grows into the hole: a tab bent out of a cut-out, a louver), the **tip** of an earlier flange, or a **side** of one (`a0 → b0` at its bend's start, `b1 → a1` at its end: the rectangle's other two edges, each as long as its flat). Every rule below is the same for each, because each is a segment of a flat's loop with the flat on its left.

- **Insets.** The bend spans `s + i₀ d → e − i₁ d` (`d` the edge's unit direction, `i₀`, `i₁` the insets; an inset within the tolerance of 0 is 0, the point the edge's own; one between that and 4 tolerances is refused, a sliver), so several flanges can sit on one edge. When the flats are laid out, every edge flanges sit on is **split** at their bends' ends into the pieces between and the tangent lines (`split_at_seats`), and each bend records its **seat**, the segment of its parent's loop that is its tangent line (`Seat`); an edge whose one flange spans it whole is left as it is, so a part without insets is built bit for bit as before (`sheetmetal_open_pin.rs`). Bends on one edge must stay at least 4 tolerances apart, measured along it: touching, the two strips' ends would lie along each other, and closer the rim between them is a sliver. The pieces of a split edge are named by the bends at their two ends, each by its flange's **id** (`part` = `from · 2³² + to`, `from` 0 at the edge's start or the id of the flange whose bend it follows, `to` 0 at its end or the id of the one it runs to), never by their place along the edge nor in the list: a flange added, deleted or moved, on the edge or anywhere in the list, renames only the piece it splits or joins (review 2026-10-28: numbered along the edge, adding a tab ahead of another gave the old name of the piece past it to the gap between them; numbered by the flanges' places in the list, deleting an earlier flange gave the name of the gap between the first two tabs to the gap between the next two). A flange's id is a document field (`Flange::id`, with the part's `next_flange_id`, format 49): 1, 2, … in order when the part is made, a new flange's the next one never used (a deleted flange's id never comes back), and `sheetMetal.edit` keeps a flange's id when it is given (`id`, as `sheetMetal.bends` lists them) or when the flange sent equals one of the part's in every field (what it sits on read through the ids of the flanges it names); a flange changed without its id is a new flange, so the names of its split edges are lost (an error where used), never given to another piece (*Names*). The fold, the sheet, the cells of holes across a bend and the pattern's outline all read the seat, not a search for the segment.
- **Corners.** A corner of the base (for reliefs and closing) is where two flanges *reach* the point two edges of the outer loop share: the one on the edge arriving there with its end inset 0, the one leaving with its start inset 0; a flange with an outline of its own reaches it when its outline's line on the edge ends there, or the relief's size short of it with a relief (which shortens the edge by that much; 2026-11-02, before then an outlined flange took no corner). Anywhere else two bends may still meet at a point (an inset flange beside a flanged edge, a flange on a side beside one on the tip, two flanges on a hole's edges): no relief or closing is made there, and the pattern check below decides whether the pieces can be cut from one sheet.
- **An outline of its own.** The flange's flat is the closed profile of a sketch (holes allowed), drawn in the base's plane as if the bend had no width (on a tip or a side, where the edge lies with every bend between the base and it of no width, so a change of thickness, radius or K-factor moves the outline with its edge: `own_outline`'s `lift`, the sum of those bends' allowances across each; review 2026-10-28, drawn where the edge lies in the pattern, such a change refused it): exactly one of its outer loop's lines lies on the edge's line (both ends within the tolerance of it) running against the edge (the flat on the far side), inside the edge's ends; every other piece lies on the far side of the line (its least distance across, exact for lines, arcs and circles, by the control points for a spline, not below −tolerance). That line is the bend's span (its insets); the outline is moved the bend allowance across and turned to start at it, the line's ends set exactly on the strip's far ends (the neighbours' ends move at most the tolerance). Its holes are holes of the flat. The flat has no tip and no sides (a flange on one is refused; its corner closes as *Closed corners* says, the outline's segment touching the bend's end standing for the side); a flange sits instead on a **line of the outline** (`line: {flange, edge}`, the outline sketch's line by its entity id, `FlangeOn::Line`, format 51): the straight segment of the flat's outer loop made by that curve, found by its source as a base edge is, and refused when it is the outline's line on the parent's edge (the bend's own tangent line), not straight, or not in the outline. From there it is an edge like any other (insets, bend reliefs, flanges on its tip), its pieces named by the outline's curve and the bends' ids (`F9:edge(4, b, curve, piece)` split as a base edge is). Tested: a rail's trapezoid leg with a lip on its far line, inset and with bend reliefs, by its flat area and volume at three scales, saved and reopened (`crates/engine/tests/sheetmetal_outline_lines.rs`). `flangeLength` is the farthest the outline reaches from the tangent line (now exact on arcs, by `reach_along`).
- **On a side.** The side's flange grows away from the flat (`across` is `−d` of the parent's bend at its start side, `+d` at its end side) and is folded about the side, so a tab wraps round the corner where the parent's bend ends; its strip touches the parent's strip only at that corner's point, which the pattern check accepts as two strips sharing a point. A flange whose tip carries flanges (inset tabs, a return) closes its corners (*Closed corners*); a side that carries a flange, closed at its end, moves to the corner's end with that flange when the end lies along it (*Closed corners*).
- **On a hole's edge.** The hole's loop is split at the bend as an outer edge is; in the pattern's outline the hole is chained together with the strip's ends and the flange's flat (it is still one hole), the strip and flat must lie inside the hole (the pattern check), and `holeToBend` leaves out a hole a flange is bent from (its edge is the bend's tangent line, not a hole beside it). A hole across a bend in a part with such a flange is cut as any other (2026-11-02; refused until then): the bend's tangent line on its parent is a segment of the hole's loop, so the cells take it from the hole (its pieces, where a cut crosses it, are traced in the hole's loop as an outer loop's are, from the hole's vertices), a flat's cell is inside its outline and outside its holes (the flange, its strip and its flat lie in the hole), and the pieces are named as a split tangent line of an outer loop, `F9:edge(9, 7, b, 0, piece)` (`EdgeKey::SplitHole`). Held by `a_slot_across_the_bend_of_a_tab_on_a_holes_edge` (`sheetmetal_cuts.rs`, three scales, K = 0.33 and ½: a slot across both tangent lines of a tab bent down from a window's edge, the volume less the slot on the flats times `T` and on the strip times `T (R + T/2) / (R + K T)`, the flat area, fold then unfold, one sheet, every other name kept).

### Flanges on a cut-out's edge

A flange may sit on a straight edge of a **cut-out** (`cutout: {sketch, edge}`, `On::Cutout(c, curve)`, format 60): a closed profile beside the base, in the part's sketch or one of its `cutouts` sketches, that lies wholly inside the base or an earlier flange's flat. The cut-out is a hole of that flat, so the rule is the one of a flange on a hole of the base: the hole runs clockwise, the flat is on the left of its edge, and the flange grows into the hole. This is a louvre or a tab bent out of a wall (built 2026-10-31 for the chassis showcase; until then only the base's own holes carried flanges, since the cut-outs were placed in their flats after every flat was laid out).

- **Placed as each flat is laid out.** A flat that holds a cut-out a flange sits on takes every cut-out that fits it (`hole_fits`, the test `place_cutouts` makes) as soon as it is laid out, in the cut-outs' order (`place_early`), so a flange later in the list finds its edge; every other flat takes its cut-outs after the corners are closed, as before, so a louvre added in one flat renumbers no hole of another (review 2026-10-31: every flat took them early once any louvre existed; `a_louvre_in_one_wall_renames_no_hole_of_another`). In the louvre's own flat a cut-out that fits only once a closed corner has extended the flat comes after the others. A part without such a flange places them after the corners, bit for bit as before. A flange whose cut-out lies in no flat laid out before it (it lies in a later flange's flat, across a bend, or past the pattern's edge) is refused with that reason.
- **Names.** Placed cut-outs are named by their place among the holes of their flat, so deleting or inserting an earlier cut-out of the same flat renames the holes after it (as for every cut-out since format 42; owed: a hole named by its sketch and curves). The cut-out's hole is named by its place in its flat as every placed cut-out is (`[13, flat, hole, segment]`), so a louvre added renames no edge but the one it is bent from; the pieces that edge is split into keep the hole's place and segment (`[16, flat, hole · 2³² + segment, from · 2³² + to]`, review of the first run: written `[13, flat, hole, part]`, the piece from the edge's start to a bend of id 2 took the name `[13, flat, hole, 2]` of the hole's third segment, a silent retarget; held by `a_louvre_added_renames_only_the_edge_it_is_bent_from`).
- **Folded.** The holes of a flange's flat are made as rings when the first bend sits on one of them (its parent is made by then), the rest after everything else as before; a closed corner's flange may carry a flange on a hole of its flat (refused before: the corner's remapping of seats now moves only the outer loop's).
- `holeToBend` leaves the cut-out out (its edge is the louvre's tangent line), as for a hole of the base.

Oracles (`crates/kernel/tests/sheetmetal_louvres.rs`, `crates/engine/tests/sheetmetal_louvres.rs`, three scales): a wall 30 high with a slot 20 × 8 and a louvre 16 wide at 45 degrees, its flat area `A − slot + w (BA_l + L_l)` and volume `T` times the flats plus `θ w T (R + T/2)` per bend at K = 0.33 and ½, fold then unfold, the louvre's angle measured; with the wall's corner closed by a mitre the louvre adds exactly its area and material; names; refusals by reason (no such cut-out or curve, listed before its wall, a slot across the bend, a round hole, a louvre too long for its slot); by command the bend table's `on`, saved and reopened to the same body and names, bad `cutout` objects as errors.

**The pattern is one sheet** (below) applies unchanged: inset flanges beside a reflex corner whose flats overlap, a tab in a hole that runs into the hole's far side or whose strip's ends lie along the hole's sides (no insets), are refused naming the flanges. A flange beside a reflex corner must be inset at that corner, or its strip's end lies along the other edge (refused as before).

**Names.** Faces are numbered by the flange's id (`F9:face(2n − 1)`, `F9:face(2n)` for the flange of id `n`), and edges are named by what they are (*Names* below), so a flange added later takes only the rim along the edge it is bent from (that edge is a tangent line now, or split into pieces named by the bends at their ends) and changes no other name.

### Jogs across a flat

A **jog** (`jogs`, format 64; `crates/kernel/src/sheetmetal/jog.rs`) is a line drawn across a flat, the base's or a flange's: the flat is cut along it into a **near side**, which keeps the flat's place, its face and its bend, and a **far side**, a flat of its own, joined by two bends of the same angle `α` (in (0, 180) degrees), radius `R` and K-factor `K`, the first turning `up` or `down` and the second back, with a **web** between them. The folded far side lies parallel to the near side, `h` from it (the offset, between the two bottom skins, so between the two top skins too).

- **The line** is a line of the part's sketch (construction geometry, so the profile detection leaves it out), drawn as if every bend had no width, as the base's own curves and a flange's outline are: across the base as drawn, or across a flange's flat where it lies beside its edge with its bend of no width. Its middle names the flat: the flat whose outline, carried back by its lift (how far it lies from where it is drawn), holds the middle (one flat, else refused). The base's near side is on the line's left; a flange's (or an earlier jog's far side's) is the side its own bend's tangent line lies on, wholly (a line across that tangent line is refused).
- **The cut.** Along the line `o + s d` (`û` to its right, the far side, `σ(p) = (p − o)·û`) the outer loop must cross the line exactly twice, at `c₁` (from the near side to the far) and `c₂` (back), by lines (the sign of `σ` at their ends) or arcs (the circle's roots inside the arc); a corner within the tolerance of the line, an arc that touches it, a spline that comes within the tolerance of it (by its control points) or a loop of one circle is refused, and so are pieces shorter than `SLIVER_TOLERANCES` tolerances (a line's by its chord, an arc's by `r · sweep`: review of 4116ed3e, arcs were let through), and two cut edges whose ids hash alike (their pieces would share a name). The near side's loop is its run of the outline from `c₂` to `c₁` closed by the chord `c₁ → c₂`, which runs along `d` with the near side on its left: the first bend's tangent line, so the bend grows across it as a flange does (a flange's near side is turned to start with its own tangent line again). Holes go to the side they lie on (the least and the most of `σ` over each curve, exact for lines, arcs and circles, by the control points for a spline); a hole the line crosses or comes within the tolerance of is refused (owed: a hole across a jog's bends, as *Holes across a bend*).
- **The web and the far side.** The web is the rectangle `w = (h − (2R + T)(1 − cos α)) / sin α` long beyond the first bend's strip (the bottom skin rises `(R + T)(1 − cos α)` round the first bend, whose outside it is, `w sin α` along the web, and `R (1 − cos α)` round the second, whose inside it is); `w` not more than the tolerance is refused, naming the offset the bends alone take. The second bend sits on the web's tip. The far side is carried `(BA₁ + w + BA₂) û` on in the pattern (`BA = α (R + K T)`), its loop starting with the chord `c₂ → c₁` so carried (the second bend's far tangent line, as a flange's flat starts with it). So the flat length across a jog grows by `2 BA + w`, and the volume by the web's slab and the two bends' `α L T (R + T/2)`.
- **In the part.** A jog's two bends are the part's bends after every flange's (`F + 2g`, `F + 2g + 1`), its web and far side the flats after them; they are laid out as soon as the flat the line crosses is (the base before any flange, a flange's flat right after it), in the jogs' order and round again until none is left that can be (a jog across an earlier jog's far side). A flange on an edge of the far side finds it there: every flange's edge is looked for by what it is (`SegId`) among the flats cut from the flat it was drawn on; a flange on an edge the line cuts is refused (*the edge it sits on is cut by a jog's line*). The fold places the far side by the two joints like any flat (a linkage is a tree whatever the list's order); the folded sheet makes the bends parents first (in the list's order, each as soon as its parent flat is made: for a part without jogs exactly the list's order, so it is made as before, bit for bit). A closed corner of a flange a jog crosses is refused (its rectangle is no longer one; owed), and so is an early cut-out (a louvre's slot) on a jog's far side, whose flange says it lies in no flat laid out before it.
- **Names.** A jog takes two ids from the flanges' (`id` for its first bend and web, `id + 1` for its second bend and far side; `next_flange_id` is past both), so its faces are `face(2·id − 1)` .. `face(2·id + 2)` less 2 as a flange's are, and nothing else is renumbered. The near side keeps every name; the far side keeps its edges' names (each segment keeps its `SegId`), and the pieces of the two edges the line cuts are `[15, n, h, side · 4 + j]` (`n` the jog's number, `h` a 48-bit hash of the cut edge's own name, `side` 0 near or 1 far, `j` the piece's place along the edge), so a jog moved to cut another edge renames its pieces instead of handing them on, and adding a jog renames only the edges it cuts (and, being another face, the far side's skins and rims: a reference to them is an error, never retargeted).
- **The press brake.** A jog is made in one hit by an offset die: `sheetMetal.bendSequence` and `bendable` take its two bends as `formed` (made from the start, no step of the order).

Oracles (`crates/kernel/tests/sheetmetal_jogs.rs`, three scales, K = 0.33 and ½, α = 60 and 90 degrees): a plate 100 × 40 jogged across at x = 50 by 10, the web, the flat length `100 + 2 BA + w`, the flat area, the volume `T 40 (100 + w) + 2 α 40 T (R + T/2)` (`A T` at K = ½), the far side's bottom skin at `z = h` and its near edge at `x = 50 + (2R + T) sin α + w cos α`, fold then unfold; a jog down steps down; a jog across a wall equals the wall cut short with two tip flanges (the web and the rest) in area, volume and the far side's place; two jogs with a hole and a wall on the far side; names (only the cut edges renamed, a second jog renames only its pieces); refusals by reason. By command (`crates/engine/tests/sheetmetal_jogs.rs`): the flat area, the bend table's jog bends, the face names kept but the cut edges' rims, saved and opened again, an edit that keeps the jog's id, the press-brake check with the jog formed, bad jogs as errors.

### Holes and cut-outs on any flat

The base's sketch is drawn in the plane of the flat pattern, so a hole of any flat is drawn where it lies in the pattern. The base is the sketch's closed profile that the flanges on base edges sit on (the only profile when no flange is on an edge; flanges on two profiles are refused); its holes are the base's. Every other closed profile of the sketch, and every closed profile of the sketches listed in `cutouts` (in the same plane, any origin, axes and side: each point carried by the rigid motion between the two frames, an arc of a frame facing the other way run from its carried end to its carried start so it stays counter-clockwise), is a **cut-out**: a hole of the one flat it lies wholly inside in the laid-out pattern. Which flat is decided by the sketch's own profile detection, as reliefs are checked: the flat's outline, its holes so far and the cut-out together must be one profile with one hole more and the flat's outer area (`hole_fits`), tried on every flat whose box meets the cut-out's. One that fits no flat but lies inside the pattern, clear of its edges, its holes and the other cuts (the pattern's outline, the holes and it are one profile with one hole more and the area less by its own), is a **hole across a bend** (*Holes across a bend* below); one that fits neither (past or touching the pattern's edge, in a relief's notch, in or touching another hole) is refused naming its curves and sketch, and so is a cut-out with a profile inside it (a loose island). The holes then go through *The pattern is one sheet* with everything else, and are folded with their flat (a flange's face gets its holes' loops; the plate cuts them through the thickness). A sketch in `cutouts` that is switched off (`when`, suppressed) cuts nothing: a vent can be an option.

A hole cut later by another feature (a boolean subtract on the folded part) is not in the part's own pattern (the pattern is what the part is folded from), but the **export carries it back** when it goes through the thickness square to the sheet (*A later cut carried back* below); the bend table and the export say which feature took the part's body (`changedBy`).

### Formed louvres

A **formed louvre** (`formedLouvres`, format 65; `crates/kernel/src/sheetmetal/formed.rs`, `regen::sheetmetal::form_louvres`; the developer's decisions of 2026-11-02) is lanced along a line of a flat and stretched by a tool into a hood behind the line (on its left), whose opening runs along the lance. A hood is not developable, so it is no flange: the flat pattern carries only the **lance** (a cut, on the layer `outline` as open lines) and the tool's **footprint** (where it lands, a mark on the layer `form`), and the part is the folded part with the footprint taken out through the thickness and the hood joined on, by booleans after the fold. The hood's shape is **parametric**, as louvre tools are sold: its lance (a construction line of the part's sketch, drawn where it lies in the flat pattern, so its length is the lance's), its width `w` (how far back it reaches), its height `h` (the opening's) and its ends, `closed` (rounded) or `open`; a sketched section can come later.

- **The section** (square to the lance, `x` back, `y` along the flat's normal on the side the hood rises to: `up`, from the bottom skin, or `down`, from the top skin) is an S of two circular arcs of one radius `ρ = (w² + h²)/(4h)`, each turning by `α = 2 atan(h/w)`: from the lip `(0, h)`, level, about `(0, h − ρ)` down to the middle `(w/2, h/2)`, then about `(w, ρ)` to `(w, 0)`, level with the flat, so the hood leaves the flat tangent to it and its lip is level. The other skin is the S offset by `T` along its normal (`ρ + T` about the first centre, `ρ − T` about the second), the rims at the lip and the back square to it. Its area is `2αρ T` exactly.
- **Ends.** Open: the section swept along the whole lance `ℓ` (`L = ℓ`); the footprint is the rectangle `ℓ × w`, and the lance runs along the front and both sides (a U). Closed: the section swept along the middle `L = ℓ − 2w` and turned a quarter about the vertical through each end of the middle (a piece of a sphere about the lip's end, smooth there as the lip is level, and of a torus beyond the inflection); the footprint is the rectangle `L × w` and a quarter disc of radius `w` at each end, its front the lance `ℓ`.
- **The volume** in closed form: the part's less `F T` (the footprint, `L w`, plus `π w²/2` closed) plus the hood's, `2αρ T L` and closed the two quarter turns by Pappus, `π ∫ x dA` over the section, `((ρ + T)³ − ρ³)(1 − cos α)/3` for the first band and `w α (ρ² − (ρ − T)²)/2 + (ρ³ − (ρ − T)³)(cos α − 1)/3` for the second.
- **The stretch.** The section's fibre from the back to the lip was `w` long in the flat and is the S, `2αρ`, after forming: `louvreStretch` is the largest `2αρ/w − 1` of the parts' louvres, a plain number for the caller's limit (the material's elongation, the tool's own rules: no catalogue). Over no louvre it has no value (an error, as *Measures over nothing*).
- **Placed** in the one flat whose outline, holes, the earlier louvres' footprints and its footprint are one profile with one hole more (`hole_fits`, the test a cut-out takes); a footprint whose box meets a hole across a bend is refused (not supported), so are a height not above the thickness by four tolerances (the lip would lie in the sheet in front), not below the width (the S would lean over its back), a radius that leaves the inner arc no `ρ − T`, a closed lance not longer than twice the width, and a boolean that fails, each naming `formedLouvres.g`.
- **The booleans.** The footprint's prism through the thickness is subtracted and the hood (the swept section, closed with its two turns joined to it) united, in the world, after the part is plated: the prism's walls and the hood's back rim lie on one another and go, the hood's top skin meets the flat's tangentially along its back. A face keeps the name of the face it is (a piece of) and answers to the others it was merged from; a face left in two pieces fails the part (owed: pieces named apart); the self-intersection check runs on the result.
- **Names, by role.** The footprint's walls `F9:face(15, id, 0, r)` and the hood's faces `F9:face(15, id, 1, 16 p + r)`, `id` the louvre's own (one id from the flanges' counter, kept by `sheetMetal.edit` as a jog's is), `p` the piece (0 the swept middle, 1 the end turned at the lance's start, 2 at its end) and `r` the face's role in it: the curve that swept it (the section's 1 and 3 the first arc's bottom and top skins, 4 and 6 the second arc's top and bottom skins, 2 the lip's rim, 5 the back rim; the footprint's 1 the lance, 2 to 4 the rest of its outline), 7 the cap on the lance's start side, 8 on its end side; carried through the union of the middle and the ends by the boolean's face sources. ### Holes across a bend

A cut-out that crosses a bend's tangent lines is cut through the bend where the bend allowance puts it (`crates/kernel/src/sheetmetal/cuts.rs`). The tangent lines (each a segment of a flat's outline that a strip continues: the parent's edge, the flange's segment 0) divide the pattern into **cells**, the flats and the strips. Each cut is split where it crosses a tangent line inside it (lines and arcs in closed form; a vertex of the cut on the line is a crossing when the pieces before and after it lie on opposite sides), each crossed tangent line where the cut crosses it, and the line's pieces inside a cut (their middle inside it, by the winding number) are dropped. Every piece of a cut lies in one cell, found by its middle (inside one strip's rectangle, else inside one flat's outline; none or two fail closed). Every piece runs with the material on its left (outlines counter-clockwise, holes and cuts clockwise, a strip using its tangent lines against their flats), so what is left of each cell is traced piece to piece by the vertices they share, by what each vertex is (a flat's corner, a crossing, a cut's corner), exactly and without a tolerance; a loop of positive area is the outside of a face, one of negative area a hole of the face whose outside goes round it. A cell may be left in several faces: a slot across a whole strip leaves the bend in two.

A piece in a flat is carried by the flat's motion. A piece in a strip is **wrapped** onto the bend's bottom skin: a point `u` along the tangent line and `s` across the strip goes to the angle `φ = s / (R + K T)` about the bend's axis, `u` along it (the neutral fibre keeps its length, so `s = BA` is `φ = θ`, where the strip's far tangent line lands). A line along the bend (its `s` constant within a thousandth of the tolerance) stays a line, a line across it (`u` constant) is an arc of the skin's circle, and any other curve's image is a cubic Hermite spline through its points with its tangents (both in closed form: `dP = axis du + r_skin dφ`, `dφ = ds / (R + K T)`), the steps halved until it holds the image within a quarter of the tolerance at their middles. Its end tangents are the image's exactly: a fitted spline's were a little off, which made a smooth crossing (a circle whose centre is on the tangent line, crossing it square) a corner of almost no angle that the plate's rim could not be traced past (found by the test, 2026-10-28). Note that the image is not tangent to the flat's piece where it crosses obliquely: the bottom skin's radius is not the neutral fibre's, so across the strip it is stretched by `r_skin / (R + K T)`; the edge has a real corner there, as the folded part does. The sheet then goes to the plate as before; the rims along a cut are its square ends.

**The volume.** A strip's element `du ds` is the bend's `du dφ (R + K T)`, whose material between the skins `R` and `R + T` is `T (R + T/2) dφ du`: a hole takes out its area on the flats times `T` and its area on the strip times `T (R + T/2) / (R + K T)` (exactly `T` at `K = ½`).

**Names.** The further faces a cell is left in come after all others, named by the face they are a piece of and their place among its pieces, `F9:face(k + j · 2³²)` for the `j`-th further face of `F9:face(k)` (a face's name has one number; review 2026-10-28: numbering them on from the last flange's flat renumbered them when a flange was added, against stable references); the pieces of a crossed tangent line are `EdgeKey::Split`, a cut's pieces `EdgeKey::Cut`. A part without a hole across a bend is built by the direct construction as before, bit for bit (`sheetmetal_open_pin.rs`). With a closed corner the part's open counterpart is built with the cuts where they lie in it too, and without them otherwise (their edges then named as they are).

**A trimmed strip** (2026-11-04; refused as owed until then): a strip whose end a closed corner's plane cuts (*Trimming a bend*) is the cell bounded by its end's line to the knee `φ₁` and the plane's curve `u(s/ρ)` on to the flange's tangent line, which ends where that curve meets it (`u(θ)`); a point is in the strip when it is inside the rectangle and on the material's side of `u(s/ρ)` beyond `s₁`. Folded, the end is the arc to the knee and the plane's ellipse, made by the one construction both paths share (`trim::ellipse`, `trim::knee`), its rim `Onto` the plane. A cut never meets the trimmed end: it keeps clear of the pattern's outline. Held by `sheetmetal_cuts_trimmed.rs` (three scales, K 0.33 to ½, either flange covering): the 120-degree corner closed by overlap of `sheetmetal_trims.rs` with round holes across the middle of both bends and a slot across the covered bend 1.5 from its trimmed end; the volume the trimmed part's closed form less each hole's share (flats `T`, strip `T (R + T/2)/(R + K T)`), within `10⁻⁸`; the flat area likewise; fold then unfold with the trimmed end on its sinusoid; the trim and its names unchanged.

**A sharp bend** (2026-11-04; refused as owed until then): a sharp bend has no face of the folded sheet (*Sharp bends*), with holes too. A hole across a rounded bend in a part that has sharp bends elsewhere is built as any other, the sheet falling into pieces at the sharp bends. A hole across the sharp bend itself is decided from the geometry: its inner skin is a line, the axis, so at each place `u` along the bend the material near the axis is the sector over the angles the hole leaves at `u`. Where the hole takes the whole width of the strip at `u`, the axis is gone there; where it takes a stretch reaching one tangent line, one sector is left, its side on the hole's rim, which meets the axis along a stretch of it as `u` runs along the hole's edge (a ruled face whose inner edge is a piece of the axis, owed); where it takes a stretch inside the strip, two sectors are left that meet only along the axis, which one solid cannot hold (not buildable). Built: every piece of a hole in a sharp bend's strip **straight across it**, from one tangent line to the other, its `u` the same at both ends within a thousandth of the tolerance (a slot or notch whose sides run square across the bend line); the hole then takes the whole bend over a stretch, the tangent lines' pieces pair up along the bend, and each stretch left is joined in as a sharp bend of its own (`sharp::Join::stretch`: the rims along its two tangent pieces taken out, its outer skin's cylinder and its two sectors put in), the hole's side there the sector between the axis and the outer skin, sharing the hole's rims' side edges in the flats. Every other hole over a sharp bend is refused, the reason and the way out given (its edges square across the bend, or a radius). The volume: a sharp strip's element takes `T/(2K)` per area (`T (R + T/2)/(R + K T)` at `R = 0`). **Names**: the stretches' outer skins as the bend's skin and its further pieces (`F9:copy(…)/F9:face(k + j·2³²)`, `j` the stretch's place along the bend), the bend's two end sectors as before, a hole's side as the rim along its piece across the strip (`F9:edge(10, cut, seg, piece)`) on that stretch's face; the joined faces are numbered by a table on the part (`Part::joined`, `Part::joined_face`), no longer by a code of the bend alone. Held by `sheetmetal_cuts_sharp.rs` (three scales, 60 to 120 degrees, up and down, K 0.33 and ½): one and two slots across a sharp wall's bend, the volume and the flat area in closed form within `10⁻⁹`, each stretch's skin a cylinder of radius `T` and three faces per stretch; a hole across a rounded bend beside a sharp one; refusals (a round hole on the tangent line, a slot at a slant, a hole inside the strip); by command (`crates/engine/tests/sheetmetal_sharp.rs`), a slot across a sharp L: `A T`, every face named once, saved and reopened alike.

**Refused, with the reason**: a cut that touches a tangent line without crossing it, runs along it, meets the end of a strip, or crosses within the tolerance of another crossing; a spline that comes within the tolerance of a tangent line (owed: its crossings found by subdivision of its Bézier pieces; a spline wholly inside a strip or a flat is built); a cut that would leave nothing of a cell. `holeToBend` measures the holes of flats; a hole across a bend is not one (it lies on the bend: its distance would be 0).

**Found and fixed (the plate's trace, 2026-10-28).** A round hole of radius 1 whose centre is on the base's tangent line, in a part 1 thick, failed the plate at 1e3 and 1e6 ("the rim could not be traced within a quarter of the tolerance in 4096 points", 0.285 of the share off at every scale); so did the same half circle as a notch of the base's own outline, with no hole across a bend at all, so the cut's construction was not the cause. Traced round by round: a circle's first steps (0.1 rad of turn) leave a cubic interpolant off by about `r · 0.1⁴ / 384`, which for `r` a million tolerances is just the share; the refinement halved only the one or two steps over the share each round, and as the global spline moved, the next ones went just over (2.5276e-4 against the share 2.5e-4 at scale 1e3, the worst step walking along the circle round after round) until the twelve rounds ran out. `plate::trace` now, when its rounds run out, starts again from the first stations and halves, whenever any step is off, every step off by more than half the share (a margin, so a halved run settles together); a trace that settled before is the same bit for bit (`sheetmetal_open_pin.rs`), and what it accepts is unchanged. Held by `a_hole_of_the_thickness_radius_on_the_tangent_line` (both, at 1e-3, 1, 1e3 and 1e6).

### A later cut carried back

`sheetmetal::later_cuts` (`crates/kernel/src/sheetmetal/later.rs`) reads the last body the part went into (the chain of `consumed_by` from the part's feature) and finds the part's skins among its faces by their surfaces (a boolean keeps the surface of a face it trims; `Made::Back` of the plate is the bottom skin, `Made::Front` the top). Every edge of a skin is laid flat: on a flat by the flat's inverse motion (its `z` must be the skin's level, within four tolerances, or the body is not the part with cuts in it: refused), on a bend by the inverse wrap (`u` along the axis, the angle `φ` from the parent's tangent line, `s = φ (R + K T)`; its distance from the axis must be the skin's radius). An edge is the later feature's when three of its points (a quarter, half and three quarters along) do not all lie within twice the tolerance of the pattern's own curves (its outline, holes, cuts and tangent lines). Each such edge laid flat is the simplest of a line, an arc (a circle when it closes) and a cubic spline through its points that holds them and the points half way between within a quarter of the tolerance (sampled at 16 steps, doubled up to 2048). The edges of each skin are chained end to end (ends within eight tolerances) into loops, turned clockwise.

A loop of the bottom skin whose points (each segment's start and its quarter points) all lie within four tolerances of a loop of the top skin, and the other way round, is a hole through the thickness square to the sheet (a drill square to a flat; a cut through a bend whose walls hold the bend's radial lines): carried, a hole of the exported pattern (`laterCuts`, and `flatArea` less its area). A loop whose box meets loops of the other skin of which none matches it is a cut at a slant or bevelled, which has no one outline in the flat: the export is **refused** naming where. A loop on one skin only (a pocket, an emboss, a part joined on by a union) and a chain that does not close (a cut that reaches the part's edge and changes its outline) are **not carried** and listed with the reason in the export's `notCarried`. The oracle: the part drawn with the same holes in a `cutouts` sketch (holes across a bend included) has the drilled part's volume, and the export's flat area loses the holes' closed forms (`crates/engine/tests/sheetmetal_cuts.rs`).

Not built: carrying a notch at the edge (the outline changed), the measures (`holeToBend`) and the bend table reading the later holes (they read the part as built).

### Corners and reliefs

Where two flanged edges of the base meet at a corner `p` (the outer loop turns left there by more than the angular tolerance), the two strips meet at `p` only (each lies on the outer side of its own edge). Without a relief the part keeps that corner: the two bends' ends meet along the vertical line at `p`, a reflex edge of the solid. A **relief** of size `r` cuts the base's corner: both edges, and the flanges on them, end `r` from `p`, and the corner is replaced by a notch from `a = p + r eₐ` to `b = p + r e_b` (`eₐ`, `e_b` the edges' unit directions away from `p`), through the base:

- `rect`: the parallelogram `p, a, a + r e_b, b` (a square at a right-angled corner, area `r²`);
- `round`: the arc of radius `r` about `p` from `a` to `b` (a quarter disc at a right angle, `π r²/4`);
- `tear`: the far arc of the circle tangent to both edges at `a` and `b` (its centre on the bisector, `r / cos(α/2)` from `p`, its radius `r tan(α/2)` for the corner's angle α), so the notch is tangent to the edges: at a right angle the square and three quarters of the disc of radius `r`, `r² + 3π r²/4`.

A relief is general geometry in the pattern (a cut of the base's outline), not a case of the fold. A corner where the loop turns right (a reflex corner: the two flanges need the same part of the sheet, open or closed, proved in *Limits* › *Reflex corners*) or goes straight on (two flanges continuing each other) is refused with the two flanges; a relief that does not fit its edges (an edge shorter than the reliefs at its ends) is refused with the flange.

### Bend reliefs

Where a bend ends inside its edge (an inset flange, a tab: the parent's edge runs on past the bend's end), the parent turns into the strip's end at a concave corner of 270 degrees, which folds to a reflex edge a press brake tears or bulges. Every such end is listed (`Part::bend_ends`: the bend, its start or end, the tangent line's end `p`), and with the part's **bend relief** (`bendRelief`: a shape, rect, round or tear, as the corner relief's, and a size `r`; format 51) a notch is cut into the parent there: the piece of the edge beside the bend ends `r` short of `p`, and the corner relief's notch is cut at `p` between the edge running on (`−d` at the bend's start, `+d` at its end) and the parent's side (`−û`, into the parent), then a line from `p − r û` back to `p`, in line with the strip's end. So the notch is the corner relief's at a square corner: `rect` the square `r × r` beside the bend's end, `round` the quarter disc of radius `r` about `p`, `tear` the square and three quarters of the disc of radius `r` tangent to the edge and to the strip's end line (area `r²`, `π r²/4`, `r² + 3π r²/4`). The bend keeps its span; nothing else moves. This is the same code for an edge of the base's outer loop, of a hole of it, and a flange's tip or side, since each is a segment of a flat's loop with the flat on its left (`split_at_seats`).

Refused, naming the flange: a relief whose piece of the edge left beside it (to the edge's end, or between it and the relief of the bend before it on the same edge) would be less than `SLIVER_TOLERANCES` tolerances; a size not above the tolerance. A notch that runs into a hole or across the parent's far side is refused by *The pattern is one sheet*. Where a closed corner's extension runs a flange's tip on past a tip flange that spans the tip's end, that tip flange's bend ends inside the extended edge too, and is listed.

**Into an extension** (2026-10-31; refused as owed until then, so the enclosure Jig stopped its returns a relief and 1 mm short of the tip's ends). The extension's piece along the tip, from its far end `far` to the tip's end `p` (or from `p` to `far` at the tip's end), is a segment of the flat's loop with the flat on its left like any other, so the notch is the same: the piece ends `r` short of `p`, the corner relief's notch is cut at `p` between the extension running on (`∓d`) and the wall (`−û` of the tip flange), and the line from `p − r û` back to `p` closes it (`close_corners`' `along_tip`, the same `notch`). The area off the flat is the same closed form as on an edge (`r²`, `π r²/4`, `r² + 3π r²/4`), as the angle at `p` between the extension and the strip's end is square for any walls (the extension runs on along the tip's own line). Refused, naming the tip flange: a relief that leaves less than `SLIVER_TOLERANCES` tolerances of the extension beside it, on either skin. On the bottom skin the run-on along the tip is checked here and the rest exactly by *A notch must fit its flat* (an end line that slants across the flat, as where a wall leaning out covers the corner, comes nearer the notch at its depth: `σ = −tan 30°` per unit of depth for a wall bent 60 degrees). On the top skin the run-on is where the end plane, carried back by the flat's inverse motion, meets `z = T`, taken at its least over the box the notch lies in (`r` along and deep for rect and round, `2r` for tear; the plane is linear, so its two corners on the tip's side bound it; conservative for round and tear), since a mitre's end slants across the thickness, by `T` for square walls (found by the enclosure Jig's range check with its returns spanning the tips: a mitre with a gap of 2 at `T = 0.8` left 0.19 beside the notch on the bottom skin, which the end plane crossed on the top skin, and the plate failed tracing it). The notch's pieces are named `F9:edge(11, b, end, j)` as an edge's notch (`Flat::notches`, the key `EdgeKey::Notch`), and the extension's piece beside it keeps its name (`edge(6, flat, segment)`), so adding the relief renames nothing (tested: every name without it kept, the new ones all `edge(11, b, …)`).

**A notch must fit its flat** (2026-10-31). A rect or round notch reaches `r` along the edge and into the flat; a tear, the disc of radius `r` tangent to the edge and to the strip's end, reaches `2r` both ways. Beside a flat's other edges nearer than that (a wall whose flat is short beside a large bend radius, where the disc crosses the wall's tangent line into its own bend; a short extension, where it crosses the extension's end) the flat's loop crosses itself: `pattern_clear` caught some of these as "flange n runs into itself", naming the wall and not the reason, and a disc wholly inside the wall's bend strip (crossing only the tangent line, which the pattern's outline leaves out) passed it and made an invalid body. **Names.** The pieces of the split edge keep their names (`F9:edge(1, curve, piece, part)` and the rest: `part` is the bends' ids at the piece's ends, whatever the piece's length); the notch's pieces are `F9:edge(11, b, end, j)`, `b` the flange's id less 1, `end` 0 at the bend's start, 1 at its end, `j` the piece's place in the notch. ### Closed corners

A corner where two flanged edges meet is **open** unless closed: each flange ends where its bend does. A **closed** corner keeps the bends apart (the strips and the relief are unchanged; the relief, if any, is the hole where the bends would cross) and runs the two flanges' **flats** on sideways past the bends' ends, along their tangent lines and tips, each to a plane of the folded walls. Let the walls `A` (on the edge arriving at the corner) and `B`, each flat as folded with its inner skin (the bend's inside: the top for a bend up), its outer skin and its unit `inward` normal (outer to inner), and `g` the gap:

- `overlap`: the covering flange `C` runs to the other's outer skin (flush, covering its end); the other `O` to `C`'s inner skin moved `g` inward (its end `g` from `C`'s inside face);
- `butt`: as overlap with `C`'s plane moved `g` inward too (`C` stops `g` short of flush; `g < T`, else it covers nothing);
- `miter` (also written `mitre`): both to the plane through the line where the outer skins meet with normal `out_A − out_B` (the points as far from both outer skins, which holds the line where the inner skins meet too), each moved `g/2` towards its own wall, so the end faces are `g` apart.

`C` is `over` when given (one of the corner's two flanges), else `A`. The extension's end in the pattern is where its plane, carried back by the flat's inverse motion, meets the flat's bottom skin (`z = 0`): its reach along the edge direction `e` from the tangent line's end and from the tip's end is `n·(q − p)/(n·e)` each, so the flat gains the quadrilateral between the two (slanted when the walls are not square to each other). The part's end face lies on the plane (the plate's `Onto` joint along that edge), so with walls square to each other the overlap's and butt's ends are square and the mitre's are bevelled at half the angle between the walls. A flange whose tip carries flanges (inset tabs, screw tabs on an enclosure's wall, a return across the whole tip) closes the same way: the extensions replace the rectangle's side at the closed end and add their pieces before and after the tip's pieces, which stay as the bends on the tip split them (`Flat::pieces` holds where each side of the rectangle begins in the split outline; the seats are carried to their new places). **An outline of its own** (2026-11-02): the outline's segments are the rectangle's generalised. Segment 0 is the tangent line, segment 1 the side at the bend's start (it touches the bend's end `a0`), the last the side at its end, and everything between plays the tip: a closed end replaces its side by the end line and runs its neighbour (the first or the last piece between, which must be a line) on along its own line to the plane, or clips it where the plane comes nearer (the reach at the far point is taken along the neighbour's own direction, `n·(q − b)/(n·e_b)`). A rectangle is the case of four segments, built bit for bit as before. Refused: an outline with nothing between its two sides (a triangle), a neighbour that is not straight. **A side that carries a flange** (2026-11-02): when the end lies along the side (the same reach at both its ends, within the tolerance: butt or overlap with walls square to each other) and runs the flat on, the side's pieces are moved by that reach to the end, keeping their names and seats, and the flanges on them and every flange beyond are moved with them in the pattern (outlines, holes, bends, bend ends) and placed again by the fold (the rest place bit for bit as before); the end's pieces that carry no bend lie on the plane (`Onto`). So a tab on a wall's side wraps round the closed corner over the other wall's outside, standing its bend radius off it. Refused, naming both: an end that does not lie along the side (walls leaning or not square to each other; a mitre of square walls at a square corner lies along it) or cuts the flange back, and a moved flange whose flat holds a cut-out placed before the corners were closed (a louvre's: it would leave the place it is drawn in). Until 2026-10-29 every flange with a flange on its tip or a side was refused ("not supported"). All of this is the general geometry of two planes and a flat, for any bend angles, radii, K-factors and convex corner angles; the fold places every flat by its bends alone, so the walls are known before the extensions are laid out. The extended flats then go through *The pattern is one sheet* (extensions that overlap in the flat are refused naming the flanges: walls leaning out at 60 degrees close by overlap and butt, but a mitre's two extensions, both growing towards the tips, cross in the flat there), are folded and plated with the rest, and regeneration's self-intersection check refuses extensions that run into each other or into another piece when bent.

Refused, with the reason (`KernelError::SheetMetal`): a gap not more than the tolerance (touching flats would make the part one solid through itself) and a butt's not less than the thickness; flanges bent opposite ways (their walls do not meet); a mitre of parallel walls or with both walls on one side of the halving plane; an end plane along the flange (`|n·e|` not above the angular tolerance); a trim this method does not build (*Trimming a bend* below); `corners` naming flanges that do not meet at a corner, listing one twice, or an `over` that is neither of its flanges.

**What a wall carries** (2026-11-04). At every corner of the base where two flanges meet, open or closed, what either flange carries (the flats and bends of its subtree: a return on its tip, a tab on its side, moved to a closed corner's end or not) is proven apart from everything on the other flange's side (its flat, extension included, its bend, and what it carries), and the other way round (`corners_clear`, after the refold); the two walls' own flats and bends are the corner's to close and are left out. Each pair by separating axes (`pieces_apart`): each piece's own directions (a flat's normal, and per line of its outline the line's normal in the flat's plane; a bend's axis and the directions square to it every 15 degrees) and the cross products of their edges' directions (a flat's lines and normal, a bend's axis), each piece's range along an axis by `flat_least` and `bend_least` (lower and upper bounds over the material). A pair no axis separates by more than the tolerance refuses the part, naming both and the way out (an inset, a shorter flange, turned the other way, a relief). Until then nothing looked at what a wall carries against its neighbour: a tab on a wall's side at a mitred corner (`R = T = 1`, gap 0.3), turned in, ran into the other wall's extension and built a body through itself (a point of the other wall was classified outside the part once the tab was added; the checker sees no defect). Held by `sheetmetal_corner_carried.rs` (three scales): that tab turned in and out refused, a point inside both shown (the kernel's point classification in the part without it, the tab's bend in closed form); returns on both walls' tips that overlap at the corner refused, a point inside both; tabs on the open ends' sides, a tab at a corner closed by overlap with its wall covering, and returns inset 8 at the corner built, the volume the part's without them plus each one's closed form `w (θ T (R + T/2) + L T)`.

### Trimming a bend

Where a closed corner's end plane comes nearer than the bend's end (walls that lean towards each other, a wide corner whose relief is too small or absent), the flange is **cut back** instead of run on (2026-11-02; refused as owed until then). The reach along the edge is signed: past the end an extension as above, within the tolerance of it the end as it is (snapped, no sliver piece), short of it a trim. Each of the two places is decided on its own:

- **At the tip** (`s_far < 0`): the flat's end line runs from its point on the tangent line to its point on the tip, which now lies inside the tip: the tip's piece next to the corner is clipped there (it keeps its name). Refused, naming the flange: a clip past that piece's other end or leaving less than `SLIVER_TOLERANCES` tolerances of it, or past a flange on the tip (its bend would be cut).
- **At the tangent line** (`s_near < 0`): the plane cuts the bend too (`crates/kernel/src/sheetmetal/trim.rs`). Each skin of the bend is the cylinder of radius `r` (the bottom skin `R + T` for a bend up, `R` down; the top the other) about the axis `S + u d + z_c ẑ` in the parent's pattern axes, its point at the angle `φ` from the parent's tangent line `S + u d + r sin φ û + (z_c − σ r cos φ) ẑ` (`σ` = 1 up, −1 down), so the plane `n·(P − q) = 0` meets it along `u(φ) = a + b sin φ + c cos φ` with `a = (n·(q − S) − z_c n_z)/(n·d)`, `b = −r (n·û)/(n·d)`, `c = σ r n_z/(n·d)`, refused where `|n·d|` is within the angular tolerance (the plane along the bend). The run of `φ` the plane takes away is found in closed form (`b sin φ + c cos φ = m sin(φ + ψ)`). A trim is built when on **both** skins that run is one interval `[φ₁, θ]` reaching the flange's tangent line with `φ₁` strictly inside (and `r φ₁`, `r (θ − φ₁)` not slivers): the bend's end is then its arc (square end) up to `φ₁` and the plane's ellipse beyond (`Curve3::Ellipse`, centred where the axis meets the plane, semi-axes `r/|n·D|` along the axis's projection and `r` square to it, its eccentric angle running with `φ`), whose rim is `Onto` the plane as the flat's end is; where the two ends meet, the plate's corner is the line from the bottom skin's `φ₁` to the top skin's `φ₁′`. In the flat pattern the strip's end is the line across the strip up to `s₁ = ρ φ₁` (`ρ = R + K T`, the neutral fibre's length kept) and then the curve `u(s/ρ)`, a cubic spline of Hermite steps in `s` with double inner knots (C¹), whose error is **proven**, not sampled: `u` is a sinusoid in `s/ρ`, so `|u⁗| ≤ hypot(b, c)/ρ⁴`, and a Hermite step `h` is off by at most `|u⁗| h⁴/384`, which the steps hold to an eighth of the tolerance (at most 2047 steps, refused beyond; the rounding of the evaluation, about `10⁻¹³` of the numbers' size, held to another eighth). The flat's end meets the tangent line exactly at the spline's end, `u(θ)`. The strip's area taken away is exact in closed form, `∫ (u − u_end) ds = (a − u_end) ρ (θ − φ₁) + b ρ (cos φ₁ − cos θ) + c ρ (sin θ − sin φ₁)`, so `flatArea` is exact.

Refused with the reason and the way out (a relief, a larger one, a mitre, or the corner open): the plane reaching the parent's tangent line (it would cut the parent flat too); cutting the bend's bottom skin but not its top one at the flange's tangent line, or in more than one run (a step the sheet cannot make: the one plate of the bottom skin has no face for it); a cut within a sliver of either tangent line. **Wherever a closed end lies near a bend that is not trimmed** (the flat run on or cut back at its tip only), the plane's cut is checked on both skins over the whole bend, in closed form (`cut(φ) = ±(u(φ) − u_end)`, a sinusoid, largest at `θ` or at a stationary angle `atan2(b, c) + kπ`), and a cut past the tolerance is allowed in two cases only: at a mitre whose halving plane passes through the corner's point on both skins (within the tolerance: a symmetric mitre), a cut no deeper than at the root `φ = 0` (the half gap the plane is shifted by, so the bend stays on its side of the halving plane, which the other wall's material does not cross); and wherever the other flange's flat, its extension included, and everything it carries (the flats and bends of its subtree: a return on its tip, a tab on its side) are **proven apart from the bend's material** by more than the tolerance (`apart_from_bend`, checked after any refold, `uncut_clear`), piece by piece: a flat over its folded outline on both skins (lines at their ends, arcs at the one angle of their least, splines by their control points), a bend over its ring sector (its arcs at both ends, at their ends and the angle of their least), lower bounds, each against the bend's own ring sector (untrimmed, a superset) along a separating axis: the bend's tangent line out of its end (the piece wholly past the bend's square end: the plane square to the tangent line through its end, which the bend's material does not cross; the rule until 2026-11-04, still measured by `clearance_past_end`), the directions square to the axis every 15 degrees (the cross-section: a return high above the bend), and those tilted out of the end (`w + c d`, `c` ½, 1, 2, 4). One separating axis is a proof; a piece none separates refuses the part. The other bend meets it only at the corner's point and is left out (review of f8a160b2: the carried flanges were left out). A subtree moved with a side to the corner's end is proven like any other (the proof runs after the refold), and what it carries is kept clear of the other wall by *Closed corners* › *What a wall carries*. Anything else is refused (review of cc7b9228: the root's cut was allowed at every corner, so at a mitre of unlike walls, whose halving plane misses the corner's point, a bend could reach past it with nothing proving the other wall left room; held by `a_mitre_of_unlike_walls_is_kept_only_where_the_other_wall_is_clear`: square walls of radii 1 and 3 build, their root cut `(R_B − R_A) + g/√2` kept by the clearance `R_A` exactly, refused once the other wall carries a return turned in over the base; a wall leaning in beside a square one of another radius is refused, where the plate failed before): a bend down whose outside (top skin) is cut, or a cut through the middle of a bend while its ends stay clear, was left square and ran through the other wall (review of 4116ed3e, 2026-11-02; the 120-degree overlap corner bent down failed the plate with an unrelated reason, or built). The trim follows the bottom skin, as everything in the pattern does: the pattern is the bottom skin's outline, so the strip's end is where the plane meets the bottom skin, `u(s/ρ)` at `φ = s/ρ`, and the top skin's `φ₁′` is only where the plate's corner line ends. A hole across a bend in a part with a trimmed bend is built (2026-11-04, *Holes across a bend* › *A trimmed strip*), and the press-brake check counts the trimmed bend's slab and ring sector as more than its material (a collision found with them is never certain, as for a strip with a hole across it).

**Names.** The arc's piece keeps the arc's name (`F9:edge(8, b, end)`), the ellipse is `F9:edge(14, b, end)`, the clipped tip piece and the end line keep the names of the tip and the side they replace, so every name of the part with the corner open is a name of the trimmed part (tested).

**Oracles** (`crates/kernel/tests/sheetmetal_trims.rs`, three scales): walls bent 120 degrees (leaning in) on two edges of a square corner, mitred with a gap `g`: the mitre plane is `x + y = W` for any angle (the outer skins' bisector), so each wall ends `g/√2` from it; the bends are not touched and the tips are clipped; the flat area `W D + BA (W + D) + L (W + σ) + L (D + σ) + L² cos θ` and the volume `W D T + θ (W + D) T (R + T/2) + Σ L T (edge + σ + L cos θ/2 − T sin θ/2)` with `σ = (R + T) sin θ − g/√2`, at K 0.33 and ½. A base corner of 120 degrees without a relief closed by overlap with a gap `0.6 R` (`R = T`), each flange covering in turn: the covered bend is trimmed at its start or its end; `sin φ₁ = a*/(R + T)` and `sin φ₁′ = a*/R` with `a* = (R − g)/cos 60°`; the volume taken from the bend `∫ (c a + y₀)/s H(a) da` over the quarter annulus beyond `a*` (elementary integrals of `√(ρ² − a²)`), from the wall `L (c ((R + T)² − R²)/2 + y₀ T)/s`, the cover's extension `L ((R + T) T − c ((R + T)² − R²)/2)/s`; the flat area likewise; fold then unfold, with every trimmed ellipse laid flat by the inverse wrap on `u(φ)` within the tolerance and the pattern's spline within a quarter of it of the sinusoid; the open part's names kept. Refusals: the gap 0.3 (the top skin uncut), the gap 1.5 (the parent's tangent line reached).

**Not built.** The reflex tab of *Limits* › *Reflex corners* (an edge shorter than the other flange's bend allowance, closed by a mitre through the corner): its trim plane passes through the corner's point on the parent's tangent line, which the rule above refuses (the plane reaching the parent's tangent line), since cutting there splits the parent's edge at the bend's end (owed with it: a seat shortened by a trim). A trim whose plane cuts one skin and not the other would need a face the plate of one sheet does not make (a step across the thickness); a relief removes it.

**Choices as expressions.** The corner's `kind` and the relief's `shape` are a name or an expression whose value picks one, 1 for the first (`corner`: 1 open, 2 butt, 3 overlap, 4 miter; `shape`: 1 rect, 2 round, 3 tear), the count a published choice parameter gives (`docs/guide/05-jigs.md`), so a Jig's choice or a table lookup drives them; a value that is not a whole number in range is refused naming the choices. In the file a name is written as itself and an expression as `{"expr": "…"}` (format 44), so an unknown name is still refused on load.

**Names kept.** Closing a corner adds pieces to a flat's outline; the part's edges are named by what they are (*Names* below), and an extension's end is named as the side it replaces (`Flat::was`), a piece an extension adds `F9:edge(6, flat, segment)`, `segment` its place in the outline as if each side of the rectangle were one segment (`Flat::added`: 1 and 3 at the start, then 3 and 5, or 5 and 7 with the start closed too, at the end), so flanges on the tip renumber none of them; on a flange whose tip carries nothing this is its place in the closed outline, as every file before format 51 has it. A reference to a tip's or a side's rim keeps its target when a Jig's choice opens or closes the corners (the side's rim is the extension's end then). Tested: every open name present in each closed kind, each rim the same area when closed by overlap, the open names back exactly. (Until 2026-10-28 the part was built a second time with every corner open only to name its edges by the faces round them; naming by what they are made that unneeded.)

**The measure.** `cornerGap` is the least gap at the parts' closed corners, measured on the solid: the least distance (`verify::distance`, with its proven lower bound) between the faces made from the corner's two flats (their skins and the rims along their edges, `sheetmetal::flat_faces`). The tests hold it, and the plain `distance` between those faces by name, to the gap given.

### The pattern is one sheet

The pattern must be cut from one sheet, so no two of its pieces may overlap, touch or lie along each other except where the pattern joins them (a bend's tangent lines, the point two strips share at a corner without relief), whatever the folded part does. This is checked on the laid-out pattern as a whole, before folding: its outline (every flat's and strip's edge that no strip continues, and the holes; the same chains the export draws) goes through the sketch's profile detection (`detect_profiles`), which refuses two curves that cross, touch or overlap away from an end they share and more than two ends at one point, naming the curves; each curve is known by the flat it came from (a strip counts with its flange), so the part fails naming the base or the flanges that meet ("the flat pattern overlaps itself: the base and flange 0 cross or touch in the flat"). What the detection accepts must be one region with all the holes and the area the pieces add up to (`flat_area`), or a piece lies wholly on another and the part fails too; anything else the detection refuses fails it (fail closed). This catches every way the pieces meet without a rule per case: a flange beside a reflex corner whose neighbour has no flange (its flat lies along that edge at 270 degrees, across it beyond), flanges on edges of a concave base that face each other and run into each other in the flat, a flange on a tip that runs back across another piece.

### Folding: bends are joints with a radius

Every flat is a body of a `Linkage` (the base is body 0 and stays in place; flange `i` is body `i + 1`), every bend a joint whose angle is given: `+θ` up (a valley in origami's convention: towards the side the normal points to), `−θ` down. Origami's `Joint` has a **radius** `r` and a unit vector `û` across the strip (from the left body to the right one). Its motion at angle ρ is

`motion(ρ) = Rot(C(ρ), D, −ρ) ∘ Shift(−|ρ| r û)`,  `C(ρ) = P + sign(ρ) r (û × D)`,

the strip's width the neutral fibre takes up at this angle taken back, then the turn about the axis `r` from the strip's start `P` on the side the body turns to. Its velocity (what the solve's derivative needs) is the turn's `−D × (x − C)` plus the turned shift's `−sign(ρ) r Rot(D, −ρ) û`, both carried by the motion before the joint. A crease is the joint with `r = 0` (exactly origami's turn); the motion and its velocity are continuous through ρ = 0, and both one-sided velocities there are the crease's, so the continuation, loop closure and rank of origami are unchanged. For a bend, `P` is where the strip starts on the neutral fibre: `z = T − K T` for a bend up (its inner skin is the top) and `z = K T` for a bend down; so the axis lies at `z = T + R` (up) or `z = −R` (down) above the tangent line, and the neutral fibre's arc from `P` over ρ ends where the far flat's strip edge lands. A sheet-metal part is a tree (no loops), so `fold_linkage` places every flat by the product of the motions along its path; a closed loop of bends (a box whose walls are joined, a tube) would be the same linkage with loops and is not built (the walls are not joined to each other).

### The part: the folded bottom skin, plated

The folded bottom skin is one sheet: per flat the planar face `z = 0` of its outline carried by its motion (lines, arcs, circles and splines of the base as they are), per bend the cylinder about its axis of radius `T + R` (bend up: the bottom skin is outside) or `R` (bend down: inside) over θ, bounded by the two tangent lines (the parent's edge and the child's) and the two arcs at the bend's ends. Every face's normal points into the material (`+z` carried; the cylinder's face reversed for a bend up), the tangent lines are edges shared by a flat and a bend (the faces are tangent there), a corner without relief is one vertex shared by the base and both bends. The sheet passes `topo::check`. The plate of all its faces on the side `out` by `T` with square ends everywhere is the part ([`plates.md`](plates.md) › *The plate*): a bend's skins are exactly the cylinders `R` and `R + T` (an offset of a cylinder is a cylinder), the flats are slabs, the ends are planes square to the sheet. Flanges that run into each other or into the base when folded are found by regeneration's self-intersection check on the solid ([`regeneration.md`](regeneration.md) › *Never through itself*): the feature fails with "flanges that run into each other or into the base when bent".

**Volume.** The model keeps the skins at `R` and `R + T` (as sheet-metal design does), so a bend's volume is `θ w ((R + T)² − R²)/2 = θ w T (R + T/2)` while its strip's area times `T` is `θ w T (R + K T)`: the volume is the flat area times `T` **exactly when `K = ½`**, and less by `θ w T² (½ − K)` per bend otherwise (the material's neutral fibre is where the length is kept, not the middle). The oracle holds both: `A T` at `K = ½` for the L and the box with every relief, and the difference in closed form at `K` = 0, 0.33, 1. (The brief's "volume = flat area × T exactly" is the case `K = ½`; for other K it would claim a conservation the geometric model does not have.)

### Sharp bends

A bend of inner radius 0 (one within the tolerance of 0 is 0; `crates/kernel/src/sheetmetal/sharp.rs`, built 2026-11-03) is the model's bend at `R = 0`, decided by the closed forms already used rather than by a convention of its own: the skins stay at `R` and `R + T` about the axis, so the inner skin is the axis itself (the line where the two flats' inner skins meet, a sharp inside corner) and the outer skin is the cylinder of radius `T` (the minimal outside radius a press brake's sharp punch leaves); the neutral fibre lies `K T` from the inner skin, so `BA = θ K T`, the setback is `T tan(θ/2)` and the deduction `2 T tan(θ/2) − θ K T`, and the bend's material is the sector of the disc of radius `T` over θ, `θ w T²/2` (so the volume is `A T` at `K = ½` and less by `θ w T² (½ − K)` otherwise, as for every bend). A sharp outside corner instead would be a part other than the one the flat pattern's allowance describes (its volume and its mould line would not follow from `R = 0`), so it is not offered. The fold is unchanged (the joint of neutral radius `K T`).

- **The solid.** The plate of the bottom skin cannot make it: the offset of the cylinder of radius `T` by `T` towards its axis is a line, and a bend down's bottom skin would be one. So a sharp bend has **no face of the folded sheet**: its two tangent lines end the flats' sheets, which fall into pieces (one shell each); each piece is plated apart (with the joints on its own edges), and every sharp bend is **joined in** exactly: the two square rims along its tangent lines (each the rectangle from the axis to the outer skin, which are the sector's own flat sides) are taken out; the outer skin's cylinder (between the parent's and the child's outer edges and two new arcs of radius `T`) and the two end sectors (each an arc and the two rims' sides) are put in on the plates' own edges and vertices; the child's inner edge goes into the parent's (both are the axis between the bend's ends). Nothing is moved and no boolean is run: each vertex is found within the tolerance of where the closed form puts it, or the part is refused, and the result is checked as every body is (`topo::check`).
- **Names.** The faces are named as the same bend's with a radius: the outer skin as that skin (`F9:copy(1)/F9:face(2n − 1)` up, `copy(0)` down), the end sectors as the rims along the bend's arcs (`F9:rim(h)/F9:face(2n − 1)`, `h` the hash of `F9:edge(8, b, end)`). So a radius changed to 0 takes away exactly the bend's inner skin (a line, no face: a reference to it is an error, never retargeted) and gives every other name back unchanged, and a radius changed back from 0 gives back every name (`a_sharp_l_by_command_keeps_its_names_and_its_closed_forms`). The rims along the tangent lines that the joining takes out have no name at any radius (with a radius the tangent lines are inner edges).
- **Measures.** `bendRadius` and the bend table's `radius` are 0 (the inner skin is the axis); the press-brake check takes the bend as any other (its ring sector from radius 0, the punch's tip at the axis), a sharp U channel clearing the straight punch by the rounded channel's closed form at `R = 0`.
- **Touching.** At `R = 0` a wall's inner skin is the plane through its tangent line and the parent's inner skin runs into the same line, so the radius no longer keeps the child's side of the part (its flat and all it carries) `R` from the rest; the checker and the self-intersection check do not see two pieces that only touch. **One proof** (`sharp::clear`, re-review of 8449951d): for every sharp bend, the child's inner skin, as the rectangle of the child flat's outline along and across the axis (a superset of the face, folded), must lie more than the tolerance from every piece of the other side (every flat and bend but the child's subtree, the parent and the bend itself), and the parent's inner skin, likewise, from every piece of the child's side but the child flat (which meets it only along the axis, at the bend's angle). Proven against the whole rectangle (re-review of bae58281: taken `SLIVER_TOLERANCES` tolerances in from its edges, the proof was blind in that band, and a rounded neighbour leaning in by just enough to reach two tolerances past the skin near the sharp wall's start built); a piece not proven apart from it is accepted only when it is a bend of the same parent with an end on the sharp bend's tangent line's end (the neighbour at a corner, which meets the skin only along the corner's line below the axis), and then only when it is proven apart from the skin less a strip `SLIVER_TOLERANCES` tolerances wide along the shared end and from the skin less such a strip along the axis: their union is the whole skin but that small square at the shared corner (re-verification of d8ecd192). Off the axis the neighbour is separated by the rectangle's own normals or by one of the directions out of the shared end tilted down the skin (`out·e1 − k e2`, k = ½, 1, 2, 4), each a separating axis: a rounded neighbour leaves the corner's line at `√(2 v R)` outward for a height `v` above the axis, so a tilt of ½ separates it by the strip's `m/2`. A formed louvre's hood is no piece of the part (it is joined by a boolean after the part is built), so each is bounded by the box of its footprint grown by the thickness, from its flat's skin to `h + T` beyond (the section rises to `h`, its other skin `T` further along its normal), and proven clear the same way when the louvres are placed (refused naming `formedLouvres.g`; the box is a bound, so a hood within about the thickness of a sharp wall's inner skin is refused although it may not touch). A piece is apart when it lies wholly past one of the rectangle's six bounding planes by the tolerance (a separating axis; the piece's range of each linear function by `flat_least` and `bend_least`, exact for lines and arcs, by control points for splines, a ring sector for a bend); otherwise the part is refused naming the piece. Lengths decide, not angles: a wall at 90 degrees and a hair, a base corner of 89.9999 degrees, are refused where the end face comes within the tolerance. Two cheaper rules stay, for what lies on a skin's edge, which the proof leaves out: two bends on one parent turning the same way whose tangent lines meet at an end, both sharp, unless their corner is closed; a flange on an edge that starts at its flat's own bend's far tangent line (a side) when either bend is sharp. And a sharp bend whose flat's far end comes within `SLIVER_TOLERANCES` tolerances of the parent's inner skin (`L sin θ`) is refused as a closed hem. Held by `sharp_walls_that_would_touch_are_refused`, `material_on_a_sharp_walls_inner_skin_is_refused_by_one_proof` and `a_hair_past_a_sharp_walls_inner_skin_and_a_hood_beside_it` (three scales; every case built a solid touching itself on d88ec622 or 8449951d: a wall leaning in at 120 degrees with R = 2 beside a sharp square wall, also inset; sharp tabs on a wall's tip and side; a sharp tab on a sharp wall's side; a lip turned in on a neighbouring wall's tip, and a hem folded back inside it, ending on a sharp wall's inner skin; the leaning wall beside a sharp wall at 90 degrees plus twice the angular tolerance, and at a base corner of 89.9999 degrees), and the same parts with a relief built.
- **Refused, with the reason** (*Limits*): 180 degrees (the flange folded onto the parent's inner skin, touching it all over: a closed hem), `θ K T` within `SLIVER_TOLERANCES` tolerances of 0 (at `K = 0` the strip has no width; the outer arcs `θ T` are then longer still), the touching above, a sharp bend trimmed by a closed corner's end plane, a hole over a sharp bend other than straight across it (*Holes across a bend* › *A sharp bend*), a sharp jog (its radius is still refused at 0). A later cut or pocket in a sharp bend's outer skin or end is not carried back onto the export: listed in `notCarried` naming the bend (a face of the later body on one of those surfaces with a vertex or a hole the part's face has not; its inner skin is a line, so a hole through it has no one outline across the strip).

Oracles (`crates/kernel/tests/sheetmetal_sharp.rs`, three scales): an L at 60, 90 and 120 degrees, up and down, K 0.33 and ½: the allowance, the flat length and area, the deduction, the volume and `volume − A T`, the angle, the developed allowance, the box (the axis at `(0, T)`, the wall's inner skin through it); a U and a Z by flat length, volume and box; a box of four sharp flanges with rect and round reliefs (`A T`); a sharp bend beside a rounded one at an unrelieved corner (the sum of the pieces); a corner closed by overlap with a relief, an inset tab with bend reliefs and a hole in a sharp wall's flat (`A T`); every sharp bend's three faces (the outer skin a cylinder of radius `T`), its measured radius 0; refusals by reason, and a radius within the tolerance of 0 made 0. By command (`crates/engine/tests/sheetmetal_sharp.rs`, three scales): the L up and down with a radius variable set to 0 and back (volume, box, bend table, names, `bendRadius`, the flat pattern exported, saved and reopened), the sharp U channel on the press brake, refusals as failed features.

### Curved bends

A **curved flange** (`curvedFlanges`, format 68; `crates/kernel/src/sheetmetal/curved.rs`; the developer's decision of 2026-11-04) is bent along an arc or a whole circle of the base's outline or of a hole of it: the rim of a round pan, a collar round a round hole, a flange on a concave notch, the rounded corners of a tray. It is a list of its own beside `flanges` (as jogs and formed louvres are), so nothing about the straight flanges, their indices and names changes; it takes one id from the flanges' counter.

- **The part is exact.** Every section square to the edge (the half-plane through the arc's axis, the line through its centre along the base's normal) is the section of a straight bend with the same `R`, `K`, `T`, `θ`, direction and flat length; the curved flange is that section turned about the axis over the arc's sweep `Δ`. So the bend's skins are pieces of **tori** (major radius the edge's radius `a`, minor `R` and `R + T`, about the axis at the bend's height) and the flat's skins pieces of **cones** (a cylinder at 90 degrees, a plane at 180), all analytic: the folded bottom skin gets a torus face (on the base's arc edge, tangent to the base) and a cone face (on the bend's far circle, tangent to the torus), bounded by circles about the axis and, at the arc's ends, by the bend's meridian arcs (radius `R` or `R + T` in the radial plane, over `θ`) and the flat's straight sides; the plate offsets them exactly (an offset of a torus is a torus, of a cone a cone). The volume is Pappus's: `Δ ∫ ρ dA` over the section, `Δ (a A + ε M)` with `A = θ T (R + T/2) + L T`, `M = (1 − cos θ)((R + T)³ − R³)/3 + sin θ L ((R + T)² − R²)/2 + cos θ T L²/2` (the same for a bend up and down), `ε` = +1 when the flange grows away from the arc's centre (a convex edge of the outline: a **shrink flange**) and −1 towards it (a concave edge, a hole's: a **stretch flange**).
- **The flat pattern is approximated, and says so.** A torus is not developable (its Gaussian curvature is not 0), so no flat pattern keeps every length. The development is the **radial** one, as practice lays such flanges out: along every radial line the section is laid out as a straight bend's (the strip `BA = θ (R + K T)` wide, then the flat `L` long), so the strip and the flat are annular sectors about the arc's centre between the radii `a`, `a + ε BA` and `a + ε (BA + L)`, and the length across the bend (the neutral fibre's) is kept exactly. Its area is `Δ/2 |r_t² − a²|`; the bend line is the arc across the middle of the strip; the outline replaces the base's arc by the two radial sides and the tip's arc.
- **The strain the approximation implies, proven.** Along the edge lengths are not kept: a material point at the pattern's radius `a + ε s` (`s` across the edge: `s = (R + K T) φ` on the bend, `BA + d` on the flat) and at the distance `r ∈ [R, R + T]` from the bend's axis lies at the radius `a + ε x` in the part (`x = r sin φ` on the bend, `r sin θ + d cos θ` on the flat), so it is stretched along the edge by `e = ε (x − s)/(a + ε s)`; across the edge the strain is the bend's own, as in a straight bend. The bound is **proven, not sampled**: `e` is affine in `r`, so its extremes over the thickness lie at `r = R` and `R + T`; on the flat it is a ratio of affine functions of `d`, monotone, so its extremes are at the flat's two ends, in closed form; on the bend a branch and bound over `φ` bounds it above on each interval by the lesser of an interval enclosure (the numerator's and the denominator's ranges) and a second-order one about the middle (`h(m) + |h'(m)| w/2 + M₂ w²/8`, `M₂` bounding `|h''| = |N''/D − 2 N' D'/D² + 2 N D'²/D³|` by the pieces' ranges), halving the interval whose bound is largest until it is within a billionth of the largest value found (at most 2048 halvings; past them the bound is the largest left, still proven, only looser), and a margin of `10⁻¹²` for the rounding. At 90 degrees it is the tip's inner fibre exactly, `(R − BA − L)/(a + BA + L)` compressed for a shrink flange and `−(R − BA − L)/(a − BA − L)` stretched for a stretch flange. The bend table (`curvedFlanges`: `strain` with `least`, `most`, `largest`, `proven`), the export (`patternExact` false, `curvedFlanges` with each one's strain, `approximationNote`, which is also written into the file itself: a DXF comment, group 999, at its start, an SVG comment after its XML declaration, so the file alone tells its reader) and the measure `bendStrain` (the largest over the parts) give it; `maxStrain` on the part (a plain number, the material's: no catalogue) refuses a curved flange whose bound is above it, naming the bound.
- **Tangent walls are one wall.** Where a curved flange's arc meets a line of the base tangent to it, and the straight flange on that line spans it to that end and is alike (angle and direction, radius, K-factor, flat length; its flat its rectangle: no outline, no closed corner, no trim), the curved flange **continues** it (`Curved::joined`): its bend's meridian arc and its flat's side are the straight flange's bend's end arc and its flat's side, edges shared by the two in the folded sheet (the cylinder and the torus are tangent along that arc, the plane and the cone along that side), so the part has no seam there, and in the pattern the strip's and the flat's sides between them are left out of the outline. A tray with rounded corners is one wall round the base: its outline is the base grown by `BA + L` all round. Two curved flanges on arcs that meet tangent and grow to the same side there, alike, continue each other the same way (`Join::Curved`: the two tori are tangent along their common meridian, the cones along their common side; the later one in the list takes the earlier one's meridian and side): an oval of arcs flanged all round is one wall. A wall beside the arc that is not alike is not continued, and their strips lie along each other in the pattern: refused (*The pattern is one sheet*).
- **Names.** The faces are a flange's by the curved flange's id, `F9:face(2 id − 1)` (the bend) and `F9:face(2 id)` (the flat), skins and rims as every flange's; its edges `F9:edge(17, id − 1, k)` (`k` 0 the far tangent line, 1 the tip, 2 and 3 the bend's ends at the arc's own start and end, 4 and 5 the flat's sides there; a joined end keeps the straight flange's names). The base's arc becomes a tangent line, so its rim goes, as a flange added on a line takes the rim along it; so does a joined straight flange's side rim. A straight flange added elsewhere renames none of its faces, and an edit that keeps its id keeps every name (`a_curved_flange_by_command`).
- **Refused, with the reason** (`curvedFlanges.g: …`): an edge that is not an arc or circle of the base (a line: one of `flanges`; a spline: its bend would be a canal surface, which has no exact surface here, owed), an arc split into several edges (a relief or a flange on it), two curved flanges on one edge; a radius within the tolerance of 0 (the inner skin would be the torus's tube shrunk to a circle; owed), a length not above the tolerance, an angle outside (0, 180] degrees or within the angular tolerance of 0, a K-factor outside [0, 1], a flange shorter than its setback (mould); an edge radius not above `R + T` by `SLIVER_TOLERANCES` tolerances (the bend would reach its own axis), a stretch flange whose flat reaches the arc's centre in the pattern, one that reaches the axis when bent (`(1 + e_min)` times the least pattern radius within a sliver of 0), a bound above `maxStrain`; a curved flange whose strip or flat overlaps another piece of the pattern; a part with a sharp bend or with a hole across a bend (the base's arc is then no longer one edge of the folded sheet). The proofs of closed corners (`corners_clear`) and trims see the straight flanges only: a curved flange beside a closed corner is held by regeneration's self-intersection check, and a straight flange whose bend is trimmed or whose flat is extended is never continued by a curved one. Not built (owed): flanges on a curved flange's tip or sides, insets, an outline of its own, closed corners and reliefs at its ends, holes across a curved bend or cut-outs in its flat, a later cut carried back through it (its skins have no one place in the flat: the export is refused naming the flange, while a later cut elsewhere on the part is carried back as before, the curved skins' own edges passed over), the press-brake check (a curved flange is formed, not bent on a brake: `bendSequence` does not see it), `holeToBend` beside it.

### The flat pattern measured: one unfold

The pattern the part is folded from is known; what the unfold adds is a measurement of it on the part. For each bend its **neutral band** (the cylinder `R + K T` about the bend's axis over θ, as long as the bend, built exactly as a sheet of one face) is laid flat by `unfold::develop`, and the flat length of its arcs is the strip's width: it must agree with `θ (R + K T)` (to a few tolerances: the development's own error); the bend table reports both (`allowance`, `developed`). The flats are planes, whose development is the motion back. Fold then unfold is the oracle: every folded flat's vertices, carried back by the inverse of its motion, land at `z = 0` on the flat's outline in the pattern, and every developed band is as wide as the pattern's strip. The closed form is used to lay out the pattern and to fold it (it is exact); the development is the independent measurement, run by the bend table and the tests, not by every regeneration (it costs a development per bend).

The pattern's **outline**: the flats' edges that no strip continues, the strips' ends (straight lines across the strip, `BA` long), chained head to tail into one closed chain per piece, with the holes; the **bend lines** are the middles of the strips. `file.export` with `sheetMetal` draws it in the sketch's axes: the outline on the layer `outline` (black, continuous), bend lines up on `bendUp` (blue, dashed), down on `bendDown` (red, dash-dot), the drawing crate's styles as origami's crease pattern uses them, every curve as it is.

### Manufacturability: measures, not a catalogue

The kernel measures, the limits are the caller's (the developer, 2026-10-26: general geometry in, domain data out):

- `bendRadius`: the smallest inner radius of the bends, measured on the solid (the smaller cylinder the plate made of each bend's face);
- `flangeLength`: the shortest flange, its flat from the bend's tangent line on the flange's side to the farthest point of its outline; with `measured: mould` plus the outside setback (from the virtual sharp);
- `holeToBend`: the least distance in the pattern (which is the distance on the part: a flat is rigid) from a hole of a flat (the base's or a flange's) to the tangent line on that flat's side of every bend beside it (a bend that starts from the flat: its parent edge; the flat's own bend: its far tangent line); in closed form for lines, arcs and circles (the critical points of the distance between a segment and each); for a spline searched by subdivision (a piece whose box is further from the line than the best found is left out, the others halved until flat within an eighth of the tolerance), so the true distance is proven within an eighth of the tolerance either way: the measure carries that as its lower and upper bound, and an assertion whose limit lies between them is *not proven*, never passed (`exact: false`, `lower` and `upper` in the detail). The reported distance is the least over the flat pieces' chords, not a distance at a place on the curve. When the search runs out of work the spline proves nothing: its lower bound is 0 and its upper bound infinite, so the hole's bounds are what its other curves prove;
- `relief`: whether every corner where two flanged edges meet has a relief;
- `cornerGap`: the least gap at the closed corners, measured on the solid (*Closed corners*);
- `flatExtent` (`axis` x or y) and `flatArea` (2026-10-31; the bend table's `flatSize` now uses the same box, so a pattern whose outline bulges past its ends with an arc, a round relief or a rounded corner, is wider there than the segment ends it was measured by before: a change of `sheetMetal.bends`): the largest width or height of the parts' flat patterns in their sketches' axes (the blank each needs: the box of the outline, lines, arcs and circles by their extremes, exact; a spline by its control points, a bound from above, so the measure carries 0 as its proven lower bound there) and the sum of their flat areas less the holes.

**Measures over nothing.** `cornerGap` with no closed corner (every corner open, or chosen open by a Jig) and `holeToBend` with no hole on a flat beside a bend have no value: a least over an empty set is no number, and taking it as a pass would also pass a part that lost its hole by mistake. Each is an error that says what the parts have (*`Plate` has no holes*, *none of the 2 holes of `Plate` lies on a flat beside a bend*, *no corner of `Box` is closed*) and how an assertion written for a design that sometimes has none is switched off: by a condition, `when` (verification.md › *Conditions*). A part's holes are those it was built with (its base sketch's inner loops and its `cutouts`); a hole a later feature cuts into the part's body (a boolean) is not one of them, and the error names the feature the body went on into (2026-10-28).

The minimum radius for a material and a thickness, a die's minimum flange, the hole distance a shop asks for are expressions in the assertions' `min` and `max`, a document table among them (`steel["S235"].minRadius`). There is no material table in 3DCraft.

### Bend sequence

`sheetMetal.bendSequence {feature, punch, die, length, clearance}` (`crates/engine/src/bendseq_cmds.rs`, the stages in `crates/kernel/src/sheetmetal/sequence.rs`; sheetmetal-r3, 2026-10-28) checks whether the part can be bent on a press brake and in which order. It changes nothing.

- **The tools** are closed polygons (lines) in the plane square to a bend, extruded `length` along it and centred on its middle (default: twice the farthest any piece lies along the axis from the bend's middle, so they reach past the whole part; a distance to a prism that reaches past every piece is its distance to the endless prism): the punch's with its tip's centre at the origin and its body along `+y`, the die's with the point under the bend on the outer skin at the origin and its body below. At bend `b` and stroke angle `ρ` both stand in the bend's frame: origin on its axis at the middle of the bend (the die's `R + T` further out), `y` along the bisector of the two flats towards the inside, `(cos ρ − 1) û + sin ρ n` normalised, which is `−sin(ρ/2) û + cos(ρ/2) n` (`û` across the strip, `n` the parent's normal on the side the bend turns to), `z` along the axis, `x = y × z`: both legs rise `ρ/2`, as on a press brake. General geometry: a gooseneck punch or a hemming die is only another polygon.
- **A stage** is the linkage folded with the bends made at their angles, `b` at `ρ` and the rest at 0 (`stage_worlds`: the same `fold` with other angles; the joint's motion at `ρ` places the flange's side as the arc over `ρ` and the rest of the strip flat beyond it). Every piece is rigid: a flat with the bends made on it, a made bend (it moves with its parent), a strip not yet bent (a slab `T` thick over its strip in the pattern, lying on its parent: `strip_slab`). So the part at a stage is the built part's faces moved piece by piece (`final_worlds` to `stage_worlds`), never a part built again; the tool is carried into each piece's built place instead of the piece to the tool (a distance does not change under a rigid motion).
- **A step** (bend `b` with a set made) clears when the proven distance (`verify::distance`, its lower bound; a piece and a tool wholly inside one another count as 0: a point of the piece inside the tool's prism, or the tool's first point inside the piece's material, `sequence::inside_piece`, exact by the winding number of a flat's loops and by the ring sector of a bend) is at least `clearance` (default the document's tolerance) at every stroke angle from 0 to θ, between the **punch** and every piece but `b`'s strip and its two flats, and between the **die counted lowered** (moved away from the punch along `−y`) by `clearance` plus the tolerance and every piece but `b`'s strip. The lowering is what tells resting from going in, for any die: the sheet lies on the die's top (its outer skin on it, from the punch's side, distance 0), which the lowered die then measures as the clearance and the tolerance, so it clears; a piece that goes into the die by more than the tolerance, or comes nearer than the clearance from any side but the punch's, does not (review 2026-10-28: the die counted every piece lying on it beside the bend as a collision, so a channel narrower than the die, or a tray's flanges on edges that meet, could not be bent in any order). Not checked, and said so in the result (`unchecked`): `b`'s own strip during its stroke (rolled up to `ρ` and flat beyond, on the child: not one of the stage's pieces), and `b`'s two flats against the punch (its tip forms the bend between them, so a sharp or square punch tip would always count as touching them; telling the forming contact from a flat closing onto the punch's flank, an acute bend with a wider punch, needs where the sheet leaves the tip, which depends on the bending method). A step's distance is measured at `θ j/8` and proven between two neighbours `h` apart when both are at least `clearance + v h/2`, `v` how fast any point of the part can move against the tools per radian of stroke (`stroke_speed`): relative to its parent the child turns about the axis by `ρ` after a shift back across the strip by `ρ r` (`r = R + K T`, the joint's motion), and the tools' frame turns about the same axis by `ρ/2`, so a point carried by the parent moves at `d/2` and one carried by the child at most `d/2 + r`, `d` its distance from the axis; a parent point keeps `d`, a child point's `d` is that of `x − ρ r û` (the turn keeps it), convex in `ρ`, so at most its value at `ρ = 0` or `θ`. So `v = d_max/2 + r` with `d_max` the farthest hull point of any checked piece (`sequence::piece_points`: points whose convex hull holds the piece's material, so the farthest of them bounds every point, a distance from a line being convex; a piece with none is an error, never a reach of 0) from the axis at the two ends of the stroke (review 2026-10-28: bounded by the pattern's diagonal, which is false for a pattern that folds out past itself: a hairpin reaches 190 from an axis with a diagonal of 119). A piece resting on the die starts only the lowering away from it, so no speed bound proves the stroke's first interval for it: for a die that lies wholly below its top (`y ≤ 0`, as the punch's side of every die does) the **rest proof** does instead. A piece whose lowest point stays above `−tolerance` (the die's top before it is lowered) over `[a, c]` keeps at least the clearance from the lowered die, whatever the die's shape below its top. The lowest point is the least of the height, a linear function, over the piece's material (`sequence::piece_lowest`, exact, no mesh slack: a flat's outline `T` thick at its segments' ends, an arc or circle also at the one angle where the height's gradient points into it, a spline at its control points, a lower bound; a strip's slab at its corners; a built bend's ring sector at the ends of its inner and outer arcs or at that angle on either, the height being linear along the radius and the length; a piece the part does not have is an error, never a height of +∞). Review 2026-10-29: the hull points stood for the material, and a flat's were its outline's box in the pattern's axes, whose corners lie past a bend line that does not run along an axis, below the die's top as soon as the stroke begins (`(R + T)(1 − cos ρ/2) − s sin(ρ/2) < 0` for a corner `s` past the tangent line), so a triangle's or a turned rectangle's flange was never proven; and a bend's outer arc was held by its tangent apexes, below its skin, so a skin touching the die's top along an inner angle was not proven either. Each height is that of a material point whose height is smooth in `ρ` with second derivative at most `w = d_max/4 + r` (a parent point accelerates `d/4` against the tools, a child point also by `r`, from differentiating the motion twice), so over `[a, c]` it is at least the lower of its heights at the ends less `w h²/8`, and where that is not enough the interval is halved (while both ends stay above `−tolerance`, at most 40 times; each height counts in the work). Quadratic in `h`, this proves a piece lifting off the die (its height grows like `ρ` or `ρ²`) in a few halvings. At a sampled angle too, a piece the distance cannot prove clear of the lowered die (a made bend's skin lying along the die's top, where the mesh's lower bound falls short of the lowering) is proven by its lowest point's height (at least `−tolerance`: then at least the clearance away). An interval that does not hold is halved, to `θ/1024`; one still not proven fails the step as *not proven* (fail closed, never a pass). Per tool a step reports `least` (the least distance proven at the angles measured), `at` (its angle) and `proven` (the least over the whole stroke: each interval's `min(la, lc) − v h/2`), a true lower bound.
- **Strokes and segmented tools** (2026-10-29). Bends on one line are made in one stroke: the same parent flat, the same signed angle (within the angular tolerance), the same radius and neutral fibre `K T` (within the tolerance), and the tangent lines on one line running the same way (both ends within the tolerance of the first's line; `one_line`). Then both turn about one axis by one motion, so the stage, the frame, the speed bound and the rest proof are the first bend's, and the pieces left out are every bend's of the stroke. By default the tools are **one piece** per stroke, centred on the middle of its bends (from the least to the most of their ends along the line) and as long as `length` or else twice the farthest a piece's hull point lies along the axis from that middle, so they reach past the whole part: this fails closed, since a tool as long as the part meets everything a shorter one would. **Segmented tooling** is asked for (`segmented: true`, or `length` as a list of one per flange, null for the bend's own): the tools are then a segment per bend of the stroke, centred on its middle and as long as given or else the bend itself, set up for each stroke; each step lists its segments' lengths (`segments`), and `unchecked` says which tooling was assumed. Per-bend lengths were the default for a day (sheetmetal-r6); review 2026-10-29 put the whole-part default back, so a tray's second bend meets the wall standing past its end unless segmented tooling is asked for (`a_tray_corner_clears_its_first_bend_and_its_second_with_a_short_punch`), and a saved `bendable` without `length` keeps its meaning (`a_saved_bendable_without_length_keeps_its_meaning`). A step's work and distances are per tool piece (the least over them). **Strokes searched** (review 2026-10-29): with one-piece tools the search's strokes are exactly the line groups; with segmented tooling each group of more than one bend is tried together and every bend also alone (`ranked_strokes`, the larger stroke first on a tie), since a segment set up at one bend leaves the others flat: forcing the group could miss the only order (0 blocked once X stands, X once 1 stands: only 0, X, 1; `segmented_tooling_does_not_force_a_stroke`) and say "no order" for certain. A collision listing gives each orientation's `certain` and the step's `stepCertain`.
- **An order given.** `order` lists the strokes to check, each a flange or a list of flanges on one line (refused when they are not, or a flange is listed twice or left out; with one-piece tools also when a stroke is not the whole of its line's bends, since a punch along the line bends them all at once: review 2026-10-29, a split order checked a step with a tab flat that the brake had bent, so `feasible` could be wrong); every step is checked in that order (all of them, so each that does not clear is listed), with no search. The search's and the check's cap on distances and heights measured is `maxWork` (default 20000, at most 10⁷); past it the result is an error that says how to go on (give the order, or a larger cap).
- **The order** is searched depth first over all orders (any bend may be made at any time: the tools see both legs alike), at each step the bends whose flanges carry the fewest others first, then the shorter flange, then the lower index; every step tried is remembered by its set made, and a set from which no order completes is not tried again. The result is the first order whose every step clears, with each step's distances per tool (above), and every step that did not clear: by bend, by the bends made before it, by tool, by piece and by the angle where it failed (or the interval no halving proved).

**Oracles** (`crates/engine/tests/sheetmetal_bendsequence.rs`, three scales): a U channel with walls 20 high clears a straight punch 10 wide, its second step's least distance the first wall's inner top corner to the punch's flank at the end of the stroke, `(R + W − H)/√2 − p` (the corner at `((R + W) cos(ρ/2) − H sin(ρ/2), (R + W) sin(ρ/2) + H cos(ρ/2))` in the bend's frame); with walls 40 high the corner reaches the flank at `ρ = 2 (atan2(R + W, H) − asin(p/√((R + W)² + H²)))` (81.4 degrees), before the bend is done, in either order: no order, both collisions named; a gooseneck punch whose throat is open towards the standing wall clears, its second step's least distance the corner to the throat's flank, `(R + W − H)/√2 + p` (5.7071). **Proven, not found** (measures-r1, 2026-10-29). A step that does not clear fails *for certain* when a tool and a piece were found nearer than the clearance at a real pair of places (the distance found, which no true distance exceeds) or wholly inside one another; a collision only not proven clear (the proven lower bound under the clearance, the distance found above it) and an interval of the stroke no halving proved are not certain. At a stroke angle a certain collision is reported before a doubtful one (the other pieces are still looked at when the first that fails is doubtful). Every step tried is in the result with `certain`, and with no order `infeasibleProven` says whether every step tried that failed failed for certain: since the search tries every step reachable through steps that clear, only then does no order exist; otherwise one may exist through a doubtful step.

**The part turned end for end** (measures-r3, 2026-10-29). The tools' `x` is `y × z` with `z` along the bend line from its start to its end, so the tools keep one handedness against the part; an asymmetric tool (a gooseneck) would stand the same way at every bend. A press brake lets the operator turn the part about the brake's vertical (the tools' `y`) between bends, which carries `(x, z)` to `(−x, −z)`; the tools are extruded symmetrically along the bend about its middle, so against them that is the tools mirrored in `x` (`mirrored`, the points reversed to keep the polygon's sense). So a step is checked as the part lies and, when that does not clear, turned (`Check::step`, `turn_or`): it clears when either clears (`turned` in the step), and fails for certain only when both fail for certain, otherwise the failure reported is the one not certain, so `infeasibleProven` and the measure's no need both ways to collide (review 2026-10-29: a U channel 40 high with the gooseneck mirrored was a certain no, while turned it clears by the gooseneck's closed form, `a_mirrored_gooseneck_clears_with_the_part_turned_end_for_end`). Tools that are their own mirror image (`symmetric`: the mirrored polygon is the same cyclic list of points, from any first point and either way round, −0 equal to 0) are checked once; the same SET of points is not enough (review 2026-10-29: `[[-2,0],[2,0],[1,2],[-1,2],[0,1]]` has a symmetric set of corners but is not symmetric, and was never turned). Turning moves the tools' polygons only: segmented tooling is set up at the bends either way, and one-piece tools are centred on the stroke. A step is listed with each orientation's failure (`turned` per collision), `certain` saying whether the step fails for certain (both orientations). Each step may be turned independently; turning costs one more stroke check for a step that does not clear as it lies.

**As an assertion: `bendable`** (`bendseq_cmds::take`). `{measure: "bendable", of, punch, die, length, clearance, maxWork, is}`: `of` the sheet-metal parts as the other sheet-metal measures take them (default all switched on), the tools as the command takes them with each coordinate a number in the document's unit or a length expression of the variables, a table's row (a Jig's tool table: `punches[tool].half`) and the outputs, `length` and `clearance` length expressions. Yes when an order is found for every part (the orders in the details), no when for one of them none exists for certain (`infeasibleProven`), otherwise **not proven** (verification.md › *Not proven by the measure itself*) with the doubtful step in words, or when the search used up its work: `maxWork` distances and heights (at most and by default the command's 20000), shared by the parts in order, each part's search given what the ones before it left. A part with a hole across a bend, a tool polygon that crosses itself, a clearance not above 0 are the assertion's errors. Not a number: the order is no value an output can hold, so there is no `bendOrder`; it is in the assertion's details (`parts[i].order`). **Cost**: an assertion measures it again only when what it depends on changes (the part's body, the tools' values, the bounds), but then the whole search. On a tray 60 × 40 of four flanges 10 high (`the_cost_of_bendable_on_a_tray_of_four_flanges`, the Check profile, optimised with debug information): tools 30 long, an order after 2653 distances in 1.6 s; tools 200 long, none (the box corner) after 7359 in 1.2 s. A Jig that checks a range of points pays that per point; `maxWork` bounds it, and an answer cut short is notProven, never passed.

**Holes across a bend** (2026-10-31; refused until then). A strip not yet bent is its slab whole, holes and all: more material than the part has, so a clearance proven to it is proven, while a collision found with it may lie in a hole and is never *certain*; a point found inside a flat by its outline (which knows no hole that crosses its tangent line) is not certain either (`Failure::Collision::real`). Which strips and flats that applies to is decided by boxes (`sheetmetal::cut_pieces`: those whose box in the pattern a cut's box meets, within the tolerance; a box holds its curve, so one left out has no hole across a bend and its slab or outline is its material exactly). Held by `crates/engine/tests/sheetmetal_holed_strips.rs`: a punch whose bar lies in the other flange's strip collides for certain without the slots and is notProven with them, never `false`, while a plain punch is proven clear of the whole slabs; and a rod whose end lies in a hole across a bend already made (formed) is a certain collision without the slot and notProven with it (review 2026-10-31: the made bend's ring sector, which knows no hole, had been left out of the narrowing, a false no). The flats and the bends as built are their faces, holes cut. So `bendable` on a lid with vents across its bends is yes or notProven, never a false no.

**Past the bend line** (2026-11-02; owed from the Jigs until then). Material of the parent past the bend's tangent line beside the bend's ends (a rack panel's ears beside an inset flange, the other wall's extension at a closed corner) goes down into the die's opening as the leg rises, while the rest of the parent lies on the die's shoulder: the rest proof, which needs a piece's lowest point over its whole material above the die's top, could not prove the stroke's first interval for it, and halving stopped at θ/1024 (`notProven` over the first 0.088 degrees). Such a piece is now **cut at its bend line** into two halves, each rigid: for a piece the parent's side carries, by the plane through the bend's axis square to the parent's flat (its tangent line); for one a child of the stroke carries, by the plane square to the child's flat through its far tangent line. The half behind the line rises from the die, the half past it goes into the opening. Each half is kept apart from each **convex piece of the die** (the die's outline cut into triangles by clipping ears, `sequence::triangles`; at most 200 points, and only when the outline is simple, `sequence::simple`: no two sides meet but neighbours at their corner, orientations within `10⁻¹²` of the sides' squared size counting as touching, so an arc's tangent apex that crosses another side leaves the die uncut (review of 4116ed3e); else this proof is not tried, not proven; each half against each triangle counts as one in `maxWork`, so the cap bounds this proof's time too) along a direction of its own: the tools' `y` or the outward normal of a side of the triangle; how far apart along it is the least of the direction over the half's material (`sequence::piece_lowest_within`: the outline clipped by the plane, its pieces at their ends and where they cross the plane, an arc also at its one stationary angle in the half, a spline over its control points, at both skins; a bend's ring sector clipped along its axis; exact when the plane is square to the flat or to the bend's axis, otherwise the half widened by the plane's lean across the thickness, a lower bound) less the most of it over the triangle, lowered as the die is counted. Separation along a direction in the tools' section bounds the distance to the die's prism from below, whatever its length. Over `[a, c]` each half's least along a fixed direction of the tools' frame is at least the lower of its values at the ends less `w h²/8` (the rest proof's bound, `w` the stroke's bound on any point's acceleration against the tools), and the interval is halved while the values at its ends hold. So a step is proven when every half clears every triangle along one of its directions by the clearance; tried after the rest proof when that fails, at a sampled angle and over an interval, so a part the rest proof proved is proven as before, bit for bit. It holds for any die outline, not only one below its top. Held by `crates/engine/tests/sheetmetal_rest_proofs.rs` (three scales, both failed before with `notProven` over `[0, 0.088]` degrees): a tray closed by overlap bent with one-piece tools over a V die 16 wide at its top clears each first bend; over a V only 1 either side the covered wall's extension, `R − g` past the line, still clears, and the covering wall's, `R + T` past it, goes into the die's shoulder, a certain collision naming it; a rack panel's ears 5 past the line beside an inset flange clear in the wide V, and go into the narrow one for certain, naming the base. The bounds in `crates/kernel/tests/sheetmetal_sequence.rs`: a D cut across its arch by a plane square to it against the closed form, the two halves' less the whole's, leaning by `λ` the closed form at `c − λT`; a bend cut along its length; a V die cut into `n − 2` counter-clockwise triangles adding up to its area, a bow tie refused.

**Arcs in the tools** (2026-11-02; until then a punch's round tip was drawn as a polygon, conservative only when drawn outside the arc). A tool's outline is its corners and, between two of them (or after the last, for the side back to the first), an optional `{"through": [x, y]}`: that side is the circular arc through the point and the side's ends (`sequence::Tool`, `circle_through`; refused when the point lies in a line with the ends, within a millionth of their spread's square, or when a `through` starts the list or follows another; two corners need both sides arcs). The prism is extruded from the exact lines and arcs (a cylinder's face per arc), so the distances (`verify::distance`, proven below by the mesh less what it can be off by, as for a bend's skins), the inside tests (`contains_points` on the exact body) and the tool's corner are those of the outline itself, not of a polygon round it. The rest proof's die below its top takes an arc's top (`Tool::top`: the circle's top where the arc passes it); the proof past the bend line cuts into triangles a polygon that holds the die (`Tool::enclosing`: an arc that bulges out by its ends and its tangents' meeting points over pieces of at most 90 degrees, one that bulges in by its chord), a superset, so a separation from it is one from the die. Turning the part end for end mirrors each arc's point with its corners, and a tool is its own mirror image when its cyclic list of corners and sides is. Oracles (`crates/engine/tests/sheetmetal_tool_arcs.rs`, three scales): a rod punch of radius `a` about the axis (two corners, both sides arcs) on the U channel keeps `√(40² + R²) − a` at both bends, from the other strip's near edge at the first and from the standing wall's bend at the second (it curls outwards from its tangent line), at every stroke angle (a disc about the axis keeps its distance to what turns about it), while the same rod drawn as an octagon round the circle comes nearer, at most by `a (1/cos(π/8) − 1)`; a rack panel's ears clear a V die whose shoulders are arcs tangent to its top and flanks; bad arcs are errors naming the reason, in the command and in `bendable`. The outline's bounds (`crates/kernel/tests/sheetmetal_sequence.rs`): the circle through three points, a round tip's arc counter-clockwise as the outline runs, its tangents' meeting points in the enclosing polygon, whose triangles add up to the flanks' rectangle and the square under the tip, a die's bump's top `c + r`, a notch held by its chord, an asymmetric arc not its own mirror image and mirrored twice itself.

**A hem pressed flat** (2026-11-02; until then an open hem was checked as one air bend to 180 degrees, which no V die makes, or listed as `formed`). `hem: {flanges, preBend, pad}` names flanges bent 180 degrees (within the angular tolerance) that are pressed flat: each is a stroke of its own (with one-piece tools no other bend may lie on its line, which the punch would bend with it; in an order given, alone), made in **two strokes**, listed as two steps (`stage` `pre-bend` and `flatten`). The **pre-bend** is an air bend from 0 to `preBend` with the punch and die, checked as every step is (`Check::step_until`: the stage, the frame, the speed bound and the rest proofs of a stroke that ends there). The **flatten** stroke takes the hem from `preBend` to 180 degrees between a **flat die**, the half space under the parent's outer skin (the parent lies on it, still), and the **pad**, an outline as the tools' (lines and arcs) in the plane square to the bend with its body above its `y = 0`, standing at every angle on the highest point of the hem's flat (`piece_lowest` of the height turned over, exact for lines and arcs; a hem whose flat has a spline in its outline is refused, as its highest point over the control points bounds it from above and would stand the pad too high: review of 4116ed3e). Its frame is the **parent's**, not the bisector's: origin on the parent's outer skin under the hem's axis at the bend's middle, `y` the parent's normal towards the inside of the hem, `z` the axis. Checked: the pad by the mesh distance (`Check::near`, as the punch) against every piece but the hem's flat and strip (what it presses); the flat die against every piece but the strip by its lowest point (at least `−tolerance`: then at least the clearance from the die counted lowered, as the rest proof has it; exact but for a spline's control points, so a piece below it is a certain collision). Between angles: in this frame only the hem's side moves, turning about the axis at rate 1 after the shift back across the strip, so a point of it moves at most `d + r` per radian and accelerates at most `d + 2r` (`d` its distance from the axis, convex in the angle, so the farthest hull point of the hem's side at either end of the stroke bounds it; `r = R + K T`); the pad moves as the flat's highest point, at most `d + r`. So per tool and piece: the pad against the parent's side at most `d + r` (`h/2` of it lost between angles), against the hem's side twice that; the die's heights of the parent's side are constant, the hem's side's lose `(d + 2r) h²/8`; halved to 1/1024 of the stroke, else not proven. Turning the part end for end mirrors the pad (unless it is its own mirror image), each stroke independently. Oracles (`crates/engine/tests/sheetmetal_hems.rs`, three scales): an L (a base 8 × 30, a wall 20 up, `R = T = 1`) with a hem 8 long on the wall's tip, pre-bent to 120 degrees, pressed by a pad 39 wide: hem first, the pad's least distance is `2(R + T) − T = 2R + T` at 180 degrees (the hem's flat's top over the wall's top skin), the flat die's the lowering, and the wall then clears, the order the search finds too; wall first, the pad's side keeps `L + R − P` = 1.5 from the standing base's inside face at every angle, and a pad 50 wide meets the base for certain; bad `hem` objects are errors naming the reason. A closed hem (radius 0) is still the sharp bend refused (*Limits*); the open hem keeps the radius the pad leaves.

**Formed before the press brake** (`formed`, 2026-10-31). A louvre is made on a punch press with a louvre tool and a jog by an offset die in one hit, not by air bending: checked as bends, a louvre's parent beside its ends dips into any die wider than its slot (until 2026-11-02, when the rest proof past the bend line was built, *Past the bend line*: a reason the chassis formed them then; they are formed on a punch press anyway), and a jog's two bends 0.6 apart cannot both lie on a die. `formed` lists such flanges: they are made from the start (every stage has them made), are no step of the order and are not checked themselves, which `unchecked` says. The chassis tray: its eight louvres and four jog bends formed, its tabs, walls and hem then bend in the order `[8, 9], [10, 11], 2, 0, 1, 3` with segmented tooling and a die 6 wide (16 wide, the die meets the jog below a tab's bend: a real collision). The measure takes `formed` and `order` for one part; with an order given its work cap is 10⁶ (the coordinator's decision, 2026-10-31: the measure runs on the assertions' one job thread and in the web build's one thread; the command keeps 10⁷), at about 2500 distances a second on the chassis tray some minutes, while a search stays at 20000. The chassis's given order takes 26108.

**Not looked for:** a bend's own strip, and its own two flats against the punch (above, listed in the result); the bend's shape during an air bend (the model bends at the final radius from the start, the rest of the strip flat); the tools' own travel and the back gauge.

### Names

The folded bottom skin's faces are `F9:face(0)` (the base), `F9:face(2n − 1)` (the bend of the flange of id `n`), `F9:face(2n)` (its flat). Its **edges are named by what they are** (`sheetmetal::edge_numbers`, from the edge's `EdgeKey` and each flat's `SegId`s), not by the faces round them: a bend's tangent lines `F9:edge(7, b, 0)` (its parent's side) and `F9:edge(7, b, 1)`, its arcs `F9:edge(8, b, end)`, a piece of a base curve `F9:edge(1, curve, piece, part)` (`part` 0 for the whole edge, else `from · 2³² + to` by the ids of the bends at the piece's ends: *Inset flanges*), a relief's `F9:edge(2, a, b, j)`, a bend relief's `F9:edge(11, b, end, j)`, a side of a flange's rectangle `F9:edge(3, b, side, part)` (1 at its bend's start, 2 the tip, 3 at its end), a curve of its own outline `F9:edge(4, b, curve, piece)`, a hole placed in a flat `F9:edge(5, f, hole, segment)`, a piece a closed corner's extension adds `F9:edge(6, f, segment)`, the pieces of holes across a bend `F9:edge(9, …)` and `F9:edge(10, …)`. In all of them **a flange is numbered by its id** (`Flange::id`, a document field since format 49): `b`, `a` the id less 1, `f` 0 for the base or the flange's id for its flat (`Part::flange_number`, `Part::flat_number`), never by its place in the list. The part's faces are the plate's of that skin (`regen::plate::plate_names_with`, shared with `feature.plate`): the top skin `F9:copy(0)/F9:face(k)`, the bottom skin `F9:copy(1)/F9:face(k)`, an end `F9:rim(h)/F9:face(k)` with `h` the hash of its edge's name. So a flange added later changes no name but the rim along the one edge it is bent from (named by the faces round them, as until 2026-10-28, adding a flange on a wall's side renamed every rim of the base, the wall and its bend: `names_stay_when_another_flange_is_added`, engine), and deleting, inserting or moving a flange in the list changes no name but those of that flange and of the edge pieces it splits or joins (rule 4; numbered by the flanges' places until 2026-10-29, deleting an earlier flange gave every later flange's names to the flange after it: `every_name_stays_in_place_when_an_earlier_flange_is_deleted_or_inserted`, engine, every kind of name at three scales).

Why `id − 1` and not the id: a part made with ids 1, 2, … in order (every part before format 49, and every part whose list was only appended to) is named exactly as when the names followed the places, so a file older than 49 keeps the meaning of every face name, and of every name that is not an edge's or a rim's, with no warning (its edges' and rims' names changed in 49 for another reason and are warned about: `migrate::sheet_metal_edge_names`). Ids are at most 2³¹ − 1 (`MAX_FLANGE_ID`, checked when a part is made or edited and when a file is read), so a face's number stays below 2³², above which the further pieces a hole across a bend leaves are counted (`face(k + j · 2³²)`); a part whose ids are used up refuses a new flange rather than take an id saving would refuse.

Not names, and so not on ids: a flange's `tip` and `side` and a corner's `flanges` and `over` name flanges by their places in the list the command is given and stored with it (the list is replaced as a whole, so they are always read against the list they were written with); corners kept by an edit that sends the flanges but no corners are carried to the new places through the ids, and refused, naming the corner, when one of their flanges is gone (review 2026-10-29: kept by place, a moved flange gave a corner's treatment to another corner that also meets: `a_corner_follows_its_flanges_when_they_move_in_the_list`). Storing them by id would make that carrying unneeded; it is not done, as the file keeps places; the pieces a hole across a bend cuts (`edge(10, cut, …)`) are numbered by the cut-outs' order, and a hole of a flange's own outline (`edge(5, f, hole, …)`) by its place in that outline's sketch: no change to the list of flanges moves them.
## The rules this adds, and why each is general

- *A step fails for certain when the distance found (between real points) is under the clearance, or a tool and a piece lie inside one another; `bendable` is false only when every step tried failed for certain*: the distance found is an upper bound on the true one for any geometry, and the search tries every step reachable through steps that clear, so the rule is sound for any part and tools; anything less is notProven.
- *`bendable`'s work is shared by the parts in the order named*: a bound on one assertion's cost, whatever the parts.
- *A bend's end is inside its edge when it is not the edge's end (exactly: an inset within the tolerance is set to the edge's end); there a bend relief is the corner relief's notch at the tangent line's end between the edge running on and the parent's side, plus the line back to the tangent line's end; the edge beside it must keep `SLIVER_TOLERANCES` tolerances*: the concave corner exists exactly where the edge runs on past the bend, for any edge of any flat (outer loop, hole, tip, side), and the notch is the one corner relief's construction at a right angle, so it holds for every shape; the sliver floor is the one every inset already keeps.
- *Bends are made in one stroke when they share the parent, the signed angle (angular tolerance), the radius and `K T` (tolerance) and lie on one line running the same way (ends within the tolerance)*: then their joints' motions are the same motion about the same axis (the joint's motion depends on nothing else), so one stage, frame and speed bound hold for all of them; anything else is two strokes.
- *The tools are one piece reaching past the whole part unless segmented tooling is asked for (`segmented`, or a `length` per flange); then a stroke's tools are one segment per bend, centred on its middle, as long as given or as the bend*: the default fails closed (a tool as long as the part meets everything a shorter one would, and more), and the result's `unchecked` states the tooling assumed; segmented tooling is set up for the bend, and the distance to a union of prisms is the least over them, so every bound of a step holds per segment.

- *A cut-out belongs to the flat whose outline, holes and the cut-out are one profile with one hole more and the flat's outer area (eight tolerances times the root of the area)*: the sketch's own profile detection at the document's tolerance, the same test as the reliefs, for lines, arcs, circles and splines alike; the flats tried are those whose box meets the cut-out's (a box holds its curve, so no flat that could hold it is skipped).
- *The base is the profile the base-edge flanges sit on*: the flanges say which; with none, one profile is required, never guessed by size.
- *A cut-out sketch is in the base's plane when the normals are parallel within the angular tolerance and every point lies within the tolerance of the plane*: the document's tolerances decide coplanarity; the opposite normal mirrors the axes, handled by running each arc the other way.
- *A part switched off (suppressed, `when` false, rolled back) is left out of a measure that names no part or names parts by a query*; *one named by its feature is refused with that reason*: a query names what fits the design as it stands; a name is a demand.

- *A joint's motion `Rot(C(ρ), −ρ) ∘ Shift(−|ρ| r û)`, `C = P + sign(ρ) r (û × D)`*: rolling a strip of width `|ρ| r` onto a cylinder of radius `r` tangent to the sheet at the strip's start, for any `r ≥ 0` and either sign; `r = 0` is the crease. Continuous with a continuous velocity through ρ = 0.
- *A corner where two flanged edges meet: the loop's left turn by more than the angular tolerance (cross product of the unit directions)*: the document's tolerance decides what is straight; a right turn or straight on is refused (the strips overlap or continue each other), never guessed.
- *A relief's notch from `a = p + r eₐ` to `b = p + r e_b` (parallelogram, arc about `p`, the circle tangent at `a` and `b`)*: defined for every convex corner angle; a tear at an angle within 1e-9 of 180 degrees (the tangent circle's centre at infinity) is refused.
- *A bend's radius within the tolerance of 0 is 0, a sharp bend: the skins at `R = 0` and `T`, its sector joined to the plates of the pieces the sheet falls into*: the closed forms of every bend hold at `R = 0` (allowance, deduction, volume), so nothing about it is a convention; the joining shares the plates' own edges and vertices, each found within the tolerance of its closed form or the part refused, and the body is checked.
- *K in [0, 1], θ in (0, π]*: the neutral fibre lies in the material; 0 is no bend and beyond π the flange passes through the base.
- *A flange's flat more than the tolerance long; a mould-line length below 180 degrees by the angular tolerance*: what is left of a length measured to the mould line must be a flat; at 180 degrees the outside surfaces are parallel.
- *The outline chained at points within the tolerance*: the pieces are made from the same arithmetic, so they meet exactly; the tolerance only decides what is one point.
- *The developed allowance agrees to a few tolerances*: the development's layout converges within half the tolerance and its outline splines are held to a quarter (developable.md); the tests allow four tolerances.
- *A spline hole's distance searched to an eighth of the tolerance, with the bounds carried to the assertion*: the subdivision's own guarantee (boxes bound the curve, flat pieces are within the share of their chord); the work limit, if reached, leaves the lower bound at 0 and the upper bound infinite (a piece left unrefined bounds nothing: its chord's distance is neither above nor below the curve's), so neither `min` nor `max` is proven by it.
- *The pattern's outline through the profile detection, and its area within eight tolerances times the root of the area*: the sketch's own test of crossing and touching curves at the document's tolerance, for lines, arcs, circles and splines alike; the area slack is the one `reliefs_fit` uses (the region's area against the pieces' sum, both exact for lines and arcs).
- *Reliefs checked against the outline*: the base's outline with its notches must still be one profile with the same holes and the outline's area (the sketch's own profile detection); otherwise refused by name. A tear relief's radius `r tan(α/2)` has no bound as α nears 180 degrees, so this is what refuses it there.
- In the plate (`plate.rs`, found here): *a traced curve's first stations at most 0.1 rad of its tangent's turn apart (summed over 64 samples), at least the 16 (4 past the ends) of before, at most an eighth of the station cap*: the spline through the first stations is then near any curve everywhere, whatever its length of arc; a 270-degree arc's rim never settled from 16 stations (*Findings*).

- *A cut-out that fits no flat is a hole across a bend when the pattern's outline, its holes, the earlier cuts and it are one profile with one hole more and the area less by its own (eight tolerances times the root of the area)*: the same profile-detection test as a hole of one flat, on the whole pattern.
- *A cut crosses a tangent line where its signed distance changes sign by more than the tolerance (lines), at the circle's two roots inside the arc and more than the tolerance from its ends (arcs and circles), or at a vertex within the tolerance of the line whose pieces either side lie on opposite sides; within the tolerance of the circle's foot it touches (refused); the crossing counts only more than the tolerance inside the tangent line's ends*: the document's tolerance decides what is on the line, for any line and arc; the side of a piece is its middle's, after splitting at every crossing, so no piece reaches across the line.
- *A piece of a cut is in the cell its middle is strictly inside (a strip's rectangle, else a flat's outline by its winding number); none or two fail closed*: cells do not overlap (*The pattern is one sheet*) and a split piece touches no tangent line but at its ends.
- *Faces traced by the vertices pieces share, by what the vertex is, not by distance*: every piece is made from the same crossing points, so the topology needs no tolerance.
- *A strip's piece is wrapped at `φ = s/(R + K T)`; along the bend (|Δs| within a thousandth of the tolerance) a line, across it (|Δu| so) an arc, else a cubic Hermite spline with the image's tangents, steps halved until within a quarter of the tolerance at their middles (at most 4096)*: the image of a line along or across a cylinder's development is exactly a line or a circle; the share is the plate's.
- *A later cut's edge is the later feature's when three of its points lie more than twice the tolerance from the pattern's own curves; a hole is carried when its loops on the two skins lie within four tolerances of each other both ways*: the skins are laid flat exactly by the inverse motions; the slack covers a quarter-tolerance fit on each skin and the boolean's own tolerance.

- *An inset within the tolerance of 0 is 0, else at least 4 tolerances; bends on one edge at least 4 tolerances apart along it; the insets leave at least 4 tolerances of the edge* (`SLIVER_TOLERANCES`): the document's tolerance decides what is one point, and a piece of an edge only a few tolerances long is a rim face the plate, the self-intersection check and the tessellator cannot tell from a degenerate one (review 2026-10-28), so it is refused, never snapped (snapping it would move the bend by more than the tolerance without saying so). The 4 itself is a chosen margin, not derived from anything: some floor of a few tolerances is needed, and 4 is the one picked. Two strips closer than that would lie along each other in the pattern, which its check refuses anyway.
- *A piece of a split edge is named by the ids of the flanges whose bends are at its two ends; a flange's id is kept when given, or when the flange sent equals one of the part's in every field (its `on` through the ids of the flanges it names), else it is new and never a used one*: what a piece is (the stretch of the edge between two bends, or an end of the edge and a bend) does not depend on any other flange, nor on the list's order, so the name changes exactly when the piece does (rule 4); a flange equal in every field to one of the part's is laid out the same, so keeping its id cannot move a name, and one that is not keeps no name.
- *A bend step clears when the punch's proven distance to every piece but the bend's strip and its two flats, and the die's lowered by the clearance and the tolerance to every piece but the bend's strip, are at least the clearance over the stroke, proven between samples by the stroke speed bound, halved to θ/1024, else not proven*: the bound `d_max/2 + R + K T` holds for any part (the joint's motion and the tools' half turn, *Bend sequence*; `d_max` from the actual pieces at both ends of the stroke, where a convex distance takes its largest value), so a pass is a proof at every angle, and what cannot be proven fails; lowering the die along the punch's direction separates exactly the contact from that side (the sheet resting on it) for any die shape, and lets nothing go in deeper than the tolerance; the rest proof holds for any die below its top and any piece (a height's second derivative bounded from the joint's motion, a smooth function's least over an interval bounded by its ends and that bound); what is not checked is said in the result.
- *A stage's pieces are rigid*: a made bend has its final shape and moves with its parent, a strip not yet bent lies flat on its parent, which holds for any tree of bends; the bend under way is exempt.
- *Edges named by what they are (`EdgeKey` and `SegId`), not by the faces round them*: what an edge is follows from how it was made (rule 4), which adding a flange elsewhere does not change; the faces round an edge and what lies near it do.
- *A corner is where two flanges reach the outer loop's shared point (inset 0 at that end, no outline)*: only there do the two strips meet as the corner's relief and closing assume; everywhere else the pattern check decides.
- *An outline's line on the edge: both ends within the tolerance of the edge's line, running against it, inside its ends within the tolerance; every other piece not below −tolerance across (exact for lines, arcs, circles; a spline by its control points, a bound that may refuse a spline that would fit)*: the flat must lie beyond its bend for the strip to join it along one line.
- *A side's flange grows away from its flat; a flange on a hole's edge into the hole*: both are the loop's own direction with the flat on the left, the rule every flange already follows.
- *Every name of the part that belongs to a flange is numbered by its id (`id − 1` for the flange, `id` for its flat), ids from 1 to 2³¹ − 1*: the id is what the document keeps for the flange through any change of the list, so no name depends on a place; `id − 1` makes a part with ids in order name as by places (older files keep their names' meaning); the bound keeps `2 · id` below the 2³² the further pieces of a face are counted from, checked where an id is made (the command, `Document::add_feature`) and where it is read (`validate`).
- *A piece cut at its bend line, each half kept apart from each triangle of the die along the tools' `y` or a side's outward normal, by the half's least along it (clipped exactly where the plane is square to the flat or the bend's axis, widened by its lean otherwise) less the triangle's most*: the triangles' union is the die, a separation along a unit direction of the section bounds the distance between prisms along the axis from below, each half is rigid over the stroke (the planes are fixed on the parent and on the child), and the least of an affine function over a clipped outline lies at the pieces' ends, crossings and one stationary angle of an arc, so the proof holds for any outline, die and part, and fails closed (not tried) where the die cannot be cut.
- *A piece's lowest point over its material: a linear height's least on a segment at its ends, on an arc at its ends or the one angle where the gradient points into the circle, on a spline over its control points, on a bend's ring sector at its arcs' ends or that angle*: a linear function's minimum over a convex set lies on its extreme points, and an arc's at its end or its one stationary point; the spline's control hull holds it (a lower bound, safe). A flat's hull points from its outline (segment ends, arcs in pieces of at most 90 degrees with their tangents' meeting points, splines' control points) hold any outline whatever its axes.

## Limits and what is owed

- **The format.** `feature.sheetMetal` is format 41 (its fixture, a box with a Z flange and tear reliefs, written by the build on the Strix, `crates/format/tests/fixtures/versions/v41.*`); `cutouts` and profiles beside the base are format 42 (a panel with a hole in a flange and a vent switched by `when`, `v42.*`); `corner`, `corners` and choices by expression are format 44 (a tray closed by a choice, one corner its own way, its relief's shape by an expression, `v44.*`). A flange's `side`, `inset_start`, `inset_end`, `outline` and `id` (with the part's `next_flange_id`; both written only when not what a file without them means: a flange's place plus 1, and past every id) are format 49 (its fixture, a part with an inset flange, a tab on a flange's side, an arch with an outline of its own and a louver, its ids not its places, written on the Strix, `v49.*`). `bend_relief` is format 51 (its fixture, a tray closed by overlap with two screw tabs on a wall's tip and rect bend reliefs, its `relief` and `cornerGap` assertions passing, and a rail whose outlined leg carries a lip on its far line (`line`) with round bend reliefs at both bends' ends, `v52.*`). `curved_flanges` and `max_strain` are format 68 (a half-round plate's curved flange and a collar round a hole, `v68.*`).
- **Flanges sit on straight edges**: of the base's outer loop or its holes, of a flange's tip or sides, inset or across the whole edge, as a rectangle or with an outline of its own (*Inset flanges, outlines, sides and holes*). Also on an edge of a cut-out in any flat (*Flanges on a cut-out's edge*). Closing a corner of an outlined flange, and at the end of a flange whose side there carries a flange, is built (2026-11-02, *Closed corners*); owed: an outline with no piece between its sides (a triangle) or a curved neighbour, and a side's flange at an end slanted across the wall.
- **Names of the pieces a cut leaves a flat or a bend in** (`face(k + j·2³²)`) are stable when a flange is added or a cut lands in another flat or bend, but `j` is the order of the pieces inside their own flat or bend: a second cut in the same one, or a slot moved so its pieces swap, renumbers them (owed: a piece named by the cut edges that bound it).
- **A sharp bend** (radius 0) is built (*Sharp bends*, 2026-11-03). A formed louvre's hood within about the thickness of a sharp bend's inner skin is refused although it may not touch it (its bound is the footprint's box grown by the thickness; owed: the hood's own surfaces). Refused with the reason: at 180 degrees (a closed hem), at a K-factor whose allowance `θ K T` is within a sliver of 0, two sharp flanges at an open corner without a relief, a sharp bend trimmed by a closed corner, a hole over a sharp bend other than straight across it (a hole's edge meeting the axis along a stretch, owed; two sectors left meeting only on the axis, not buildable), a sharp jog; a later cut across a sharp bend is not carried back onto the export (owed).
- **Curved bends** are built on arcs and circles of the base, their flat pattern approximated with the strain it implies proven (*Curved bends*, 2026-11-04); along a spline edge (a canal surface) and with a radius of 0 they are refused, owed. A conical bend (a straight bend line whose radius changes along it) is developable and could be built by a strip of varying width; not built.
- **Reflex corners** between two flanges that reach them are refused, open or closed, because they cannot be cut from one sheet (*Reflex corners* below); convex corners close (*Closed corners*); walls that lean towards each other are trimmed (*Trimming a bend*; a plane that cuts one skin of a bend and not the other, or reaches the parent's tangent line, is refused), and corners between a flange and a tip flange (on the side of a flange) do not arise.
- **Closed corners' ends** lie on their planes: square for walls square to each other, bevelled otherwise (a mitre always). The flat pattern is the bottom skin's outline; a laser cuts square, so a bevelled end is cut to the bottom skin and finished, or a mitre's gap chosen to take that up (the export does not mark bevels).
- **Reliefs** are per part, the same at every corner, and the bend relief the same at every end of a bend inside its edge (*Bend reliefs*, a notch cut into a closed corner's extension too).
- **Pieces that are not neighbours** (2026-11-04, review of 6e1f3a30). The kernel proves clear only what lies beside a corner (*Closed corners* › *What a wall carries*), a bend a closed corner's plane cuts (*Trimming a bend*) and a sharp bend's inner skins (*Sharp bends* › *Touching*). Other pairs of pieces that are not neighbours (opposite walls' returns meeting over the base, a return on a return turned through the base) and the formed louvres' hoods are caught only by regeneration's self-intersection check on the mesh ([`regeneration.md`](regeneration.md) › *Never through itself*), which finds what crosses but not what only touches within its resolution. Owed: `pieces_apart` over every pair of pieces that are not neighbours.
- **Holes** are the base sketch's and the `cutouts` sketches' closed profiles, on any flat and across bends (*Holes across a bend*; a spline that crosses a tangent line is refused, owed), and a hole cut later by another feature is carried back onto the exported pattern when it goes through square to the sheet (*A later cut carried back*), not seen by `holeToBend` (owed).
- **A flat pattern that overlaps itself** is refused, naming the pieces (*The pattern is one sheet*); a pattern laid out in pieces (a part cut from several sheets and joined) is not built.
- **Many cut-outs** cost a profile detection per cut-out and flat whose boxes meet, each over the flat's holes so far: quadratic in the cut-outs of one flat (a perforated panel of thousands of holes); a benchmark of that is owed, and a box tree of the placed holes would make it n log n.
- **Bend sequence and press-brake collision**: built (*Bend sequence*), with what it does not look for listed there; tools are outlines of lines and circular arcs (*Arcs in the tools*). The rest proof past a closed corner and for material past the bend line beside its ends (a rack panel's ears), owed from the Jigs until 2026-11-02, is built (*Past the bend line*).

- **Reflex corners** (sheetmetal-r3, 2026-10-28: proved, not owed). Two flanges that reach a corner `p` where the base's outer loop turns right (by any angle `τ` in (−180°, 0)) need the same part of the sheet, so no closing makes the corner from one sheet. *Open:* near `p` strip A is the rectangle with its corner at `p` spanning `−d_A` (back along its edge) and `out_A`, strip B the one spanning `out_B` and `d_B`; both are 90-degree cones at `p`, and they must lie in the wedge outside the base there, whose angle is `180° + τ < 180°`; two 90-degree cones in a wedge narrower than 180 degrees overlap (measured from `−d_A`, cone A is `[0°, 90°]` and cone B `[90° + τ, 180° + τ]`, which share `−τ` degrees for `τ ≥ −90°` and `180° + τ` below), so the strips overlap in an open set beside `p`, whatever their widths and lengths. *Closed* (mitre, butt, overlap, any gap): for the walls to meet, the flat of A must run on to the plane of wall B for every height `h` of it, so in the pattern flat A's end runs from `p + BA_A out_A` along `out_A`, which for `τ ≥ −90°` lies in cone B (flat B's end likewise in cone A for `τ < −90°`); the trim of each strip to the mitre plane `n·(x − p) = 0` (it passes through `p`, as the outer skins' planes are both `R + T` from their edges) keeps of strip A the points `a ≥ ρ sin(b/ρ)` and of strip B the points `b ≥ ρ sin(a/ρ)` (at a square corner of two quarter bends; `a`, `b` the distances from `p` along `−d_A` and `d_B`, `ρ = R + K T`), so without a gap both keep the corner's diagonal `a = b` (as `ρ sin(a/ρ) < a`), and with any gap the flats still share the square `[max(BA, c), len]²` beyond the strips (`c` the trimmed flat's end, `len` the other edge's length), which is empty only when an edge is shorter than the other flange's bend allowance (a tab narrower than about `2T`). So the refusal is exact for an open corner and for every closed one but that degenerate tab (not built: it would need the trimmed bend below, to be cut from one sheet only with a mitre gap). The error says so and gives the way out: inset the flanges at the corner far enough that their pieces keep apart (the pattern check decides; at a square corner both, since a strip reaching the corner lies along the other edge) or make one wall a part of its own. Held by `flanges_that_reach_a_reflex_corner_are_refused_with_the_reason` (kernel, every closing kind, three scales, and the inset way out built).
- **Trimming a bend**: built 2026-11-02 (*Trimming a bend* in the Method), with what it refuses listed there; owed: the reflex tab (a seat shortened by a trim) and steps across the thickness (a plane cutting one skin of a bend only).

- **Forming, jogs across a flat, hems pressed flat** (found by the chassis, 2026-10-31). A louvre here is a tab bent out of a slot (a lanced-and-bent vent): a formed louvre (lanced on one side and stretched into a hood) is not developable: built 2026-11-02 as a forming feature (*Formed louvres*), the hood a parametric tool's shape and its stretch a measure. A jog is made of two opposite flanges on a tip (an edge), or across the middle of a flat (`jogs`, *Jogs across a flat*, 2026-11-02; a hole across a jog's line is cut across its bends, 2026-11-04; a jog across a spline or a circle, and a closed corner of a jogged flange are refused, owed). A closed hem (radius 0, flattened) is the sharp bend refused above; an open hem (a 180-degree flange of the radius the tooling leaves) is built, and the press-brake check bends it as one air-bend stroke, or, given `hem`, as a pre-bend and a flatten stroke between a flat die and a pad (*A hem pressed flat*, 2026-11-02; the flat die is a half space under the parent, so a part whose standing walls reach below it there, such as the chassis tray, keeps the air bend: a flat die of finite outline is owed). A cut-out that a flange sits on and whose sketch is switched off (`when`) fails the part naming the flange (flanges have no `when` of their own).
- **The methods owed for those three** (plan in `plan/sheetmetal-forming.md`, r1-smrelief 2026-10-31). A hem pressed flat: built 2026-11-02 (*A hem pressed flat*). A jog across a flat: built 2026-11-02 (*Jogs across a flat*). A formed louvre: built 2026-11-02 (*Formed louvres*; the developer's decisions of that day: a parametric hood of length, width, height and ends, the lance and the tool's mark in the flat pattern, the hood joined by a boolean after the fold).
- **What the chassis's range check refuses** (`craft3d-cli check-range` over thickness 0.3 to 3, radius 0.2 to 6, K-factor 0 to 1, width and depth 60 to 400, wall 20 to 80, 129 and 265 points, `--deep` on the second: no panic, no invalid body, every refusal an error naming the flange or the sketch). Honest, each saying what to change: a jog whose middle flat would be shorter than nothing (`flanges.4: a flange's length has to be more than the tolerance`, when `2 (R + T/2)(1 − cos α)` exceeds the offset: the radius too large for the jog's angle); slots that overlap or lie past their wall when the depth or the wall is small (the cut-out's sketch names the curves; a louvre whose slot lies across a bend says it lies in no flat laid out before it); a port past the back wall; a closed mitre whose two extensions meet in the corner of the flat pattern at a thin sheet with a small radius and K-factor (now says so and that a larger gap helps: `a_mitre_whose_extensions_meet_in_the_flat_says_so_and_a_larger_gap_builds`); assertions the design's own limits fail (a bend radius below the hem's, a hole nearer than `2.5 t + r`). Those are the design's ranges, not the kernel's: the example's sizes are fixed, the louvre columns and the jog's middle flat depend on the depth and the radius.
- **No unfold of a foreign part**: the flat pattern is the one the part was folded from, measured by the unfold; recognising flats and bends on an imported solid and unfolding it is not built.
