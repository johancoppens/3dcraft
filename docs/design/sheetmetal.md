# Sheet metal

A part bent from one sheet: a flat base with flanges bent along straight edges, each bend with an inner radius `R` and a K-factor `K`, corner reliefs where two bends meet, the flat pattern with its bend allowance, the bend table, and the manufacturability measures as assertion measures. Roadmap: [`../roadmap.md`](../roadmap.md) › *R1* (the sheets block, item 3 of `plan/r1-sheets.md`, built 2026-10-27). Code: `crates/kernel/src/sheetmetal.rs` (the pattern, the fold, the solid, the measures), `crates/kernel/src/origami/solve.rs` (`Joint` with a radius), `crates/regen/src/sheetmetal.rs` (the feature in regeneration), `crates/engine/src/sheetmetal_cmds.rs` (`feature.sheetMetal`, `sheetMetal.edit`, `sheetMetal.bends`, the measures `bendRadius`, `flangeLength`, `holeToBend`, `relief`, `cornerGap`, the flat pattern's export). Guide: [`../guide/09-features.md`](../guide/09-features.md) › *Sheet metal*.

**Laid out in closed form, measured by the unfold.** The flat pattern is laid out by the closed form `BA = θ (R + K T)` (exact) and folded from it; the unfold (`unfold::develop` on each bend's neutral band) only measures it, in the bend table and the tests, and must agree within a few tolerances. It never lays the pattern out (*The flat pattern measured* below).

**One fold, one unfold, one offset.** Nothing here folds, lays flat or offsets by a method of its own: the bends are joints of origami's linkage ([`origami.md`](origami.md) › *One fold mechanism*), the solid is the plate of the folded bottom skin ([`plates.md`](plates.md)), and the bend allowance is measured by laying each bend's neutral band flat with the unfold ([`developable.md`](developable.md)). This follows the design written in [`plates.md`](plates.md) › *Sheet metal* before it was built, with the changes listed under *Findings and history*.

## Method

### The flat pattern first

The part is defined by its flat pattern, in the plane of the base's sketch (its x and y; `z` along its normal, the side a bend `up` turns to; the bottom skin at `z = 0`, the top at `z = T`). The base is the sketch's one closed profile (lines, arcs, circles, splines; holes allowed). A flange sits on a straight edge `s → e` of a flat, with the flat on its left (the outer loop runs counter-clockwise, so this is the loop's own direction), `û` the unit vector to its right (away from the flat): one of the base's outer edges, by its line's entity id, or the *tip* (far edge) of an earlier flange. The pattern grows across the edge by

- the bend's **strip**, `BA = θ (R + K T)` wide: the bend allowance, the length of the neutral fibre over the angle θ (Machinery's Handbook, *Sheet metal work* › *Bend allowance*), the neutral fibre lying `K T` from the inner surface;
- the flange's **flat**, a rectangle as wide as the edge and `L` long when the length is measured from the bend's tangent line, or `L − (R + T) tan(θ/2)` when it is measured from the virtual sharp of the outside surfaces (the outside mould line; the outside setback `(R + T) tan(θ/2)` holds for any θ below 180 degrees: the two outside surfaces' planes meet that far from each tangent line). Its tip, `b0 → b1`, has the flange on its left, so a flange on it grows the same way.

So the flat length of a chain of flats and bends is the sum of the flats and `Σ θᵢ (Rᵢ + Kᵢ T)`, in closed form: the oracle of the L, the U, the Z and the box. The bend deduction (DIN 6935's *Ausgleichswert* with its sign turned) is `2 (R + T) tan(θ/2) − BA`, how much shorter the flat is than the two flanges measured to the virtual sharp; both are reported in the bend table.

### Inset flanges, outlines, sides and holes

A flange sits on a straight edge `s → e` of a flat with the flat on its left: an edge of the base's outer loop or of a **hole** of the base (a hole runs clockwise, so the base is on its left there too and the flange grows into the hole: a tab bent out of a cut-out, a louver), the **tip** of an earlier flange, or a **side** of one (`a0 → b0` at its bend's start, `b1 → a1` at its end: the rectangle's other two edges, each as long as its flat). Every rule below is the same for each, because each is a segment of a flat's loop with the flat on its left.

- **Insets.** The bend spans `s + i₀ d → e − i₁ d` (`d` the edge's unit direction, `i₀`, `i₁` the insets; an inset within the tolerance of 0 is 0, the point the edge's own; one between that and 4 tolerances is refused, a sliver), so several flanges can sit on one edge. When the flats are laid out, every edge flanges sit on is **split** at their bends' ends into the pieces between and the tangent lines (`split_at_seats`), and each bend records its **seat**, the segment of its parent's loop that is its tangent line (`Seat`); an edge whose one flange spans it whole is left as it is, so a part without insets is built bit for bit as before (`sheetmetal_open_pin.rs`). Bends on one edge must stay at least 4 tolerances apart, measured along it: touching, the two strips' ends would lie along each other, and closer the rim between them is a sliver. The pieces of a split edge are named by the bends at their two ends, each by its flange's **id** (`part` = `from · 2³² + to`, `from` 0 at the edge's start or the id of the flange whose bend it follows, `to` 0 at its end or the id of the one it runs to), never by their place along the edge nor in the list: a flange added, deleted or moved, on the edge or anywhere in the list, renames only the piece it splits or joins (review 2026-10-28: numbered along the edge, adding a tab ahead of another gave the old name of the piece past it to the gap between them; numbered by the flanges' places in the list, deleting an earlier flange gave the name of the gap between the first two tabs to the gap between the next two). A flange's id is a document field (`Flange::id`, with the part's `next_flange_id`, format 49): 1, 2, … in order when the part is made, a new flange's the next one never used (a deleted flange's id never comes back), and `sheetMetal.edit` keeps a flange's id when it is given (`id`, as `sheetMetal.bends` lists them) or when the flange sent equals one of the part's in every field (what it sits on read through the ids of the flanges it names); a flange changed without its id is a new flange, so the names of its split edges are lost (an error where used), never given to another piece (*Names*). The fold, the sheet, the cells of holes across a bend and the pattern's outline all read the seat, not a search for the segment.
- **Corners.** A corner of the base (for reliefs and closing) is where two flanges *reach* the point two edges of the outer loop share: the one on the edge arriving there with its end inset 0, the one leaving with its start inset 0, neither with an outline of its own. Anywhere else two bends may still meet at a point (an inset flange beside a flanged edge, a flange on a side beside one on the tip, two flanges on a hole's edges): no relief or closing is made there, and the pattern check below decides whether the pieces can be cut from one sheet.
- **An outline of its own.** The flange's flat is the closed profile of a sketch (holes allowed), drawn in the base's plane as if the bend had no width (on a tip or a side, where the edge lies with every bend between the base and it of no width, so a change of thickness, radius or K-factor moves the outline with its edge: `own_outline`'s `lift`, the sum of those bends' allowances across each; review 2026-10-28, drawn where the edge lies in the pattern, such a change refused it): exactly one of its outer loop's lines lies on the edge's line (both ends within the tolerance of it) running against the edge (the flat on the far side), inside the edge's ends; every other piece lies on the far side of the line (its least distance across, exact for lines, arcs and circles, by the control points for a spline, not below −tolerance). That line is the bend's span (its insets); the outline is moved the bend allowance across and turned to start at it, the line's ends set exactly on the strip's far ends (the neighbours' ends move at most the tolerance). Its holes are holes of the flat. The flat has no tip and no sides (a flange on one, and a corner, are refused: owed is naming a flange by a line of that outline). `flangeLength` is the farthest the outline reaches from the tangent line (now exact on arcs, by `reach_along`).
- **On a side.** The side's flange grows away from the flat (`across` is `−d` of the parent's bend at its start side, `+d` at its end side) and is folded about the side, so a tab wraps round the corner where the parent's bend ends; its strip touches the parent's strip only at that corner's point, which the pattern check accepts as two strips sharing a point. A side carrying a flange, a tip carrying an inset flange, and an outlined flange are not closed at a corner (a closed corner rebuilds the rectangle of a flange to run it on: refused naming the flange).
- **On a hole's edge.** The hole's loop is split at the bend as an outer edge is; in the pattern's outline the hole is chained together with the strip's ends and the flange's flat (it is still one hole), the strip and flat must lie inside the hole (the pattern check), and `holeToBend` leaves out a hole a flange is bent from (its edge is the bend's tangent line, not a hole beside it). A hole across a bend in a part with such a flange is refused (owed: the cells traced through a hole's tangent lines).

**The pattern is one sheet** (below) applies unchanged: inset flanges beside a reflex corner whose flats overlap, a tab in a hole that runs into the hole's far side or whose strip's ends lie along the hole's sides (no insets), are refused naming the flanges. A flange beside a reflex corner must be inset at that corner, or its strip's end lies along the other edge (refused as before).

**Names.** Faces are numbered by the flange's id (`F9:face(2n − 1)`, `F9:face(2n)` for the flange of id `n`), and edges are named by what they are (*Names* below), so a flange added later takes only the rim along the edge it is bent from (that edge is a tangent line now, or split into pieces named by the bends at their ends) and changes no other name.

### Holes and cut-outs on any flat

The base's sketch is drawn in the plane of the flat pattern, so a hole of any flat is drawn where it lies in the pattern. The base is the sketch's closed profile that the flanges on base edges sit on (the only profile when no flange is on an edge; flanges on two profiles are refused); its holes are the base's. Every other closed profile of the sketch, and every closed profile of the sketches listed in `cutouts` (in the same plane, any origin, axes and side: each point carried by the rigid motion between the two frames, an arc of a frame facing the other way run from its carried end to its carried start so it stays counter-clockwise), is a **cut-out**: a hole of the one flat it lies wholly inside in the laid-out pattern. Which flat is decided by the sketch's own profile detection, as reliefs are checked: the flat's outline, its holes so far and the cut-out together must be one profile with one hole more and the flat's outer area (`hole_fits`), tried on every flat whose box meets the cut-out's. One that fits no flat but lies inside the pattern, clear of its edges, its holes and the other cuts (the pattern's outline, the holes and it are one profile with one hole more and the area less by its own), is a **hole across a bend** (*Holes across a bend* below); one that fits neither (past or touching the pattern's edge, in a relief's notch, in or touching another hole) is refused naming its curves and sketch, and so is a cut-out with a profile inside it (a loose island). The holes then go through *The pattern is one sheet* with everything else, and are folded with their flat (a flange's face gets its holes' loops; the plate cuts them through the thickness). A sketch in `cutouts` that is switched off (`when`, suppressed) cuts nothing: a vent can be an option.

A hole cut later by another feature (a boolean subtract on the folded part) is not in the part's own pattern (the pattern is what the part is folded from), but the **export carries it back** when it goes through the thickness square to the sheet (*A later cut carried back* below); the bend table and the export say which feature took the part's body (`changedBy`).

### Holes across a bend

A cut-out that crosses a bend's tangent lines is cut through the bend where the bend allowance puts it (`crates/kernel/src/sheetmetal/cuts.rs`). The tangent lines (each a segment of a flat's outline that a strip continues: the parent's edge, the flange's segment 0) divide the pattern into **cells**, the flats and the strips. Each cut is split where it crosses a tangent line inside it (lines and arcs in closed form; a vertex of the cut on the line is a crossing when the pieces before and after it lie on opposite sides), each crossed tangent line where the cut crosses it, and the line's pieces inside a cut (their middle inside it, by the winding number) are dropped. Every piece of a cut lies in one cell, found by its middle (inside one strip's rectangle, else inside one flat's outline; none or two fail closed). Every piece runs with the material on its left (outlines counter-clockwise, holes and cuts clockwise, a strip using its tangent lines against their flats), so what is left of each cell is traced piece to piece by the vertices they share, by what each vertex is (a flat's corner, a crossing, a cut's corner), exactly and without a tolerance; a loop of positive area is the outside of a face, one of negative area a hole of the face whose outside goes round it. A cell may be left in several faces: a slot across a whole strip leaves the bend in two.

A piece in a flat is carried by the flat's motion. A piece in a strip is **wrapped** onto the bend's bottom skin: a point `u` along the tangent line and `s` across the strip goes to the angle `φ = s / (R + K T)` about the bend's axis, `u` along it (the neutral fibre keeps its length, so `s = BA` is `φ = θ`, where the strip's far tangent line lands). A line along the bend (its `s` constant within a thousandth of the tolerance) stays a line, a line across it (`u` constant) is an arc of the skin's circle, and any other curve's image is a cubic Hermite spline through its points with its tangents (both in closed form: `dP = axis du + r_skin dφ`, `dφ = ds / (R + K T)`), the steps halved until it holds the image within a quarter of the tolerance at their middles. Its end tangents are the image's exactly: a fitted spline's were a little off, which made a smooth crossing (a circle whose centre is on the tangent line, crossing it square) a corner of almost no angle that the plate's rim could not be traced past (found by the test, 2026-10-28). Note that the image is not tangent to the flat's piece where it crosses obliquely: the bottom skin's radius is not the neutral fibre's, so across the strip it is stretched by `r_skin / (R + K T)`; the edge has a real corner there, as the folded part does. The sheet then goes to the plate as before; the rims along a cut are its square ends.

**The volume.** A strip's element `du ds` is the bend's `du dφ (R + K T)`, whose material between the skins `R` and `R + T` is `T (R + T/2) dφ du`: a hole takes out its area on the flats times `T` and its area on the strip times `T (R + T/2) / (R + K T)` (exactly `T` at `K = ½`).

**Names.** The further faces a cell is left in come after all others, named by the face they are a piece of and their place among its pieces, `F9:face(k + j · 2³²)` for the `j`-th further face of `F9:face(k)` (a face's name has one number; review 2026-10-28: numbering them on from the last flange's flat renumbered them when a flange was added, against stable references); the pieces of a crossed tangent line are `EdgeKey::Split`, a cut's pieces `EdgeKey::Cut`. A part without a hole across a bend is built by the direct construction as before, bit for bit (`sheetmetal_open_pin.rs`). With a closed corner the part's open counterpart is built with the cuts where they lie in it too, and without them otherwise (their edges then named as they are).

**Refused, with the reason**: a cut that touches a tangent line without crossing it, runs along it, meets the end of a strip, or crosses within the tolerance of another crossing; a spline that comes within the tolerance of a tangent line (owed: its crossings found by subdivision of its Bézier pieces; a spline wholly inside a strip or a flat is built); a cut that would leave nothing of a cell. `holeToBend` measures the holes of flats; a hole across a bend is not one (it lies on the bend: its distance would be 0).

**Found and fixed (the plate's trace, 2026-10-28).** A round hole of radius 1 whose centre is on the base's tangent line, in a part 1 thick, failed the plate at 1e3 and 1e6 ("the rim could not be traced within a quarter of the tolerance in 4096 points", 0.285 of the share off at every scale); so did the same half circle as a notch of the base's own outline, with no hole across a bend at all, so the cut's construction was not the cause. Traced round by round: a circle's first steps (0.1 rad of turn) leave a cubic interpolant off by about `r · 0.1⁴ / 384`, which for `r` a million tolerances is just the share; the refinement halved only the one or two steps over the share each round, and as the global spline moved, the next ones went just over (2.5276e-4 against the share 2.5e-4 at scale 1e3, the worst step walking along the circle round after round) until the twelve rounds ran out. `plate::trace` now, when its rounds run out, starts again from the first stations and halves, whenever any step is off, every step off by more than half the share (a margin, so a halved run settles together); a trace that settled before is the same bit for bit (`sheetmetal_open_pin.rs`), and what it accepts is unchanged. Held by `a_hole_of_the_thickness_radius_on_the_tangent_line` (both, at 1e-3, 1, 1e3 and 1e6).

### A later cut carried back

`sheetmetal::later_cuts` (`crates/kernel/src/sheetmetal/later.rs`) reads the last body the part went into (the chain of `consumed_by` from the part's feature) and finds the part's skins among its faces by their surfaces (a boolean keeps the surface of a face it trims; `Made::Back` of the plate is the bottom skin, `Made::Front` the top). Every edge of a skin is laid flat: on a flat by the flat's inverse motion (its `z` must be the skin's level, within four tolerances, or the body is not the part with cuts in it: refused), on a bend by the inverse wrap (`u` along the axis, the angle `φ` from the parent's tangent line, `s = φ (R + K T)`; its distance from the axis must be the skin's radius). An edge is the later feature's when three of its points (a quarter, half and three quarters along) do not all lie within twice the tolerance of the pattern's own curves (its outline, holes, cuts and tangent lines). Each such edge laid flat is the simplest of a line, an arc (a circle when it closes) and a cubic spline through its points that holds them and the points half way between within a quarter of the tolerance (sampled at 16 steps, doubled up to 2048). The edges of each skin are chained end to end (ends within eight tolerances) into loops, turned clockwise.

A loop of the bottom skin whose points (each segment's start and its quarter points) all lie within four tolerances of a loop of the top skin, and the other way round, is a hole through the thickness square to the sheet (a drill square to a flat; a cut through a bend whose walls hold the bend's radial lines): carried, a hole of the exported pattern (`laterCuts`, and `flatArea` less its area). A loop whose box meets loops of the other skin of which none matches it is a cut at a slant or bevelled, which has no one outline in the flat: the export is **refused** naming where. A loop on one skin only (a pocket, an emboss, a part joined on by a union) and a chain that does not close (a cut that reaches the part's edge and changes its outline) are **not carried** and listed with the reason in the export's `notCarried`. The oracle: the part drawn with the same holes in a `cutouts` sketch (holes across a bend included) has the drilled part's volume, and the export's flat area loses the holes' closed forms (`crates/engine/tests/sheetmetal_cuts.rs`).

Not built: carrying a notch at the edge (the outline changed), the measures (`holeToBend`) and the bend table reading the later holes (they read the part as built).

### Corners and reliefs

Where two flanged edges of the base meet at a corner `p` (the outer loop turns left there by more than the angular tolerance), the two strips meet at `p` only (each lies on the outer side of its own edge). Without a relief the part keeps that corner: the two bends' ends meet along the vertical line at `p`, a reflex edge of the solid. A **relief** of size `r` cuts the base's corner: both edges, and the flanges on them, end `r` from `p`, and the corner is replaced by a notch from `a = p + r eₐ` to `b = p + r e_b` (`eₐ`, `e_b` the edges' unit directions away from `p`), through the base:

- `rect`: the parallelogram `p, a, a + r e_b, b` (a square at a right-angled corner, area `r²`);
- `round`: the arc of radius `r` about `p` from `a` to `b` (a quarter disc at a right angle, `π r²/4`);
- `tear`: the far arc of the circle tangent to both edges at `a` and `b` (its centre on the bisector, `r / cos(α/2)` from `p`, its radius `r tan(α/2)` for the corner's angle α), so the notch is tangent to the edges: at a right angle the square and three quarters of the disc of radius `r`, `r² + 3π r²/4`.

A relief is general geometry in the pattern (a cut of the base's outline), not a case of the fold. A corner where the loop turns right (a reflex corner: the two flanges need the same part of the sheet, open or closed, proved in *Limits* › *Reflex corners*) or goes straight on (two flanges continuing each other) is refused with the two flanges; a relief that does not fit its edges (an edge shorter than the reliefs at its ends) is refused with the flange.

### Closed corners

A corner where two flanged edges meet is **open** unless closed: each flange ends where its bend does. A **closed** corner keeps the bends apart (the strips and the relief are unchanged; the relief, if any, is the hole where the bends would cross) and runs the two flanges' **flats** on sideways past the bends' ends, along their tangent lines and tips, each to a plane of the folded walls. Let the walls `A` (on the edge arriving at the corner) and `B`, each flat as folded with its inner skin (the bend's inside: the top for a bend up), its outer skin and its unit `inward` normal (outer to inner), and `g` the gap:

- `overlap`: the covering flange `C` runs to the other's outer skin (flush, covering its end); the other `O` to `C`'s inner skin moved `g` inward (its end `g` from `C`'s inside face);
- `butt`: as overlap with `C`'s plane moved `g` inward too (`C` stops `g` short of flush; `g < T`, else it covers nothing);
- `miter` (also written `mitre`): both to the plane through the line where the outer skins meet with normal `out_A − out_B` (the points as far from both outer skins, which holds the line where the inner skins meet too), each moved `g/2` towards its own wall, so the end faces are `g` apart.

`C` is `over` when given (one of the corner's two flanges), else `A`. The extension's end in the pattern is where its plane, carried back by the flat's inverse motion, meets the flat's bottom skin (`z = 0`): its reach along the edge direction `e` from the tangent line's end and from the tip's end is `n·(q − p)/(n·e)` each, so the flat gains the quadrilateral between the two (slanted when the walls are not square to each other). The part's end face lies on the plane (the plate's `Onto` joint along that edge), so with walls square to each other the overlap's and butt's ends are square and the mitre's are bevelled at half the angle between the walls. All of this is the general geometry of two planes and a flat, for any bend angles, radii, K-factors and convex corner angles; the fold places every flat by its bends alone, so the walls are known before the extensions are laid out. The extended flats then go through *The pattern is one sheet* (extensions that overlap in the flat are refused naming the flanges: walls leaning out at 60 degrees close by overlap and butt, but a mitre's two extensions, both growing towards the tips, cross in the flat there), are folded and plated with the rest, and regeneration's self-intersection check refuses extensions that run into each other or into another piece when bent.

Refused, with the reason (`KernelError::SheetMetal`): a gap not more than the tolerance (touching flats would make the part one solid through itself) and a butt's not less than the thickness; flanges bent opposite ways (their walls do not meet); a mitre of parallel walls or with both walls on one side of the halving plane; an end plane along the flange (`|n·e|` not above the angular tolerance); a reach not more than the tolerance at the tangent line or at the tip (the flange would be cut back, not run on: walls that lean towards each other, or a wide corner, 120 degrees, without a relief large enough, where the covered wall already reaches past the cover's inside face; owed: trimming a flange, which would also trim its bend); `corners` naming flanges that do not meet at a corner, listing one twice, or an `over` that is neither of its flanges.

**Choices as expressions.** The corner's `kind` and the relief's `shape` are a name or an expression whose value picks one, 1 for the first (`corner`: 1 open, 2 butt, 3 overlap, 4 miter; `shape`: 1 rect, 2 round, 3 tear), the count a published choice parameter gives (`docs/guide/05-jigs.md`), so a Jig's choice or a table lookup drives them; a value that is not a whole number in range is refused naming the choices. In the file a name is written as itself and an expression as `{"expr": "…"}` (format 44), so an unknown name is still refused on load.

**Names kept.** Closing a corner adds pieces to a flat's outline; the part's edges are named by what they are (*Names* below), and an extension's end is named as the side it replaces (`Flat::was`), a piece an extension adds `F9:edge(6, flat, segment)`. A reference to a tip's or a side's rim keeps its target when a Jig's choice opens or closes the corners (the side's rim is the extension's end then). Tested: every open name present in each closed kind, each rim the same area when closed by overlap, the open names back exactly. (Until 2026-10-28 the part was built a second time with every corner open only to name its edges by the faces round them; naming by what they are made that unneeded.)

**The measure.** `cornerGap` is the least gap at the parts' closed corners, measured on the solid: the least distance (`verify::distance`, with its proven lower bound) between the faces made from the corner's two flats (their skins and the rims along their edges, `sheetmetal::flat_faces`). The tests hold it, and the plain `distance` between those faces by name, to the gap given.

### The pattern is one sheet

The pattern must be cut from one sheet, so no two of its pieces may overlap, touch or lie along each other except where the pattern joins them (a bend's tangent lines, the point two strips share at a corner without relief), whatever the folded part does. This is checked on the laid-out pattern as a whole, before folding: its outline (every flat's and strip's edge that no strip continues, and the holes; the same chains the export draws) goes through the sketch's profile detection (`detect_profiles`), which refuses two curves that cross, touch or overlap away from an end they share and more than two ends at one point, naming the curves; each curve is known by the flat it came from (a strip counts with its flange), so the part fails naming the base or the flanges that meet ("the flat pattern overlaps itself: the base and flange 0 cross or touch in the flat"). What the detection accepts must be one region with all the holes and the area the pieces add up to (`flat_area`), or a piece lies wholly on another and the part fails too; anything else the detection refuses fails it (fail closed). This catches every way the pieces meet without a rule per case: a flange beside a reflex corner whose neighbour has no flange (its flat lies along that edge at 270 degrees, across it beyond), flanges on edges of a concave base that face each other and run into each other in the flat, a flange on a tip that runs back across another piece.

### Folding: bends are joints with a radius

Every flat is a body of a `Linkage` (the base is body 0 and stays in place; flange `i` is body `i + 1`), every bend a joint whose angle is given: `+θ` up (a valley in origami's convention: towards the side the normal points to), `−θ` down. Origami's `Joint` has a **radius** `r` and a unit vector `û` across the strip (from the left body to the right one). Its motion at angle ρ is

`motion(ρ) = Rot(C(ρ), D, −ρ) ∘ Shift(−|ρ| r û)`,  `C(ρ) = P + sign(ρ) r (û × D)`,

the strip's width the neutral fibre takes up at this angle taken back, then the turn about the axis `r` from the strip's start `P` on the side the body turns to. Its velocity (what the solve's derivative needs) is the turn's `−D × (x − C)` plus the turned shift's `−sign(ρ) r Rot(D, −ρ) û`, both carried by the motion before the joint. A crease is the joint with `r = 0` (exactly origami's turn); the motion and its velocity are continuous through ρ = 0, and both one-sided velocities there are the crease's, so the continuation, loop closure and rank of origami are unchanged. For a bend, `P` is where the strip starts on the neutral fibre: `z = T − K T` for a bend up (its inner skin is the top) and `z = K T` for a bend down; so the axis lies at `z = T + R` (up) or `z = −R` (down) above the tangent line, and the neutral fibre's arc from `P` over ρ ends where the far flat's strip edge lands. A sheet-metal part is a tree (no loops), so `fold_linkage` places every flat by the product of the motions along its path; a closed loop of bends (a box whose walls are joined, a tube) would be the same linkage with loops and is not built (the walls are not joined to each other).

### The part: the folded bottom skin, plated

The folded bottom skin is one sheet: per flat the planar face `z = 0` of its outline carried by its motion (lines, arcs, circles and splines of the base as they are), per bend the cylinder about its axis of radius `T + R` (bend up: the bottom skin is outside) or `R` (bend down: inside) over θ, bounded by the two tangent lines (the parent's edge and the child's) and the two arcs at the bend's ends. Every face's normal points into the material (`+z` carried; the cylinder's face reversed for a bend up), the tangent lines are edges shared by a flat and a bend (the faces are tangent there), a corner without relief is one vertex shared by the base and both bends. The sheet passes `topo::check`. The plate of all its faces on the side `out` by `T` with square ends everywhere is the part ([`plates.md`](plates.md) › *The plate*): a bend's skins are exactly the cylinders `R` and `R + T` (an offset of a cylinder is a cylinder), the flats are slabs, the ends are planes square to the sheet. Flanges that run into each other or into the base when folded are found by regeneration's self-intersection check on the solid ([`regeneration.md`](regeneration.md) › *Never through itself*): the feature fails with "flanges that run into each other or into the base when bent".

**Volume.** The model keeps the skins at `R` and `R + T` (as sheet-metal design does), so a bend's volume is `θ w ((R + T)² − R²)/2 = θ w T (R + T/2)` while its strip's area times `T` is `θ w T (R + K T)`: the volume is the flat area times `T` **exactly when `K = ½`**, and less by `θ w T² (½ − K)` per bend otherwise (the material's neutral fibre is where the length is kept, not the middle). The oracle holds both: `A T` at `K = ½` for the L and the box with every relief, and the difference in closed form at `K` = 0, 0.33, 1. (The brief's "volume = flat area × T exactly" is the case `K = ½`; for other K it would claim a conservation the geometric model does not have.)

### The flat pattern measured: one unfold

The pattern the part is folded from is known; what the unfold adds is a measurement of it on the part. For each bend its **neutral band** (the cylinder `R + K T` about the bend's axis over θ, as long as the bend, built exactly as a sheet of one face) is laid flat by `unfold::develop`, and the flat length of its arcs is the strip's width: it must agree with `θ (R + K T)` (to a few tolerances: the development's own error); the bend table reports both (`allowance`, `developed`). The flats are planes, whose development is the motion back. Fold then unfold is the oracle: every folded flat's vertices, carried back by the inverse of its motion, land at `z = 0` on the flat's outline in the pattern, and every developed band is as wide as the pattern's strip. The closed form is used to lay out the pattern and to fold it (it is exact); the development is the independent measurement, run by the bend table and the tests, not by every regeneration (it costs a development per bend).

The pattern's **outline**: the flats' edges that no strip continues, the strips' ends (straight lines across the strip, `BA` long), chained head to tail into one closed chain per piece, with the holes; the **bend lines** are the middles of the strips. `file.export` with `sheetMetal` draws it in the sketch's axes: the outline on the layer `outline` (black, continuous), bend lines up on `bendUp` (blue, dashed), down on `bendDown` (red, dash-dot), the drawing crate's styles as origami's crease pattern uses them, every curve as it is.

### Manufacturability: measures, not a catalogue

The kernel measures, the limits are the caller's (the developer, 2026-10-26: general geometry in, domain data out):

- `bendRadius`: the smallest inner radius of the bends, measured on the solid (the smaller cylinder the plate made of each bend's face);
- `flangeLength`: the shortest flange, its flat from the bend's tangent line on the flange's side to the farthest point of its outline; with `measured: mould` plus the outside setback (from the virtual sharp);
- `holeToBend`: the least distance in the pattern (which is the distance on the part: a flat is rigid) from a hole of a flat (the base's or a flange's) to the tangent line on that flat's side of every bend beside it (a bend that starts from the flat: its parent edge; the flat's own bend: its far tangent line); in closed form for lines, arcs and circles (the critical points of the distance between a segment and each); for a spline searched by subdivision (a piece whose box is further from the line than the best found is left out, the others halved until flat within an eighth of the tolerance), so the true distance is proven within an eighth of the tolerance either way: the measure carries that as its lower and upper bound, and an assertion whose limit lies between them is *not proven*, never passed (`exact: false`, `lower` and `upper` in the detail). The reported distance is the least over the flat pieces' chords, not a distance at a place on the curve. When the search runs out of work the spline proves nothing: its lower bound is 0 and its upper bound infinite, so the hole's bounds are what its other curves prove;
- `relief`: whether every corner where two flanged edges meet has a relief;
- `cornerGap`: the least gap at the closed corners, measured on the solid (*Closed corners*).

**Measures over nothing.** `cornerGap` with no closed corner (every corner open, or chosen open by a Jig) and `holeToBend` with no hole on a flat beside a bend have no value: a least over an empty set is no number, and taking it as a pass would also pass a part that lost its hole by mistake. Each is an error that says what the parts have (*`Plate` has no holes*, *none of the 2 holes of `Plate` lies on a flat beside a bend*, *no corner of `Box` is closed*) and how an assertion written for a design that sometimes has none is switched off: by a condition, `when` (verification.md › *Conditions*). A part's holes are those it was built with (its base sketch's inner loops and its `cutouts`); a hole a later feature cuts into the part's body (a boolean) is not one of them, and the error names the feature the body went on into (2026-10-28).

The minimum radius for a material and a thickness, a die's minimum flange, the hole distance a shop asks for are expressions in the assertions' `min` and `max`, a document table among them (`steel["S235"].minRadius`). There is no material table in 3DCraft.

### Bend sequence

`sheetMetal.bendSequence {feature, punch, die, length, clearance}` (`crates/engine/src/bendseq_cmds.rs`, the stages in `crates/kernel/src/sheetmetal/sequence.rs`; sheetmetal-r3, 2026-10-28) checks whether the part can be bent on a press brake and in which order. It changes nothing.

- **The tools** are closed polygons (lines) in the plane square to a bend, extruded `length` along it and centred on its middle (default: twice the farthest any piece lies along the axis from the bend's middle, so they reach past the whole part; a distance to a prism that reaches past every piece is its distance to the endless prism): the punch's with its tip's centre at the origin and its body along `+y`, the die's with the point under the bend on the outer skin at the origin and its body below. At bend `b` and stroke angle `ρ` both stand in the bend's frame: origin on its axis at the middle of the bend (the die's `R + T` further out), `y` along the bisector of the two flats towards the inside, `(cos ρ − 1) û + sin ρ n` normalised, which is `−sin(ρ/2) û + cos(ρ/2) n` (`û` across the strip, `n` the parent's normal on the side the bend turns to), `z` along the axis, `x = y × z`: both legs rise `ρ/2`, as on a press brake. General geometry: a gooseneck punch or a hemming die is only another polygon.
- **A stage** is the linkage folded with the bends made at their angles, `b` at `ρ` and the rest at 0 (`stage_worlds`: the same `fold` with other angles; the joint's motion at `ρ` places the flange's side as the arc over `ρ` and the rest of the strip flat beyond it). Every piece is rigid: a flat with the bends made on it, a made bend (it moves with its parent), a strip not yet bent (a slab `T` thick over its strip in the pattern, lying on its parent: `strip_slab`). So the part at a stage is the built part's faces moved piece by piece (`final_worlds` to `stage_worlds`), never a part built again; the tool is carried into each piece's built place instead of the piece to the tool (a distance does not change under a rigid motion).
- **A step** (bend `b` with a set made) clears when the proven distance (`verify::distance`, its lower bound; a piece and a tool wholly inside one another count as 0: a point of the piece inside the tool's prism, or the tool's first point inside the piece's material, `sequence::inside_piece`, exact by the winding number of a flat's loops and by the ring sector of a bend) is at least `clearance` (default the document's tolerance) at every stroke angle from 0 to θ, between the **punch** and every piece but `b`'s strip and its two flats, and between the **die counted lowered** (moved away from the punch along `−y`) by `clearance` plus the tolerance and every piece but `b`'s strip. The lowering is what tells resting from going in, for any die: the sheet lies on the die's top (its outer skin on it, from the punch's side, distance 0), which the lowered die then measures as the clearance and the tolerance, so it clears; a piece that goes into the die by more than the tolerance, or comes nearer than the clearance from any side but the punch's, does not (review 2026-10-28: the die counted every piece lying on it beside the bend as a collision, so a channel narrower than the die, or a tray's flanges on edges that meet, could not be bent in any order). Not checked, and said so in the result (`unchecked`): `b`'s own strip during its stroke (rolled up to `ρ` and flat beyond, on the child: not one of the stage's pieces), and `b`'s two flats against the punch (its tip forms the bend between them, so a sharp or square punch tip would always count as touching them; telling the forming contact from a flat closing onto the punch's flank, an acute bend with a wider punch, needs where the sheet leaves the tip, which depends on the bending method). A step's distance is measured at `θ j/8` and proven between two neighbours `h` apart when both are at least `clearance + v h/2`, `v` how fast any point of the part can move against the tools per radian of stroke (`stroke_speed`): relative to its parent the child turns about the axis by `ρ` after a shift back across the strip by `ρ r` (`r = R + K T`, the joint's motion), and the tools' frame turns about the same axis by `ρ/2`, so a point carried by the parent moves at `d/2` and one carried by the child at most `d/2 + r`, `d` its distance from the axis; a parent point keeps `d`, a child point's `d` is that of `x − ρ r û` (the turn keeps it), convex in `ρ`, so at most its value at `ρ = 0` or `θ`. So `v = d_max/2 + r` with `d_max` the farthest hull point of any checked piece (`sequence::piece_points`: points whose convex hull holds the piece's material, so the farthest of them bounds every point, a distance from a line being convex; a piece with none is an error, never a reach of 0) from the axis at the two ends of the stroke (review 2026-10-28: bounded by the pattern's diagonal, which is false for a pattern that folds out past itself: a hairpin reaches 190 from an axis with a diagonal of 119). A piece resting on the die starts only the lowering away from it, so no speed bound proves the stroke's first interval for it: for a die that lies wholly below its top (`y ≤ 0`, as the punch's side of every die does) the **rest proof** does instead. A piece whose lowest point stays above `−tolerance` (the die's top before it is lowered) over `[a, c]` keeps at least the clearance from the lowered die, whatever the die's shape below its top. The lowest point is the least of the height, a linear function, over the piece's material (`sequence::piece_lowest`, exact, no mesh slack: a flat's outline `T` thick at its segments' ends, an arc or circle also at the one angle where the height's gradient points into it, a spline at its control points, a lower bound; a strip's slab at its corners; a built bend's ring sector at the ends of its inner and outer arcs or at that angle on either, the height being linear along the radius and the length; a piece the part does not have is an error, never a height of +∞). Review 2026-10-29: the hull points stood for the material, and a flat's were its outline's box in the pattern's axes, whose corners lie past a bend line that does not run along an axis, below the die's top as soon as the stroke begins (`(R + T)(1 − cos ρ/2) − s sin(ρ/2) < 0` for a corner `s` past the tangent line), so a triangle's or a turned rectangle's flange was never proven; and a bend's outer arc was held by its tangent apexes, below its skin, so a skin touching the die's top along an inner angle was not proven either. Each height is that of a material point whose height is smooth in `ρ` with second derivative at most `w = d_max/4 + r` (a parent point accelerates `d/4` against the tools, a child point also by `r`, from differentiating the motion twice), so over `[a, c]` it is at least the lower of its heights at the ends less `w h²/8`, and where that is not enough the interval is halved (while both ends stay above `−tolerance`, at most 40 times; each height counts in the work). Quadratic in `h`, this proves a piece lifting off the die (its height grows like `ρ` or `ρ²`) in a few halvings. At a sampled angle too, a piece the distance cannot prove clear of the lowered die (a made bend's skin lying along the die's top, where the mesh's lower bound falls short of the lowering) is proven by its lowest point's height (at least `−tolerance`: then at least the clearance away). An interval that does not hold is halved, to `θ/1024`; one still not proven fails the step as *not proven* (fail closed, never a pass). Per tool a step reports `least` (the least distance proven at the angles measured), `at` (its angle) and `proven` (the least over the whole stroke: each interval's `min(la, lc) − v h/2`), a true lower bound.
- **The order** is searched depth first over all orders (any bend may be made at any time: the tools see both legs alike), at each step the bends whose flanges carry the fewest others first, then the shorter flange, then the lower index; every step tried is remembered by its set made, and a set from which no order completes is not tried again. The result is the first order whose every step clears, with each step's distances per tool (above), and every step that did not clear: by bend, by the bends made before it, by tool, by piece and by the angle where it failed (or the interval no halving proved).

**Oracles** (`crates/engine/tests/sheetmetal_bendsequence.rs`, three scales): a U channel with walls 20 high clears a straight punch 10 wide, its second step's least distance the first wall's inner top corner to the punch's flank at the end of the stroke, `(R + W − H)/√2 − p` (the corner at `((R + W) cos(ρ/2) − H sin(ρ/2), (R + W) sin(ρ/2) + H cos(ρ/2))` in the bend's frame); with walls 40 high the corner reaches the flank at `ρ = 2 (atan2(R + W, H) − asin(p/√((R + W)² + H²)))` (81.4 degrees), before the bend is done, in either order: no order, both collisions named; a gooseneck punch whose throat is open towards the standing wall clears, its second step's least distance the corner to the throat's flank, `(R + W − H)/√2 + p` (5.7071). Every step's `proven` lies between the clearance and its `least`. Resting on the die: a channel 10 wide between its tangent lines (the other strip flat on the 30-wide die) clears with a punch 2 wide, its first step's die distance exactly the lowering at `ρ = 0`; a tray's two flanges on edges that meet clear their first bends in either order, and the second collides with the punch on the standing wall (the classic box corner) unless the tools are shorter than the edge (`length` 26 clears); a Z as narrow (one wall up, one down) collides with the die in either order (the made wall goes into it). A die bar a quarter of the thickness thin wholly inside the flats collides at `ρ = 0`. A flange on a slanted edge (a triangle, a rectangle turned 30 degrees, a hexagon) clears, the base proven lying on the die (`a_flange_on_a_slanted_edge_is_proven_lying_on_the_die`). The bounds themselves (`crates/kernel/tests/sheetmetal_sequence.rs`, three scales): a flat's hull points lie in its outline and none past the bend line, on a triangle and a turned rectangle; a bend's lowest point under a height whose level planes touch its outer skin at an inner angle is `−(R + T)` exactly, while its hull's least lies below; an arched flat's lowest point is its arc's in closed form. The speed bound (`bendseq_cmds` unit test, three scales): on the hairpin, every vertex's displacement against the tools between 257 stroke angles is at most `v` per radian, and `d_max` is at least the farthest vertex, which is more than 1.5 times the old diagonal bound. Refused: a part with a hole across a bend (a flat strip with a hole is not built as a piece; owed), a tool polygon that is not one simple outline, a clearance not above 0, more than 20000 distances.

**Not looked for:** a bend's own strip, and its own two flats against the punch (above, listed in the result); the bend's shape during an air bend (the model bends at the final radius from the start, the rest of the strip flat); the tools' own travel and the back gauge.

### Names

The folded bottom skin's faces are `F9:face(0)` (the base), `F9:face(2n − 1)` (the bend of the flange of id `n`), `F9:face(2n)` (its flat). Its **edges are named by what they are** (`sheetmetal::edge_numbers`, from the edge's `EdgeKey` and each flat's `SegId`s), not by the faces round them: a bend's tangent lines `F9:edge(7, b, 0)` (its parent's side) and `F9:edge(7, b, 1)`, its arcs `F9:edge(8, b, end)`, a piece of a base curve `F9:edge(1, curve, piece, part)` (`part` 0 for the whole edge, else `from · 2³² + to` by the ids of the bends at the piece's ends: *Inset flanges*), a relief's `F9:edge(2, a, b, j)`, a side of a flange's rectangle `F9:edge(3, b, side, part)` (1 at its bend's start, 2 the tip, 3 at its end), a curve of its own outline `F9:edge(4, b, curve, piece)`, a hole placed in a flat `F9:edge(5, f, hole, segment)`, a piece a closed corner's extension adds `F9:edge(6, f, segment)`, the pieces of holes across a bend `F9:edge(9, …)` and `F9:edge(10, …)`. In all of them **a flange is numbered by its id** (`Flange::id`, a document field since format 49): `b`, `a` the id less 1, `f` 0 for the base or the flange's id for its flat (`Part::flange_number`, `Part::flat_number`), never by its place in the list. The part's faces are the plate's of that skin (`regen::plate::plate_names_with`, shared with `feature.plate`): the top skin `F9:copy(0)/F9:face(k)`, the bottom skin `F9:copy(1)/F9:face(k)`, an end `F9:rim(h)/F9:face(k)` with `h` the hash of its edge's name. So a flange added later changes no name but the rim along the one edge it is bent from (named by the faces round them, as until 2026-10-28, adding a flange on a wall's side renamed every rim of the base, the wall and its bend: `names_stay_when_another_flange_is_added`, engine), and deleting, inserting or moving a flange in the list changes no name but those of that flange and of the edge pieces it splits or joins (rule 4; numbered by the flanges' places until 2026-10-29, deleting an earlier flange gave every later flange's names to the flange after it: `every_name_stays_in_place_when_an_earlier_flange_is_deleted_or_inserted`, engine, every kind of name at three scales).

Why `id − 1` and not the id: a part made with ids 1, 2, … in order (every part before format 49, and every part whose list was only appended to) is named exactly as when the names followed the places, so a file older than 49 keeps the meaning of every face name, and of every name that is not an edge's or a rim's, with no warning (its edges' and rims' names changed in 49 for another reason and are warned about: `migrate::sheet_metal_edge_names`). Ids are at most 2³¹ − 1 (`MAX_FLANGE_ID`, checked when a part is made or edited and when a file is read), so a face's number stays below 2³², above which the further pieces a hole across a bend leaves are counted (`face(k + j · 2³²)`); a part whose ids are used up refuses a new flange rather than take an id saving would refuse.

Not names, and so not on ids: a flange's `tip` and `side` and a corner's `flanges` and `over` name flanges by their places in the list the command is given and stored with it (the list is replaced as a whole, so they are always read against the list they were written with); corners kept by an edit that sends the flanges but no corners are carried to the new places through the ids, and refused, naming the corner, when one of their flanges is gone (review 2026-10-29: kept by place, a moved flange gave a corner's treatment to another corner that also meets: `a_corner_follows_its_flanges_when_they_move_in_the_list`). Storing them by id would make that carrying unneeded; it is not done, as the file keeps places; the pieces a hole across a bend cuts (`edge(10, cut, …)`) are numbered by the cut-outs' order, and a hole of a flange's own outline (`edge(5, f, hole, …)`) by its place in that outline's sketch: no change to the list of flanges moves them.
## The rules this adds, and why each is general

- *A cut-out belongs to the flat whose outline, holes and the cut-out are one profile with one hole more and the flat's outer area (eight tolerances times the root of the area)*: the sketch's own profile detection at the document's tolerance, the same test as the reliefs, for lines, arcs, circles and splines alike; the flats tried are those whose box meets the cut-out's (a box holds its curve, so no flat that could hold it is skipped).
- *The base is the profile the base-edge flanges sit on*: the flanges say which; with none, one profile is required, never guessed by size.
- *A cut-out sketch is in the base's plane when the normals are parallel within the angular tolerance and every point lies within the tolerance of the plane*: the document's tolerances decide coplanarity; the opposite normal mirrors the axes, handled by running each arc the other way.
- *A part switched off (suppressed, `when` false, rolled back) is left out of a measure that names no part or names parts by a query*; *one named by its feature is refused with that reason*: a query names what fits the design as it stands; a name is a demand.

- *A joint's motion `Rot(C(ρ), −ρ) ∘ Shift(−|ρ| r û)`, `C = P + sign(ρ) r (û × D)`*: rolling a strip of width `|ρ| r` onto a cylinder of radius `r` tangent to the sheet at the strip's start, for any `r ≥ 0` and either sign; `r = 0` is the crease. Continuous with a continuous velocity through ρ = 0.
- *A corner where two flanged edges meet: the loop's left turn by more than the angular tolerance (cross product of the unit directions)*: the document's tolerance decides what is straight; a right turn or straight on is refused (the strips overlap or continue each other), never guessed.
- *A relief's notch from `a = p + r eₐ` to `b = p + r e_b` (parallelogram, arc about `p`, the circle tangent at `a` and `b`)*: defined for every convex corner angle; a tear at an angle within 1e-9 of 180 degrees (the tangent circle's centre at infinity) is refused.
- *A bend's radius more than the tolerance*: a sharp bend's inner skin is an edge, which the plate does not make; refused as unsupported (owed), not rounded silently.
- *K in [0, 1], θ in (0, π]*: the neutral fibre lies in the material; 0 is no bend and beyond π the flange passes through the base.
- *A flange's flat more than the tolerance long; a mould-line length below 180 degrees by the angular tolerance*: what is left of a length measured to the mould line must be a flat; at 180 degrees the outside surfaces are parallel.
- *The outline chained at points within the tolerance*: the pieces are made from the same arithmetic, so they meet exactly; the tolerance only decides what is one point.
- *The developed allowance agrees to a few tolerances*: the development's layout converges within half the tolerance and its outline splines are held to a quarter (developable.md); the tests allow four tolerances.
- *A spline hole's distance searched to an eighth of the tolerance, with the bounds carried to the assertion*: the subdivision's own guarantee (boxes bound the curve, flat pieces are within the share of their chord); the work limit, if reached, leaves the lower bound at 0 and the upper bound infinite (a piece left unrefined bounds nothing: its chord's distance is neither above nor below the curve's), so neither `min` nor `max` is proven by it.
- *The pattern's outline through the profile detection, and its area within eight tolerances times the root of the area*: the sketch's own test of crossing and touching curves at the document's tolerance, for lines, arcs, circles and splines alike; the area slack is the one `reliefs_fit` uses (the region's area against the pieces' sum, both exact for lines and arcs).
- *Reliefs checked against the outline*: the base's outline with its notches must still be one profile with the same holes and the outline's area (the sketch's own profile detection); otherwise refused by name. A tear relief's radius `r tan(α/2)` has no bound as α nears 180 degrees, so this is what refuses it there.
- In the plate (`plate.rs`, found here): *a traced curve's first stations at most 0.1 rad of its tangent's turn apart (summed over 64 samples), at least the 16 (4 past the ends) of before, at most an eighth of the station cap*: the spline through the first stations is then near any curve everywhere, whatever its length of arc; a 270-degree arc's rim never settled from 16 stations (*Findings*).

- *A cut-out that fits no flat is a hole across a bend when the pattern's outline, its holes, the earlier cuts and it are one profile with one hole more and the area less by its own (eight tolerances times the root of the area)*: the same profile-detection test as a hole of one flat, on the whole pattern.
- *A cut crosses a tangent line where its signed distance changes sign by more than the tolerance (lines), at the circle's two roots inside the arc and more than the tolerance from its ends (arcs and circles), or at a vertex within the tolerance of the line whose pieces either side lie on opposite sides; within the tolerance of the circle's foot it touches (refused); the crossing counts only more than the tolerance inside the tangent line's ends*: the document's tolerance decides what is on the line, for any line and arc; the side of a piece is its middle's, after splitting at every crossing, so no piece reaches across the line.
- *A piece of a cut is in the cell its middle is strictly inside (a strip's rectangle, else a flat's outline by its winding number); none or two fail closed*: cells do not overlap (*The pattern is one sheet*) and a split piece touches no tangent line but at its ends.
- *Faces traced by the vertices pieces share, by what the vertex is, not by distance*: every piece is made from the same crossing points, so the topology needs no tolerance.
- *A strip's piece is wrapped at `φ = s/(R + K T)`; along the bend (|Δs| within a thousandth of the tolerance) a line, across it (|Δu| so) an arc, else a cubic Hermite spline with the image's tangents, steps halved until within a quarter of the tolerance at their middles (at most 4096)*: the image of a line along or across a cylinder's development is exactly a line or a circle; the share is the plate's.
- *A later cut's edge is the later feature's when three of its points lie more than twice the tolerance from the pattern's own curves; a hole is carried when its loops on the two skins lie within four tolerances of each other both ways*: the skins are laid flat exactly by the inverse motions; the slack covers a quarter-tolerance fit on each skin and the boolean's own tolerance.

- *An inset within the tolerance of 0 is 0, else at least 4 tolerances; bends on one edge at least 4 tolerances apart along it; the insets leave at least 4 tolerances of the edge* (`SLIVER_TOLERANCES`): the document's tolerance decides what is one point, and a piece of an edge only a few tolerances long is a rim face the plate, the self-intersection check and the tessellator cannot tell from a degenerate one (review 2026-10-28), so it is refused, never snapped (snapping it would move the bend by more than the tolerance without saying so). The 4 itself is a chosen margin, not derived from anything: some floor of a few tolerances is needed, and 4 is the one picked. Two strips closer than that would lie along each other in the pattern, which its check refuses anyway.
- *A piece of a split edge is named by the ids of the flanges whose bends are at its two ends; a flange's id is kept when given, or when the flange sent equals one of the part's in every field (its `on` through the ids of the flanges it names), else it is new and never a used one*: what a piece is (the stretch of the edge between two bends, or an end of the edge and a bend) does not depend on any other flange, nor on the list's order, so the name changes exactly when the piece does (rule 4); a flange equal in every field to one of the part's is laid out the same, so keeping its id cannot move a name, and one that is not keeps no name.
- *A bend step clears when the punch's proven distance to every piece but the bend's strip and its two flats, and the die's lowered by the clearance and the tolerance to every piece but the bend's strip, are at least the clearance over the stroke, proven between samples by the stroke speed bound, halved to θ/1024, else not proven*: the bound `d_max/2 + R + K T` holds for any part (the joint's motion and the tools' half turn, *Bend sequence*; `d_max` from the actual pieces at both ends of the stroke, where a convex distance takes its largest value), so a pass is a proof at every angle, and what cannot be proven fails; lowering the die along the punch's direction separates exactly the contact from that side (the sheet resting on it) for any die shape, and lets nothing go in deeper than the tolerance; the rest proof holds for any die below its top and any piece (a height's second derivative bounded from the joint's motion, a smooth function's least over an interval bounded by its ends and that bound); what is not checked is said in the result.
- *A stage's pieces are rigid*: a made bend has its final shape and moves with its parent, a strip not yet bent lies flat on its parent, which holds for any tree of bends; the bend under way is exempt.
- *Edges named by what they are (`EdgeKey` and `SegId`), not by the faces round them*: what an edge is follows from how it was made (rule 4), which adding a flange elsewhere does not change; the faces round an edge and what lies near it do.
- *A corner is where two flanges reach the outer loop's shared point (inset 0 at that end, no outline)*: only there do the two strips meet as the corner's relief and closing assume; everywhere else the pattern check decides.
- *An outline's line on the edge: both ends within the tolerance of the edge's line, running against it, inside its ends within the tolerance; every other piece not below −tolerance across (exact for lines, arcs, circles; a spline by its control points, a bound that may refuse a spline that would fit)*: the flat must lie beyond its bend for the strip to join it along one line.
- *A side's flange grows away from its flat; a flange on a hole's edge into the hole*: both are the loop's own direction with the flat on the left, the rule every flange already follows.
- *Every name of the part that belongs to a flange is numbered by its id (`id − 1` for the flange, `id` for its flat), ids from 1 to 2³¹ − 1*: the id is what the document keeps for the flange through any change of the list, so no name depends on a place; `id − 1` makes a part with ids in order name as by places (older files keep their names' meaning); the bound keeps `2 · id` below the 2³² the further pieces of a face are counted from, checked where an id is made (the command, `Document::add_feature`) and where it is read (`validate`).
- *A piece's lowest point over its material: a linear height's least on a segment at its ends, on an arc at its ends or the one angle where the gradient points into the circle, on a spline over its control points, on a bend's ring sector at its arcs' ends or that angle*: a linear function's minimum over a convex set lies on its extreme points, and an arc's at its end or its one stationary point; the spline's control hull holds it (a lower bound, safe). A flat's hull points from its outline (segment ends, arcs in pieces of at most 90 degrees with their tangents' meeting points, splines' control points) hold any outline whatever its axes.

## Limits and what is owed

- **The format.** `feature.sheetMetal` is format 41 (its fixture, a box with a Z flange and tear reliefs, written by the build on the Strix, `crates/format/tests/fixtures/versions/v41.*`); `cutouts` and profiles beside the base are format 42 (a panel with a hole in a flange and a vent switched by `when`, `v42.*`); `corner`, `corners` and choices by expression are format 44 (a tray closed by a choice, one corner its own way, its relief's shape by an expression, `v44.*`). A flange's `side`, `inset_start`, `inset_end`, `outline` and `id` (with the part's `next_flange_id`; both written only when not what a file without them means: a flange's place plus 1, and past every id) are format 49 (its fixture, a part with an inset flange, a tab on a flange's side, an arch with an outline of its own and a louver, its ids not its places, written on the Strix, `v49.*`).
- **Flanges sit on straight edges**: of the base's outer loop or its holes, of a flange's tip or sides, inset or across the whole edge, as a rectangle or with an outline of its own (*Inset flanges, outlines, sides and holes*). Not built: a flange on a line of an outlined flange, on an edge of a flange's hole, a bend relief at an inset bend's ends (without one, the parent's edge turns into the bend's end there: a concave corner of 270 degrees in the pattern between the rest of the edge and the strip's end, which folds to a reflex vertical edge of the solid, sound geometry but a corner a press brake tears or bulges; owed: a notch of the relief's shape at each end of the tangent line, `size` deep into the parent), closing a corner of an outlined, side-carrying or tip-inset flange.
- **Names of the pieces a cut leaves a flat or a bend in** (`face(k + j·2³²)`) are stable when a flange is added or a cut lands in another flat or bend, but `j` is the order of the pieces inside their own flat or bend: a second cut in the same one, or a slot moved so its pieces swap, renumbers them (owed: a piece named by the cut edges that bound it).
- **A sharp bend** (radius 0) is refused, by choice: its inner skin would be an edge and its outer a cylinder of radius `T`, which the one offset (the plate of a sheet tangent across its bends) cannot make; building it at some smallest radius instead would be a part other than the one asked for (its allowance and its box change), so the refusal asks for the radius the tooling makes. The general method owed: a bend whose inner skin is the edge (the plate with a crease on the inside, joined by the edge, and the outer skin's cylinder about it), with the K-factor's neutral fibre at `K T` from the crease.
- **Curved bends** (a flange on an arc or a spline edge) are refused: bending a flange along a curved edge is not a bend but a stretch or shrink flange (forming): the bend region is a piece of a torus (an arc edge) or of a canal surface, whose Gaussian curvature is not zero, so it is not developable and has no flat pattern that keeps lengths. The method owed: a formed flange whose flat pattern keeps the neutral fibre's length across the bend (the strip `θ (R + K T)` wide, square to the edge's curve, as the bend allowance does) and reports the stretch along it (the fibre at the flange's tip is longer or shorter than at the edge by `L × angle swept`), with the strain as a measure for the caller's limit. A conical bend (a straight bend line whose radius changes along it) is developable and could be built by the same strip with a varying radius; not built.
- **Reflex corners** between two flanges that reach them are refused, open or closed, because they cannot be cut from one sheet (*Reflex corners* below); convex corners close (*Closed corners*); walls that lean towards each other so far that closing would cut a flange back are refused (owed: trimming a flange at its tip, and its bend: the method in *Trimming a bend* below), and corners between a flange and a tip flange (on the side of a flange) do not arise.
- **Closed corners' ends** lie on their planes: square for walls square to each other, bevelled otherwise (a mitre always). The flat pattern is the bottom skin's outline; a laser cuts square, so a bevelled end is cut to the bottom skin and finished, or a mitre's gap chosen to take that up (the export does not mark bevels).
- **Reliefs** are per part, the same at every corner; a relief at an inset bend's end (a bend relief) is not built (above).
- **Holes** are the base sketch's and the `cutouts` sketches' closed profiles, on any flat and across bends (*Holes across a bend*; a spline that crosses a tangent line is refused, owed), and a hole cut later by another feature is carried back onto the exported pattern when it goes through square to the sheet (*A later cut carried back*), not seen by `holeToBend` (owed).
- **A flat pattern that overlaps itself** is refused, naming the pieces (*The pattern is one sheet*); a pattern laid out in pieces (a part cut from several sheets and joined) is not built.
- **Many cut-outs** cost a profile detection per cut-out and flat whose boxes meet, each over the flat's holes so far: quadratic in the cut-outs of one flat (a perforated panel of thousands of holes); a benchmark of that is owed, and a box tree of the placed holes would make it n log n.
- **Bend sequence and press-brake collision**: built (*Bend sequence*), with what it does not look for listed there; tools are polygons of lines (arcs owed: a punch's round tip is drawn as a polygon, which is conservative only when drawn outside the arc).

- **Reflex corners** (sheetmetal-r3, 2026-10-28: proved, not owed). Two flanges that reach a corner `p` where the base's outer loop turns right (by any angle `τ` in (−180°, 0)) need the same part of the sheet, so no closing makes the corner from one sheet. *Open:* near `p` strip A is the rectangle with its corner at `p` spanning `−d_A` (back along its edge) and `out_A`, strip B the one spanning `out_B` and `d_B`; both are 90-degree cones at `p`, and they must lie in the wedge outside the base there, whose angle is `180° + τ < 180°`; two 90-degree cones in a wedge narrower than 180 degrees overlap (measured from `−d_A`, cone A is `[0°, 90°]` and cone B `[90° + τ, 180° + τ]`, which share `−τ` degrees for `τ ≥ −90°` and `180° + τ` below), so the strips overlap in an open set beside `p`, whatever their widths and lengths. *Closed* (mitre, butt, overlap, any gap): for the walls to meet, the flat of A must run on to the plane of wall B for every height `h` of it, so in the pattern flat A's end runs from `p + BA_A out_A` along `out_A`, which for `τ ≥ −90°` lies in cone B (flat B's end likewise in cone A for `τ < −90°`); the trim of each strip to the mitre plane `n·(x − p) = 0` (it passes through `p`, as the outer skins' planes are both `R + T` from their edges) keeps of strip A the points `a ≥ ρ sin(b/ρ)` and of strip B the points `b ≥ ρ sin(a/ρ)` (at a square corner of two quarter bends; `a`, `b` the distances from `p` along `−d_A` and `d_B`, `ρ = R + K T`), so without a gap both keep the corner's diagonal `a = b` (as `ρ sin(a/ρ) < a`), and with any gap the flats still share the square `[max(BA, c), len]²` beyond the strips (`c` the trimmed flat's end, `len` the other edge's length), which is empty only when an edge is shorter than the other flange's bend allowance (a tab narrower than about `2T`). So the refusal is exact for an open corner and for every closed one but that degenerate tab (not built: it would need the trimmed bend below, to be cut from one sheet only with a mitre gap). The error says so and gives the way out: inset the flanges at the corner far enough that their pieces keep apart (the pattern check decides; at a square corner both, since a strip reaching the corner lies along the other edge) or make one wall a part of its own. Held by `flanges_that_reach_a_reflex_corner_are_refused_with_the_reason` (kernel, every closing kind, three scales, and the inset way out built).
- **Trimming a bend** (owed for walls that lean in at a convex corner, and the tab above): a plane `n·(P − q) = 0` cuts the neutral cylinder (axis point `C`, direction `D`, radius `ρ = R + K T`, angle `φ` from the parent's tangent line, `e₁`, `e₂` square to `D`) along `u(φ) = (n·(q − C) − ρ (n·e₁ cos φ + n·e₂ sin φ)) / (n·D)`, so the strip's end in the flat pattern is the curve `u(s/ρ)` over `s` in `[0, BA]` (a sinusoid, fitted by a spline within a quarter of the tolerance, refused where `n·D` is within the angular tolerance of 0: the plane along the bend), the bend's face on each skin is bounded by the plane's ellipse arc instead of a circle, the flat's end by the plane's line, and the plate's rims along them are `Onto` joints to the plane (as an extension's end is now). Owed with it: the plate's trace of a rim along an ellipse on a cylinder skin, the cells of holes across a bend whose strip end is a curve, and the closed forms (the volume cut off a bend by a plane through its axis is `θ`-independent: half the ring's cross-section times the length along the axis, which the tests can hold).

- **No unfold of a foreign part**: the flat pattern is the one the part was folded from, measured by the unfold; recognising flats and bends on an imported solid and unfolding it is not built.
