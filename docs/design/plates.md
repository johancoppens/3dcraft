# Plates, edge joints and sheet metal

Parts cut from sheet stock: a plywood panel bent onto a developable hull, a lid of a box, a bent sheet-metal bracket. A *plate* is faces (a sheet, developable or not) given a thickness on a side; *joints* say how two plates meet along an edge they share; *sheet metal* adds bends with an inner radius and a K-factor, and the flat pattern with its bend allowance. Roadmap: [`../roadmap.md`](../roadmap.md) › *R1* (the sheets block, 2026-10-26, items 2 and 3 of `plan/r1-sheets.md`). Code: `crates/kernel/src/plate.rs` (the plate and its joints), `crates/regen/src/plate.rs` (the feature in regeneration), `crates/engine/src/plate_cmds.rs` (`feature.plate`). Guide: [`../guide/09-features.md`](../guide/09-features.md) › *Plates and joints*.

The rule of the block: one fold mechanism and one unfold mechanism in the kernel. Plates do not lay anything flat themselves: the flat pattern of a plate is the development of one of its skins by `feature.unfold` ([`developable.md`](developable.md)); sheet metal folds with origami's linkage and unfolds with the same development (*Sheet metal* below).

## Method

### The plate

A plate of the faces `F` at the levels `lo < hi` along each face's outward normal `N` is the set `{p + s N(p) : p ∈ F, s ∈ [lo, hi]}`. The levels come from the side: `in` is `−t..0` (the faces are the plate's outside: a hull's panels drawn as the hull's outside), `out` is `0..t`, `mid` is `−t/2..t/2`. Its faces:

- **Two skins per face**: the face's surface offset to `hi` (the *front*, facing along `N`) and to `lo` (the *back*, facing against it). Analytic surfaces offset in closed form (a plane to a parallel plane, a cylinder, cone, sphere or torus to another radius); a freeform surface to a B-spline within a quarter of the tolerance of its exact offset, its regularity proven first (`craft3d_geom::offset`: `1 − dκ > 0` for both principal curvatures, by Bernstein bounds), continued past its borders as far as the joints reach. Offsets of a developable are developable (an offset of a ruled surface with a constant tangent plane along each ruling keeps that property), so a plate on a developable is developable on both skins.
- **One rim per boundary edge** (an edge only one face of `F` uses): the surface the plate ends on there, given by the edge's joint (below).
- **Edges inside `F`** become an edge between the skins of the two faces at each level, the shared edge offset along the shared normal. The faces must be tangent there: their normals may differ by at most what keeps the two offsets within a quarter of the tolerance of each other at the larger level (`|ΔN| · max(|lo|, |hi|) ≤ tol/4`). A crease would open the skins on one side and cross them on the other; it is refused, naming the edge and its angle: plate the faces apart and join them with a joint.

**The rims and their corners are found the same way for every joint.** At stations along the edge `C`, in the plane `Π(t)` through `C(t)` square to its tangent, the two points where the rim meets the back and the front skin are each the meeting point of three surfaces (two skins and `Π`, or a skin, `Π` and a plane that holds the rim's line), found by Newton's method from the point the tangent planes give (`kernel::tweak::meet`, the same solver that finds the vertices of the face operations). The stations are refined where the cubic through the traced points leaves the true point by more than a quarter of the tolerance at the middle of a step (up to 4096 points per edge, refused beyond).

| Joint | The rim | Its corners at a station |
|---|---|---|
| `square` (default) | swept by the faces' normal along `C` | `C + lo N`, `C + hi N` |
| `bevel` by `α` | the line through `C` in `Π` turned from `N` by `α` about the tangent, outward at the front for `α > 0` | that line meeting each skin |
| `mitre` | ruled from where the two plates' *away* skins meet to where their *toward* skins meet; the neighbour's mitre runs between the same two curves | `away ∩ away′ ∩ Π`, `toward ∩ toward′ ∩ Π` |
| `touch` | square, through where the *toward* skins meet: the inner edges touch | `toward ∩ toward′ ∩ Π`, and that point's foot on the faces moved to the other level |
| `over` | a piece of the neighbour's *away* skin (flush with it) | `toward ∩ away′ ∩ Π`, `away ∩ away′ ∩ Π` |
| `under` | a piece of the neighbour's *toward* skin (resting on it) | `toward ∩ toward′ ∩ Π`, `away ∩ toward′ ∩ Π` |
| `onto` a surface | a piece of that surface (a world or datum plane, or any face by its name: a stem, a transom) | `skin ∩ surface ∩ Π` for each skin, from where the skin's and the surface's tangent planes meet in `Π` |

*Toward* and *away* say which skin of each plate faces the other plate's material: for this plate the skin toward the neighbour is the front when the neighbour's inward direction `b′` (into its face, square to the edge, tangent to it) points along this plate's normal (`b′ · N > 0`), else the back; the neighbour's toward skin likewise by `b · N′`. This is the one rule that decides every joint; it does not ask which plate is "inside" or which way the faces were drawn. When `b′ · N` or `b · N′` is zero within the angular tolerance the plates continue each other in line along the edge and have no corner to join at (refused); when either changes sign along the edge (the plates turn through in line) the joint is refused at that place.

**Ends on a surface.** An `onto` end lies on a surface the user names (a datum plane through the stem, the stem's own face). Two panels joined by `touch` along a chine whose ends both lie on one surface meet there in one corner: each panel's inner chine edge ends where its toward skin, the other panel's toward skin and the surface meet, which is one point for both (the bow and the stern of a hull meet edge to edge); with square ends they stop apart by about the thickness times the angle between the panels, which the tangent planes give in closed form (the hull test holds both). The surface must cut across the plate: where it holds the plate's inward direction within the angular tolerance it runs along the plate and is refused.

**Joints that are left out.** A joint whose edge, neighbour or end surface is made only by a feature that is off (suppressed, inactive, rolled back) is left out at regeneration, as the face operations leave out faces that are absent (`regen::pass`), so that edge ends square while the feature is off. There is no warning channel on a feature's result; what was built is in `document.inspect` › `followed` (the parameters as built, the joints without the one left out).

Two plates joined with the matching joints (mitre with mitre, touch with touch, over with under) share their joint surface or line exactly: a mitre's two rims are one ruled surface (traced from either side to the same curves within the tolerance), an over/under pair shares the line `toward ∩ away′` and the over plate's rim lies on the under plate's skin, the under plate's rim on the over plate's. So no gap and no overlap, which `document.interference` confirms (touch on a face, never overlap). The kayak's case: hull panels drawn as the hull's outside with side `in`, the chine joints `touch` (inner edges touch, the outside V filled by the builder's epoxy fillet) or `mitre`; the deck `over` the sheer panel, the sheer panel `under` the deck: the deck rests on top of the sheer panel and covers its edge flush with its outside.

**Corners.** At a vertex where two boundary edges meet, the plate's vertex on each skin is where that skin and the two rims meet (`meet`), and the edge across the plate between those two vertices is where the two rims meet, traced in planes square to the chord between the vertices (where the rims say nothing more, one rim or two that continue each other, the chord's plane keeps the point nearest the chord). The rims and the skin edges of each boundary edge are traced past its ends far enough for the corners to lie on them, and that reach is worked out before anything is traced: at each corner the plate's vertex by the tangent planes (each skin's tangent plane meeting the planes of the two rims, each rim's plane through its two corners at the edge's end, found as the Newton steps' start is, and the edge's tangent there), its distance past the edge's end along the edge, and a margin of twice the thicknesses beyond; where the rims' planes do not meet in a point (rims that continue each other) the margin alone. The edge's own curve is continued as itself and its points put on the faces' surfaces continued; the surfaces are continued three times as far as the furthest reach. Nothing is tried again: a corner that the curves still do not reach, or vertices where the skin and the rims do not meet in a point, are refused with the place. Points traced at the middle of a step are kept for the next round when the step stays whole.

**Simplest curves and surfaces.** Each edge is the simplest of a line, a circle and the cubic spline through its traced points that holds every traced point within a quarter of the tolerance; a ruled rim (`square`, `bevel`, `mitre`, `touch`) is the B-spline of degree 1 across between the two traced splines (its rulings the segments between the corners at each station, which is what that rim is), and the plane its control points lie in when they lie in one within a quarter of the tolerance. So a flat plate with straight edges is bounded by planes only, and its volume is exact.

**Never a wrong solid.** The plate passes `craft3d_topo::check`; that no rim crosses another or a skin (a plate thicker than its faces are narrow, a reflex corner whose rims run into the plate, a joint whose reach runs through the plate) is checked on the whole body by regeneration's self-intersection check (`face_ops::not_through_itself`), as for every body built from offsets.

**Names.** The front skin of a face is its copy in instance 0 (`F9:copy(0)/F5:startCap(e1,e2,e3,e4)`), the back skin its copy in instance 1; the rim along an edge is `F9:rim(h)/<face>`, after the face of the sheet the edge bounds, with `h` a 48-bit FNV-1a hash of the edge's name as written. A face's name never lists faces (only an edge's or a vertex's may, `between[…]`, one list deep), and the edge of a developable or a loft is named by its faces, so a rim cannot carry the edge's name itself; the hash stands for it and is as persistent: it changes exactly when the edge's name does. Two edges of one face whose names hash alike (one in 2⁴⁷ per pair) fail the feature rather than share a name. Edges and vertices of the plate are named by their faces. A sketch on a skin is placed as on a thickened layer's (`regen::sketch`).

### Plate or thicken

Both give faces a thickness as a body of their own; they differ in where the layer ends. `feature.thicken` (older than sheets, [`face-operations.md`](face-operations.md)) takes faces of a solid and ends the layer on the solid's neighbouring faces continued, by two offsets of the solid and a boolean: the layer fits the solid it lies on. `feature.plate` takes faces of any body, a sheet above all, and ends the layer on rims of its own, one per boundary edge, square or by a joint with a neighbouring plate. One does not call the other: thicken needs the solid around the faces, which a sheet does not have, and a plate's rims are not a solid's faces. On a solid's faces, use thicken to follow the solid, a plate to cut a panel to its own outline.

### The flat pattern of a plate

`feature.unfold {faces: [a skin of the plate]}` lays a skin flat with the map back to the part ([`developable.md`](developable.md)); its outline follows the joints because the skin is bounded by them: a mitred edge's front and back outlines differ by the mitre, an `over` plate's skin reaches across the neighbour's edge. For plywood the neutral layer is about the middle; a skin's outline differs from the middle's by the plate's mean curvature times half the thickness (zero on a plane, `t/2R` relative across a cylinder). Which outline to cut is the maker's choice; the middle surface of a plate is the face itself for side `mid`.

### Sheet metal (on fold and unfold)

Built 2026-10-27 as [`sheetmetal.md`](sheetmetal.md) describes it, on `feature.fold`'s linkage and the unfold's development; that document has the method as built and what changed from the design below, which is kept as it was written:

- **A bend** is a joint of the linkage whose motion is the turn about its axis after the shift that takes up the strip of the flat pattern the bend uses: with the bend line where the strip starts, `û` across it, the strip `BA(θ) = θ (R + K T)` wide (the bend allowance: inner radius `R`, thickness `T`, K-factor `K` the neutral fibre's distance from the inner surface over `T`; Machinery's Handbook, *Sheet metal* › *Bend allowance*; DIN 6935 gives the same through the bend deduction `v = BA − 2 (R + T) tan(θ/2)` for θ ≤ 90°), the far flat moves by `motion(θ) = Rot(axis, θ) ∘ Shift(−BA(θ) û)`, the axis parallel to the bend line at the height `T + R` above the flat for a bend up and `−R` below it for a bend down (`R` from the inner skin, which is the top for a bend up). Its velocity is the turn's plus the turned shift's, `−(R + K T)` along `û` turned: both closed forms in `θ`. Origami's forest, loop closure, continuation and rank stay as they are: folding a crease pattern and bending a part are one mechanism. (Owed in origami's module: `Joint` with this motion and velocity; today it turns only.)
- **The solid.** Folded, the bottom skin of the part is one sheet whose faces are tangent along the bend lines: each flat's bottom face placed by its linkage motion, and between two flats the cylinder of radius `R + T` (a bend up: the bottom skin is outside) or `R` (a bend down) over the angle `θ`. That sheet plated to the side `out` by `T` is the part, through the plate method above (*Edges inside `F`*: tangent faces make one solid); so a bend's skins are the cylinders `R` and `R + T` exactly and the flats are slabs.
- **The flat pattern** (3D → flat) is not one development: the neutral fibre lies at `(1 − K) T` above the bottom skin in a bend up and at `K T` in a bend down, so for `K ≠ ½` no single sheet of the part is neutral everywhere. Each flat is developed on its own (a plane), each bend's band on its neutral cylinder `R + K T` (r1-develop's `develop` on that cylinder: width `θ (R + K T)` in closed form), and the pieces are laid side by side by the linkage's inverse motions in the plane. Fold then unfold gives back the pattern, unfold then fold the part: the round trip of the oracles; the flat length of a part with straight bends is the sum of its flats and `Σ θᵢ (Rᵢ + Kᵢ T)` in closed form.
- **Flanges** (3D design: a flat added along an edge at an angle with a bend between) are a sheet-metal part grown by one flat and one bend; the flange length is measured from the outside mould line (the virtual sharp) or to the tangent line, as the command says.
- **Corner reliefs**: where two bends meet at a corner, a relief cut (rectangular, round or tear-shaped, of a given width and depth past the bend zone) is a hole in the flat pattern before folding: general geometry in the pattern, not a special case of the fold.
- **Manufacturability, as measures, not a catalogue.** The kernel measures, per bend and per flange, the inner radius, the flange length (from the bend's tangent line), the distance from each hole to the nearest bend's tangent line, and whether a relief is present where two bends meet; as assertion measures (`bendRadius`, `flangeLength`, `holeToBend`, `relief`). The limits (minimum radius for a material and thickness, the die's minimum flange) come in as expressions or document tables (`table.set`), never as a built-in material table.

What this needs from the two mechanisms (said to the coordinator, not built into their modules here): from origami (b) a reader of a plate's outline with arcs and holes and its bend lines (the arrangement of lines and curves), (c) the folded result with thickness and names not keyed to sketch curve ids; from develop, the development of a band on a given offset of its surface (the neutral cylinder `R + K T`, which is a cylinder the kernel builds exactly, so this is `develop` on a face of a body made for the purpose).

## The rules this adds, and why each is general

- *Levels from the side* (`in`, `out`, `mid`): the three placements of material against a surface, along its own normal; nothing about which way the faces were drawn beyond that normal.
- *Toward and away by `b′ · N` and `b · N′`*: true for any two plates meeting at an edge at any angle; zero exactly when they continue each other in line (refused with the reason, since a joint there has no corner); a change of sign along the edge is refused at the place.
- *Stations refined at a quarter of the tolerance; at most 4096 per curve; twelve rounds*: the fit is held to the true curve at the middles of its steps, as every traced curve in the kernel; the caps bound the work and are refused, not guessed past.
- *A crease between faces of one plate when `|ΔN| · max(|lo|, |hi|) > tol/4`*: the face operations' rule for faces tangent only so far (`offset_normal::TURN_SHARE`, with its words `TURN_TOO_FAR`): the gap the two offsets would leave at that level.
- *The reach past an edge's end: where the tangent planes put the corner, plus twice the thicknesses*: the first-order place of the corner, and a margin for the curvature the tangent planes leave out; a corner past it is refused, never guessed, and one within it is exact.
- *The surfaces continued three times as far as the furthest reach; a corner past an edge's end that lands on the border of a skin's parameters refused*: a surface is continued by a length divided by its fastest speed across the border (`offset_face::continued`), so where it is slower it reaches less far; a corner on the border of a continuation is no point of the true surface and is never taken.
- *Traced corners held to a quarter of the tolerance by `meet`, the plate's vertices to half of it*: the rows are fitted to a quarter, so an edge lies within half the tolerance of its faces; a vertex on two fitted skins that are tangent there (each within a quarter of its exact surface) can only be held to the two quarters together.
- *The simplest of line, circle, spline within a quarter of the tolerance; a ruled rim whose control points lie in a plane within that is the plane*: a choice of representation of the same geometry within the share of the tolerance every fit in the kernel has (the loft does the same with `flat_or`).
- *A bevel's angle strictly between −90 and 90 degrees*: at ±90 the rim lies in the skins.
- *An `onto` surface refused where it holds the plate's inward direction within the angular tolerance's sine*: it runs along the plate there and cuts no end.
- *A rim's name by a 48-bit hash of its edge's name; a collision fails the feature*: a face name never lists faces (names.md), so the edge's name cannot be inside it; the hash is a function of that name only.

- *A freeform face's surface continued, per border, by the most any vertex of the face within the reach of that border needs: the border's fastest speed over the speed at the vertex, at most 64* (2026-10-27): the corners of a plate lie past the ends of its edges, at the face's vertices; continuing by the fastest speed (`offset_face::reached_each`) leaves a slower point short, as a corner by a short ruling of a developable beside long ones; a vertex where the surface moves across the border by no more than the tolerance over that parameter's range (a fan's apex) needs nothing there; past a factor of 64 the continuation would be extrapolated far beyond what the surface says, and a corner still not reached is refused.

- *A freeform skin held to a quarter of the tolerance only within the plate's reach of its face, as a length along the surface* (2026-10-27): the continuation past a border is a box of parameters (a tensor-product surface can be continued no other way and keep its parameters), raised by the slowest vertex on that border (above), so where the surface moves fast across the border it reaches far beyond anything of the plate; the offset is fitted over the whole box through samples of the exact offset, but measured and refined only at the points whose distance from the face's domain, along the surface on the straight path in parameters from the domain's nearest point (no shorter than the surface's own distance), is at most twice the furthest corner past an edge's end, plus twice the thickness (`HELD_REACH`): everything of the plate lies within the furthest reach (a corner further out is refused), and the twofold margin is for a path in parameters longer than the way on the surface and for the solvers' steps towards a corner. Beyond it the skin interpolates the exact offset at its samples and is used by nothing (`craft3d_geom::offset::Held`). The margin is made a guarantee: every vertex of a freeform skin and 17 points along each of its edges are put on the face's surface continued and measured from its domain the same way, and a plate with one past the held reach is refused, naming the corner (`held_by`; `a_corner_past_where_the_skin_is_held_is_refused`). One offset fit makes at most 2²⁵ evaluations of the exact offset (about ten seconds in release; with 2¹⁸ samples it could otherwise make about 10⁸) and is refused past that, "did not converge within the budget".
- *A spline edge continued past its ends as the function its end spans are, re-expressed once over the longer domain and evaluated inside it* (2026-10-28, `craft3d_geom::NurbsCurve::extended`, the curve version of `NurbsSurface::extended`): inside its own domain the edge is evaluated as it always was; past it the points are those the spline gives there, but without the rounding of evaluating a short end span many spans out (its basis functions grow as a power of how far out and cancel). The rounding of the new control points stays, fixed: the continuation is one smooth curve, known about as well as the end span's polynomial is (its control points' rounding times the cube of how many end spans out); analytic curves are continued by their closed forms as before.
- *A ruled rim whose two traced rows are each a circle (the simplest-curve test above), about one axis (the second centre on the first's axis and its plane square to it, each within a quarter of the tolerance, the two more than the tolerance apart along it), and every traced corner and the middle of every ruling between them within a quarter of the tolerance of the cylinder (radii equal within that) or the cone through the two circles, is that cylinder or cone* (2026-10-27): the same choice of representation within the same share as the plane is; a square rim round a hole or a slot's arc in a flat plate is the hole's cylinder, a bevelled one a cone, exactly as an extrusion's side is.
- *A circle fitted to a traced run runs the way the run turns about its axis, the sum of the signed angles between consecutive points* (2026-10-27): three points say which way a run goes only while it is less than a turn (*Findings*).

## Oracles (held to)

`crates/kernel/tests/plate.rs`, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6 with the tolerance scaled:

- A flat rectangle's plate on every side: six planar faces, volume `A t` to 1e-12, the centroid at `±t/2` or 0 by side.
- A flat plate with a round hole: `(A − πr²) t` (to 1e-6: the hole's rim is a cubic within a quarter of the tolerance of its cylinder).
- A plate on a cylinder of radius `R` over the angle `θ` and height `h`: `R θ h t` in the middle exactly (the Steiner formula `V = ∫∫ (1 − 2Hs + Ks²) ds dA`, with `K = 0` and levels `±t/2`, so the mean curvature's term cancels: do Carmo §3-3, the parallel surfaces), `θ h ((R + t)² − R²)/2` and `θ h (R² − (R − t)²)/2` to the two sides.
- An L of a floor and a wall sharing an edge, plates of thickness `t` inside the corner: mitre `l (a t − t²/2)` each, the mitre face the plane `x = z` through the shared edge; touch `l (a − t) t`, the corner square left open; over/under `l a t` and `l (b − t) t` (and the other way round); in every case the union of the two plates has the volume of their sum (no overlap) and, but for touch, the L they make `l t (a + b − t)`.
- A bevel by `α`: `A t + l tan α (hi² − lo²)/2` for every side and both signs of `α`.
- The same plate twice is the same body; refusals by reason (no thickness, no faces, a face not there, a neighbour the edge does not run along, plates in line, a bevel past square).

`crates/engine/tests/plate.rs`, at three scales: the L by commands (`sketch.rect`, `feature.fill`, `feature.plate` with every joint), the volumes, `document.interference` saying touch on the mitre face (area `l t √2`) and on the lap (area `l t`), touch along an edge for `touch`, never overlap; a thickness variable followed, `feature.edit` of side and joints, the face names (copies and rims), a save and reopen to the same bodies and names; refusals as errors with the parameter, failed features with the joint.

## Findings and history

- 2026-10-27 (the sea kayak's second finding): a plate on a developable panel was refused, "the joint reaches past the faces' surfaces continued beyond their borders", while the same panel as an extrusion's face was plated: the panel's surface was continued across its rulings by the reach over the fastest speed there, the longest ruling, so by the shortest ruling at the panel's end (14.5 mm against 190) it reached 4 mm where the corner lay 8 mm out. Each border is now continued far enough past every vertex on it (*The rules*); held by `kernel/tests/developable.rs` › `a_plate_on_a_developable_with_short_and_long_rulings_has_its_closed_form_volume` (rulings 10 to 190, the volume `T·Area ∓ T²/2 ∫κ dA` at three scales) and the repro batch (`engine/tests/kayak_findings.rs`).

- 2026-10-27 (r1-kayakgaps2; the sheet-metal enclosure's finding 6 and the rack's 1, 3 and 5): (1) a stadium slot whose end radius is about the thickness (r 1.5 in a 1.5 mm sheet, r 2 in a 3 mm one) broke the plate ("face cannot be tessellated", an invalid body, or the self-check out of work). Each arc's rim and skin edges are traced past the arc's ends by twice the thickness, 2t/r radians each way, so for r/t below about 1.27 the traced run goes more than a whole turn; `circle_through` took the circle's direction from its first, middle and last points, which then lie the other way round, and each edge ran round the wrong half of its circle (the skins' hole loops outside their faces, the rims' loops reversed). A circle now runs the way its points turn (*The rules*). (2) The rim round a circular hole was the ruled spline between its two traced circles, so `select.query` by radius found nothing on it, measures on it were slow (45 s for an area filter) and whether the plate could be measured depended on where it lay; it is now the hole's cylinder (*The rules*), and the slot's arcs' rims are cylinders too. Held by `kernel/tests/plate.rs` › `a_flat_plate_with_a_hole_keeps_the_hole` (one cylinder of the hole's radius, no freeform face, the volume to 1e-12 where a cubic fit gave 1e-6) and `a_narrow_slot_is_cut_by_planes_and_cylinders` (r 1.5 with the thicknesses 1, 1.5 and 2, at three scales: planes and cylinders only, the volume `(A − π r² − 2 r l) t` to 1e-12), and `engine/tests/sheet_findings.rs` (the three repros: built, the deep check clean, the volumes at three placements, the radius query on the plate as on the extrusion).
- 2026-10-27 (r1-kayakgaps2, the sea kayak's repro 14, diagnosed, owed): a plate on a developable hull panel (`feature.developable` between two chines, its `onto` ends on the end planes and `touch` chines) fails as "the offset of the freeform face could not be approximated within the tolerance", while the same panel plated without joints is built in 0.8 s. ## Limits and what is owed

- **Sheet metal** (item 3 of the block) is built (2026-10-27): [`sheetmetal.md`](sheetmetal.md).
- **The format.** `feature.plate` (with its joints, `onto` included) and the role `rim` are format 40 (its fixture, with onto ends on a datum and a world plane, written by the build on the Strix, `crates/format/tests/fixtures/versions/v40.*`).
- A plate whose faces meet at a crease is refused: plate them apart and join them.
- Two plates that touch over a freeform face (an over/under lap of twisted panels) are what they should be (each one's rim lies on the other's skin within the tolerance, checked in the hull oracle), but the boolean refuses their union as coincident freeform faces at the edge of the tolerance ([`booleans.md`](booleans.md)); flat and cylindrical laps unite. The two plates continue the shared surface each in its own way (each continues it as far as its own joints need), so they are the same surface within the tolerance, not the same representation; making them one would let the boolean see it.
- A joint is between two plates along one edge each; three plates meeting along one edge (a T joint) are two joints the user chooses.
- The rim of a closed boundary edge (a hole) under `over` or `under` (a hole whose wall rests on another plate) is traced like any other but not held to an oracle.
- The outline to cut for plywood is a skin's; the middle surface's outline, when the side is not `mid`, needs a sheet at the middle (a plate of `mid` on the same faces gives it).
