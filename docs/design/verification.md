# Design verification

How an agent (or a person) checks a design by asking it questions instead of looking at a picture: the queries `measure.*` and the assertions the document keeps (`assert.*`). Roadmap: [`../roadmap.md`](../roadmap.md) › *V1*. Protocol: [`../control-protocol.md`](../control-protocol.md) › *Verification*. Code: `crates/kernel/src/boolean/probe.rs` (questions asked of one solid), `crates/engine/src/verify.rs` (the geometry), `crates/engine/src/verify_cmds.rs` (the queries and the measures), `crates/engine/src/assert_cmds.rs` (assertions), `crates/doc/src/assertion.rs` (the data), `crates/engine/tests/verification.rs` (the oracles).

Why (the developer, 2026-10-21): "make the plate twice as thick" silently turned the housing's bolt holes into blind holes. An assertion "the bolt holes go through" flags it at the edit that breaks it. V1 is also the measure of the CAD gym: a task's intent written as assertions is what a model is scored against.

## Method

### What is measured: subjects

A measure is taken *of* something named the way `select.query` names things ([`selection.md`](selection.md)): a body by its feature's name or id, a list of them, a query `{"kind", "where"}`, or a bare filter, which gets the measure's own kind (faces for `distance`, `through` and `area`; bodies or faces for `thickness`; bodies for the rest). The engine runs the query through `select_cmds::query`, the same filters `select.query` evaluates, so a subject such as `{"hole": true, "madeBy": "Drill"}` follows the design: an edit that adds a hole adds it to the subject. A face that a boolean split into pieces keeping its name is one item with all its pieces.

A body named by its feature is that feature's own body: once a later feature takes it, the subject is in error (*the body of `Case` went into F6, `Vented`*), the meaning every file written before 2026-10-28 has, and kept. `{"body": name or id, "follow": true}` (`follow` is the default of this form; `false` is the exact meaning) is the newest body that grew from it (`Ctx::grown_from`): the body is followed along `consumed_by` while it went into the next feature as the body that feature changes (the one source of a transform, a fillet, a shell, a pattern; a boolean's target; a union's tool, whose material the union keeps). Following stops, with an error naming the feature, where no single body grew from it: a subtraction's or an intersection's tool (its material is not in the result, or only the part the target shares) and a split (several pieces). The rule reads only the document's feature kinds and the regeneration's `consumed_by`, so it holds for any feature that takes a body, and a kind added later that takes one source is followed without a change. The feature is stored by id like every other subject (`body` is one of the keys `by_id` rewrites), so a rename keeps it; the stored measure is the same JSON text, so no format change.

### The geometry: a mesh first, then the exact faces

Every geometric answer is found coarse on a mesh and settled on the exact B-rep. The mesh of a body (chordal tolerance 1/1000 of its diagonal, at least ten times the document's tolerance, 12° angle criterion, at most 400 000 triangles; coarser by four, up to three times, when over budget) only says *where* to look; the number comes from the exact geometry:

- **`craft3d_kernel::boolean::probe::Probe`** wraps the booleans' own preparation of a solid (each face's outline: its loops on its surface, made when first needed) and answers three questions with the booleans' own tools: `line_hits` (every place a line meets a face, from the exact curve–surface intersection `hits::curve_hits`, kept to the face by `Outline::side`, with whether it is at an edge, tangent, or the line lies on the surface, and the outward normal's sign along the line), `on_face` (a point of a surface inside, on the boundary of, or outside the face) and `place` (a point inside, outside or on the solid, the boolean's ray classification).
- **The nearest point of a face** to a point: the foot on its surface (closed form for the analytic kinds, the surface's own projection for splines) when it lies on the face, else the nearest point of its edges (closed form for lines and circles, kept within each edge's range; a circle's parameter tried a turn either way).

### The queries

- **Distance** (`measure.distance`): pairs of faces in order of their boxes' distance; triangle pairs (point–triangle and segment–segment, after Ericson) while a pair can still beat the best by twice the chord; at most 60 million triangle pairs. The best few face pairs (up to 16) are settled by projecting onto each face in turn (at most 400 steps, until neither point moves by 1e-13 of the coordinates; each projection starts from where the last one on its face landed, and each step that came nearer jumps ahead along its way, twice as far each time while that comes nearer: *Settling on nearly parallel faces*, 2026-10-27), and the least wins: that is a distance between two real points, so no less than the least distance. Projection can stop at a local minimum on a spline. What is *proven* is `atLeast`: per pair of faces the mesh's least distance less how far each face can lie from its triangles (0 for a flat face bounded by lines, r (1 − cos 6°) for a round of radius r held to the mesh's 12° angle, or the sagitta of a chord-long side, the chordal tolerance otherwise), and the boxes' distance for pairs not compared; where that is short of the least found (by more than a quarter of the tolerance, or 1e-12 of the distance) the pair's exact faces are bounded instead (*The proven distance* below), and the better of the two counts. A nearer pair of points met there is taken as the least found.
- **Clearance** (`measure.clearance`): the distance per pair of bodies (`document.interference` only tells clear, touch and overlap, without a distance); within twice the tolerance the kernel's interference (`document.interference`) tells touch from overlap; farther apart, a vertex of each placed in the other tells a body inside another (overlap). An overlap has no clearance (null), and an assertion on it fails with "overlap". `atLeast` as for the distance.
- **Wall thickness** (`measure.thickness`): the ray method. At sampled points of every face (the middles of mesh triangles put on the surface, at most 6000 per body, evenly per face) a line goes into the material along the inward normal; the thickness there is how far it goes before it meets a face (`line_hits`, from four tolerances on, no further than twice the least found so far). Then a bound, proven, below every such line (`atLeast`): per pair of faces a line could leave and come out through (box distance under the least found), the most of their boxes' distance; for two flat faces, none when n_a·n_b ≥ 0 (the line cannot come out there), else the planes' distance when parallel and the heights of each face's corners over the other's plane; for a flat face and a round one, the heights over the plane of only those triangles of the round where the line could come out (n_a·n_b < 0, with the turn of the normal within a triangle as margin), none when there are none, and none for a cylinder whose axis lies along the flat face's normal; coaxial cylinders |r₁ − r₂|; a face with itself: none for a flat face or a hole's wall, 2r for a convex cylinder or sphere, min(2r, 2(R − r)) for a convex torus, 2(R − r) for a concave one, 0 for anything else; otherwise the mesh's distance of the two faces over the triangle pairs where the line could come out, less what each face can be off by. Pairs are taken by their boxes' distance until that is above the bound so far (at most 200 000 pairs, 20 million triangle pairs; past a cap the boxes bound the rest). The places that bound points to (a corner nearest the other face's plane and its foot on that plane, the nearest corners) are tried too, and the least few samples on different faces (8) are then moved along their face by a pattern search (eight directions in the tangent plane, the step halved when none is better, down to a tenth of the tolerance) to where the thickness is least. The least found is a thickness at a real place; the bound is what no place can be below. A line from near an acute edge (a hole entering a round at a slant) is as short as the edge is sharp: the ray method's thickness there tends to 0, which is what it is; measure from chosen faces (`of` a query of faces) to leave such edges out. Measured from a set of faces only those are sampled: the top of a plate gives the plate's thickness although a hole's wall near the edge is thinner.
- **Ray** (`measure.ray`): `line_hits` per body, merged by distance; entering where the outward normal faces against the ray.
- **Goes through** (`measure.through`): for a cylindrical or conical face, its axis, its extent along it and its narrowest circle (a cylinder's radius; a cone's least distance of its edges from the axis); the point of the axis in the middle of that extent must be outside the solid (inside: the face is a boss, not a hole). Then the whole bore must be clear, not one line: a cylinder on the axis of the narrowest radius less a margin (ten tolerances, or a millionth of the radius), as long as the body along the axis and beyond, must have no point in common with the body (`craft3d_kernel::boolean::interference`, the boolean's exact intersection). Where it has, `blocked` gives the middle of the common volume (or the contact points) and the body's faces around it. A blind hole with a coaxial vent and a bore a rib blocks a third of are not through, though their axes are clear; a counterbore's own face is not through (its step blocks its radius: ask about the bore's face). This is "light passes through": a hole through a plate with more of the same body further along its axis is not through. One boolean per face.
- **Symmetry** (`measure.symmetry`): every vertex of the mesh of the faces, and every triangle's middle put on its surface (a cylinder's mesh has no vertex inside the face), is mirrored in the plane, and the distance from the mirror image to the nearest face of the set is found exactly; the largest is the deviation (default bound ten times the document's tolerance). Every face is mirrored, so a feature on one side only is found from its own faces, which land in the material or the air of the other side. Planes: `xy`, `yz`, `zx`, a datum plane, or a point and a normal.

### The proven distance

`crates/engine/src/verify_bound.rs`, with the supports of `craft3d_geom::support`. One method for every pair of faces, whose bounds close in on the least distance, and whose shortcuts for the analytic kinds are exact by construction rather than cases of a configuration:

- **Strata.** A face is its open interior, its edges' open curves and its vertices. The least distance of two faces is reached at a pair of points each in the relative interior of one stratum; unless it is 0 that pair is *critical* there (the line between them along the surface's normal, square to the edge's tangent; a vertex has no condition). So the least is the least, over the pairs of strata of the two faces, of what their critical pairs reach.
- **Pieces and their supports.** A stratum is cut into pieces: a face into boxes of its surface's parameters, starting from one that holds the whole face (a plane's from its edges' extents along the frame; a cylinder's or a cone's heights from its edges, a full turn around; a sphere's and a torus's whole domain; a spline's own domain), an edge into intervals of its range. For any direction `d` a piece has a proven least of `d · x`: closed form for the analytic kinds (a plane is least at a corner of its box; a cylinder, cone, sphere or torus is `d · c + ρ(v) g(u) + h(v)` with `g = A cos u + B sin u` and `ρ ≥ 0`, so the least over `u` is `ρ min g` and what is left is linear or again a cosine; a line's segment at an end, a circle's or ellipse's arc as `g`), and for a spline the least over its Bézier net cut to the piece by blossoming (positive weights: the convex hull), which closes in quadratically as the piece is halved; minus an allowance of rounding of 2e-15 of the magnitudes. A piece also has a cone around its normals (a surface) or tangents (an edge) where one is known: a plane's normal; a cylinder's and a cone's turn with `u` alone, by half the piece's width; a sphere's and a torus's by half the widths of `u` and `v` together; a circle's tangent by half its width, an ellipse's times the ratio of its axes; a spline edge's from the differences of its net (for a rational piece every pair `P_j − P_i`, `i < j`, since `C' = Σ w_i w_j (P_j − P_i) |K_ij| / w²`); a polynomial spline face's from the cones of its net's u- and v-differences (axes `a`, `b`, half-angles `α`, `β`: the normal is within `asin((2 sin(α/2) + 2 sin(β/2)) / |a × b|)` of `a × b`); none for a rational spline face, a cone's piece that reaches the apex, or a piece wider than half a turn.
- **A face's pieces and the face.** A piece is kept whole even where it reaches past the face: it then holds every point of the face it covers, and more, so what it proves still holds. A piece whose box meets none of the face's edges (each edge halved, up to 14 times, until its pieces' boxes are clear of it) is connected and does not cross the boundary, so it is all in or all out of the face: its middle tells which (`Probe::on_face`), and a piece out of the face is dropped.
- **The bound on a pair of pieces** is the most of: (1) their boxes' distance; (2) along a direction `d`, the least of `d · q` over the one less the most of `d · p` over the other (`|q − p| ≥ d · (q − p)`), for `d` from one box's middle to the other's, along each piece's normal cone (an edge's: the part of that line square to its tangent), along each kind's axis and a plane's normal: two parallel planes, or a flat face and a cylinder along it, are exact at once, whatever bounds them; (3) about the axis of a cylinder, cone, torus or circle of either: for any two points `|p − q| ≥ |(ρ_p, z_p) − (ρ_q, z_q)|` (distance from the axis and height along it), so each piece is drawn in that half-plane (a coaxial cylinder or line along the axis as a segment, a cone as a slanted one, a torus or a sphere centred on the axis as a circle, a coaxial circle as a point, a plane square to the axis as a segment from its nearest to its farthest; anything else as the box of its heights and distances) and their 2D distance is a bound: coaxial kinds (a pin in a hole, a countersunk head in its seat, an edge's circle round a pin) are exact; (4) across a cylinder's axis, the distance of the projections (a cylinder along it is its circle, a plane along it a segment, a line along it a point, anything else the box of its projection): a pin off the axis of its hole is exact. A margin is taken off where an axis or a direction is one only to rounding (below 1e-12), and the body's largest entity tolerance where an edge or a vertex takes part.
- **Pairs that hold no critical pair are dropped.** With `w = q − p` boxed along a frame about a piece's cone axis: a surface's critical pair needs `|w⊥| ≤ tan β |w∥|` somewhere in the box, an edge's `|w∥| ≤ tan γ |w⊥|`; where the box rules that out the pair holds nothing the least can be at, the other strata hold what its boundary reaches. A box holding `w = 0` is never ruled out (touching or crossing faces). This is what keeps a piece that reaches past its face from costing a bound: the part beyond holds no critical pair the face does not hold at its edge.
- **Two faces of one plane** (normals parallel to 1e-12, apart along them by no more than the tolerance and a quarter of the distance to prove): their interiors are left out of each other's when a vertex of each, put on the other's plane, is out of the other face. Points of one plane are apart within it, never along its normal, so only coincident points (an overlap) could be critical; the faces overlap only if one's boundary meets the other face (an edge's pair with that face is then at 0, which bounds the pair of faces anyway) or one holds the other, which the vertices rule out. Without this, two coplanar faces side by side (the end faces of two gears in mesh) are cut down until every piece is smaller than the gap between them.
- **A flat face and what lies in or beside its plane** (2026-10-31, the planetary gearbox's planet in its ring). Two more pairs of strata hold no critical pair: (1) an edge or a vertex in the face's plane (by its piece's extent along the normal, to the document's linear tolerance and a quarter of the distance to prove, as two faces of one plane are) with a point of it out of the face (a vertex against a face's interior is critical only where the line between them is along the face's normal, the first-order condition at the face's point; an edge's where it is also square to the edge's tangent; within the plane neither can be unless the points coincide): as for two faces of one plane, only coincident points could be critical, and the edge meets the face only by crossing its boundary (a pair at 0 then) or by lying in it, which the point rules out; (2) a face or an edge that lies on one side of the plane (its first piece's extent along the normal, to rounding) and whose cone holds no normal along the plane's (a face) or no tangent square to it (an edge): a critical pair needs the line between its points along both normals, and a touch of its open interior from one side would need the same. Gears in mesh have end faces in one plane with each other's flanks and their edges beside them; without these, the end faces were cut until every piece was smaller than the backlash (100 000 pairs, proving 0.0199 of 0.0466 mm; now 3 108 pairs prove 0.04638).
- **A flat face square to an axis is no nearer the axis than its edges** where the axis's foot is out of the face: the distance from the foot is convex and least at the foot alone, so least over the face on its boundary (a line's segment and a circle in closed form, other edges by their pieces' boxes, less the body's tolerance; once per face and axis). In the half-plane about the axis a flat piece is drawn from that distance at least: the end face of a bore is at its radius however its pieces straddle the hole, so a shaft in a bore 0.01 wider is proven in 5 pairs (before, the pieces round the hole were cut down to the gap).
- **About a sphere's centre** `c`, `|p − q| ≥ ||p − c| − |q − c||`; a sphere about `c` is at its radius exactly, anything else at its box's distances. Concentric spheres are exact.
- **The search** takes the pair with the least bound first (ties in the order made: deterministic) and halves the piece that spreads more along the line between their boxes' middles (the change of `d · x` end to end and the bow at the middle, per direction of its parameters; where nothing keeps the two apart yet (a bound of 0) a twentieth of its length counts too, so pieces lying in one another are cut down to size; with no line, one middle at the other, its length alone; the larger piece where neither spreads), until no pair's bound is below the least found less what is close enough, or 100 000 pairs are spent (past them, the pairs of strata not yet looked at are bounded by their boxes alone): the proof is then the least bound left, honestly short of the value found. Pairs of strata are taken once each per distance (an edge shared by two faces of a pair), at most a million per distance. A pair of points met on the way, each on its face (a face's piece known to be in it, or on an edge or a vertex), nearer than the least found is the new least found.
- **Limits.** A rational spline face has no normal cone, so its pieces are never dropped as non-critical: where a trimmed rational face's pieces reach past its edge toward the other face, the bound closes in as those pieces shrink (linearly, not quadratically). A pair of curved faces at one distance all along a curve without a symmetry the shortcuts know (a spline wall at a constant distance from another) is bounded piece by piece along that curve, within the work allowed.

### The proven thickness

`verify_bound::prove_rays`, with `verify::thickness_bound` (2026-10-31). The bound of a thickness is per pair of faces `(a, b)`: below the length of every line that leaves `a` into the material along its inward normal and comes out of the body through `b`. The cheap bounds and the mesh's (*The queries* › *Wall thickness*) come first; where they are short of the least found (by more than a quarter of the tolerance, or 1e-12 of it), the pair is bounded on its exact faces by the search of *The proven distance*, over the faces alone (no strata: a line's ends need not be critical for a distance), and the better of the two counts. Pairs are taken in rounds by their boxes' distance while that is below the bound so far; each round's short pairs are proven before the next looks further, so a pair that the mesh left at 0 (two faces that share an edge) no longer stops the search. The work is shared: 100 000 pairs of pieces per thickness; past it the least bound left counts, honestly short (notProven when an assertion needs more).

The bound on a pair of pieces `P` (of `a`) and `Q` (of `b`) is the distance bound of *The proven distance* (boxes, supports along directions, about an axis, across a cylinder's axis), and with `w = q − p = −t n_a(p)`, `t > 0`:

- **Oriented cones.** A face's piece has a cone around its outward normals (the support's cone, which holds the normals or their opposites, oriented by the outward normal at its middle: a connected regular piece's normals cannot pass from one side to the other without leaving a cone narrower than a right angle). Outward is the surface's normal, reversed for a reversed face.
- **Coming out.** The line comes out of the body through `b` only where it does not point into `b`: `n_b(q) · w ≥ 0`, so `n_a · n_b ≤ 0` for some pair of normals. When the angle between the two cones' axes plus their half-angles is below a right angle (less 1e-7 for the rounding of `acos`), the pair is dropped. (A line that comes out exactly at an edge does so through a face it does not point into: at a convex edge both, at a concave one at least the one it was inside of.)
- **Along the inward normals.** `w` lies in the cone about `e = −c_a` of half-angle `β`: with `w · e` between `lo` and `hi` and the least `|w⊥|` across it `s` (all from the supports), the pair is dropped when `hi < 0` or `s > tan(β + 1e-7) · hi`, else `|w| ≥ |(max(lo, s / tan(β + 1e-7)), s)|`. The 1e-7 is the rounding of the computed axis and of the frame across it, so even a plane (`β = 0`) is held to that, never to exactly its normal.
- **An extrusion's lines are square to its direction.** A cylinder along its axis, and a spline of degree 1 one way whose two rows of control points are each the other moved by nearly one vector, with equal weights (`verify::extruded`): its rulings `C1(u) − C0(u)` are weighted means of the rows' moves, so they tilt from the first move by at most `tilt` (the largest move's distance from it over its length, plus the rounding of the differences, relative to the move; recognised up to 1e-9), and its normals have `|n · z| ≤ tilt`. So `|w · z| ≤ tilt · D` with `D` the farthest corners of the two boxes: the pair is dropped when the extents of `P` and `Q` along `z` are apart by more.
- **Through the axis or the centre.** A line along the inward normal of a convex cylinder (sphere) passes through its axis (centre) at length `r`, and a point of it at `t` is `|t − r|` from there; so a piece `Q` at most `d` from the axis (centre: the farthest corner of its box, the distance being convex) is reached after at least `r − min(d, r)`. A shelled round's wall (its lines meet the sharp inner edge on the axis) is proven at once.
- **Lines that lie in a face or miss it** (kept from before, per pair of faces): a line along a direction in which `b` is a cylinder over a curve (any direction in a plane, an extrusion's direction) lies in `b` or misses it, and lying in a face is not coming out (`line_hits` marks it `along`): two planes at right angles, a plane and an extrusion along its normal, an extrusion and a plane square to its direction, each to 1e-12 of an angle (the extrusion's tilt counted in): the angle within which the probe calls a line `along` a face. This is the one rule held to an angle rather than to rounding.
- **Proven no further than asked.** An assertion's `min` is the most a proof needs (`Ctx::stop`): pairs whose bound reaches it are not cut further, for a distance as for a thickness. A measure command proves as close to the value found as it can. One search is kept across the rounds of a thickness, so the pieces of a face are made once.

**Cones far from the origin** (2026-10-31, review): a spline piece's cone comes from differences of its cut net's points, and those points are off by up to the rounding of the net's reach (2e-15 of the magnitudes de Casteljau works with); a difference of length `h` can then turn by about `2 · 2e-15 · reach / h`. The cone is widened by four times that (never less than 1e-12), and a piece whose differences are not longer than what rounding moves them by has no cone. A tiny piece a million from the origin keeps a cone, wider (`support.rs`, `a_tiny_piece_far_away_has_a_cone_widened_by_its_rounding`).

Every rule holds for any shape: they are the definition of the line (along the inward normal, coming out of the body) applied to sets that hold the pieces' points and normals, the cones and supports of `craft3d_geom::support` (closed form for the analytic kinds, the Bézier net for splines).

**Cost** (release, Strix, tests in parallel; `adaptive_proofs.rs`): a fat torus (R 5, r 4), whose proof spends the whole 100 000 pairs and stops at 7.32 of its 8, 0.52 s; a plate 300 × 300 × 10 with 841 holes (about 1700 faces) proven to 3.9999999999983 of its 4 in 1.54 s, and an assertion `min 3 mm` on it passes in 1.21 s; the shelled case 0.38 s, the clamp 0.24 s. **Where a thickness's time goes** (2026-10-31, r1-proofperf; the spline ring of `jig_sample.rs`, a rational face, `min 2 mm` notProven). Profiled in release: of a measure's 7.4 s, the proof (*this section*) took 0.5 s, spending its 100 000 pairs of pieces on the face against itself (no normal cone: bounded at 0, as before); the lines cast at the 1942 samples took 5.5 s and those of the search along the faces 1.4 s, nearly all of it the lines against the rational face (booleans.md › *A crossing closed in on*, which now takes them in about three quarters of the time). No budget was added and none changed: every proven answer is the one it was. **The lines are kept** (`verify::KEPT_LINES`): a line's answer depends on the body, the tolerance, the face, the point and the length allowed only, so the lines of a thickness are kept, for up to 4 bodies (of at most 4096 faces, 65,536 lines each), each told by its full text (`Debug`, every number to its bits); a body equal to one measured before (each point of `document.sample` regenerates a fresh copy, the same wherever the values sampled do not reach it) casts none again and gets the very answers. Release, on the Strix, the two commits measured side by side (Checks 41239570790 / 41239577518, before / after; the runner busy with a third run): the measure cold 9.0 s / 6.7 s; the assertion first checked 9.1 s / 7.1 s; `document.sample` of five points after it 41.3 s / 3.5 s (each point then the proof's 0.5 s and its mesh). The whole `jig_sample.rs` in the gate's debug build 129.6 s (without time limits) / 21.5 s (with the default minute), so its three tests of this fixture run under the default limit again. A Jig whose sampled values do change the body pays the first figure per point.

**Limits.** A cone's piece that reaches its apex has no cone (its normal is not defined there), so a pit coming to a point is bounded there only by its boxes. A wall at one thickness all along a curved face (a fillet against a parallel round) is bounded piece by piece within the work allowed; where the round's lines pass through an axis or a centre the focal bound takes it at once.

### Assertions

An assertion is document data (`craft3d_doc::Assertion`, format 28): a name, what is measured (the measure's name and parameters as a canonical JSON text, subjects as `select.query` filters with the features they name by name written as ids, so renaming a feature keeps it measured) and what it must be (`Expect`: `Is(bool)`, `Equal {value, tol}` or `Within {min, max}`; values are expressions in the document's variables, renamed with them, so `"eq": "t"` follows the variable `t` like a formula). Not part of the history: regeneration does not read them and they never stop a feature from being built.

The measures: `volume`, `area` (of bodies or faces), `mass` (bodies with a material, kg), `count` (the number of things a query finds), `extent` (the size of the bodies' box along x, y or z), `boxMin` and `boxMax` (where that box begins and ends along the axis), `centerOfMass` (the coordinate along x, y or z of the bodies' centre of mass: each body's centroid weighted by its mass, so every body needs a material), `distance`, `clearance`, `thickness`, `through`, `overlap` (whether any pair of the bodies overlaps; `is` must be given) and `symmetric`; what lies on one side of a plane and the floating equilibrium ([`sections-and-levels.md`](sections-and-levels.md): `volumeBelow`, …, `levelFor`, and `floatingOffset`, `floatingHeel`, `floatingTrim`, `floatingGM`, `floatingGML`, `floatingStable`), the sheet-metal measures and the press-brake check `bendable` ([`sheetmetal.md`](sheetmetal.md)), the fold measures ([`origami.md`](origami.md)) and `value`. A plain number is in the measure's own unit (document units, their square or cube; kg); an expression with units is converted. In the expression language a power binds to the number with its unit: `"50 cm^3"` is (50 cm)³, so a bound written so would pass silently. That form (a number, a unit, `^`) is refused wherever a measure or an assertion reads an expression, with the form meant: `50 * (1 cm)^3`, or the plain number.

**Proven, not found.** A measure gives what it found and, where it searched for a least (a thickness, a distance, a clearance), bounds proven on the true value: `atLeast` (nothing is below it) and the found value (a real place, so the least is no more). A bound holds when the whole of that range is within it (`min` against `atLeast`, `max` against the found value), fails when none of it is (a real place thinner than `min`: failed for certain), and is otherwise **not proven**: a third state, shown like a warning (`notProven`, never passed), with the value found and what is proven. What is measured exactly (volume, area, mass, count, extent) has both ends equal. A bound is met within the document's tolerance (of a length; its square or cube for an area or a volume, and a millionth of a millionth of the bound for rounding).

**Not proven by the measure itself** (2026-10-29). A measure that searches may end without an answer it can stand by, neither yes nor no: the floating equilibrium's Newton search that does not settle (`floating…`), the bend sequence's proof that meets a step it can tell neither clear nor colliding or runs out of its work (`bendable`). It says so (`Outcome::unproven`, with the reason), and the assertion is **notProven** with that reason, whatever its bound: never passed, never failed, and an output of it unknown. That is not an error (the question is well put; the answer is not found), and not a guess. An error of the question (no bodies, a displacement more than the bodies hold, a tool polygon that crosses itself) stays an error.

**Checking.** What an assertion depends on is its key: its text and bounds, the units and tolerance, the values of the variables its expressions name, the bodies (by their `Arc`, which regeneration keeps for a feature an edit did not touch) and faces its subjects name now (and every body when it has no subject), and a datum plane's place. The engine works the keys out per revision, cheaply (the subjects' queries), and measures only the assertions whose key it has no answer for, in a job on a thread of its own, one job at a time, two assertions of it at once on threads of their own (since 2026-10-26; `assert_check.rs`), each answer shown as it comes in; a host that works a piece at a time (the page) measures them one per piece between its frames (`Session::prepare_some`, [`engine.md`](engine.md) › *A piece at a time*); the answers stay by key (beyond the current ones, up to 256), so an edit that leaves an assertion's subjects alone, or an undo that brings the same bodies back, measures nothing. Whoever asks says how long it waits: the window never (an assertion being measured is *pending*, drawn as inactive, and the window asks again every quarter second), an edit's result two seconds (then `pending` counts what is still measured), `document.inspect` two seconds, `assert.add`, `assert.edit`, `assert.list` (`wait: false` not at all) and `document.check` until every answer is in. Each check is caught on its own: one that fails to measure (its subject names nothing, the geometry refused) is in *error*, which counts as broken; one that panics is an error that says so. A measured number is compared with the bounds as above (eq ± tol: tol defaults to a millionth of the value, at least ten times the document's tolerance for a length, 0 for a count).

**Names that change.** Features a subject names by name are stored as ids, and so are those named by a subject written before the feature existed, at the moment a feature is renamed (`feature.rename` writes them as ids first). `variable.rename` renames the variable in the bounds and in the expressions inside a measure (a symmetry plane's point, its `tol`).

**Where a broken one shows**, like a failed feature: in the result of the command whose edit broke it (`assertions: {count, passed, broken: [{name, status, measured, unit, expected, message}], unproven, pending}` on every feature command and `variable.set`), in `document.check` (a problem per broken assertion, `ok` false; a warning per assertion not proven, `ok` unchanged; and the summary), in `document.inspect` (`assertions`, each with its status), in `assert.list`, and in the window's tree (a section *Assertions* under the features, failed ones in the failed colour with their message) and `ui.get` (`assertions`). An assertion may be written before the model meets it, or before its subject exists: it is in error until then.

### Outputs

An **output** is a read-only variable computed after regeneration (`craft3d_doc::Output`, format 43, `crates/engine/src/output_cmds.rs`; decided by the developer 2026-10-27, `docs/decisions.md`). It is one of two kinds: **measured** (`variable.measure {name, measure, ...}`: a measure as an assertion takes it, `levelFor`, `centroidBelow`, `sectionInertia`, `thickness`, `mass`, …, kept as the same canonical JSON with features by id) or **computed** (`variable.output {name, expr}`: an expression of the variables and of other outputs). Other outputs use it (in their expressions and in the expressions inside their measures: the waterline `T` as the `offset` of `KB`), and so do assertions (in their bounds, and through the measure `value {expr}`, an expression checked against bounds exactly). Nothing that regeneration reads may: a variable, a feature or sketch parameter, a feature's condition, a datum, a public parameter's range, a named set's query, a body's colour or density.

- **Why outputs defined by a measure, not a measure function in the expression language.** A function `levelFor(Hull, V)` inside an expression would put geometry into `craft3d-expr` (layer 1, which knows no bodies), make every expression evaluation potentially a boolean of the whole model, and make it impossible to tell from the parser where geometry may be read; it would also have to repeat in function syntax every measure's subject queries and options, which the assertion measures already have as JSON. An output defined by a measure command reuses that vocabulary whole (one list of measures, their parameters, units and proven bounds; their storage with features by id; their renaming; their cache keys and background measuring), gives each measured value a name with a unit, and keeps the expression language pure: an expression only names values, and whether a name is an input or an output is decided by the document, so the one-way rule is a check on names, not on syntax. Picking a field out of a command's JSON result (`measure.below` → `volume`) was rejected too: those results are numbers without units or proven bounds and their shape is not a contract.
- **One way, by construction.** Regeneration reads only `Document::variables`; outputs are not in that table, so no geometry can depend on them however a file is written (an expression in a feature that names an output fails as an unknown variable). Every edit (`commands::edit_merging`) is refused when it makes something regeneration reads use an output, naming each use (*the variable `beam` uses `GM`, an output: …*), whichever command made it (`variable.set`, a feature, `feature.when`, `set.define`, `variable.output` for a name an input already uses); a use a file brought is left for the edit that mends it. An output's name is unique among variables, tables and outputs, and `variable.set`, `param.set` and `variable.publish` refuse an output by name.
- **No cycles.** The outputs' uses of each other (names in their expressions and in their measures' expressions, not the names of bodies, faces, kinds or axes) form a graph; `variable.output` and `variable.measure` refuse an output that would lie on a cycle, naming it (*`B` would depend on itself (B → A → B)*, the shortest way back by a breadth-first search). One on a cycle in a file is unknown with the cycle named, and so is what uses it.
- **Computed in stages, with the assertions.** The measured outputs are measured as assertions without a bound, by the same keys, memo and background job (`assert_check.rs`): one call resolves every output whose outputs are resolved (an expression at once; a measure from the memo, or into the next job), then gives each assertion whose outputs are resolved its key (which holds the values of the outputs it uses), and starts a job with what is missing. A chain of measures (T, then KB and I at T) takes one job per link. Each output is measured once per what it depends on: an edit of `KG` re-measures only the assertion on GM, not the waterline or the moments; an undo takes the answers from the memo. The outputs of a revision are kept once all are computed, so the same document gives the same outputs, bit for bit (rule 5).
- **Unknown, never a silent number.** An output is *unknown*, with the reason, when its measure is in error (no subject, a volume outside (0, total)), when its measure says it is not proven (a floating equilibrium that does not settle, a bend sequence neither found nor refuted: *Not proven by the measure itself* above), when the range its measure proves is wider than the document's tolerance of its dimension (or a part in 10^9 for a power that is not positive: a thickness found at 2 mm but proven only above 1.96 mm is no value), when a measure gives no number (a clearance of bodies that overlap), when its expression does not evaluate, when it lies on a cycle, and when an output it uses is unknown (*it uses `Tover`, which is unknown: …*). An assertion that uses an unknown output, anywhere, is **notProven** with that reason; one whose outputs are being measured is pending. A measured output is a length to its power in the document's unit (a volume in mm³ in a mm document), a mass in kg, a count or a yes-or-no (1 or 0) as a plain number; a computed one has its expression's dimension. `output.list`, `document.inspect` (`outputs`) and `param.list` (`outputs`, read-only rows under a Jig's form) give each with `status` (known, unknown, pending), `value` in the document's units, `unit` and `message`.
- **What a measure does not bound.** A measure that reports no proven range (a curvature sampled on a grid of each face, a symmetry's deviation at sampled points, a box found on a mesh within its stated error) gives its output the value it found, taken as exact: such an output is as good as its measure, which says what it is (`measure.developability`: *a sampled largest, not a proven bound*). Only a measure that does report a range (a thickness, a distance) can make its output unknown for being unproven.
- **The key follows tables too.** An output's or an assertion's key holds every table its expressions look up in directly (`loads[1].litres` in a `volume`), whole: a table edit changes no variable, and without it the output would stay what it was.
- **A file with an output of both kinds, or of neither** (edited by hand), is refused when it opens, naming the output, rather than read as one of them.
- **Renames and deletes.** `variable.rename` renames an input in the outputs' expressions and in their measures' expressions; an output by the same command, everywhere it is used (other outputs, their measures, assertions' bounds and measures). `feature.rename` keeps an output's subjects by id. `variable.delete` of an output is refused while another output or an assertion uses it (`force` deletes it; what used it is then unknown or in error).
- **The slack of a bound on a negative power.** `decide` allows a bound the document's tolerance to the power of the measure's dimension; for a negative power (a curvature, 1 / length) that slack grew as the tolerance shrank (1e12 for a curvature at 1e-6) and passed every `max`. It is now rounding only for a power that is not positive (found with the measure `value` of `1 / GM`).
- **Limits.** Outputs are not public parameters and have no form of their own outside a Jig's Parameters tab (there is no Variables panel). A measure command (`measure.below`, …) does not see outputs; only assertions and outputs do. The ranges a measure proves are not carried through an output's expression (interval arithmetic): an output is known only when its measure is exact within the tolerance.

**Label and unit (2026-10-30).** An output may have a `label` (what a form shows instead of its name) and a `unit` to show it in (`MPa`, `N m`, `N/mm²`), of its dimension; they change only how it is shown. Without a unit it is shown in its dimension's default (docs/design/document.md › *Mechanical units*). `output.list` gives `label` and `text` ("Bending stress 10 MPa"); the Parameters tab shows the label. An assertion's bounds read units the same way: `max: "235 MPa"` on a stress, a plain number in the default unit (MPa for a stress, N for a force, kg for a mass).

### Conditions

An assertion may carry a condition, `when` (`craft3d_doc::Assertion::when`, format 46), as a feature does (`feature.when`): an expression of the variables and the outputs, evaluated against the document as it is when the assertion is checked. While it is 0 the assertion is **inactive** (`AssertionStatus::Inactive`): nothing else of it is worked out or measured, and it counts as neither passed nor failed nor notProven: `assert.list` counts it under `inactive` and its `ok` is true when every other one passed; an edit's summary and `document.check` count it under `inactive` and list it under neither `broken` nor `unproven`, so `document.sample` (`craft3d-cli check-range`) passes a point where it is off. A condition with a unit, or one that does not evaluate (a name that is nothing), is the assertion's *error*, and one that needs an unknown output makes it *notProven*. The condition is renamed with a variable or an output, holds a variable in use against `variable.delete`, and is part of the assertion's key (its names' values), so flipping it measures nothing else again. Why a condition rather than a vacuous pass: a measure over nothing (no closed corner for `cornerGap`, no hole beside a bend for `holeToBend`) has no value, and calling it passed would also pass a design that lost the hole by mistake; the Jig that knows which choice makes the thing says so with the same condition (2026-10-28, from two Jigs' findings).

**Lazy over outputs.** An output, or an assertion, waits until every output its expressions name is computed (known or unknown), so what it is never depends on what happened to be measured first; then its expressions are evaluated as the expression language does: the branch of `if` (or `choose`, `&&`, `||`) that is not taken is not needed. An output without a value is an error only where it is used (`ErrorKind::DependencyFailed`), so an output or an assertion is unknown or notProven only through an output it needed (`Ctx::unknown_used` records every one needed, from any expression evaluated in the measure's context: a bound, the condition, a measure's `offset`, `volume`, `expr`; the check is undecided only when the error it reports is about one of them, `Ctx::unknown_in`, so a later unrelated error, a typo in the condition, stays an error). `if(corner == 1, gap, cg)` with `corner` at 1 is `gap` whether or not `cg` has a value. The cycle check stays on the names written (an `if` does not hide a cycle), so the graph is the same at every value of the variables: a lazy mutual pair, `A = if(on == 1, B, 0 mm)` whose taken branch needs `B` and `B = if(on == 1, 2 mm, A)` whose untaken branch names `A`, is refused as the cycle A → B → A, although at `on` = 1 neither would need the other's value.

### Bounds

At most 10 000 items per subject, 200 bodies per geometric measure (10 000 for volume, area and mass), 500 pairs of bodies per clearance, 2000 pairs of bodies whose boxes meet per overlap (pairs whose boxes, grown by twice the tolerance, do not meet are found by a sweep along x and cost nothing), 4 million pairs of faces and 60 million pairs of triangles per distance, 6000 samples per body for a thickness and per symmetry, 200 000 pairs of faces and 20 million pairs of triangles for a thickness's bound, 16 places settled exactly, 400 steps per settling, 1000 ray hits listed, 1000 assertions per document, 16 KiB per stored measure. Past a bound the answer is an error that says what to narrow.

### Proof over the range

A Jig (a document with public parameters, [`document.md`](document.md) › *Public parameters and document info*) must give a valid model at every value its form lets through, not only at the values its author tried. `document.sample {samples, seed, corners, maxCorners, choices, deep, strict, budgetMs, timeLimitMs, maxFailures, values}` (`crates/engine/src/sample_cmds.rs`) checks it over its range, on a copy: the open document, its revision and its undo history do not change.

- **Points, in a fixed order.** The defaults; the corners of the box of the ranges (each parameter that moves at its low or high end: a number or integer at its min or max, a side not given being the default; a boolean off or on; a choice its first or last), all 2^n while that is at most `maxCorners` (default 64, at most 4096), else that many chosen by the seed, all-low and all-high first (`samples` does not cut them; `counts.cornersInBox` is 2^n, `counts.corner` how many were tried); every other choice of each choice parameter with the rest at the defaults; then `samples` (default 32, at most 10 000) random points. Points are made one at a time as the run reaches them. A number without min and max is only tried at its default and listed under `unbounded`.
- **A point is a fraction per parameter**, worked into a value against the ranges *at that point*: a number `lo + u (hi − lo)`, on its step from `lo` when it has one; an integer one of the whole numbers (on its step) in the range, uniformly; a choice or a boolean one of its values within its min and max, uniformly (a boolean whose max is another parameter, `opt` only with `big`, stays off while that is off). A range that uses other parameters (`max: 2 * d`) is evaluated after them, again until no value moves (at most once per parameter and once more), so a point always lies in the ranges it implies; away from the defaults, a parameter the point leaves at its default (a choice point's other parameters) that lies outside its range there is moved to the nearest value in it (`param_cmds::nearest_in_range`), as a form and `param.set {clamp: true}` move it (a choice that narrows the range of a count), and the move is listed with the point (`clamped: [{parameter, from, to}]`, also on check-range's line for it); a choice point whose choice lies outside that parameter's own range at the defaults is not made (`counts.skipped`): the form does not offer it, and moving it would repeat another choice; the defaults themselves are checked as they are; ranges that keep pushing each other on (`a ≤ b`, `b ≤ a + 1`, both at their high end) fail the point with *did not settle after N rounds* and the last values. A value on a step is held to `[lo, hi]`: `-0.3 + 3 × 0.1` is 5.6e-17, and the range `[-0.3 mm, 0]` ends at 0. Values are written as `param.set` takes them, in the parameter's unit (`12.5 mm`, `20`), and listed with every failing point, so `param.set {values}` reproduces it.
- **Randomness** is SplitMix64 seeded by `seed` (rule 5), one stream for a subset of corners and one for the random points (the seed with a constant of each mixed in), so the random points are the same whatever `corners` and `maxCorners` are: the same seed gives the same points and the same verdict on every platform. Times are reported per point (`ms`) and summed up (`timing`: median, max, total, the slowest point) but are part of the verdict only with `budgetMs`. In the browser there is no clock: `ms` is null and `budgetMs` is refused.
- **Each point** is set as `param.set` sets it (`param_cmds::prepare_set`: a point it refuses, such as a range that is empty there, fails with its reason), regenerated in a session of its own with a regeneration cache of its own and the host's budget of bodies (nothing of the open document's cache is reused; not through `Session::execute`, so recovery does not see it). It is not quite from nothing: the kernel's trees of freeform surfaces are shared within the process, so a surface the open document has met may be quicker; after each point the trees no open document holds are let go of (`Session::release_trees_now`), and what the kernel keeps per process between operations (the intersections of surfaces and the projectors and pieces of their surfaces, the projections onto surfaces, the meshes of faces, the bodies that passed their check, what is remembered of bodies that are gone: `sample_cmds::forget_kept`) is let go of after every point where the host asks for that (`Session::set_lean_sampling`: `craft3d-cli check-range`, whose runs may take hours), else once at the end of the run (a window's, a minute by default, whose open document keeps its answers warm meanwhile); no result changes, and checked by `document.check` (`deep` with `deep`): failed variables and features, invalid bodies, invalid parameters, broken assertions. An assertion not proven there (`notProven`) is no problem but not passed either: the point counts under `unproven`, not `passed` (`passed + unproven + failed` is the number of points), and is listed under `unprovenPoints` with its values and assertions; with `strict` it fails the point (*not proven (strict)*). `ok` is false only when a point failed. The bodies are measured (mass properties from the exact faces, which shows an unbounded or unmeasurable face) at the defaults and the corners, where the extremes of the range are, and, with `deep`, also at the choice and random points: measuring a part with many curved faces costs several times its regeneration (the welding table Jig, 65 points: 9.3 s wall and 47 s CPU with every point measured, 2.4 s and 3.0 s without, 2026-10-23). A feature whose body is a solid at the defaults must have a solid at the point too.
- **A limit on the whole run:** `timeLimitMs`, checked between points (the first point is always checked), 60 s when not given, so a window or an agent over the control channel or MCP waits no longer; `null` is no limit, as `craft3d-cli check-range` runs it. In the browser, which has no clock, a run stops after 128 points instead. A run that stops says `complete: false` and `stoppedBy` (`timeLimit`, `pointCap`), and its `ok` is false when a point failed and null otherwise, never true.
- **Bounds:** 10 000 random points, 4096 corners, 4096 choice points, `maxFailures` (default 100) failing points listed with their values and problems (`failed` counts all, `truncated` says when there are more); as many points with unproven assertions (`unprovenTruncated`).
- **As it goes:** each point is handed, as soon as it is checked, to the sink of the calling thread if there is one (`craft3d_engine::with_point_sink`, as `craft3d_regen::progress` does for regeneration): `craft3d-cli check-range` prints a line per point, so a run killed by a timeout has said what it found. The sink answers whether to go on: false stops the run before the next point, `stoppedBy: "caller"`. Points are checked one after the other on the calling thread; were they ever spread over worker threads, the sink would hear nothing from those.

`craft3d-cli check-range FILE [--samples N] [--seed S] [--deep] [--budget-ms M] [--max-corners N] [--no-corners] [--json]` (and `--time-limit-ms M`, `--strict`; no limit by default) runs it on a file and prints a line per point as it is checked, then a report: *84 points (1 default, all 64 corners, …): 80 passed, 4 with unproven assertions, 0 failed*, a block per failing point and per point with an assertion not proven (or the result as JSON, the lines per point then on standard error): the check a Jig repository's CI runs. On Linux with glibc it runs the check in a process of its own whose allocator keeps two arenas (`MALLOC_ARENA_MAX=2`, unless the environment sets it), so its memory holds one point's peak (finding of 2026-10-26). That process is told who started it (`CRAFT3D_CHECK_PARENT`, which is also what keeps it from starting another) and stops before its next point when that one is gone (a timeout or a kill that reached only the starter), exit code 4; killed by a signal itself (the out-of-memory killer), the starter exits with 3, the check's error, not 1. Exit codes: 0 no point failed (with `--strict`: none failed or unproven), 1 a point failed, 2 a usage error (also `--samples` above 10 000 or `--max-corners` above 4096), 3 the file could not be opened or checked (no public parameters, say), 4 the run stopped at its time limit with no point failing so far.

The laser box (`plan/laserbox.md`) with nine public parameters (width 100–300 mm on 1 mm steps, depth, height, thickness 2–6 mm, finger width 8–20 mm, kerf, clearance, thumb and lid-hole radii): 73 points (64 of 512 corners, 8 random) all pass, regeneration median 180 ms, max 418 ms (optimised debug build); deep on 13 points passes. Test: `jig_sample.rs` (`a_choice_from_a_table_is_varied_like_a_list_of_choices`: corners at the first and last row, every other row a choice point, every row among the random points).
- **2026-10-25, R1 testers and Jig authors.** (1) A CubeSat Jig needs its centre of mass within ±20/±20/±70 mm of the geometric centre (CDS 2.2.11): the measure `centerOfMass` (of, axis), a length like `extent`; without a material the mass measure's message. Tested against the balance point of two blocks of different densities at three scales. Found with it: an assertion's key (what it depends on) held no materials, so after `body.material` a `mass` assertion kept its old answer (no body changes); the key of `mass` and `centerOfMass` now holds the document's materials. (2) `interference` with `is: true` meant *none overlap*, which reads backwards (a tester only understood it through `document.interference`). It is now `overlap`, true when a pair overlaps, with `is` required (a press fit and a clearance are both written, neither is a default), and the message is a sentence: *Bolt overlaps Plate by 12 mm^3, expected no overlap*, or *no overlap*. Saved documents are migrated (format 36, `v35_to_v36`: `interference` with `is: b` becomes `overlap` with `is: !b`), so a stored assertion means what it meant and shows its measured flag the same way round as what it must be; `assert.add`/`assert.edit` still accept the old name from callers and write it as `overlap`. (A first version read the old form in `check` without a migration: its `measured` was the overlap flag while the stored expectation was the old `is`, so a passed assertion showed false against true; the review caught it.) (3) `"tol": "0 mm^3"` was refused as a power of a quantity (`N unit^k` is (N unit)^k); for N = 0 and 1 the two readings agree, so those are taken, when the number stands on its own (`2e1 mm^3` is still refused). (5) The same Jig must keep anything from sticking out more than 6.5 mm beyond the rails on +x and on -x separately (CDS 2.2.3); an extent bounds only their sum: `boxMin` and `boxMax` (of, axis), the sides of the same box `extent` measures. (6) An `extent` of a gear whose involute flanks (from `sketch.curve`) were cut measured 44.99 x 17 mm where it is 43.99 x 15: the box was `Body::held_bbox`, which takes a Bézier or NURBS face (or NURBS edge) by its whole control net, untrimmed. `extent`, `boxMin` and `boxMax` now keep the exact held box only for analytic bodies (`Surface::is_freeform`, `Curve3::is_freeform`); a body with freeform geometry is meshed at a hundred-thousandth of the side of a square of its area (at least ten linear tolerances) within a budget of 2 million triangles, coarsened as the viewer's mesh is when over it (`tessellate_within_budget`). The box reached is the mesh's shrunk by the body's largest entity tolerance (at least the document's: an edge of a STEP import or an intersection curve lies that far from its faces); the box it stays within is the mesh's grown by twice the chordal tolerance and kept within the held box. That outer side holds within the tessellator's measured tolerance, not by proof: the tessellator checks a triangle's deviation at its centroid and side midpoints and a chord's at its quarters, so its bound is a measured one, and the factor two is a margin beside it; only the held box is a true bound. The assertion decides on that range like a thickness: passed, failed, or notProven when a bound falls inside it, after looking again on meshes eight times finer up to three times while the floor allows (`MAX_REFINE`); the value then says *at most* the outer side. Where no mesh fits the budget (or the body has no area to size one by), the box reached is not known and the range is all the held box allows.
