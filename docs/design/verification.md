# Design verification

How an agent (or a person) checks a design by asking it questions instead of looking at a picture: the queries `measure.*` and the assertions the document keeps (`assert.*`). Roadmap: [`../roadmap.md`](../roadmap.md) › *V1*. Protocol: [`../control-protocol.md`](../control-protocol.md) › *Verification*. Code: `crates/kernel/src/boolean/probe.rs` (questions asked of one solid), `crates/engine/src/verify.rs` (the geometry), `crates/engine/src/verify_cmds.rs` (the queries and the measures), `crates/engine/src/assert_cmds.rs` (assertions), `crates/doc/src/assertion.rs` (the data), `crates/engine/tests/verification.rs` (the oracles).

Why (the developer, 2026-10-21): "make the plate twice as thick" silently turned the housing's bolt holes into blind holes. An assertion "the bolt holes go through" flags it at the edit that breaks it. V1 is also the measure of the CAD gym: a task's intent written as assertions is what a model is scored against.

## Method

### What is measured: subjects

A measure is taken *of* something named the way `select.query` names things ([`selection.md`](selection.md)): a body by its feature's name or id, a list of them, a query `{"kind", "where"}`, or a bare filter, which gets the measure's own kind (faces for `distance`, `through` and `area`; bodies or faces for `thickness`; bodies for the rest). The engine runs the query through `select_cmds::query`, the same filters `select.query` evaluates, so a subject such as `{"hole": true, "madeBy": "Drill"}` follows the design: an edit that adds a hole adds it to the subject. A face that a boolean split into pieces keeping its name is one item with all its pieces.

### The geometry: a mesh first, then the exact faces

Every geometric answer is found coarse on a mesh and settled on the exact B-rep. The mesh of a body (chordal tolerance 1/1000 of its diagonal, at least ten times the document's tolerance, 12° angle criterion, at most 400 000 triangles; coarser by four, up to three times, when over budget) only says *where* to look; the number comes from the exact geometry:

- **`craft3d_kernel::boolean::probe::Probe`** wraps the booleans' own preparation of a solid (each face's outline: its loops on its surface, made when first needed) and answers three questions with the booleans' own tools: `line_hits` (every place a line meets a face, from the exact curve–surface intersection `hits::curve_hits`, kept to the face by `Outline::side`, with whether it is at an edge, tangent, or the line lies on the surface, and the outward normal's sign along the line), `on_face` (a point of a surface inside, on the boundary of, or outside the face) and `place` (a point inside, outside or on the solid, the boolean's ray classification).
- **The nearest point of a face** to a point: the foot on its surface (closed form for the analytic kinds, the surface's own projection for splines) when it lies on the face, else the nearest point of its edges (closed form for lines and circles, kept within each edge's range; a circle's parameter tried a turn either way).

### The queries

- **Distance** (`measure.distance`): pairs of faces in order of their boxes' distance; triangle pairs (point–triangle and segment–segment, after Ericson) while a pair can still beat the best by twice the chord; at most 60 million triangle pairs. The best few face pairs (up to 16) are settled by projecting onto each face in turn (at most 400 steps, until neither point moves by 1e-13 of the coordinates), and the least wins. Exact for planes, cylinders, cones, spheres and tori in any position (the projections are closed forms and the alternation stops at the nearest pair); on splines as exact as the surface's projection.
- **Clearance** (`measure.clearance`): the distance per pair of bodies; within twice the tolerance the kernel's interference (`document.interference`) tells touch from overlap; farther apart, a vertex of each placed in the other tells a body inside another (overlap). An overlap has no clearance (null), and an assertion on it fails with "overlap".
- **Wall thickness** (`measure.thickness`): the ray method. At sampled points of every face (the middles of mesh triangles put on the surface, at most 6000 in all, evenly per face) a line goes into the material along the inward normal; the thickness there is how far it goes before it meets a face (`line_hits`, from four tolerances on). The least few samples on different faces are then moved along their face by a pattern search (eight directions in the tangent plane, the step halved when none is better, down to a thousandth of the tolerance) to where the thickness is least. Exact where the wall's sides are parallel planes or concentric (the function is flat or has a smooth minimum the search reaches); elsewhere the least found. Measured from a set of faces (`of` a query of faces), only those are sampled: the top of a plate gives the plate's thickness although a hole's wall near the edge is thinner.
- **Ray** (`measure.ray`): `line_hits` per body, merged by distance; entering where the outward normal faces against the ray.
- **Goes through** (`measure.through`): for a cylindrical or conical face, its axis and its extent along it (from points of its edges); the point of the axis in the middle of that extent must be outside the solid (inside: the face is a boss, not a hole), and the axis from there must meet no face of the body either way. A blocked direction says which face and how far beyond the face's end, so a drill stopped short reads "blocked by the hole's own end, 0 beyond it". This is "light passes through": a hole through a plate with more of the same body further along its axis is not through.
- **Symmetry** (`measure.symmetry`): every vertex of the mesh of the faces, and every triangle's middle put on its surface (a cylinder's mesh has no vertex inside the face), is mirrored in the plane, and the distance from the mirror image to the nearest face of the set is found exactly; the largest is the deviation (default bound ten times the document's tolerance). Every face is mirrored, so a feature on one side only is found from its own faces, which land in the material or the air of the other side. Planes: `xy`, `yz`, `zx`, a datum plane, or a point and a normal.

### Assertions

An assertion is document data (`craft3d_doc::Assertion`, format 27): a name, what is measured (the measure's name and parameters as a canonical JSON text, subjects as `select.query` filters with the features they name by name written as ids, so renaming a feature keeps it measured) and what it must be (`Expect`: `Is(bool)`, `Equal {value, tol}` or `Within {min, max}`; values are expressions in the document's variables, renamed with them, so `"eq": "t"` follows the variable `t` like a formula). Not part of the history: regeneration does not read them and they never stop a feature from being built.

The measures: `volume`, `area` (of bodies or faces), `mass` (bodies with a material, kg), `count` (the number of things a query finds), `extent` (the size of the bodies' box along x, y or z), `distance`, `clearance`, `thickness`, `through`, `interference` (none of the bodies overlap) and `symmetric`. A plain number is in the measure's own unit (document units, their square or cube; kg); an expression with units is converted, but note that in the expression language a power binds to the number with its unit: `"31095 mm^3"` is (31095 mm)³; write the number, or `31095 * (1 mm)^3`.

**Checking.** The engine checks every assertion against the current regeneration when something asks how they stand and remembers the result per revision (`OpenDocument::assertions`), so an assertion is measured once per edit however many readers ask. Each check is caught on its own: one that fails to measure (its subject names nothing, the geometry refused) is in *error*, which counts as broken; one that panics is an error that says so. A measured number is compared with the bounds (eq ± tol: tol defaults to a millionth of the value, at least ten times the document's tolerance for a length, 0 for a count).

**Where a broken one shows**, like a failed feature: in the result of the command whose edit broke it (`assertions: {count, passed, broken: [{name, status, measured, unit, expected, message}]}` on every feature command and `variable.set`), in `document.check` (a problem per broken assertion, `ok` false, and the summary), in `document.inspect` (`assertions`, each with its status), in `assert.list`, and in the window's tree (a section *Assertions* under the features, failed ones in the failed colour with their message) and `ui.get` (`assertions`). An assertion may be written before the model meets it, or before its subject exists: it is in error until then.

### Bounds

At most 10 000 items per subject, 200 bodies per geometric measure, 500 pairs of bodies per clearance or interference, 4 million pairs of faces and 60 million pairs of triangles per distance, 6000 samples per thickness and symmetry, 16 places settled exactly, 400 steps per settling, 1000 ray hits listed, 1000 assertions per document, 16 KiB per stored measure. Past a bound the answer is an error that says what to narrow.

## Findings and history

- **2026-10-22, slice 1.** The queries above and assertions; format 27. Oracles (`crates/engine/tests/verification.rs`): the distance between blocks and from a block's edge to a cylinder at scales 0.01, 1 and 1000 to 1e-9 relative; clearance clear, touch, inside and crossing; the thickness of a drilled plate (7 = 10 − 3 from a hole to the edge, 8 from the top), of a ligament of 1 and of a tube of 2 to 1e-6; a ray through a plate and across a hole (six hits at closed-form distances); holes through, blind after the plate is made 16 thick (blocked at the hole's own end), a boss refused as a hole; symmetry of a part and a one-sided bore breaking it; the housing showcase symmetric about its mirror plane and front to back (spline sides), not top to bottom. The developer's case reproduced: the housing's `HalfBase` extruded 16 mm instead of 8, the bolt holes still 20 mm high from z = −6, breaks "the bolt holes go through" in the edit's own result (blocked at z = 14) while the feature stays ok; holes 30 mm high pass again.
- First thickness test expected 8 for the drilled plate and got 7: the holes are 10 from the edge with radius 3, so the wall between a hole and the edge is the thinnest place. The measure was right; the test's closed form was not.
- The first symmetry sampled only mesh vertices and called a block with a one-sided bore symmetric: a cylinder's mesh has vertices only on its two circles, which mirror onto the top and bottom faces. Triangle middles put on the surface sample the inside of every face.
