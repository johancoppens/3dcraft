# Design verification

How an agent (or a person) checks a design by asking it questions instead of looking at a picture: the queries `measure.*` and the assertions the document keeps (`assert.*`). Roadmap: [`../roadmap.md`](../roadmap.md) › *V1*. Protocol: [`../control-protocol.md`](../control-protocol.md) › *Verification*. Code: `crates/kernel/src/boolean/probe.rs` (questions asked of one solid), `crates/engine/src/verify.rs` (the geometry), `crates/engine/src/verify_cmds.rs` (the queries and the measures), `crates/engine/src/assert_cmds.rs` (assertions), `crates/doc/src/assertion.rs` (the data), `crates/engine/tests/verification.rs` (the oracles).

Why (the developer, 2026-10-21): "make the plate twice as thick" silently turned the housing's bolt holes into blind holes. An assertion "the bolt holes go through" flags it at the edit that breaks it. V1 is also the measure of the CAD gym: a task's intent written as assertions is what a model is scored against.

## Method

### What is measured: subjects

A measure is taken *of* something named the way `select.query` names things ([`selection.md`](selection.md)): a body by its feature's name or id, a list of them, a query `{"kind", "where"}`, or a bare filter, which gets the measure's own kind (faces for `distance`, `through` and `area`; bodies or faces for `thickness`; bodies for the rest). The engine runs the query through `select_cmds::query`, the same filters `select.query` evaluates, so a subject such as `{"hole": true, "madeBy": "Drill"}` follows the design: an edit that adds a hole adds it to the subject. A face that a boolean split into pieces keeping its name is one item with all its pieces.

### The geometry: a mesh first, then the exact faces

Every geometric answer is found coarse on a mesh and settled on the exact B-rep. The mesh of a body (chordal tolerance 1/1000 of its diagonal, at least ten times the document's tolerance, 12° angle criterion, at most 400 000 triangles; coarser by four, up to three times, when over budget) only says *where* to look; the number comes from the exact geometry:

- **`craft3d_kernel::boolean::probe::Probe`** wraps the booleans' own preparation of a solid (each face's outline: its loops on its surface, made when first needed) and answers three questions with the booleans' own tools: `line_hits` (every place a line meets a face, from the exact curve–surface intersection `hits::curve_hits`, kept to the face by `Outline::side`, with whether it is at an edge, tangent, or the line lies on the surface, and the outward normal's sign along the line), `on_face` (a point of a surface inside, on the boundary of, or outside the face) and `place` (a point inside, outside or on the solid, the boolean's ray classification).
- **The nearest point of a face** to a point: the foot on its surface (closed form for the analytic kinds, the surface's own projection for splines) when it lies on the face, else the nearest point of its edges (closed form for lines and circles, kept within each edge's range; a circle's parameter tried a turn either way).

### The queries

- **Distance** (`measure.distance`): pairs of faces in order of their boxes' distance; triangle pairs (point–triangle and segment–segment, after Ericson) while a pair can still beat the best by twice the chord; at most 60 million triangle pairs. The best few face pairs (up to 16) are settled by projecting onto each face in turn (at most 400 steps, until neither point moves by 1e-13 of the coordinates), and the least wins: that is a distance between two real points, so no less than the least distance. Projection can stop at a local minimum on a spline; what is *proven* is `atLeast`: the mesh's least distance per pair of faces less how far each face can lie from its triangles (0 for a flat face bounded by lines, r (1 − cos 6°) for a round of radius r held to the mesh's 12° angle, or the sagitta of a chord-long side, the chordal tolerance otherwise), and the boxes' distance for pairs not compared.
- **Clearance** (`measure.clearance`): the distance per pair of bodies; within twice the tolerance the kernel's interference (`document.interference`) tells touch from overlap; farther apart, a vertex of each placed in the other tells a body inside another (overlap). An overlap has no clearance (null), and an assertion on it fails with "overlap". `atLeast` as for the distance.
- **Wall thickness** (`measure.thickness`): the ray method. At sampled points of every face (the middles of mesh triangles put on the surface, at most 6000 per body, evenly per face) a line goes into the material along the inward normal; the thickness there is how far it goes before it meets a face (`line_hits`, from four tolerances on, no further than twice the least found so far). Then a bound, proven, below every such line (`atLeast`): per pair of faces a line could leave and come out through (box distance under the least found), the most of their boxes' distance; for two flat faces, none when n_a·n_b ≥ 0 (the line cannot come out there), else the planes' distance when parallel and the heights of each face's corners over the other's plane; for a flat face and a round one, the heights over the plane of only those triangles of the round where the line could come out (n_a·n_b < 0, with the turn of the normal within a triangle as margin), none when there are none, and none for a cylinder whose axis lies along the flat face's normal; coaxial cylinders |r₁ − r₂|; a face with itself: none for a flat face or a hole's wall, 2r for a convex cylinder or sphere, min(2r, 2(R − r)) for a convex torus, 2(R − r) for a concave one, 0 for anything else; otherwise the mesh's distance of the two faces over the triangle pairs where the line could come out, less what each face can be off by. Pairs are taken by their boxes' distance until that is above the bound so far (at most 200 000 pairs, 20 million triangle pairs; past a cap the boxes bound the rest). The places that bound points to (a corner nearest the other face's plane and its foot on that plane, the nearest corners) are tried too, and the least few samples on different faces (8) are then moved along their face by a pattern search (eight directions in the tangent plane, the step halved when none is better, down to a tenth of the tolerance) to where the thickness is least. The least found is a thickness at a real place; the bound is what no place can be below. A line from near an acute edge (a hole entering a round at a slant) is as short as the edge is sharp: the ray method's thickness there tends to 0, which is what it is; measure from chosen faces (`of` a query of faces) to leave such edges out. Measured from a set of faces only those are sampled: the top of a plate gives the plate's thickness although a hole's wall near the edge is thinner.
- **Ray** (`measure.ray`): `line_hits` per body, merged by distance; entering where the outward normal faces against the ray.
- **Goes through** (`measure.through`): for a cylindrical or conical face, its axis, its extent along it and its narrowest circle (a cylinder's radius; a cone's least distance of its edges from the axis); the point of the axis in the middle of that extent must be outside the solid (inside: the face is a boss, not a hole). Then the whole bore must be clear, not one line: a cylinder on the axis of the narrowest radius less a margin (ten tolerances, or a millionth of the radius), as long as the body along the axis and beyond, must have no point in common with the body (`craft3d_kernel::boolean::interference`, the boolean's exact intersection). Where it has, `blocked` gives the middle of the common volume (or the contact points) and the body's faces around it. A blind hole with a coaxial vent and a bore a rib blocks a third of are not through, though their axes are clear; a counterbore's own face is not through (its step blocks its radius: ask about the bore's face). This is "light passes through": a hole through a plate with more of the same body further along its axis is not through. One boolean per face.
- **Symmetry** (`measure.symmetry`): every vertex of the mesh of the faces, and every triangle's middle put on its surface (a cylinder's mesh has no vertex inside the face), is mirrored in the plane, and the distance from the mirror image to the nearest face of the set is found exactly; the largest is the deviation (default bound ten times the document's tolerance). Every face is mirrored, so a feature on one side only is found from its own faces, which land in the material or the air of the other side. Planes: `xy`, `yz`, `zx`, a datum plane, or a point and a normal.

### Assertions

An assertion is document data (`craft3d_doc::Assertion`, format 28): a name, what is measured (the measure's name and parameters as a canonical JSON text, subjects as `select.query` filters with the features they name by name written as ids, so renaming a feature keeps it measured) and what it must be (`Expect`: `Is(bool)`, `Equal {value, tol}` or `Within {min, max}`; values are expressions in the document's variables, renamed with them, so `"eq": "t"` follows the variable `t` like a formula). Not part of the history: regeneration does not read them and they never stop a feature from being built.

The measures: `volume`, `area` (of bodies or faces), `mass` (bodies with a material, kg), `count` (the number of things a query finds), `extent` (the size of the bodies' box along x, y or z), `distance`, `clearance`, `thickness`, `through`, `interference` (none of the bodies overlap) and `symmetric`. A plain number is in the measure's own unit (document units, their square or cube; kg); an expression with units is converted. In the expression language a power binds to the number with its unit: `"50 cm^3"` is (50 cm)³, so a bound written so would pass silently. That form (a number, a unit, `^`) is refused wherever a measure or an assertion reads an expression, with the form meant: `50 * (1 cm)^3`, or the plain number.

**Proven, not found.** A measure gives what it found and, where it searched for a least (a thickness, a distance, a clearance), bounds proven on the true value: `atLeast` (nothing is below it) and the found value (a real place, so the least is no more). A bound holds when the whole of that range is within it (`min` against `atLeast`, `max` against the found value), fails when none of it is (a real place thinner than `min`: failed for certain), and is otherwise **not proven**: a third state, shown like a warning (`notProven`, never passed), with the value found and what is proven. What is measured exactly (volume, area, mass, count, extent) has both ends equal. A bound is met within the document's tolerance (of a length; its square or cube for an area or a volume, and a millionth of a millionth of the bound for rounding).

**Checking.** What an assertion depends on is its key: its text and bounds, the units and tolerance, the values of the variables its expressions name, the bodies (by their `Arc`, which regeneration keeps for a feature an edit did not touch) and faces its subjects name now (and every body when it has no subject), and a datum plane's place. The engine works the keys out per revision, cheaply (the subjects' queries), and measures only the assertions whose key it has no answer for, on a thread of its own, one job at a time (`assert_check.rs`; in a browser in line); the answers stay by key (beyond the current ones, up to 256), so an edit that leaves an assertion's subjects alone, or an undo that brings the same bodies back, measures nothing. Whoever asks says how long it waits: the window never (an assertion being measured is *pending*, drawn as inactive, and the window asks again every quarter second), an edit's result two seconds (then `pending` counts what is still measured), `document.inspect` two seconds, `assert.add`, `assert.edit`, `assert.list` (`wait: false` not at all) and `document.check` until every answer is in. Each check is caught on its own: one that fails to measure (its subject names nothing, the geometry refused) is in *error*, which counts as broken; one that panics is an error that says so. A measured number is compared with the bounds as above (eq ± tol: tol defaults to a millionth of the value, at least ten times the document's tolerance for a length, 0 for a count).

**Names that change.** Features a subject names by name are stored as ids, and so are those named by a subject written before the feature existed, at the moment a feature is renamed (`feature.rename` writes them as ids first). `variable.rename` renames the variable in the bounds and in the expressions inside a measure (a symmetry plane's point, its `tol`).

**Where a broken one shows**, like a failed feature: in the result of the command whose edit broke it (`assertions: {count, passed, broken: [{name, status, measured, unit, expected, message}], unproven, pending}` on every feature command and `variable.set`), in `document.check` (a problem per broken assertion, `ok` false; a warning per assertion not proven, `ok` unchanged; and the summary), in `document.inspect` (`assertions`, each with its status), in `assert.list`, and in the window's tree (a section *Assertions* under the features, failed ones in the failed colour with their message) and `ui.get` (`assertions`). An assertion may be written before the model meets it, or before its subject exists: it is in error until then.

### Bounds

At most 10 000 items per subject, 200 bodies per geometric measure (10 000 for volume, area and mass), 500 pairs of bodies per clearance or interference, 4 million pairs of faces and 60 million pairs of triangles per distance, 6000 samples per body for a thickness and per symmetry, 200 000 pairs of faces and 20 million pairs of triangles for a thickness's bound, 16 places settled exactly, 400 steps per settling, 1000 ray hits listed, 1000 assertions per document, 16 KiB per stored measure. Past a bound the answer is an error that says what to narrow.

### Proof over the range

A Jig (a document with public parameters, [`document.md`](document.md) › *Public parameters and document info*) must give a valid model at every value its form lets through, not only at the values its author tried. `document.sample {samples, seed, corners, maxCorners, choices, deep, budgetMs, timeLimitMs, maxFailures, values}` (`crates/engine/src/sample_cmds.rs`) checks it over its range, on a copy: the open document, its revision and its undo history do not change.

- **Points, in a fixed order.** The defaults; the corners of the box of the ranges (each parameter that moves at its low or high end: a number or integer at its min or max, a side not given being the default; a boolean off or on; a choice its first or last), all 2^n while that is at most `maxCorners` (default 64, at most 4096), else that many chosen by the seed, all-low and all-high first; every other choice of each choice parameter with the rest at the defaults; then `samples` (default 32, at most 10 000) random points. Points are made one at a time as the run reaches them. A number without min and max is only tried at its default and listed under `unbounded`.
- **A point is a fraction per parameter**, worked into a value against the ranges *at that point*: a number `lo + u (hi − lo)`, on its step from `lo` when it has one; an integer one of the whole numbers (on its step) in the range, uniformly; a choice or a boolean one of its values, uniformly. A range that uses other parameters (`max: 2 * d`) is evaluated after them, again until no value moves (at most once per parameter and once more), so a point always lies in the ranges it implies; ranges that keep pushing each other on (`a ≤ b`, `b ≤ a + 1`, both at their high end) fail the point with *did not settle after N rounds* and the last values. A value on a step is held to `[lo, hi]`: `-0.3 + 3 × 0.1` is 5.6e-17, and the range `[-0.3 mm, 0]` ends at 0. Values are written as `param.set` takes them, in the parameter's unit (`12.5 mm`, `20`), and listed with every failing point, so `param.set {values}` reproduces it.
- **Randomness** is SplitMix64 seeded by `seed` (rule 5), one stream for a subset of corners and one for the random points (the seed with a constant of each mixed in), so the random points are the same whatever `corners` and `maxCorners` are: the same seed gives the same points and the same verdict on every platform. Times are reported per point (`ms`) and summed up (`timing`: median, max, total, the slowest point) but are part of the verdict only with `budgetMs`. In the browser there is no clock: `ms` is null and `budgetMs` is refused.
- **Each point** is set as `param.set` sets it (`param_cmds::prepare_set`: a point it refuses, such as a range that is empty there, fails with its reason), regenerated in a session of its own with a regeneration cache of its own and the host's budget of bodies (nothing of the open document's cache is reused; not through `Session::execute`, so recovery does not see it). It is not quite from nothing: the kernel's trees of freeform surfaces are shared within the process, so a surface the open document has met may be quicker; after each point the trees no open document holds are let go of (`Session::release_trees_now`), and checked by `document.check` (`deep` with `deep`): failed variables and features, invalid bodies, invalid parameters, broken assertions. The bodies are measured (mass properties from the exact faces, which shows an unbounded or unmeasurable face) at the defaults and the corners, where the extremes of the range are, and, with `deep`, also at the choice and random points: measuring a part with many curved faces costs several times its regeneration (the welding table Jig, 65 points: 9.3 s wall and 47 s CPU with every point measured, 2.4 s and 3.0 s without, 2026-10-23). A feature whose body is a solid at the defaults must have a solid at the point too.
- **A limit on the whole run:** `timeLimitMs`, checked between points (the first point is always checked), 60 s when not given, so a window or an agent over the control channel or MCP waits no longer; `null` is no limit, as `craft3d-cli check-range` runs it. In the browser, which has no clock, a run stops after 128 points instead. A run that stops says `complete: false` and `stoppedBy` (`timeLimit`, `pointCap`), and its `ok` is false when a point failed and null otherwise, never true.
- **Bounds:** 10 000 random points, 4096 corners, 4096 choice points, `maxFailures` (default 100) failing points listed with their values and problems (`failed` counts all, `truncated` says when there are more).

`craft3d-cli check-range FILE [--samples N] [--seed S] [--deep] [--budget-ms M] [--max-corners N] [--no-corners] [--json]` (and `--time-limit-ms M`; no limit by default) runs it on a file and prints a report (or the result as JSON): the check a Jig repository's CI runs. Exit codes: 0 every point passed, 1 a point failed, 2 a usage error (also `--samples` above 10 000 or `--max-corners` above 4096), 3 the file could not be opened or checked (no public parameters, say), 4 the run stopped at its time limit with no point failing so far.

## Findings and history

- **2026-10-22, slice 1.** The queries above and assertions; format 28 (27 went to named sets, merged first). Oracles (`crates/engine/tests/verification.rs`): the distance between blocks and from a block's edge to a cylinder at scales 0.01, 1 and 1000 to 1e-9 relative; clearance clear, touch, inside and crossing; the thickness of a drilled plate (7 = 10 − 3 from a hole to the edge, 8 from the top), of a ligament of 1 and of a tube of 2 to 1e-6; a ray through a plate and across a hole (six hits at closed-form distances); holes through, blind after the plate is made 16 thick (blocked at the hole's own end), a boss refused as a hole; symmetry of a part and a one-sided bore breaking it; the housing showcase symmetric about its mirror plane and front to back (spline sides), not top to bottom. The developer's case reproduced: the housing's `HalfBase` extruded 16 mm instead of 8, the bolt holes still 20 mm high from z = −6, breaks "the bolt holes go through" in the edit's own result (blocked at z = 14) while the feature stays ok; holes 30 mm high pass again.
- First thickness test expected 8 for the drilled plate and got 7: the holes are 10 from the edge with radius 3, so the wall between a hole and the edge is the thinnest place. The measure was right; the test's closed form was not.
- The first symmetry sampled only mesh vertices and called a block with a one-sided bore symmetric: a cylinder's mesh has vertices only on its two circles, which mirror onto the top and bottom faces. Triangle middles put on the surface sample the inside of every face.

- **2026-10-22, review of slice 1** (three checks could pass a design that did not meet them). (1) A `min` thickness passed on the least of a sample: a plate pocketed to 3 with a 45° groove leaving 1.5 and ten pockets leaving 2.5 reported 2.5 and passed `min 2`; now the proven bound decides, the bound's places are searched (the groove's 1.5 is found and the assertion fails), and what is neither is not proven. (2) `through` cast one line down the axis: a blind hole with a 0.3 vent and a bore a rib blocks a third of passed; now the whole bore must be clear (a cylinder of the bore's radius against the body, exactly). (3) `max "50000 mm^3"` read as (50000 mm)³ passed on a 1e6 mm³ block; the form is refused. (4) Every edit measured every assertion on the caller's thread (the pier: clearance of 20 × 25 bodies 163 s, thickness of 200 bodies 352 s, in the window at the first sync); now by key, off the caller's thread, pending until done. On the housing a whole-body thickness takes about 7 s (lines against spline faces) and finds 7e-6 mm where the oil holes enter the sleeve at a slant: a knife edge.

- **2026-10-23, proof over the range** (Jigs phase 4). The laser box (`plan/laserbox.md`) with nine public parameters (width 100–300 mm on 1 mm steps, depth, height, thickness 2–6 mm, finger width 8–20 mm, kerf, clearance, thumb and lid-hole radii): 73 points (64 of 512 corners, 8 random) all pass, regeneration median 180 ms, max 418 ms (optimised debug build); deep on 13 points passes. The welding table (`plan/weldtable.md`, without welds) with width 24–48 in, depth 24–36 in, kerf, clearance and tabs per cell 1–3: 37 points (all 32 corners) pass, median 75 ms, max 198 ms. Corners repeat where one range ends at another's value (`w` from 10 mm to `2 * d` with `d` at 5 mm): they are checked twice rather than told apart.

- **2026-10-23, a choice from a table was not varied.** A choice parameter whose labels come from a table (`choicesFrom`) counted as having no choices in the ranges the run reads (`param_cmds::ranges` took the length of the parameter's own `choices`, empty for it): it did not move, so `craft3d-cli check-range` on the pot knob reported 0 choice points and kept the shaft at its default in every corner and random point. The count is now `Document::choices_of` (the table's rows), like the form's. Test: `jig_sample.rs` (`a_choice_from_a_table_is_varied_like_a_list_of_choices`: corners at the first and last row, every other row a choice point, every row among the random points).
