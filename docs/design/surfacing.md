# 3DCraft design: Freeform surfacing (curve networks)

Sheets through networks of curves: the Coons patch of four boundary curves, the Gordon surface of u- and v-curves that cross, lofts along guide curves, and edges held in position on a face or tangent (G1) to one. Code: `crates/geom/src/network.rs` (the geometry), `crates/geom/src/network/continuity.rs` (the proven continuity measure), `crates/regen/src/network.rs` (curves and faces from the history, the sheet body and its names), `crates/engine/src/surface_cmds.rs` (`feature.surface.network`, `feature.surface.coons`), `crates/engine/src/continuity_cmds.rs` (`measure.continuity`), `crates/regen/src/loft.rs` › `guided` (`feature.loft` with `guides`). The plan, with stages 1 (analysis), 3 (G2) and 4 (blends, n-sided corners), is `plan/class-a.md`; this is stages 2 and 3, and stage 1 is [*Stage 1: analysis*](#stage-1-analysis) below.

## Method

**Exact, never fitted.** Every result is a B-spline surface built from the curves by exact operations on homogeneous control points (`[x w, y w, z w, w]`): knot insertion, degree elevation, Bézier pieces, composition with a polynomial, Bernstein products, interpolation at given parameters. Nothing is sampled into being, so there is no approximation whose error would need a bound. Arcs and circles take part as rational curves (`network::arc`: quarter-turn rational quadratic pieces, end weights 1).

**Curves.** A network curve is a sketch's one open chain or edges of earlier bodies end to end (`doc::Rail`, as a developable's rails; `regen::developable::rail_of`). Its pieces become one spline (`network::chain`): every piece raised to one degree, its homogeneous points scaled so it meets the piece before in four dimensions, and where two pieces meet tangentially (within the angular tolerance plus the direction a tolerance's worth of position makes over the shorter piece, `angular + tol / shorter`: what a joint that meets within the tolerance may differ by; a corner taken for smooth only has its speeds evened, which is harmless) each piece gets the length of its control polygon as its share of the parameter and its end speeds brought to that rate by the exact Hermite reparametrisation of `refine::reparametrized` (three times its degree), so the joined curve is once differentiable in its parameter, as a single spline is. Corners stay corners.

**The Gordon surface** (`network::gordon`; Gordon 1969, Piegl & Tiller §10.5):
1. *Crossings.* Every u-curve must cross every v-curve within the tolerance: closest points by Gauss-Newton on `|C(s) − D(t)|²` from the best pair of samples (8 per knot span, at least 32). Each curve is turned to run from the other family's first curve to its last; it must cross the other family in order, and end on the first and last curve of the other family (those bound the sheet).
2. *Common crossing parameters.* A family's crossing parameters are the means over its curves. A curve crossed elsewhere is cut at its crossings and each piece composed with the increasing cubic taking the common parameters to its own; the slopes at the crossings are the harmonic means of the neighbouring secants (Fritsch and Butland), so every cubic's Bernstein slopes are at most 2 and it increases, and the curve stays once differentiable in its parameter. Exact: the same points at three times the degree. A Bernstein slope `α` is the factor by which the curve's speed at that crossing falls below its mean over the piece; a direction computed from so small a derivative carries a relative rounding error of about `ε/α`, and the rounding of a point is ε times its coordinates' size, so below `α = ε · max(1, |P| / c) / angular` (`|P|` the largest of the curve's points, `c` the piece's chord: scale-free; crossings spaced far more unevenly than the family's) the curve is refused by name. Its size once re-parametrised is counted first (its own knot spans plus the crossings, three times the degree) and refused above a curve's `MAX_CONTROL_POINTS`; the pieces are composed after cutting them with knots inserted all at once (`composed_batched`).
3. *Weights.* A rational curve may be multiplied by a constant without changing. The constants that make crossing curves agree in their homogeneous points solve `ln rᵢ − ln sⱼ = ln w_v(v̄ᵢ) − ln w_u(ūⱼ)` in closed form (row and column means of a two-way table). Where the table has a residual no constants make every pair agree; there is no threshold on it: what it costs is proven on the result (*Checked*, below) and held to the tolerance. A curve that is a point (a pole) takes the weights of its neighbour in its family: a point's weight function is free.
4. *Boolean sum.* `S = A + B − T`: the u-curves interpolated across v at the common parameters (degree `min(3, count − 1)`, knots by averaging), the v-curves across u likewise, minus the tensor interpolant of the crossing points (the v-curves' points there, so the u-curves are reproduced exactly and the v-curve `j` as `V_j + Σᵢ Lᵢ(v)(a_ij − b_ij)`, moved by the interpolant of the differences of the homogeneous crossing points). The three are brought to one degree and knot vector each way and added. The sum reproduces exactly any surface that lies in the spaces of both interpolations: a bilinear patch from its sides, a tensor B-spline from its iso-curves at parameters whose averages are its knots, the rational cylinder and sphere octant from their circles and lines.
5. *The size, before it is made.* Every curve re-parametrised for alignment brings its own knot preimages (at three times its degree), and a family takes the union of its curves' knots: about `(n + m·k)·3p` control points each way for `m` curves of `k` knots crossing `n`. So the size of each net (control points `nu × nv`) is computed from the knots alone (raising a degree leaves every inner knot `degree` times, so no curve needs raising to count it) before the skins are made, again before the nets are brought to one description and before each tangent edge's refinement, and refused above `MAX_NETWORK_POINTS` = 2²⁰ (32 MB of homogeneous points per net) with the reason. Knots are inserted all at once (Boehm–Prautzsch refinement, Piegl & Tiller A5.4; `Raw::insert_all`, `elevate_batched`, `bezier_pieces_batched`), so making a net costs its size times its degree: the budget bounds time and memory both. Finding the crossings compares every pair of samples of every pair of curves (8 per knot span), refused above 2²⁸ pairs. A 32 × 32 grid of 30-point curves, each crossed at its own parameters, is refused in seconds instead of taking tens of gigabytes; the blow-up itself is not reduced (owed: an approximation within half the tolerance with a proven bound, or preimages shared within the family).

**The Coons patch** is the Gordon surface of two curves each way (the bilinearly blended Coons patch); `feature.surface.coons` takes four curves round the patch.

**Edges held.** Each of the four edges (`uFirst`, `uLast`, `vFirst`, `vLast`) is free, in position on a face (its curve lies on the face: checked at 64 places per knot span and more, refused beyond the tolerance), or tangent to a face:
- *The crossing curves leave along the face.* Each curve crossing the edge gets its end tangent projected into the face's tangent plane there, keeping its speed, times the edge's weight: the control point beside the end moves (the curve raised to degree 3 at least, a knot inserted so that point's basis function ends before the next crossing, so nothing else of the curve changes). A curve that is itself a held edge keeps its shape and must already leave along the face (else the corner is refused).
- *The side.* Each crossing curve's direction there, against the edge's own, about the face's normal: the sine `((Ĉ′ × t̂) · m)` gives the side of the edge it leaves to along the face. Within the angular tolerance of zero (square to the face, or along the edge) it is refused; curves that leave to opposite sides (a patch that would fold over its edge, its normal turned over between them) are refused by name; the side is what the result is checked against.
- *The cross derivative put into the tangent planes.* After the sum, along the edge (the net turned so the edge is `v = 0`): the target cross derivative is `E(u) = Σ aₖ(u) Fₖ(u) + b(u) C′(u) + c(u) C(u)` in homogeneous terms, `C` the edge curve and `Fₖ` exact fields whose projections span the face's tangent planes along it: for a plane its two axes (constant); for a cylinder its axis and `d × (A − w o)`; for a cone `A − w·apex` and `d × (A − w o)`; for a sphere `eₖ × (A − w c)`; for a spline face the edge must run along one of its parameter lines, and the edge curve is replaced by that line (exactly, cut to the edge) with the face's homogeneous derivative across it as the field, both carried through every change of parameter. The scalars interpolate, at the crossings, the least-squares coefficients of the derivative the surface already has there (which lies in the plane, by the first step), found scale-free: the homogeneous points written about the edge's first point with the weight measured in the extent of the first two rows (`[x − c w, …, L w]`, so every coordinate is a length), the columns of unit length, Householder QR with column pivoting, a column within `max(angular, tol / L)` of the others' span (the edge lies on its face within the tolerance, so its direction may leave the face's planes by that much) taken as dependent and given no coefficient (the basic solution: no regularisation biases it, dependent columns cannot blow it up). What is left of the derivative outside the planes is taken off the second row there, which moves that crossing curve's second control point: refused where that move exceeds the tolerance. Between the crossings the change is what puts the interpolated derivative into the planes, of the order of its drift off them (a fraction of the ribbon between the edge and the second row; not held to the tolerance, which would refuse every curved edge); a column kept just above the cutoff could get coefficients of about `1 / cutoff`, so a change of a second-row control point larger than its distance from the edge (plus the tolerance) is refused. Then `E − S_v(u, 0)` (an exact spline, by Bernstein products) is added to the second row of control points times `(t_{q+1} − t₁)/q`, with the degree across at least 3 and the knots across arranged so that row's basis function ends before the next u-curve: every curve of the network stays on the surface, every other edge keeps its position and cross derivative.
- *Checked.* The angle between the oriented tangent planes is then found along every tangent edge (32 places per knot span, 64 at least; reported as `tangency`): the patch's `along × into` against the face's normal turned to the side, `atan2(|n × m|, n · m)` from 0 to π, so a normal turned over counts as far off as it is. Where the two derivatives are within the angular tolerance of parallel, or either vanishes, the patch has no tangent plane there and the edge is refused (only an end where the crossing family's first or last curve is a pole is passed). The construction is exact, so the angular tolerance itself is the limit; above it the feature is refused. That is where two held edges meeting at a corner show a twist conflict.
- *Every curve against the result.* Each curve of the network, as the surface was built through it (after the bending), is compared with the surface's own iso-curve there, a proven bound: with `S = V + D` in homogeneous terms the distance is `|D_xyz − p D_w| / w_S`, at most `(max |D_k,xyz − c D_k,w| + R max |D_k,w|) / min w_S,k` by the convex hulls (`R` the reach of `V`'s points from `c`). It takes in the crossing gaps, the weights that no constants could make agree, the interpolation's amplification across, and what a tangent edge's change moved; it is reported as `mismatch` and held to the tolerance (refused beyond, the curve named).

**Edges held in curvature (G2).** `Condition::Curvature` (`"kind": "curvature"`) is a tangent edge whose patch also takes the face's normal curvature across it. With the tangent plane shared along the edge, the normal curvature along the edge and the twist agree by themselves (the normals agree along the curve, so their derivative along it does), so G2 is one more number per point: the normal curvature in the direction the patch leaves the edge. In homogeneous terms it is linear in the second derivative across, `X₂`:
- *A plane* `L·X = 0`, a *quadric* `XᵀQX = 0` (cylinder, cone, sphere; `Q` written about the edge's first point, so nothing cancels far from the origin): `G(X(u, v)) = O(v³)` along the edge, which given G0 and G1 is `N·X₂ = r` with `N = L`, `r = 0`, or `N = Q X₀`, `r = −X₁ᵀQX₁`.
- *A spline face* `Φ` along its parameter line: the patch is `λ Φ(σ, τ)` to second order, so `X₂ − T` lies in the span of `C, C′, F` (the edge `C = κ Φ(s(u), t₀)`, `F = Φ_t` along it), `T = b² C″ + 2ab F′ + (a²/κ) G`, `G = Φ_tt` along the line, with `X₁ = a F + b C′ + c C`. `Φ_tt` comes from the face's net (the across splines differentiated twice) and is carried through every change of parameter, as `F` is.

Three steps, as for G1:
1. *The crossing curves.* After the end tangent is set, the third control point of each curve crossing the edge is moved along the face's normal so that the curve's `N·X₂ = r` holds (one linear equation, one unknown), with a knot inserted so that its basis function ends before the next crossing (`localize_rows`, three rows instead of two).
2. *Along an analytic face, the third row by constrained least squares.* `Δ` (the change of `X₂`) must satisfy `N·Δ = ρ = r − N·X₂` as splines, exactly: the equation of the Bernstein coefficients of every piece between the edge's knots and crossings, at degree `deg N + D`. `Δ` is sought in splines of degree `D = max(P, ⌈(P + 11)/3⌉, deg ρ)` (`P` the edge's; at least `ρ`'s, since the edge may be a curve of lower degree raised and `N·Δ` must reach every term of `ρ` whatever `N`'s true degree: found on a dome over a rational arc raised to degree 4, where a smaller `D` left the equations unsolvable by 1e-5), twice differentiable at every break (the net's own knots, and the crossings that are not one of them, taken as the net has them so that no two knots lie within rounding of each other), which leaves more unknowns (`4(D − 2)` per break) than independent equations (at most `P + D + 3`, counting the four of a crossing); at every crossing `Δ` takes the least change that puts that crossing curve right (after step 1 that is rounding; a move of the third control point beyond the tolerance is refused). Of all such `Δ` the one of least size (in lengths about the edge's first point, its weight times its extent) is taken: Householder QR of the equations' transpose with the equations pivoted, an equation within the angular tolerance of the others' span taken as dependent (the rule of the tangent coefficients), `Rᵀ y = b`, `Δ = Q y`. The work is capped (`MAX_CURVATURE_WORK`, 2³³ multiplications) and refused beyond. Only the third row of control points changes, and its basis function across ends before the next u-curve: the first two rows (G0, G1) and every other curve of the network stay. As for the second row, a change of a third-row control point larger than its distance from the edge (plus the tolerance) is refused (the ribbon bound, both holds). With no curve between two held edges of a family, a curvature edge keeps its rows to the first half (three rows at each end need six).
3. *Along a spline face, by fields.* `X₂ = T + α C + β C′ + γ F`: `T` is exact (Bernstein products of `hold_tangent`'s own scalars `a`, `b` with the fields), the scalars `α, β, γ` interpolate at the crossings the parts of the derivative the surface already has there (scale-free least squares, as for G1), and the change goes on the third row. Exact everywhere, since the span terms do not change `N·X₂`.

*The seam's parametrisation.* Along a spline face the family crossing the edge takes its common crossing parameters from the face's line (the line cut to the edge, over `[0, 1]`) instead of the means, so the line needs no change of parameter and the patch's edge is the face's line in one parametrisation: what the measure compares at. Only one edge of a family can dictate that; two edges of one family held in curvature to spline faces are built where their crossings agree (*Findings on the modern mouse*, below), and refused otherwise (owed: comparing through the change of parameter). Edges held only tangent keep the means, so G1 results are as before.

*Kinks of a curvature hold* (2026-11-01). Along a spline face the second derivative across that the face asks for is only continuous at the face's simple knots (`b² C″` of a cubic line among it: its derivative along the edge jumps there), and spread across the sheet by the fair profile `G(v)` that kink became one in the sheet's derivative along the edge wherever `G` reaches: a crease inside the sheet, of the angle `|Δδ′| G(v) / |S_u|` (the fuel tank's side: 2.6e-3 across its middle, along the crown's knots; its offset tore by that angle times the distance and was refused as not approximated). No sheet avoids it altogether (a derivative along that stays continuous for every `v > 0` has `S_uvv` continuous up to the edge, and the edge asks for one that jumps), but it can be kept small: the change is split into a twice differentiable cubic on the kinks (through the change at its Greville points, each crossing in place of the nearest, so the network's curves stay) spread as before, and what is left, which holds the kinks and is small, spread over a band beside the edge (`kinks_apart`), halved from the reach until the crease it leaves (its profile is `O(band²)`) is within a quarter of the angular tolerance, but no narrower than the rows' rounding allows: across spans `h` the seam's second derivative is the rows' points over `h²`, and the halving stops where their rounding (8 ε of their size over `h²` and the speed across squared) would reach a quarter of the measure's curvature threshold (the tank's corner H 190, L 680, W 300 measured 1.4e-9 per mm against 1.1e-9 with a band of 1/256). The sum at the edge is the change asked, exactly: the seam is as before. A change without kinks of consequence (every analytic face) is spread as it was. **Removed at its source, on request** (`acrossDegree`, format 62): a network interpolated across with degree 5 (`feature.surface.network {"acrossDegree": 5}`, `network::gordon_across`) whose curves are of degree 5 too is four times differentiable along its curves' crossings, so a sheet held in curvature to it sees no kink. The default stays 3: every saved network is built as before (the mesh digests of the 141 example, showcase and benchmark exports are identical with the field left out). *Checked.* Every edge held in curvature is measured on the result by `continuity` (below), between the patch and its face, and refused unless G2 is proven; the report is `Patch::seams` (and `network.continuity` in the feature's result).

**The continuity measure** (`network::continuity`, `measure.continuity`, the assertion and output measure `continuity`). For an edge, the order reached (G0, G1, G2) and the largest gap in position, in the angle between the tangent planes and in normal curvature across the edge, each with a *proven* bound over the whole edge, the largest found (where), and its threshold:
- *Rows.* One face is a spline the edge runs along a parameter line of: its point `X₀`, `X₀′` and the first and second derivatives across `X₁`, `X₂` are exact splines along the line cut to the edge (`iso_track` with the second row). The other is implicit (plane, quadric) or a spline along a parameter line in the same parametrisation (rows `Y₀, Y₀′, Y₀″, Y₁, Y₁′, Y₂`); where the two lines do not correspond, the position gap shows it.
- *Residuals.* Each gap is `|R(u)| / D(u)`, `R` a polynomial on every piece between the rows' knots that vanishes where the faces meet to that order (built by Bernstein products of the rows' coefficients), `D` a product of positive polynomials and norms of vector polynomials. Position: the exact distance to a plane, sphere or cylinder (`|XᵀQX| / (w(|P(x − cw)| + Rw))`), to a cone's generatrix in the point's meridian plane, or between corresponding points of two splines; each is an upper bound on the distance to the face. Angle: `|W × G| / (|W||G|)`, `W = V₀′ × V₁` the patch's normal (times `w⁴`), `G` the face's normal (`(QX₀)_xyz`, which for a sphere and a cylinder points along the normal at the nearest point, and for a cone is the normal of the quadric's level surface through the point; `W_b` for a spline). Curvature: against an implicit face the second-order contact `h″ = d²/dv² g(S)` over `|∇g| |S_v|²`, in homogeneous terms `P₂ = H₂w² − 4ww₁H₁ + (6w₁² − 2ww₂)H` (`H = XᵀQX` and its derivatives across; `L·X` likewise for a plane), which is exactly the difference of the normal curvatures in the direction the patch leaves the edge wherever the tangent planes agree; against a spline face `n_b·S_vv − II_b(S_v)`, with `S_v` put into its tangent plane by Cramer's rule and `II_b` through `det[Y, Y_a, Y_t, Y_ab] = −ω⁴ (B_a × B_t)·B_ab`, over `|S_v|²`, one polynomial over a product of norms (numerator degree `4P + 20q`, held below `MAX_SEAM_DEGREE` = 384).
- *Which points are compared* (2026-11-01, the coordinator's decision). A point of `a`'s boundary corresponds to a point of `b`'s own boundary; each face's derivatives are taken at its own boundary, as continuity is defined in CAD. How far apart the boundaries lie, in any direction, is the position gap (G0); it is not carried into the higher orders as if `b` were evaluated off its boundary, so `b`'s variation across the seam is excluded by that definition. What is carried is the shift along the seam. The rows put the faces side by side at the same parameter; the point of `b`'s boundary that is the same seam point as `X_a(s)` is its nearest within the edge (the edge's ends clamp it) and within the piece and as far into each neighbour as the piece is long. It is no farther from `X_a(s)` than `X_b(s)`, so `|X_b(s*) − X_b(s)| ≤ 2B` (`B` the piece's position bound, which a half keeps from its parent). With `m ≤ |X_b′|`, `M₂ ≥ |X_b″|` over the piece and its neighbours and `M₂ ℓ ≤ m` over that reach `ℓ`, `Δ = |s* − s| ≤ 4B / (m + √(m² − 4 M₂ B))`, about `2B / m` (`shift_window`; else the whole reach, and halving narrows it). Within a piece each second-order gap `R(s, τ) = N / (m^{5/2} g)` (`m = |W_b|²` of `b` alone, `g` of `a` alone) gains `Δ sup |∂_τ R(s, s)| + Δ²/2 sup |∂²_τ R|`: the first a polynomial formed by forward differentiation along `b`'s rows (dual numbers on the Bernstein polynomials, so its terms cancel as `N`'s do), the second from sizes alone (`Δ²` makes it small); the angle's sine gains `Δ sup |∂W_b| / inf |W_b|`. Where `s*` falls in the neighbour (for `s` within `Δ` of a break `k`), `|R(s, s*)| ≤ |R(k_P, k_N)| + Δ (D_τ + D_s + Δ C)`: the gaps of `a`'s rows at the break against `b`'s on the other side, the derivatives along `b` over the neighbour (with `a` at the break) and along `a` over the piece (with `b` at the break) as dual polynomials, and the mixed derivative from sizes; each side's rows are its own, so a knot where `b` is not C² (its curvature jumps) is counted, and one where it is C⁰ in its parameter but smooth in shape is not penalised (the gaps do not depend on the parameter). The angle adds `b`'s change of normal across the break. Against a quadric `g = 0` (Hessian `2A` of norm `H`: 2 for a sphere or cylinder, `2 max(1, tan² α)` for a cone) the face is a surface given whole, with no boundary curve of its own in the measure, so `a`'s point `x` corresponds to its foot point `y` on the face (`|x − y| ≤ B`, the position bound being a distance to the face); the rows compare with the level surface through `x`, which differs from the face at `y` in form by at most `H · H B / (G (G − H B))` (`G ≤ |∇g(x)|`; for a sphere or cylinder `B / (r (r − B))`, `r` the level surface's radius: about `B / R²`, and covering the closed form `|1/r − 1/R|`) and, for a cone only, in normal by `2 H B / G`, added to the angle and, times `a`'s `|S_xy| / (|S_x| |S_y|)`, to the second order; a plane's level surfaces are parallel planes and nothing is added. The values found are lower bounds less the same terms, and claim nothing within a window of a piece's ends. The closing arithmetic of these bounds is taken with `γ_{k+32}` of room, `k` the roundings counted on the longest chain of the sizes (`Sizes::k`, about 150 for the spline numerators).
- *Proven.* A polynomial lies within the convex hull of its Bernstein coefficients, so `max |R| / min D` over a piece is a bound (the lower bound of a norm is the square root of the least coefficient of its square). A piece whose bound does not decide is halved until it does or `MAX_SEAM_PIECES` (4096) are spent, each half cut straight from the piece of the edge it came from (the blossom at its ends, `Poly::piece_of`: one cut's rounding, `γ_{4n}` of the magnitude, at any depth); then that order is not reached, never guessed. The values found at five places per piece (and three per half) are lower bounds (rounding taken off): a gap found beyond its threshold proves the order is not reached. For an exact construction the residuals are rounding, so the first pieces decide.
- *Pieces.* Rows are cut into Bézier pieces between all their knots (both faces', as parameters of the edge): each piece is blossomed from the knot span of the row it lies in (`row_pieces`, de Boor's algorithm at the piece's two ends), in each face's own parameter along the line, and reversed exactly where the line runs against the edge. Nothing is reparametrised, so no knot is rounded; the gaps are invariant under an affine change of each face's parameter along the edge (each is a ratio of one order in the derivatives along, and `b`'s second fundamental form is taken on its own derivatives), so each face keeps its own scale and direction; the points compared must still correspond: they are taken at the same local parameter of each piece, and how far apart they are is the position gap. A derivative of a spline whose pieces join only in position (the exact products of the G1 hold join at C⁰) has a knot once more than its degree; a piece lies in one span, so that needs nothing special.
- *Rounding.* Each coefficient carries a bound on its error through every sum and product (running error analysis). The rows are formed from the stored net by differences first (`spline_rows`): the B-spline derivative nets across and along (`p (P_{k+1} − P_k) / Δ`), the first difference each way of the points as stored (`(P_b − P_a) w_b + (P_a − o)(w_b − w_a)`: for equal weights the second term is zero exactly and the first one rounding relative to the difference; for unequal weights the second adds `γ₄ |P_a − o| |w_b − w_a|`), then combined at the line with basis values that are nonnegative and sum to 1 (on a clamped border one is 1 and the others 0, exactly). Each coefficient's bound is carried with it: a difference adds `γ₁` of its result, a factor `p / Δ` `γ₃`, the combination at the line `γ_{7q+8}` of `Σ N̂ᵢ |Pᵢ|` (Cox–de Boor values within `γ_{6q}`, every quantity nonnegative), a piece's blossom `γ_{11p}` of its span's coordinates. So every row, the derivatives along the edge too, carries the rounding of its own size (the tank's finding 1). Each product of degrees `m` and `n` adds `γ_k`, `k = 4 (m + n) + min(m, n) + 8`, times the magnitudes.
- *Thresholds*, from the document: the tolerance; the angular tolerance; and for curvature `min(angular / L, 2 tol / L²)`, `L` the length of the edge's control polygon: a difference `Δκ` turns the faces' normals apart by `Δκ d` and opens a gap of `Δκ d² / 2` at a distance `d` from the seam, held within the angular tolerance and the tolerance over the seam's own length. G1-only and G2 pairs differ by orders more than that; a G2 patch disturbed by ten times the threshold in curvature is G1, by a hundredth of it still G2 (`geom/tests/network_g2.rs`).
- *As a number.* The assertion and output measure is the order: −1 none, 0 G0, 1 G1, 2 G2. It is proven at least that; at most it where the next gap was seen beyond its threshold, else not proven either way (so `min 2` on an undecided seam is *notProven*, not passed).

**Names** (rule 4). The face is `F9:side(k₁, k₂, k₃, k₄)` after the four edge curves (the first and last u-curve, the first and last v-curve), each by the id of its sketch, or of the feature of its first edge. Its edges are `F9:startEdge(f, k)` and `F9:endEdge(f, k)` (`f` 0 for the u family, 1 for v) after their curve; its corners `F9:startVertex(e, kᵤ, kᵥ)` on the first u-curve and `F9:endVertex(e, kᵤ, kᵥ)` on the last (`e` 0 at the first v-curve, 1 at the last). A pole is one vertex and no edge. Inserting a curve inside a family changes no name.

**A loft with guides** (`feature.loft {"guides": [...]}`): open sections as u-curves and the guides as v-curves of a network; the first and last guide are the sheet's edges. 2 to 64 of each; a section's `start` is refused (an open section is run the way the guides cross it), and the network's messages are told in sections and guides. `Document::validate` checks the guides as a network's curves (at most 64, at most 1024 edges each, every edge a name).

**Thickening and stitching.** A network sheet is a sheet body of one face: `feature.plate` makes it a solid (held to a quarter tube's closed-form volume; `feature.thicken` takes faces of solids only), and booleans cut with it as with any sheet. Sewing several network sheets into one body is `topo::sew` (the STEP importer's), not yet reachable as a command (*Limits*).

**The seam measure** (`network::seam_gaps`): the largest distance between two surfaces along a curve and the largest angle between their tangent planes, at given places, with the largest change between neighbouring places; an estimate, kept for the tests of stage 2. The proven measure is `continuity` (above).

## Limits

- No sweep along two rails yet. The method owed: copies of the profile placed by the similarity that takes its ends to the rails' points at stations along them, and the Gordon surface through the copies and the rails.
- A loft with guides takes open sections only (owed: a periodic Gordon surface for closed sections) and is not ruled or closed; `feature.edit` changes its sections, not its guides.
- Two held edges at one corner pass only where their faces ask the same twist there (their second fundamental forms agree along the corner's directions, as on one smooth face); otherwise the measured tangency refuses the feature. Owed: a corner blend (stage 4, Gregory or subdivided patches).
- Tangency to a torus is refused (owed: its rational form, as the trimmed faces write it). A spline face is held along any curve on it (*Holds and the measure along any curve on a spline face*). The parameter line is found from the edge projected at 4 places per knot span (33 to 1025); on a face closed in a direction the coordinates are read across its seam (the fixed one nearest the first place's, the running one followed from place to place by whole turns, `round((x − last) / period)`), and an edge along a line that crosses the seam is refused (owed: the iso-curve joined across the seam).
- Networks larger than the budget (*The size, before it is made*) are refused; the blow-up of re-parametrised families is not reduced yet.
- Networks of rational curves whose weights cannot be made to agree by constants are refused (owed: a common rational reparametrisation, or a polynomial approximation with a proven bound).
- The surface's degree grows: three times a curve's degree where its crossings need a new parameter, three times again across a smooth joint of a chain. Every curve reparametrised for alignment makes the whole family that degree.
- Sheets are sewn two at a time along the edges they share (`feature.boolean {"op": "sew"}`, 2026-10-31); sewing several at once, or sheets with loose entities, is owed.
- Curvature (G2) is held only where the patch's edge runs along one family's boundary: two edges of one family held in curvature to spline faces pass only where the crossing curves cross both lines at the same parameters (within what moves the second a tenth of the tolerance; owed: comparing through the change of parameter); two held edges at one corner pass only where the measure proves both seams G2 (owed: corner blends, stage 4).
- `continuity` needs one spline face the edge runs along a parameter line of; the other may be a spline face along any curve of it recovered from the edge (not a sampled projection). Two analytic faces (a plane meeting a cylinder) and a torus are refused (owed: an analytic face written across the edge; the torus's quartic form). A spline face against another spline face is compared at corresponding points, so both lines must share the parametrisation (the network's sheets do; otherwise the position gap refuses it; owed: comparing at the feet with a Lipschitz bound). For a cone the angle is to the normal of the quadric's level surface through the patch's point (the cone's own on it).
- The curvature hold along an analytic face solves a dense least-squares problem; its work is capped at 2³³ multiplications (an edge of hundreds of knots is refused). Degree: along an analytic face the patch's degree along the edge becomes at least `2P` (`deg ρ`); two edges of one family held in curvature compound it (the two-cylinder blend: degree 20 along); beyond `MAX_DEGREE` it is refused. Along a spline face it grows by up to 6 (`T`). Owed: a lower-degree `Δ` where `N`'s true degree is found (degree reduction tested exactly).

## Trimmed faces and N-sided fills

Built 2026-10-31 (trimfill). Two features close the gap the class-A mouse found: a face of an existing surface bounded by curves on it (`feature.surface.trim`), and a smooth patch inside a closed loop of 3 to 6 curves, four included (`feature.surface.nsided`; fewer than 3 or more than 6 are refused). Code: `crates/geom/src/trim.rs` (composition, the uv curves), `crates/geom/src/network/nsided.rs` (the fill), `crates/regen/src/trimfill.rs` (curves and faces from the history, bodies, names), `crates/engine/src/trimfill_cmds.rs` (`feature.surface.trim`, `feature.surface.nsided`). Tests: `geom/src/trim.rs` (composition against the surface along the curve, roots, refusals), `geom/tests/network_nsided.rs`, `engine/tests/surface_trim.rs`, `engine/tests/surface_nsided.rs`.

### A face on a surface, bounded by curves on it

**What it makes.** A sheet body of one face whose surface is the source face's surface itself (a clone: the exact NURBS or plane, never refitted), bounded by one outer loop and any holes. Each loop is curves end to end (each a sketch's chain or closed loop, or edges of earlier bodies, as a network's curves are taken), closed within the tolerance.

**Boundaries in parameter space, made exact on the surface.** A boundary is a curve `c(t) = (u(t), v(t))` in the surface's parameters; its edge is `S(c(t))`, which for a (rational) tensor-product B-spline `S` of degree `(p, q)` and a polynomial `c` of degree `d` is a (rational) B-spline of degree `d(p + q)`, computed exactly by functional composition (DeRose, Goldman, Hagen & Mann, *Functional composition algorithms via blossoming*, ACM TOG 12(2), 1993; here by Bernstein products, as `refine::bernstein_product`): `c` is cut where `u(t)` or `v(t)` crosses a knot of `S` inside the domain (the roots of `U − kW` on a piece, isolated by Bernstein subdivision: a piece whose coefficients share one strict sign has none, one with a single sign change is bisected on values; coefficients within an absolute floor, `1e-12 (|k| + range) max W`, count as zero, so a curve along a knot line crosses nothing instead of being chased to the depth limit, 2026-11-01). A curve that leaves the domain is refused by name wherever it does so, not only at its samples: `U − (u₀ − r) W`, `(u₁ + r) W − U` and the same in v must stay positive, proven by their Bernstein forms (all coefficients positive) or by the same root isolation (`r` a rounding reach), and on each piece, in the local parameters `(s, σ)` of the Bézier patch it lies in, `S(c(t)) = Σᵢⱼ Hᵢⱼ Bᵢᵖ(s(t)) Bⱼ^q(σ(t))` on homogeneous points `Hᵢⱼ`, each `Bᵢᵖ(s(t)) = (p choose i) s(t)ⁱ (1 − s(t))ᵖ⁻ⁱ` a Bernstein polynomial of degree `pd`. The pieces are joined with knots of full multiplicity (C⁰ in the representation, as smooth as `S∘c` is). So the edge lies on the surface to rounding, nothing is fitted, and the face's parameter-space region is exactly the one `c` bounds. Composed degrees above `MAX_DEGREE` (32) are refused with the degree named (owed: a cubic fit with a proven bound, the difference of two Bézier forms). Analytic faces: below.

**Where the uv curves come from.** Three ways, per loop:
- *In parameter space* (`"space": "parameter"`): a sketch read as the surface's parameter square, `x` as `u` and `y` as `v` in percent of the domain (`[0, 100]²`; percent and not `[0, 1]`, found at a million units: a unit square's sides were shorter than the document's tolerance, so the sketch had no profile): `c` is the sketch's curves mapped affinely, exactly; arcs as their rational quadratic pieces (a rational `c` composes the same way on homogeneous coordinates, degree `d(p + q)` with the weight).
- *Projected to the closest points* (`"space": "model"`, the default): each curve's points are taken to their closest points on the surface (`Surface::project_from`, walked from sample to sample), and `c` is the cubic interpolating those parameters at the curve's own parameters (`refine::interpolate`), with as many samples as make `S(c(t))` lie within half the tolerance of the projected curve at the midpoints between them (16, doubled until it does, at most 4096 per piece, refused beyond). Sampled, not proven: the edge is on the surface exactly, its distance from the true projection is checked at the midpoints and reported as `deviation` (owed: a bound from the projection's derivative).
- *Projected along a direction* (`"along": [x, y, z]`): as above with the line through each point along the direction met with the surface (Newton on `S(u, v) − P − λ d`, from the closest point), refused where a line misses or meets the surface at a grazing angle.
On a plane, projection is an affine map of the curve, so the edge is the projected curve exactly (control points mapped, weights kept); the plane takes no parameter-space boundaries (its domain is unbounded).

**Analytic faces by their rational forms** (2026-10-31). A cylinder, cone, sphere or torus is written as a rational B-spline that is the same surface (`trim::rational_form`: the surface of revolution of its meridian, Piegl & Tiller §8.5, the circle's rational quadratic quarter turns times the meridian's line or arc, weights multiplied), its turn starting opposite the curves' mean angle about the axis so that they lie inside its domain away from its seam; a sphere is written about a frame whose axis is square to the curves' mean direction, so they sit on its equator, away from its poles. The curves are projected onto that form and composed with it exactly (degrees `3·(2 + 1) = 9` on a cylinder or cone, `3·(2 + 2) = 12` on a sphere or torus); the face keeps its analytic surface, and its loops are turned by comparing the form's normal with the face's. Refused by name: curves that go round the axis (a loop encircling it), reach a cone's apex, come within 10° of the poles of any frame about their middle, or go round a torus's tube. Parameter-space curves are taken only on spline faces.

**Loops.** The pieces of a loop must meet end to end in parameter space within the tolerance (measured in space), and the loop is turned so that the outer loop runs counter-clockwise about the face's outward normal in the parameters (clockwise for a reversed face) and holes the other way, as `topo::check`'s region rule asks; a loop that crosses itself or the domain's border is refused by name. The face keeps the source face's `reversed`, so its normal is the source's.

**Names.** The face `F9:face(0)`; each edge after its curve, `F9:startEdge(k, s)` (`k` the sketch's id, or the hash of the boundary's first edge's name as the fill's keys, `s` the piece's source: the sketch curve's id, or the edge's place in its list); a vertex after the two pieces it joins, `F9:startVertex(k₁, s₁, k₂, s₂)`, the two pairs in ascending order so the name does not depend on which way the loop runs (roles a copy can carry: a transform or mirror of the face names its edges by them; `edge` and `vertex` take one number) (the same twice for a closed curve of one piece). Editing a boundary's shape renames nothing; replacing a curve by another renames only its edge and vertices.

### The N-sided fill

**Why not one patch.** A tensor-product surface has four sides: three or five sides in one patch need a degenerate corner (a pole) or a corner of 180°, and both fan the zebra stripes out. Gregory and Charrot–Gregory patches (Charrot & Gregory, *A pentagonal surface patch for computer aided geometric design*, CAGD 1, 1984) and Várady's transfinite patches (Várady, Rockwood & Salvi, *Transfinite surface interpolation over irregular n-sided domains*, CAD 43(11), 2011) are not tensor-product B-splines, so they would be approximated before they could be stored, measured by `continuity`, or written to STEP. 3DCraft uses the scheme of splitting the N-gon into N four-sided patches around a central point (Hahn, *Filling polygonal holes with rectangular patches*, in *Theory and Practice of Geometric Modeling*, Springer 1989; Gregory & Hahn, *A C² polygonal surface patch*, CAGD 6, 1989): every corner is regular (inner corners `2π/N` on average; the test holds the sine between a corner's two derivatives above 0.3), every patch an exact B-spline, nothing approximated.

**The layout.** The loop's curves `E₀ … E_{N−1}` run end to end, `E_k` from corner `V_k` to `V_{k+1}`. Each is cut exactly at its parameter midpoint `M_k` (`refine::piece`). A spoke `S_k` runs from the centre `C` to `M_k`. Patch `k` is the Coons patch with corners `C, M_k, V_{k+1}, M_{k+1}`: its first u curve (`v = 0`) is `S_k`, its first v curve (`u = 0`) `S_{k+1}`, its last v curve the second half of `E_k`, its last u curve the first half of `E_{k+1}`. Every spoke runs from the centre in both patches it bounds, so the two patches see it in the same parametrisation (what `continuity` compares at).

**The outer edges** take the user's hold on both halves: free; position (the curve checked on its face at 65 places); tangent (G1 to a plane, cylinder, cone or sphere along any curve on it, a spline face along one of its parameter lines). A weight is not used.

**The inner seams, through ribbon fields.** Each spoke gets a field `T_k(s)` across it (a cubic Hermite, raised to degree five), and the plane `span(S_k′, T_k)` is the tangent plane both patches beside the spoke take along it, so they meet with one tangent plane (G1).

**The centre, and why it cannot conflict.** Where two held edges meet at a corner, the patch's twist is asked twice; the two agree when both faces have the same second fundamental form on the corner's two directions (the *vertex enclosure* condition; Peters, *Smooth interpolation of a mesh of curves*, Constr. Approx. 7, 1991). So every spoke's plane field is made, at `C`, from one quadric: a tangent plane `Π` (normal `n_c`) and a symmetric form `II_C` on it, the paraboloid `Q(x, y) = C + x e₁ + y e₂ + ½ II_C((x, y)) n_c`. With `d_k` the spoke's direction at `C` and `t_k = n_c × d_k`: `S_k′(0) = λ d_k`, the normal part of `S_k″(0)` is `λ² II_C(d_k, d_k)`, `T_k(0) = t_k`, `T_k′(0) = λ II_C(d_k, t_k) n_c`. At `M_k` the field turns to the boundary: `T_k(1)` along `E_k′(M_k)`, and where `E_k` is held to a face `F`, the spoke leaves `M_k` in `F`'s tangent plane, square to `E_k`, with `F`'s normal curvature across, and `T_k′(1)`'s normal part is `F`'s twist. The spoke is the quintic Hermite of its two ends' positions, tangents and second derivatives (tangential parts of the second zero). A spoke whose plane turns within 10° of its tangent anywhere is refused.

**The patches: bicubically blended Coons patches with cross-boundary fields** (Farin, *Curves and Surfaces for CAGD*, ch. 22; the Boolean sum `P₁ + P₂ − P₁₂` of the cubic Hermite blends of the boundary curves and their cross-boundary derivatives in each direction, less the bicubic of the corner data). Each side's field across is `D(t) = Σ sᵢ(t) Aᵢ(t)`, `Aᵢ` the side's fields (a spoke's `T_k`; a held face's fields as the network takes them: two axes for a plane, the cylinder's, cone's and sphere's linear fields, a spline face's derivative across its parameter line) with the side's own derivative and itself, the scalars `sᵢ` cubic on `[0, 1]` with values and slopes at the two corners; a free side's field is the cubic Hermite of its corner values. All in homogeneous coordinates, so rational curves (arcs; each half brought to end weights 1 by its Möbius reparametrisation) take part exactly. At each corner the field's value is the other side's tangent there, and its slope is the corner's twist: the bilinear Coons patch's twist of the four curves (`−C₀′ + C₁′ − C₂′ + C₃′ − P₀₀ + P₁₀ + P₀₁ − P₁₁`), moved by the least that puts it into what every held side meeting there allows (`T − Σ sᵢ Aᵢ′ ∈ span Aᵢ`, alternating projections). The scalars are the *least-norm* coefficients (Gram–Schmidt basis of the columns about the corner, every coordinate a length): a basic solution would depend on which of a sphere's three dependent fields came first, and gave the octant's three patches three shapes. So every side's field lies in its face's tangent planes all along, exactly, and the corner twists agree by construction: the patches meet their faces and each other with one tangent plane, which `continuity` then proves.

*Why not the network's holds* (finding, 2026-10-31). The first build put each quad through `gordon` with every edge held, the spokes to ribbon surfaces `S_k + τ T_k + ½ τ² K_k`. Free outer edges gave G1 seams within 1e-11 rad, but held outer edges (the octant held tangent to its sphere) were refused at 2e-3 rad, though the ribbons' and the sphere's second fundamental forms agreed at every corner to 1e-6: each hold interpolates its scalars between the crossings (linearly with two), so the twist one hold puts on the corner control point differs *tangentially* from the other's, and the second hold's overwrite tilts the first hold's planes near the corner at second order. Interior crossing curves (iso-curves of the free patch) brought it to 3e-6 rad, never to rounding, and raised the degree past 32 with curvature holds. Owning the cross-boundary fields and the corner twists makes it exact.

*How close the bicubic build filled* (first build, replaced by the quintic one below): caps within 3.6e-4, 1.6e-4 and 1.1e-4 of the radius, the error falling as the square of the cap's size.

*A plate on a fill* (finding, 2026-10-31). `feature.plate` refused a fill with "the freeform face has a pole or a border or corner drawn into a point". No patch has a pole, and each passes `offset::regular`: the plate continues every face past its vertices by three times the furthest its corners lie plus a thickness (7 mm on 8 mm patches for a 1 mm plate), and a degree-8 polynomial continued past its border by its own size turned over out there. The plate now tries the continuation at full, half and a quarter of that length (never below the furthest corner with a thickness) where the skin loses its normal, folds or is not fitted within the tolerance (at 1e-3 scale the pentagon's continued offset was not fitted), the length its skins are held over by the same share, and checks what the skins reach as before (`kernel/src/plate.rs` › `layered_within`). *Work* (2026-11-01, review): a try that is going to fail refines its fit towards "not fitted", the dearest way to fail (one patch's half-length try took 115 s, its quarter-length try 0.16 s to succeed), so the plate of a pentagon's five patches took 7 minutes. Each try with a shorter one still to come is now bounded on its own (`LADDER_TRY_WORK`, a million units; past it the shorter one is tried) and the whole plate by `PLATE_WORK` plus a share per span, counted, not clocked (`geom::allowance`): past it the plate is refused with the reason, at the same point on every run. The five patches then plate in 13 s in release (from 7 minutes). *Tested:* one patch of a pentagon's fill plates to its area × thickness (`engine/tests/surface_nsided.rs`, at one scale, outside the checked runner: in a debug build these patches are slow); the ladder on a square whose surface, continued the full length, loses its normal and, continued half, does not (`kernel/tests/plate.rs` › *a face whose full continuation loses its normal*, three scales; refused without the shorter tries). The octant's plate is not tested.

**In curvature (G2): biquintic blending with second cross-boundary fields** (2026-10-31). The patches are the *biquintically* blended Coons patches: the Boolean sum of the quintic Hermite blends of each side with its first field across `D` (`S_v`) and its second `E` (`S_vv`), less the tensor quintic of the corner jets `J_kl = ∂u^k ∂v^l S`, `k, l ≤ 2`. A held side is written with its face as a *ribbon*, an exact parametrisation `R(σ, ρ)` of the face with the side's curve as `ρ = 0`: for a plane a translation of the curve in the plane, for a cylinder or cone a translation along the axis, a scaling about the apex or a rotation about the axis (the one whose direction across stays furthest from the curve's), for a sphere a rotation about an axis square to the curve's mean plane (homogeneous: a rotation by `2 atan t` is rational of degree two, `R = (R̃(t)(X − o w) + o w (1 + t²), w (1 + t²))`), for a spline face the face itself along its parameter line. With `f = R_ρ`, `g = R_ρρ` along the side:
- tangent (G1): `D = a f + b c′ + c c` (quintic scalars), in the face's tangent planes all along;
- curvature (G2): also `E = b² c″ + 2ab f′ + a² g + α c + β c′ + γ f`: the patch is the face to second order across (`S = λ R(σ, τ) + O(v³)`, the network's spline-face condition, here exact for every kind).
The scalars' values and first two derivatives at each end come from the corner jets by least-norm coefficients, level by level (`D`, then `D′` from the twist, `D″`, then `E`, `E′`, `E″` with the derivatives of `b² c″ + …` at the end). At each corner the unknown jets are found order by order, each the least move from a start (the bilinear twist, zero above) into what every held side meeting there allows: `J₁₁` from both sides' `D′`, `J₂₁` from one side's `D″` and the other's `E′`, `J₁₂` likewise, `J₂₂` from both `E″`. These agree when the two faces at a corner are one surface to the fourth order, and the construction makes them so:
- *At the centre* every spoke's ribbon is the paraboloid `Q`'s jet to the fourth order: the spoke `Q(s λ d)` (its third and fourth derivatives zero), its field `t + s λ II(d, t) n`, its second field `II(t, t) n`;
- *at a midpoint* the spoke runs along the face's line across (`R(σ_M, ρ(s))`) and its ribbon is the face about it (`κ R_σ`, `κ² R_σσ` along that line), to the fourth order: so at the midpoint corner the spoke's side and the curve's side are the same face written twice;
- *between*, each of spoke, field and second field is a spline of two pieces with the jets at both ends (degrees 6, 5 and 4: five, four and three jets each end), joined with three continuous derivatives in the middle (closed form).
A free curve's midpoint takes a plane across it. The patches are of degree 14 each way.

*Proven, else said.* Every inner seam and held curve is measured by `continuity`. Where a seam or curve asked in curvature is not proven G2 (or the build in curvature is refused), the fill is built again with every curvature hold tangent, and each seam and curve says what was asked, what was built and why, in the measure's words; never claimed.

*How close it follows a sphere.* Caps of 3, 5 and 6 great-circle arcs 17° from the pole held in curvature: within 3.2e-6, 8.6e-6 and 1.1e-5 of the radius, every seam and curve proven G2 at the default angular tolerance (1e-9 rad), at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6 (`geom/tests/network_nsided.rs`); half as wide 2.0e-7, 4.5e-7, 6.0e-7: the error falls as the **fourth** power of the size (ratios 16 to 19). Held tangent (quintic, G1): 4.1e-5, 2.1e-5, 4.7e-5 (the bicubic G1 build before: 3.6e-4, 1.6e-4, 1.1e-4). *The octant* in curvature: its seams are G2 to rounding (bounds 2.4e-7 against a threshold of 1.6e-7, found 0), but the measure spends its allowance (4096 pieces) on these long rational seams of degree 14 before it decides, so the octant falls back to tangent and says so; owed: a tighter bound for long seams (the tank's finding too).

**A rise** (`rise`, a length): the centre moved along the fill's normal from where the curves put it, a dome or (negative) a scoop; the spokes and ribbons follow it (`geom/tests/network_nsided.rs` › *a risen pentagon*: the centre exactly there, every seam G2).

**The class-A mouse's thumb scoop** (2026-10-31; taken out of the showcase mouse 2026-11-01, kept as a fill test, `geom/tests/network_nsided.rs` › *a sunk scoop*). *Why it came out:* its inner seams were only tangent, and its zebra stripes curled irregularly inside it with pointed tips at its ends, not class-A; asked in curvature, the two long spokes (14.5 mm) end with a curvature bound of 1.2e-10 per mm against a threshold of 6.9e-11 (`angular / L`), nothing found, the measure's 4096 pieces spent: the bound stops at the rounding floor of the products (the residual is exactly zero; each product of degree `m` and `n` carries `γ_k`, `k = 4(m + n) + min(m, n) + 8`, from the binomial recurrence, and halving a piece does not shrink it). Owed: products in the scaled Bernstein basis (`C(n, i) cᵢ`, an exact convolution, so `γ` falls to about `min(m, n) ε`), or rows re-centred per piece. ## Holds and the measure along any curve on a spline face

Built 2026-11-01 (mouse-modern). The trimmed faces left one gap: a sheet could be held G1 or G2 to a spline face, and `continuity` could prove it, only along one of the face's parameter lines, and a trimmed edge on a spline drum is none. Code: `crates/geom/src/network/ribbon.rs` (the ribbon, the curve recovered or projected), `crates/geom/src/network/tracked.rs` (the measure's six rows with their rounding), `crates/geom/src/trim.rs` › `BezierNet`, `compose_net_pieces` (homogeneous nets composed with a curve). Tests: `geom/tests/network_ribbon.rs`, `network::ribbon::tests`.

**The ribbon.** A spline face `Φ(u, v)` and a curve on it `c(t) = (u(t), v(t))` in its parameters give `R(t, ρ) = Φ(c(t) + ρ e)`, `e` the unit vector of the parameter axis that stays furthest from the curve's direction (the least sine between `c′` and the axis sampled at 16 places a piece, the larger of the two taken; below 0.05 for both refused, owed: `e` along the curve's normal in parameter space). `R` is an exact local parametrisation of the face in which the curve is the line `ρ = 0`, so its rows across are what a parameter line gives: the line `R = Φ∘c`, the field `R_ρ = Φ_e∘c` and the second field `R_ρρ = Φ_ee∘c`, `Φ_e`, `Φ_ee` the face's homogeneous derivative nets (differences of its control points, patch by patch). Each is composed with `c` exactly, as a trimmed face's edges are (*A face on a surface*: the curve cut where it crosses a knot line, the Bézier patch it lies in composed with it by Bernstein products), here on any homogeneous net (`BezierNet`, `compose_net`), so the derivative nets, whose weights need not be positive, compose the same way. The holds take them where they took a parameter line's rows: the G1 hold's fields `F = R_ρ`, the G2 hold's `T = b² C″ + 2ab F′ + (a²/κ) G` with `G = R_ρρ` (*Edges held in curvature*, the spline-face case) hold for any regular parametrisation of the face, which `R` is. Nothing is fitted.

**Where `c` comes from.** An edge that is a curve of the face (a trimmed face's edge, a projected curve, a patch's edge built here) is `Φ∘c` in its own parameter, `c` piecewise polynomial between the edge's knots. So `c` is *recovered*: on every Bézier piece of the edge, the parameters of `k + 1` points (closest points, polished by Gauss–Newton to the rounding of the point) give the polynomial of degree `k` through them, the pieces sharing their ends; degrees 1, 2 and 3 are tried in turn (a quadratic fitted as a cubic is the same curve of a higher degree, and its composition too), then `deg / (p + q)` when the edge's degree says so (up to 5). `c` is accepted only where `Φ∘c` and the edge are proven within a hundredth of the tolerance: their distance is `|a_xyz b_w − b_xyz a_w| / (a_w b_w)`, bounded piece by piece by the convex hulls of the products' Bernstein coefficients. For a hold only, an edge that is not recovered (a curve within the tolerance of the face but not on it) is projected: each point of it within the tolerance of its closest point (else the edge is not on the face), `c` the cubic through the projections (`trim::project_points`, within half the tolerance at the midpoints). The hold then builds the patch along `Φ∘c`, so the patch's edge is a curve of the face, which the measure recovers.

**The measure along such a curve.** `continuity` needs one face whose parameter line the edge is (the patch); the other may now be a spline face along any curve of it. Its rows are the ribbon's, from the *recovered* curve only: a curve only projected is not measured (refused with the reason: "not proven"), so nothing a sampled projection gives is claimed as proven. The faces can be given in either order.

**Crossings a rounding apart.** Projection and Gauss–Newton find crossings to about 1e-9 of a curve's parameter; a curve crossed that far from its family's parameters was re-parametrised, tripling its degree for nothing (a composed edge of degree 10 became 30, past the 32 a spline may have). Now a curve is left as it is where taking the family's parameters moves it at most a tenth of the tolerance (its speed, bounded by the derivative's control points, times the largest shift): the surface passes it at the family's parameters, and by how much is the proven `mismatch` held to the tolerance, as for every curve. The same rule lets **both edges of one family be held in curvature to spline faces**: the first gives the family its parameters (its line's own), the second is accepted when its line is crossed at the same ones within that tenth of the tolerance (both seams keep one parametrisation each, as the measure compares them), and refused otherwise with the shift named (owed: comparing through a change of parameter).

### The rows' rounding, differentiated first

*Finding.* The first measure along a cylinder drum cut by an ellipse (radius 2 mm, the band held G2) proved position and angle but not curvature: "curvature along" was bounded at 9.9e-7 against a threshold of 3.1e-7, nothing found. Setting the ribbon's allowance to zero changed nothing: the floor was the patch's own rows. The measure formed `X₀` about a point of the edge, then differentiated it twice as a spline, and each derivative carried twice its source's allowance times `p / Δ` (degree 16 on spans of 1/12: 192): the rounding of the *points* (`ε` of their distance from that point), amplified by `(2 · 192)²`. That is the long seam's floor of the tank's Finding 1 as well.

*Method* (`tracked.rs`). The six rows `X₀, X₀′, X₀″, X₁, X₁′, X₂` are made from derivative *nets* taken first, on the stored control points: the homogeneous difference about the edge's point `o` is formed as `w_{i+1}(x_{i+1} − x_i) + (x_i − o)(w_{i+1} − w_i)` (exactly the difference of the local values), whose rounding is a few `ε` of its own two terms, not of the points. Higher derivatives difference those nets, each coefficient carrying its bound (`f (e_i + e_{i+1}) + 3 ε |value|`). Every later step is linear with known coefficient sums and carries the bounds alongside: the combination across at the line (basis values, nonnegative, summing to one; exactly one and zeros on a clamped border, which adds nothing), the cut to the edge (`16 (p + 2) ε` of the row where a cut is made), the scale to the edge's parameter; for a ribbon, the nets' Bézier patches (the bounds and the values' magnitudes extracted the same way), the composition (the bounds composed as magnitudes, plus `(4 D + 6 (p + q) + 24) ε` of the magnitudes composed), and the products with `c`'s derivatives: `Y₀′ = Φ_u∘c u′ + Φ_v∘c v′`, `Y₀″ = Φ_uu∘c u′² + 2 Φ_uv∘c u′v′ + Φ_vv∘c v′² + Φ_u∘c u″ + Φ_v∘c v″`, `Y₁′ = Φ_eu∘c u′ + Φ_ev∘c v′`, piece by piece (each Bernstein coefficient of a product a convex combination of products of coefficients). A rational face's homogeneous derivatives jump across its knot lines of full multiplicity (a circle's quarter joins), so the ribbon's rows are kept as pieces that need not meet (every inner knot once more than the degree), which the measure's pieces already take.

*Result.* On the drum the bounds fell from 9.9e-7 to orders below the threshold, G2 proven at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6. The far-from-origin rows tests (`continuity::tests`, double-double references) hold with the new allowances.

**Oracles** (`geom/tests/network_ribbon.rs`, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6, the default angular tolerance 1e-9 and a tolerance of 1e-6 times the scale): a third of a cylinder written as a rational spline drum, cut by the slanted ellipse `z = 0.6 x + 0.2 R` (its curve in the drum's parameters through the projections, the edge composed exactly), a band held G2 to it: the seam proven G2 (the drum first or the band first), and the band's normal curvature across the seam the cylinder's closed form `−cos²φ / R` (φ the direction's angle from the circles) within 1e-7 / R at 25 places; held only tangent, its tangency within the angular tolerance. A sphere-like bicubic drum (a 6 × 6 net on a sphere) cut along a slanted, bowed curve in its parameters across both knot lines: proven G2, and the band's curvature across the drum's own (from its second fundamental form at the same point and direction) within 1e-7 / R. A band whose far curve has other knots than the seam: G2. The seams lifted: the edge moved off the drum by twice the tolerance is no longer a curve of it and is not measured ("not proven"); the second row moved so the planes open by ten times the angular tolerance is G0; the third row moved by ten times the curvature threshold is G1, by a hundredth of it still G2. An edge pushed off the drum by a third of the tolerance: held tangent through its projection, the patch's edge recovered and its seam proven G1, the pushed edge itself refused by the measure. *Not G2 there:* held in curvature, the crossing curves still end on the pushed edge, a third of the tolerance off the line the patch takes, and the patch's curvature along the seam follows them by about 5e-9 of the drum's curvature, beyond the threshold, so the hold is refused (the network's own refusal, correctly).

**Fair across the seam (2026-10-30, g2-fair).** *Finding.* The guide's fin (a sheet held in curvature to a post of radius 20) is G2 at the seam, yet zebra stripes hooked beside it. The cause was the construction, not the mesh: each condition moved one control point (the second for tangency, the third for curvature) and the knots that localised them were halved towards the edge (0.5, 0.25, 0.125), so the whole change sat in the first spans, as the second derivative of one basis function (positive, then a deep negative lobe). *Method* (`network/fair.rs`). The change `d = D G(t)` uses a scalar spline `G` on the curve's knots that meets the conditions at the edge (`G(0) = 0`; tangency: `G′(0) = 1`, `G″(0) = 0`; curvature: `G′(0) = 0`, `G″(0) = 1`) and has the least `∫ G‴²` (Farin, ch. 23: the energy of fairing, here with its end conditions; a linear system in the free coefficients, its Gram matrix by Gauss–Legendre with `q − 2` nodes per span, exact, and banded (`|i − j| ≤ q`): the third derivatives are taken per span from the degree `q − 3` basis (Cox–de Boor) and three difference steps, and the system is solved by a banded LDLᵀ after scaling to unit diagonal. It is solved on the reach's own grid of 8 spans (the grid knots are the curve's, put there by `localize`) and written on the curve's knots by knot refinement, exactly: on a curve's own dense knots the system's conditioning would fall as the fifth power of the span (2¹⁶ spans read as singular), on the grid it is always the same small system, and work and room stay linear in the control points, `fair::tests`). The conditions are linear, so the seam gets exactly what the one-point move gave it and `continuity` proves it as before. The reach is cut into 8 equal spans: to the next crossing curve (ending there with every derivative), or, when that is the far edge, over the whole curve keeping only its last point when the far edge is free (half way when it is held itself). The refusals judge the spread change by the row the one-point move had (the profile's shape is fixed, its size is the change's). *What no such change can do:* one that ends with the curve's own position and tangent has `∫ Δκ ds ≈ 0` and `∫ (L − s) Δκ ds ≈ 0`, so on a reach that ends at a crossing curve or a held edge (closed) the change of curvature must change sign twice, the curvature swinging past the sheet's own once; on an open reach (a free far edge, only its point kept) only `∫ (L − s) Δκ ds ≈ 0` holds and one sign change suffices; for a straight curve the swing is about 0.38 of the change at the seam (the quintic `t²(1 − t)³`). *Result:* the fin's curvature runs from 0.05 at the seam to −0.137 and back to the sheet's own at the far edge, one change of sign, one turn, `max |dκ/ds|` 0.042 (2.3 times the tangent fin's, was 13 times); pinned for a plane, a cylinder, a sphere and a spline face (`curvature_held_sheets_are_fair_across_their_seam`: at most one turn and one change of sign along the crossing lines, a swing past the band of the face's and the tangent sheet's curvature of at most 0.75 of the change at the seam, at most three times as steep as the tangent sheet). *Far from the origin* the holds worked on differences of coordinates a million times the network's size, and the curvature hold's least squares amplified their rounding (a dome of 1.5e-3 a million units out: its third row's change 12.3 in weight against 0.004 on main, by luck of the rounding); `gordon` now builds a network farther from the origin than its extent about its centre and moves the result back (each control point rounded by `|c| ε`). *Also found:* `coefficients` scaled a column of pure rounding (a sphere's field at a dome's corner a million units out, 4.7e-10 against 1.6e-3) to unit length and gave it a coefficient of −7.6; a column no larger than `64 ε (|c| w + L)` (the rounding of the coordinates it came from) now takes none.

## Stage 1: analysis

The tools that show surface quality (2026-10-30). Views, not features: `ui.set {"analysis": …}` (`ui_egui::state::AnalysisState`), `view.render`'s `analysis` and the window's Analysis toggle parse the same object (`craft3d_engine::analysis::AnalysisSpec`); nothing is saved in a document, and no analysis is the plain picture bit for bit. Code: `crates/render/src/analysis.rs` (the definition, the CPU oracle), `crates/gpu/src/scene.wgsl` › `analysis_of` (the same per fragment), `crates/engine/src/analysis.rs` (values, combs, measures), `crates/engine/src/analysis_cmds.rs` (`measure.draft`, `measure.curvature`), `crates/geom/src/curvature.rs` › `curve_curvature`.

**One colour per sample, in place of the shading.** Every mode is a function of what the rasteriser already interpolates per sample (perspective-correctly): the normal (the tessellator's vertex normals are the exact surface's, so between vertices the error is the interpolation's, of the order of the angle criterion's square), the point, and a value per vertex. Both renderers evaluate the same formulas from the numbers of `analysis::Resolved`, computed once per picture in `f64` (the GPU narrows them last), and both draw an analysis in the plain look (the studio look is set aside while one is on).

- *Zebra.* An environment of parallel light tubes along an axis `d` in the screen plane (`direction` turns it), at infinity. The reflected ray `r = 2(n·v)n − v` (the normal turned to the viewer) has the angle `φ = atan2(r·b₂, r·b₁)` about `d` (`b₁` towards the viewer, `b₂ = d × b₁`); the phase `φ/2π · stripes + offset` (`stripes` rounded to a whole number, so the stripes close up behind) is dark where its fraction is below `width`. Closed form on a sphere seen square on: along the vertical through its middle `φ = −2θ` (θ the angle of the normal from the view), so the stripes are evenly spaced in the angle on the sphere, and the picture is mirror symmetric (the stripes of a tunnel on a sphere are a pencil of arcs through the two points where the normal bisects `v` and `d`, not circles). On a cylinder seen square to its axis the normal does not change along a ruling, nor does its interpolation, so the stripes are straight along the axis.
- *Highlight lines* (Beier & Chen, *Highlight-line algorithm for realtime surface-quality assessment*, CAD 26(4), 1994). View independent: light lines in a plane `M = +Z` a quarter of its diagonal above the model's box (close, so that the lines spread little over a curved surface), along `E = (cos a, sin a, 0)`, spaced a diagonal over `stripes`. The normal line through the point meets the plane at `Q = P + tN`, `t = (A − P)·M / (N·M)`; the phase is `(Q − A)·(M × E) / spacing + offset`. Where `|N·M| < 0.05` (`GRAZING`) the normal line runs nearly along the plane and the sample has no phase: the phase grows as `stripes / |N·M|`, and this bound keeps the GPU's `f32` phase within about 10⁻³ of a period of the CPU's (found when the torus's grazing band put pixels 225 levels apart at 10⁻³). Since the band limit (below) the sample there is the stripes' mean, `width` dark, which is what the filtered stripes just above the bound already are at the zoom where the bound is usually seen, the whole model in view (their footprint grows as `1 / |N·M|²`), so the band does not show there; seen close, the stripes just above the bound are resolved and a step to the mean remains. Before, it was the neutral colour, a grey band a few levels off the mean beside it.
- *Band limit* (2026-10-31, the tank's Finding 6). Neither stripe has a bound on its frequency: highlight lines crowd as `1 / |N·M|²` towards a vertical face, zebra stripes on strong curvature and at the poles where the reflected ray runs along the tubes; finer than a sample, a point-sampled square wave aliases into moiré, rings and noise (the tank's sides). Both renderers now filter the wave over the sample's footprint in phase, `f = |∂p/∂x| + |∂p/∂y|` over the samples' grid, with a tent `±f` wide (`analysis::dark_share`): the wave less its mean is periodic, its second integral is closed form (`wave_integral`), and the tent's average is `width` plus that integral's second difference over `f` divided by `f²`. An edge becomes an S-shaped ramp `2f` wide centred where the edge was (an edge does not move), and stripes finer than a sample fade to their mean within `w(1 − w)/f²`. A box over `f` (the first try) left `w(1 − w)/f` over the mean, a faint moiré where `f` is near one period, visible on the tank's side and a sphere at 512 stripes; the tent (the box taken twice) took it away. The derivatives are exact on the CPU: per triangle the screen-space barycentric weights have constant derivatives, so the perspective-correct normal (before it is made unit) and point have `∂A = Σ ∂bᵢ ιᵢ (Aᵢ − A) / q` (`render::stripe_slopes`), chained through the phase by hand (`Resolved::footprint`: the unit normal's `(dN − n(n·dN))/|N|`, the direction to the viewer's in perspective, the reflected ray's angle for zebra; the normal line's meeting with the light plane for highlight lines), checked against a central difference of the phase. The GPU takes the interpolants' derivatives from `dpdxFine`/`dpdyFine` at the top of `analysis_of` (uniform control flow) and chains them the same way, so the only difference between the two is the screen difference over one sample against the derivative of an interpolant that is nearly linear across it (exactly so in orthographic views), not of the strongly nonlinear phase. The footprint is the L1 norm of the phase's gradient, deliberately: it is what `fwidth` gives, it bounds the phase's change over the sample's square in every direction, and it costs no square root; for stripes running diagonally across the samples it is up to √2 wider than the gradient's length, so they blur up to √2 more, which errs on the side of no aliasing. The filter is evaluated in three regimes so that nothing cancels (review of 9cc2c64f: the tent's second difference, values of order 0.1 differenced and divided by `f²`, gave shares of 0.86 to 0.95 for 1 in `f32` at `f = 10⁻⁴`, noise below 10⁻⁵, and in `f64` was wrong at 10⁻⁸, so a few stripes on a gentle panel seen close speckled on the GPU): where the tent reaches no edge (`f` at most the distance to the nearest of 0, `width`, 1) the point sample, exact; where it can reach one edge only (`2f < min(width, 1 − width)`) the tent's share past that edge, a quadratic in `d / f` (`d` the signed distance to it), which does not cancel; only wider the second difference, where the terms are no longer small against `f²`. A footprint that is not a number or above 10⁶ periods, or a phase that is not finite, is the mean outright; zero is the point sample.
- *Curvature map.* The value per vertex comes from the exact surface: each vertex of the scene's mesh is projected onto its face's surface (from the previous vertex's parameters, else from scratch, and only accepted within four chordal tolerances of the vertex) and `craft3d_geom::curvature::curvatures` gives `K` and `H` there from the exact second derivatives; `H` is signed positive where the body is convex (outward normal; for a sheet, its face's normal, so a network dome may come out negative), the largest is `|H| + √(H² − K)`. Interpolated per sample like the normal and coloured by a five-stop ramp over `range` (default: symmetric about 0 to the 95th percentile of the magnitudes, from 0 for the largest, so a pole or a sharp blend does not wash the rest out), lit by `0.55 + 0.45 |n·v|` so the shape still reads. A vertex without a value (no normal, a projection that missed) is the neutral colour.
- *Refined for the map* (2026-10-30, g2-fair): the curvature map interpolates a value per vertex, and the scene's mesh follows the chordal and angle criteria, not the curvature's change: the guide's G2 fin had 28 triangles and a centroid a quarter of the largest |H| off. `view.render` draws the map on `analysis::refined_map`: an edge inside a face whose ends' values differ by more than 2 % of the map's range is split at its midpoint put on the exact surface (its normal and exact value), a triangle with one, two or three split edges becomes two, three or four (conforming), border edges are split too, their new point kept on the chord, edges shorter than the scene's tolerance are not, at most 8 rounds. `view.render`'s CPU and GPU pictures draw that same refined mesh, so their parity is untouched; the window draws it too (2026-10-30, studio-cut: `make_analysis` keeps the refined mesh in `ViewAnalysis::mesh`, which the viewport uploads with the map's values and `ui.screenshot` draws, its `triangles` the refined mesh's; held by `ui-egui/tests/analysis.rs`). A border edge's new point stays on its chord (the neighbouring face keeps that chord: no crack) and takes the surface's value and normal (the normal oriented by the face's `reversed`); at most a million vertices are added to the scene's.
- *Draft.* `s = n·p` with `p` the unit pull and `n` the normal as the mesh has it (outward), the draft angle `asin s`: `s ≥ sin(minDraft)` in, `s ≤ −sin(minDraft)` out, between them too shallow; lit like the map.
- *Combs.* Curves sampled at `density` places; at each the curvature vector `κN = (C′ × C″) × C′ / |C′|⁴` (`curve_curvature`: exact second derivatives, closed forms for lines, circles and ellipses, basis derivatives A2.3 and the quotient rule for NURBS; for an iso-curve `S(u₀, ·)` from `S_v` and `S_vv`) and a spike `−scale · κN` away from the centre of curvature; the line through the tips. `scale` automatic makes the largest spike a tenth of the model's diagonal. Edges are every edge (or the edges named) of the bodies in view; an iso-curve is at a fraction of the face's parameter box as its mesh spans it (not clipped to the trim). The window draws them as overlay lines (`LineKind::Comb`, the theme's `comb`), tested against the depth like a trace; `view.render` draws them with `analysis::draw_lines` (one pixel, hidden behind a body by its depth).

**Tolerance, GPU against CPU** (`crates/gpu/tests/analysis.rs`, every adapter; the doubt pixels are compared too: the GPU must be within 2 levels of one of the CPU's pictures, the plain one or a varied one, and the share in doubt is held per scene to a small margin over its measured share): outside the band of one pixel around silhouettes and face edges and around where a face hides another part of itself (the CPU frame's distance jumps by more than 2 % between neighbours: the torus's inner rim over its far side, a silhouette the face-id band does not see), and outside the pixels *in doubt*, every channel within 2 levels and at most 1/1024 of the pixels compared more than 1 level apart. A pixel is in doubt where a CPU picture drawn with one thing varied by what `f32` can be off by is more than 2 levels off the plain one there: the stripes shifted by `4·10⁻⁶ · count` of a period (zebra, about 128 roundings of the largest phase `count / 2`; it relies on the adapter's `atan2` being within about 64 ULP, as the adapters tested are, not on WGSL's bound of 4096 ULP) or `2·10⁻⁵ · count` (highlight lines, six roundings of their largest phase, about `26 · count`), the grazing bound by ±10⁻⁴ of itself, the least draft by ±10⁻⁴ rad, the normals turned by ±10⁻⁵ rad about two axes (fifty times an interpolated `f32` normal's rounding; this holds the highlight lines where their phase moves fastest with the normal, `stripes / |N·M|²` periods per radian), or the camera moved by ±1/128 of a sample across and up (the rasteriser's subpixel grid is 1/256: a sample that close to an edge between two triangles may fall in either, and the stripes' footprint, from each triangle's own derivatives, jumps there). A pixel in doubt must match one of the CPU's pictures, at most 1/1024 of them not. The shares measured: zebra 0.0011 (sphere and cylinder; 0 at 512 stripes), highlight lines 0.0023 (5 lines) and 0.0028 (20 lines, a moiré before), the map 0, draft 0.0008 and 0.0009; 4 stripes seen close on a sphere through a 2° lens with an edge at the middle (the footprint about 10⁻⁴, where the second difference made the GPU up to 225 levels off on 20 000 pixels) 0.014 (highlight lines) and 0.0085 (zebra), the edges nearly sharp and so decisions again. *Changed with the band limit* (2026-10-31): before it, a pixel was in doubt where a variant differed at all and the shifts were 10⁻³ (zebra) and 10⁻² (highlight lines) of a period, generous for a decision between two colours; with the stripes a continuous grey such a shift moves every ramp by more than the tolerance (half the pixels in doubt), while what `f32` is really off by moves them by hundredths of a level. The shares fell from 0.012 and 0.118, and the 20 highlight lines on the torus, which parity could not hold in their moiré, hold. The map has no decision: its ramp is continuous. The vertex layout carries the value and whether there is one (56 bytes a vertex, was 48).

**Measures.**

- `draft {faces, direction, minDraft}` (and `measure.draft`): the least draft angle (radians as an assertion, degrees in the command's result) and `shareBelow`, the share of the faces' area below `minDraft` (not `min`, which is an assertion's bound). A plane is exact (its normal, oriented by the face). Elsewhere the normals at the vertices of a mesh within 2·10⁻⁴ of the body's size and 8° between neighbours (exact there) give the least at a real place, the upper bound; the lower bound (`atLeast`) is the least over the triangles of a bound per triangle. On a cylinder it is exact: the normal depends only on the angle about the axis, `n·d = s R cos(θ − φ)`, and its least over the triangle's angular range is the ends' or `−R` (the bound is then the true least over the meshed face, however long the cylinder; a cylinder tilted 30° from the pull: `atLeast` −30° to 10⁻⁹). On a sphere it is the triangle's least corner less `2 asin(c / 2r)` (`c` its longest side: at most the 8° the mesh allows between neighbours, so within about 8° of the least); on a torus less `κ_max · π/2 · c` (`κ_max` the larger of `1/minor` and `1/|major − minor|`), within about 12°. So an assertion is decided where its bound is that far from the least found. On a cone and a spline face the least is sampled (`proven: false`, `atLeast` null) and an assertion on it is not proven. The share is by triangles, each classed by its corners' mean normal (sampled, except on planes). Each face's triangles are gathered once per body (the cost is the mesh's, not faces × triangles).
- `curvature {of, at}` (and `measure.curvature`): the face nearest to `at` by its mesh, the exact surface's nearest point on it, and there `K` (the number), `H`, `k1`, `k2`. Without `at` it is the largest |K| of the faces, as before.

### Limits of stage 1

- The draft of a cone or a spline face is sampled, not proven (owed: a bound on the normal's turn over a patch from the surface's second derivative bounds, as `geom::offset::curvature_bound` gives for a rational Bézier patch).
- An iso-curve comb spans the face's parameter box, not only its trimmed region.
- The map's values are computed on the window's thread when the analysis, the scene or the theme changes (a projection per vertex: about a second for a million vertices), not in the background.
- `continuity {edge}` (G0 to G3 along a seam, proven) is stage 3's measure (`r1-surf-ca3`), not repeated here.
