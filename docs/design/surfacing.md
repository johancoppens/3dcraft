# 3DCraft design: Freeform surfacing (curve networks)

Sheets through networks of curves: the Coons patch of four boundary curves, the Gordon surface of u- and v-curves that cross, lofts along guide curves, and edges held in position on a face or tangent (G1) to one. Code: `crates/geom/src/network.rs` (the geometry), `crates/geom/src/network/continuity.rs` (the proven continuity measure), `crates/regen/src/network.rs` (curves and faces from the history, the sheet body and its names), `crates/engine/src/surface_cmds.rs` (`feature.surface.network`, `feature.surface.coons`), `crates/engine/src/continuity_cmds.rs` (`measure.continuity`), `crates/regen/src/loft.rs` › `guided` (`feature.loft` with `guides`). The plan, with stages 1 (analysis), 3 (G2) and 4 (blends, n-sided corners), is `plan/class-a.md`; this is stages 2 and 3, and stage 1 is [*Stage 1: analysis*](#stage-1-analysis) below.

## Method

**Exact, never fitted.** Every result is a B-spline surface built from the curves by exact operations on homogeneous control points (`[x w, y w, z w, w]`): knot insertion, degree elevation, Bézier pieces, composition with a polynomial, Bernstein products, interpolation at given parameters. Nothing is sampled into being, so there is no approximation whose error would need a bound. Arcs and circles take part as rational curves (`network::arc`: quarter-turn rational quadratic pieces, end weights 1).

**Curves.** A network curve is a sketch's one open chain or edges of earlier bodies end to end (`doc::Rail`, as a developable's rails; `regen::developable::rail_of`). Its pieces become one spline (`network::chain`): every piece raised to one degree, its homogeneous points scaled so it meets the piece before in four dimensions, and where two pieces meet tangentially (within the angular tolerance plus the direction a tolerance's worth of position makes over the shorter piece, `angular + tol / shorter`: what a joint that meets within the tolerance may differ by; a corner taken for smooth only has its speeds evened, which is harmless) each piece gets the length of its control polygon as its share of the parameter and its end speeds brought to that rate by the exact Hermite reparametrisation of `refine::reparametrized` (three times its degree), so the joined curve is once differentiable in its parameter, as a single spline is. Corners stay corners.

**The Gordon surface** (`network::gordon`; Gordon 1969, Piegl & Tiller §10.5):
1. *Crossings.* Every u-curve must cross every v-curve within the tolerance: closest points by Gauss-Newton on `|C(s) − D(t)|²` from the best pair of samples (8 per knot span, at least 32). Each curve is turned to run from the other family's first curve to its last; it must cross the other family in order, and end on the first and last curve of the other family (those bound the sheet).
2. *Common crossing parameters.* A family's crossing parameters are the means over its curves. A curve crossed elsewhere is cut at its crossings and each piece composed with the increasing cubic taking the common parameters to its own; the slopes at the crossings are the harmonic means of the neighbouring secants (Fritsch and Butland), so every cubic's Bernstein slopes are at most 2 and it increases, and the curve stays once differentiable in its parameter. Exact: the same points at three times the degree. A Bernstein slope `α` is the factor by which the curve's speed at that crossing falls below its mean over the piece; a direction computed from so small a derivative carries a relative rounding error of about `ε/α`, and the rounding of a point is ε times its coordinates' size, so below `α = ε · max(1, |P| / c) / angular` (`|P|` the largest of the curve's points, `c` the piece's chord: scale-free; crossings spaced far more unevenly than the family's) the curve is refused by name. Its size once re-parametrised is counted first (its own knot spans plus the crossings, three times the degree) and refused above a curve's `MAX_CONTROL_POINTS`; the pieces are composed after cutting them with knots inserted all at once (`composed_batched`).
3. *Weights.* A rational curve may be multiplied by a constant without changing. The constants that make crossing curves agree in their homogeneous points solve `ln rᵢ − ln sⱼ = ln w_v(v̄ᵢ) − ln w_u(ūⱼ)` in closed form (row and column means of a two-way table). Where the table has a residual no constants make every pair agree; there is no threshold on it: what it costs is proven on the result (*Checked*, below) and held to the tolerance. A curve that is a point (a pole) takes the weights of its neighbour in its family: a point's weight function is free.
4. *Boolean sum.* `S = A + B − T`: the u-curves interpolated across v at the common parameters (degree `min(3, count − 1)`, knots by averaging), the v-curves across u likewise, minus the tensor interpolant of the crossing points (the v-curves' points there, so the u-curves are reproduced exactly and the v-curve `j` as `V_j + Σᵢ Lᵢ(v)(a_ij − b_ij)`, moved by the interpolant of the differences of the homogeneous crossing points). The three are brought to one degree and knot vector each way and added. The sum reproduces exactly any surface that lies in the spaces of both interpolations: a bilinear patch from its sides, a tensor B-spline from its iso-curves at parameters whose averages are its knots, the rational cylinder and sphere octant from their circles and lines.
5. *The size, before it is made.* Every curve re-parametrised for alignment brings its own knot preimages (at three times its degree), and a family takes the union of its curves' knots: about `(n + m·k)·3p` control points each way for `m` curves of `k` knots crossing `n`. So the size of each net (control points `nu × nv`) is computed from the knots alone (raising a degree leaves every inner knot `degree` times, so no curve needs raising to count it) before the skins are made, again before the nets are brought to one description and before each tangent edge's refinement, and refused above `MAX_NETWORK_POINTS` = 2²⁰ (32 MB of homogeneous points per net) with the reason. Knots are inserted all at once (Boehm–Prautzsch refinement, Piegl & Tiller A5.4; `Raw::insert_all`, `elevate_batched`, `bezier_pieces_batched`), so making a net costs its size times its degree: the budget bounds time and memory both. Finding the crossings compares every pair of samples of every pair of curves (8 per knot span), refused above 2²⁸ pairs. A 32 × 32 grid of 30-point curves, each crossed at its own parameters, is refused in seconds instead of taking tens of gigabytes; the blow-up itself is not reduced (owed: an approximation within half the tolerance with a proven bound, or preimages shared within the family).

**The Coons patch** is the Gordon surface of two curves each way (the bilinearly blended Coons patch); `feature.surface.coons` takes four curves round the patch.

**Edges held.** Each of the four edges (`uFirst`, `uLast`, `vFirst`, `vLast`) is free, in position on a face (its curve lies on the face: checked at 64 places per knot span and more, refused beyond the tolerance), or tangent to a face:
- *The crossing curves leave along the face.* Each curve crossing the edge gets its end tangent projected into the face's tangent plane there, keeping its speed, times the edge's weight: the control point beside the end moves (the curve raised to degree 3 at least, a knot inserted so that point's basis function ends before the next crossing, so nothing else of the curve changes). A curve that is itself a held edge keeps its shape and must already leave along the face (else the corner is refused).
- *The side.* Each crossing curve's direction there, against the edge's own, about the face's normal: the sine `((Ĉ′ × t̂) · m)` gives the side of the edge it leaves to along the face. Within the angular tolerance of zero (square to the face, or along the edge) it is refused; curves that leave to opposite sides (a patch that would fold over its edge, its normal turned over between them) are refused by name; the side is what the result is checked against.
- *The cross derivative put into the tangent planes.* After the sum, along the edge (the net turned so the edge is `v = 0`): the target cross derivative is `E(u) = Σ aₖ(u) Fₖ(u) + b(u) C′(u) + c(u) C(u)` in homogeneous terms, `C` the edge curve and `Fₖ` exact fields whose projections span the face's tangent planes along it: for a plane its two axes (constant); for a cylinder its axis and `d × (A − w o)`; for a cone `A − w·apex` and `d × (A − w o)`; for a sphere `eₖ × (A − w c)`; for a spline face the edge must run along one of its parameter lines, and the edge curve is replaced by that line (exactly, cut to the edge) with the face's homogeneous derivative across it as the field, both carried through every change of parameter. The scalars interpolate, at the crossings, the least-squares coefficients of the derivative the surface already has there (which lies in the plane, by the first step), found scale-free: the homogeneous points written about the edge's first point with the weight measured in the extent of the first two rows (`[x − c w, …, L w]`, so every coordinate is a length), the columns of unit length, Householder QR with column pivoting, a column within `max(angular, tol / L)` of the others' span (the edge lies on its face within the tolerance, so its direction may leave the face's planes by that much) taken as dependent and given no coefficient (the basic solution: no regularisation biases it, dependent columns cannot blow it up). What is left of the derivative outside the planes is taken off the second row there, which moves that crossing curve's second control point: refused where that move exceeds the tolerance. Between the crossings the change is what puts the interpolated derivative into the planes, of the order of its drift off them (a fraction of the ribbon between the edge and the second row; not held to the tolerance, which would refuse every curved edge); a column kept just above the cutoff could get coefficients of about `1 / cutoff`, so a change of a second-row control point larger than its distance from the edge (plus the tolerance) is refused. Then `E − S_v(u, 0)` (an exact spline, by Bernstein products) is added to the second row of control points times `(t_{q+1} − t₁)/q`, with the degree across at least 3 and the knots across arranged so that row's basis function ends before the next u-curve: every curve of the network stays on the surface, every other edge keeps its position and cross derivative.
- *Checked.* The angle between the oriented tangent planes is then found along every tangent edge (32 places per knot span, 64 at least; reported as `tangency`): the patch's `along × into` against the face's normal turned to the side, `atan2(|n × m|, n · m)` from 0 to π, so a normal turned over counts as far off as it is. Where the two derivatives are within the angular tolerance of parallel, or either vanishes, the patch has no tangent plane there and the edge is refused (only an end where the crossing family's first or last curve is a pole is passed). The construction is exact, so the angular tolerance itself is the limit; above it the feature is refused. That is where two held edges meeting at a corner show a twist conflict.
- *Every curve against the result.* Each curve of the network, as the surface was built through it (after the bending), is compared with the surface's own iso-curve there, a proven bound: with `S = V + D` in homogeneous terms the distance is `|D_xyz − p D_w| / w_S`, at most `(max |D_k,xyz − c D_k,w| + R max |D_k,w|) / min w_S,k` by the convex hulls (`R` the reach of `V`'s points from `c`). It takes in the crossing gaps, the weights that no constants could make agree, the interpolation's amplification across, and what a tangent edge's change moved; it is reported as `mismatch` and held to the tolerance (refused beyond, the curve named).

**Edges held in curvature (G2).** `Condition::Curvature` (`"kind": "curvature"`) is a tangent edge whose patch also takes the face's normal curvature across it. With the tangent plane shared along the edge, the normal curvature along the edge and the twist agree by themselves (the normals agree along the curve, so their derivative along it does), so G2 is one more number per point: the normal curvature in the direction the patch leaves the edge. In homogeneous terms it is linear in the second derivative across, `X₂`:
- *A plane* `L·X = 0`, a *quadric* `XᵀQX = 0` (cylinder, cone, sphere; `Q` written about the edge's first point, so nothing cancels far from the origin): `G(X(u, v)) = O(v³)` along the edge, which given G0 and G1 is `N·X₂ = r` with `N = L`, `r = 0`, or `N = Q X₀`, `r = −X₁ᵀQX₁`.
- *A spline face* `Φ` along its parameter line: the patch is `λ Φ(σ, τ)` to second order, so `X₂ − T` lies in the span of `C, C′, F` (the edge `C = κ Φ(s(u), t₀)`, `F = Φ_t` along it), `T = b² C″ + 2ab F′ + (a²/κ) G`, `G = Φ_tt` along the line, with `X₁ = a F + b C′ + c C`. `Φ_tt` comes from the face's net (the across splines differentiated twice) and is carried through every change of parameter, as `F` is.

Three steps, as for G1:
1. *The crossing curves.* After the end tangent is set, the third control point of each curve crossing the edge is moved along the face's normal so that the curve's `N·X₂ = r` holds (one linear equation, one unknown), with a knot inserted so that its basis function ends before the next crossing (`localize_rows`, three rows instead of two).
2. *Along an analytic face, the third row by constrained least squares.* `Δ` (the change of `X₂`) must satisfy `N·Δ = ρ = r − N·X₂` as splines, exactly: the equation of the Bernstein coefficients of every piece between the edge's knots and crossings, at degree `deg N + D`. `Δ` is sought in splines of degree `D = max(P, ⌈(P + 11)/3⌉, deg ρ)` (`P` the edge's; at least `ρ`'s, since the edge may be a curve of lower degree raised and `N·Δ` must reach every term of `ρ` whatever `N`'s true degree: found on a dome over a rational arc raised to degree 4, where a smaller `D` left the equations unsolvable by 1e-5), twice differentiable at every break (the net's own knots, and the crossings that are not one of them, taken as the net has them so that no two knots lie within rounding of each other), which leaves more unknowns (`4(D − 2)` per break) than independent equations (at most `P + D + 3`, counting the four of a crossing); at every crossing `Δ` takes the least change that puts that crossing curve right (after step 1 that is rounding; a move of the third control point beyond the tolerance is refused). Of all such `Δ` the one of least size (in lengths about the edge's first point, its weight times its extent) is taken: Householder QR of the equations' transpose with the equations pivoted, an equation within the angular tolerance of the others' span taken as dependent (the rule of the tangent coefficients), `Rᵀ y = b`, `Δ = Q y`. The work is capped (`MAX_CURVATURE_WORK`, 2³³ multiplications) and refused beyond. Only the third row of control points changes, and its basis function across ends before the next u-curve: the first two rows (G0, G1) and every other curve of the network stay. As for the second row, a change of a third-row control point larger than its distance from the edge (plus the tolerance) is refused (the ribbon bound, both holds). With no curve between two held edges of a family, a curvature edge keeps its rows to the first half (three rows at each end need six).
3. *Along a spline face, by fields.* `X₂ = T + α C + β C′ + γ F`: `T` is exact (Bernstein products of `hold_tangent`'s own scalars `a`, `b` with the fields), the scalars `α, β, γ` interpolate at the crossings the parts of the derivative the surface already has there (scale-free least squares, as for G1), and the change goes on the third row. Exact everywhere, since the span terms do not change `N·X₂`.

*The seam's parametrisation.* Along a spline face the family crossing the edge takes its common crossing parameters from the face's line (the line cut to the edge, over `[0, 1]`) instead of the means, so the line needs no change of parameter and the patch's edge is the face's line in one parametrisation: what the measure compares at. Only one edge of a family can dictate that; two edges of one family held in curvature to spline faces are refused (owed: comparing through the change of parameter). Edges held only tangent keep the means, so G1 results are as before.

*Checked.* Every edge held in curvature is measured on the result by `continuity` (below), between the patch and its face, and refused unless G2 is proven; the report is `Patch::seams` (and `network.continuity` in the feature's result).

**The continuity measure** (`network::continuity`, `measure.continuity`, the assertion and output measure `continuity`). For an edge, the order reached (G0, G1, G2) and the largest gap in position, in the angle between the tangent planes and in normal curvature across the edge, each with a *proven* bound over the whole edge, the largest found (where), and its threshold:
- *Rows.* One face is a spline the edge runs along a parameter line of: its point `X₀`, `X₀′` and the first and second derivatives across `X₁`, `X₂` are exact splines along the line cut to the edge (`iso_track` with the second row). The other is implicit (plane, quadric) or a spline along a parameter line in the same parametrisation (rows `Y₀, Y₀′, Y₀″, Y₁, Y₁′, Y₂`); where the two lines do not correspond, the position gap shows it.
- *Residuals.* Each gap is `|R(u)| / D(u)`, `R` a polynomial on every piece between the rows' knots that vanishes where the faces meet to that order (built by Bernstein products of the rows' coefficients), `D` a product of positive polynomials and norms of vector polynomials. Position: the exact distance to a plane, sphere or cylinder (`|XᵀQX| / (w(|P(x − cw)| + Rw))`), to a cone's generatrix in the point's meridian plane, or between corresponding points of two splines; each is an upper bound on the distance to the face. Angle: `|W × G| / (|W||G|)`, `W = V₀′ × V₁` the patch's normal (times `w⁴`), `G` the face's normal (`(QX₀)_xyz`, which for a sphere and a cylinder points along the normal at the nearest point, and for a cone is the normal of the quadric's level surface through the point; `W_b` for a spline). Curvature: against an implicit face the second-order contact `h″ = d²/dv² g(S)` over `|∇g| |S_v|²`, in homogeneous terms `P₂ = H₂w² − 4ww₁H₁ + (6w₁² − 2ww₂)H` (`H = XᵀQX` and its derivatives across; `L·X` likewise for a plane), which is exactly the difference of the normal curvatures in the direction the patch leaves the edge wherever the tangent planes agree; against a spline face `n_b·S_vv − II_b(S_v)`, with `S_v` put into its tangent plane by Cramer's rule and `II_b` through `det[Y, Y_a, Y_t, Y_ab] = −ω⁴ (B_a × B_t)·B_ab`, over `|S_v|²`, one polynomial over a product of norms (numerator degree `4P + 20q`, held below `MAX_SEAM_DEGREE` = 384).
- *Proven.* A polynomial lies within the convex hull of its Bernstein coefficients, so `max |R| / min D` over a piece is a bound (the lower bound of a norm is the square root of the least coefficient of its square). A piece whose bound does not decide is halved (de Casteljau) until it does or `MAX_SEAM_PIECES` (4096) are spent; then that order is not reached, never guessed. The values found at five places per piece (and three per half) are lower bounds (rounding taken off): a gap found beyond its threshold proves the order is not reached. For an exact construction the residuals are rounding, so the first pieces decide.
- *Pieces.* Rows are cut into Bézier pieces between all their knots; a derivative of a spline whose pieces join only in position (the exact products of the G1 hold join at C⁰) has a knot once more than its degree, so its pieces do not meet: `pieces_any` takes each piece's points from where the last ended, as far on as that knot's multiplicity (the same pieces as before wherever the old cutter applied; `times` uses it too).
- *Rounding.* Each coefficient carries a bound on its error through every sum and product (running error analysis): what enters gets `16 (p + 2) ε` of the magnitudes it came from (cutting into pieces, derivatives, the move to the local origin), each product of degrees `m` and `n` adds `4 (m + n + 2) ε` times the magnitudes.
- *Thresholds*, from the document: the tolerance; the angular tolerance; and for curvature `min(angular / L, 2 tol / L²)`, `L` the length of the edge's control polygon: a difference `Δκ` turns the faces' normals apart by `Δκ d` and opens a gap of `Δκ d² / 2` at a distance `d` from the seam, held within the angular tolerance and the tolerance over the seam's own length. G1-only and G2 pairs differ by orders more than that; a G2 patch disturbed by ten times the threshold in curvature is G1, by a hundredth of it still G2 (`geom/tests/network_g2.rs`).
- *As a number.* The assertion and output measure is the order: −1 none, 0 G0, 1 G1, 2 G2. It is proven at least that; at most it where the next gap was seen beyond its threshold, else not proven either way (so `min 2` on an undecided seam is *notProven*, not passed).

**Names** (rule 4). The face is `F9:side(k₁, k₂, k₃, k₄)` after the four edge curves (the first and last u-curve, the first and last v-curve), each by the id of its sketch, or of the feature of its first edge. Its edges are `F9:startEdge(f, k)` and `F9:endEdge(f, k)` (`f` 0 for the u family, 1 for v) after their curve; its corners `F9:startVertex(e, kᵤ, kᵥ)` on the first u-curve and `F9:endVertex(e, kᵤ, kᵥ)` on the last (`e` 0 at the first v-curve, 1 at the last). A pole is one vertex and no edge. Inserting a curve inside a family changes no name.

**A loft with guides** (`feature.loft {"guides": [...]}`): open sections as u-curves and the guides as v-curves of a network; the first and last guide are the sheet's edges. 2 to 64 of each; a section's `start` is refused (an open section is run the way the guides cross it), and the network's messages are told in sections and guides. `Document::validate` checks the guides as a network's curves (at most 64, at most 1024 edges each, every edge a name).

**Thickening and stitching.** A network sheet is a sheet body of one face: `feature.plate` makes it a solid (held to a quarter tube's closed-form volume; `feature.thicken` takes faces of solids only), and booleans cut with it as with any sheet. Sewing several network sheets into one body is `topo::sew` (the STEP importer's), not yet reachable as a command (*Limits*).

**The seam measure** (`network::seam_gaps`): the largest distance between two surfaces along a curve and the largest angle between their tangent planes, at given places, with the largest change between neighbouring places; an estimate, kept for the tests of stage 2. The proven measure is `continuity` (above).

## Limits

- No sweep along two rails yet. The method owed: copies of the profile placed by the similarity that takes its ends to the rails' points at stations along them, and the Gordon surface through the copies and the rails.
- A loft with guides takes open sections only (owed: a periodic Gordon surface for closed sections) and is not ruled or closed; `feature.edit` changes its sections, not its guides.
- Two held edges at one corner pass only where their faces ask the same twist there (their second fundamental forms agree along the corner's directions, as on one smooth face); otherwise the measured tangency refuses the feature. Owed: a corner blend (stage 4, Gregory or subdivided patches).
- Tangency to a torus, or to a spline face along a curve that is not one of its parameter lines, is refused (owed: a tangent ribbon fitted with a proven bound, held to half the tolerance). The parameter line is found from the edge projected at 4 places per knot span (33 to 1025); on a face closed in a direction the coordinates are read across its seam (the fixed one nearest the first place's, the running one followed from place to place by whole turns, `round((x − last) / period)`), and an edge along a line that crosses the seam is refused (owed: the iso-curve joined across the seam).
- Networks larger than the budget (*The size, before it is made*) are refused; the blow-up of re-parametrised families is not reduced yet.
- Networks of rational curves whose weights cannot be made to agree by constants are refused (owed: a common rational reparametrisation, or a polynomial approximation with a proven bound).
- The surface's degree grows: three times a curve's degree where its crossings need a new parameter, three times again across a smooth joint of a chain. Every curve reparametrised for alignment makes the whole family that degree.
- No new sewing command: several sheets are not stitched into a closed body by a feature yet.
- Curvature (G2) is held only where the patch's edge runs along one family's boundary: two edges of one family held in curvature to spline faces are refused (only one face's line can give the family its parameters; owed: comparing through the change of parameter); two held edges at one corner pass only where the measure proves both seams G2 (owed: corner blends, stage 4).
- `continuity` needs one spline face the edge runs along a parameter line of. Two analytic faces (a plane meeting a cylinder) and a torus are refused (owed: an analytic face written across the edge; the torus's quartic form). A spline face against another spline face is compared at corresponding points, so both lines must share the parametrisation (the network's sheets do; otherwise the position gap refuses it; owed: comparing at the feet with a Lipschitz bound). For a cone the angle is to the normal of the quadric's level surface through the patch's point (the cone's own on it).
- The curvature hold along an analytic face solves a dense least-squares problem; its work is capped at 2³³ multiplications (an edge of hundreds of knots is refused). Degree: along an analytic face the patch's degree along the edge becomes at least `2P` (`deg ρ`); two edges of one family held in curvature compound it (the two-cylinder blend: degree 20 along); beyond `MAX_DEGREE` it is refused. Along a spline face it grows by up to 6 (`T`). Owed: a lower-degree `Δ` where `N`'s true degree is found (degree reduction tested exactly).

## Stage 3 (G2) notes

Built 2026-10-30 (surf-ca3) on stage 2: `hold_tangent` localises three rows when asked, `bend` sets the third control point (`bend_curvature`), and the third row is solved by `hold_curvature_implicit` (constrained least squares) or `hold_curvature_fields` (exact fields). The measure lives in `crates/geom/src/network/continuity.rs`. Stage 1 (analysis views: zebra, reflection lines, curvature maps) can call `continuity` for its seam report.

**What G2 means in the measure (2026-10-30, surf-ca3b review fixes).** `a`'s second derivatives along `b`'s normal match `b`'s second fundamental form on the edge's tangent vectors: across `(n_b·S_vv − II_b(S_v,S_v))/|S_v|²`, twist `(n_b·S_uv − II_b(S_u,S_v))/(|S_u||S_v|)` and along `(n_b·S_uu − II_b(S_u,S_u))/|S_u|²`, all three polynomial residuals held to the curvature threshold. The normals' own difference is G1's: `a`'s own forms differ by at most `|n_a − n_b| |S_xy|`. Adding that term was tried and refused every held seam: the angle's *bound* is a rounding bound (about 1e-11 rad on the test seams, the true angle far below), and times `|S_xy|`, which carries the parametric acceleration, it reached the curvature threshold at 1e-3 scale (dome 4.4e-7 vs 4.2e-7). Owed for a stricter G2: `|S_xy|`'s tangential part removed and the angle bounded tighter. Before, only the cross term was checked with `n_b`: a sheet whose cross tangent turns about a straight edge within the angular tolerance (θ(u) = ½·angular·sin 8πu, S_vv = 0) read as G2 although its twist is about 12.6× the threshold (`network_g2.rs` › `a_twist_within_the_angular_tolerance_is_not_g2`). `II_b` is the level surface's form at `a`'s point for an implicit face, and the spline face's at the corresponding point on the projections.

**Rounding far from the origin.** The rows across, `X₁ = Σ cᵢPᵢ` and `X₂`, were formed in global coordinates: at a million units their cancellation is of the points' size, not covered by an allowance of the rows' own size (about 8× the curvature threshold for a unit patch). The faces and the edge are now written about a point of the edge first (exact by Sterbenz near it), and each row enters with `16(p+q+4)ε · Σ|cᵢ| · M`, `Σ|cᵢ|` bounded from the knots (`2p/D_p`, times `2(p−1)/D_{p−1}` for the second) and `M` the net's local magnitude (`spline_rows`; checked against a double-double reference in `continuity.rs` tests). Products use `γ_k`, `k = 4(m+n) + min(m,n) + 8` (the binomial recurrence is inexact above about n = 56). The measure's work is capped (`MAX_SEAM_WORK`); past it an undecided order is not reached and `exhausted` says so.

**Fair across the seam (2026-10-30, g2-fair).** *Finding.* The guide's fin (a sheet held in curvature to a post of radius 20) is G2 at the seam, yet zebra stripes hooked beside it. Measured on the exact surface along the lines leaving the seam (`geom/tests/network_fair.rs`): the normal curvature went from the post's 1/20 at the seam to −0.351 at v = 0.125 and back to +0.051 at v = 0.25, four changes of sign, three turns, `max |dκ/ds|` 0.247 (the tangent-held fin: one change of sign, 0.018; the free sheet: 0.004). The cause was the construction, not the mesh: each condition moved one control point (the second for tangency, the third for curvature) and the knots that localised them were halved towards the edge (0.5, 0.25, 0.125), so the whole change sat in the first spans, as the second derivative of one basis function (positive, then a deep negative lobe). *Method* (`network/fair.rs`). The change `d = D G(t)` uses a scalar spline `G` on the curve's knots that meets the conditions at the edge (`G(0) = 0`; tangency: `G′(0) = 1`, `G″(0) = 0`; curvature: `G′(0) = 0`, `G″(0) = 1`) and has the least `∫ G‴²` (Farin, ch. 23: the energy of fairing, here with its end conditions; a linear system in the free coefficients, its Gram matrix by Gauss–Legendre with `q − 2` nodes per span, exact). The conditions are linear, so the seam gets exactly what the one-point move gave it and `continuity` proves it as before. The reach is cut into 8 equal spans: to the next crossing curve (ending there with every derivative), or, when that is the far edge, over the whole curve keeping only its last point when the far edge is free (half way when it is held itself). The curves crossing the edge are bent so (`bend`, `bend_curvature`); the curvature hold's correction between the crossings (`put_on_third_row`) is spread by the same profile but keeps a free far edge's position, tangent plane and curvature (`End::Kept`: spread to it, it bent a dome's far edge from −1.4 to −9.6 between two crossings); the tangent hold's drift correction stays on its one row (spread, it moved a far-from-origin dome's rows past their ribbon). The refusals judge the spread change by the row the one-point move had (the profile's shape is fixed, its size is the change's). *What no such change can do:* one that ends with the curve's own position and tangent has `∫ Δκ ds ≈ 0` and `∫ (L − s) Δκ ds ≈ 0`, so the curvature must swing past the sheet's own once; for a straight curve the swing is about 0.38 of the change at the seam (the quintic `t²(1 − t)³`). *Result:* the fin's curvature runs from 0.05 at the seam to −0.137 and back to the sheet's own at the far edge, one change of sign, one turn, `max |dκ/ds|` 0.042 (2.3 times the tangent fin's, was 13 times); pinned for a plane, a cylinder, a sphere and a spline face (`curvature_held_sheets_are_fair_across_their_seam`: at most one turn and one change of sign along the crossing lines, a swing past the band of the face's and the tangent sheet's curvature of at most 0.75 of the change at the seam, at most three times as steep as the tangent sheet). *Far from the origin* the holds worked on differences of coordinates a million times the network's size, and the curvature hold's least squares amplified their rounding (a dome of 1.5e-3 a million units out: its third row's change 12.3 in weight against 0.004 on main, by luck of the rounding); `gordon` now builds a network farther from the origin than its extent about its centre and moves the result back (each control point rounded by `|c| ε`). *Also found:* `coefficients` scaled a column of pure rounding (a sphere's field at a dome's corner a million units out, 4.7e-10 against 1.6e-3) to unit length and gave it a coefficient of −7.6; a column no larger than `64 ε (|c| w + L)` (the rounding of the coordinates it came from) now takes none.

**Degree growth.** Each G2 edge along an analytic face raises the patch's degree across: the third row's change Δ has degree `D = 2P` in the common case (P the degree along), and two curvature edges of one family give `4P` (the two-cylinder blend: degree 20). The measure's bound polynomials have degree `5p` (implicit face) or `4p + 20q` (spline face), refused past `MAX_SEAM_DEGREE`.

## Stage 1: analysis

The tools that show surface quality (2026-10-30). Views, not features: `ui.set {"analysis": …}` (`ui_egui::state::AnalysisState`), `view.render`'s `analysis` and the window's Analysis toggle parse the same object (`craft3d_engine::analysis::AnalysisSpec`); nothing is saved in a document, and no analysis is the plain picture bit for bit. Code: `crates/render/src/analysis.rs` (the definition, the CPU oracle), `crates/gpu/src/scene.wgsl` › `analysis_of` (the same per fragment), `crates/engine/src/analysis.rs` (values, combs, measures), `crates/engine/src/analysis_cmds.rs` (`measure.draft`, `measure.curvature`), `crates/geom/src/curvature.rs` › `curve_curvature`.

**One colour per sample, in place of the shading.** Every mode is a function of what the rasteriser already interpolates per sample (perspective-correctly): the normal (the tessellator's vertex normals are the exact surface's, so between vertices the error is the interpolation's, of the order of the angle criterion's square), the point, and a value per vertex. Both renderers evaluate the same formulas from the numbers of `analysis::Resolved`, computed once per picture in `f64` (the GPU narrows them last), and both draw an analysis in the plain look (the studio look is set aside while one is on).

- *Zebra.* An environment of parallel light tubes along an axis `d` in the screen plane (`direction` turns it), at infinity. The reflected ray `r = 2(n·v)n − v` (the normal turned to the viewer) has the angle `φ = atan2(r·b₂, r·b₁)` about `d` (`b₁` towards the viewer, `b₂ = d × b₁`); the phase `φ/2π · stripes + offset` (`stripes` rounded to a whole number, so the stripes close up behind) is dark where its fraction is below `width`. Closed form on a sphere seen square on: along the vertical through its middle `φ = −2θ` (θ the angle of the normal from the view), so the stripes are evenly spaced in the angle on the sphere, and the picture is mirror symmetric (the stripes of a tunnel on a sphere are a pencil of arcs through the two points where the normal bisects `v` and `d`, not circles). On a cylinder seen square to its axis the normal does not change along a ruling, nor does its interpolation, so the stripes are straight along the axis.
- *Highlight lines* (Beier & Chen, *Highlight-line algorithm for realtime surface-quality assessment*, CAD 26(4), 1994). View independent: light lines in a plane `M = +Z` a quarter of its diagonal above the model's box (close, so that the lines spread little over a curved surface), along `E = (cos a, sin a, 0)`, spaced a diagonal over `stripes`. The normal line through the point meets the plane at `Q = P + tN`, `t = (A − P)·M / (N·M)`; the phase is `(Q − A)·(M × E) / spacing + offset`. Where `|N·M| < 0.05` (`GRAZING`) the normal line runs nearly along the plane and the sample is the neutral colour: the phase grows as `stripes / |N·M|`, and this bound keeps the GPU's `f32` phase within about 10⁻³ of a period of the CPU's (found when the torus's grazing band put pixels 225 levels apart at 10⁻³).
- *Curvature map.* The value per vertex comes from the exact surface: each vertex of the scene's mesh is projected onto its face's surface (from the previous vertex's parameters, else from scratch, and only accepted within four chordal tolerances of the vertex) and `craft3d_geom::curvature::curvatures` gives `K` and `H` there from the exact second derivatives; `H` is signed positive where the body is convex (outward normal; for a sheet, its face's normal, so a network dome may come out negative), the largest is `|H| + √(H² − K)`. Interpolated per sample like the normal and coloured by a five-stop ramp over `range` (default: symmetric about 0 to the 95th percentile of the magnitudes, from 0 for the largest, so a pole or a sharp blend does not wash the rest out), lit by `0.55 + 0.45 |n·v|` so the shape still reads. A vertex without a value (no normal, a projection that missed) is the neutral colour.
- *Refined for the map* (2026-10-30, g2-fair): the curvature map interpolates a value per vertex, and the scene's mesh follows the chordal and angle criteria, not the curvature's change: the guide's G2 fin had 28 triangles and a centroid a quarter of the largest |H| off. `view.render` draws the map on `analysis::refined_map`: an edge inside a face whose ends' values differ by more than 2 % of the map's range is split at its midpoint put on the exact surface (its normal and exact value), a triangle with one, two or three split edges becomes two, three or four (conforming), border edges and edges shorter than the scene's tolerance are not split, at most 8 rounds. Both renderers draw the same refined mesh, so parity is untouched. The window still draws the scene's mesh (owed: the viewport's upload of a mesh of its own for the map).
- *Draft.* `s = n·p` with `p` the unit pull and `n` the normal as the mesh has it (outward), the draft angle `asin s`: `s ≥ sin(minDraft)` in, `s ≤ −sin(minDraft)` out, between them too shallow; lit like the map.
- *Combs.* Curves sampled at `density` places; at each the curvature vector `κN = (C′ × C″) × C′ / |C′|⁴` (`curve_curvature`: exact second derivatives, closed forms for lines, circles and ellipses, basis derivatives A2.3 and the quotient rule for NURBS; for an iso-curve `S(u₀, ·)` from `S_v` and `S_vv`) and a spike `−scale · κN` away from the centre of curvature; the line through the tips. `scale` automatic makes the largest spike a tenth of the model's diagonal. Edges are every edge (or the edges named) of the bodies in view; an iso-curve is at a fraction of the face's parameter box as its mesh spans it (not clipped to the trim). The window draws them as overlay lines (`LineKind::Comb`, the theme's `comb`), tested against the depth like a trace; `view.render` draws them with `analysis::draw_lines` (one pixel, hidden behind a body by its depth).

**Tolerance, GPU against CPU** (`crates/gpu/tests/analysis.rs`, every adapter; the doubt pixels are compared too: the GPU must be within 2 levels of one of the CPU's pictures, the plain one or a shifted one, and the share in doubt is held per scene to a small margin over its measured share): outside the band of one pixel around silhouettes and face edges and around where a face hides another part of itself (the CPU frame's distance jumps by more than 2 % between neighbours: the torus's inner rim over its far side, a silhouette the face-id band does not see), and outside the pixels *in doubt*, every channel within 2 levels and at most 1/1024 of the pixels compared more than 1 level apart. A pixel is in doubt where a sample sits on a decision within what `f32` can tell: the CPU picture drawn with the stripes shifted by ±10⁻³ of a period (zebra), by ±10⁻² of a period and the grazing bound by ±10⁻⁴ of itself (highlight lines, whose phase moves by up to `stripes / |N·M|²` periods per radian of the normal), or the least draft by ±10⁻⁴ rad, or with the normals turned by ±10⁻⁵ rad about two axes (fifty times an interpolated `f32` normal's rounding; this catches the highlight lines' samples, whose phase moves fastest with the normal), differs there; the shares measured (every adapter alike) are zebra 0.012, highlight lines 0.118, the map 0.0013, draft 0.0017, and each scene's cap is a quarter above; a pixel in doubt must match one of the CPU's pictures, at most 1/1024 of them not (two samples each going the other way under different shifts). The map has no decision: its ramp is continuous. The vertex layout carries the value and whether there is one (56 bytes a vertex, was 48).

**Measures.**

- `draft {faces, direction, minDraft}` (and `measure.draft`): the least draft angle (radians as an assertion, degrees in the command's result) and `shareBelow`, the share of the faces' area below `minDraft` (not `min`, which is an assertion's bound). A plane is exact (its normal, oriented by the face). Elsewhere the normals at the vertices of a mesh within 2·10⁻⁴ of the body's size and 8° between neighbours (exact there) give the least at a real place, the upper bound; the lower bound (`atLeast`) is the least over the triangles of a bound per triangle. On a cylinder it is exact: the normal depends only on the angle about the axis, `n·d = s R cos(θ − φ)`, and its least over the triangle's angular range is the ends' or `−R` (the bound is then the true least over the meshed face, however long the cylinder; a cylinder tilted 30° from the pull: `atLeast` −30° to 10⁻⁹). On a sphere it is the triangle's least corner less `2 asin(c / 2r)` (`c` its longest side: at most the 8° the mesh allows between neighbours, so within about 8° of the least); on a torus less `κ_max · π/2 · c` (`κ_max` the larger of `1/minor` and `1/|major − minor|`), within about 12°. So an assertion is decided where its bound is that far from the least found. On a cone and a spline face the least is sampled (`proven: false`, `atLeast` null) and an assertion on it is not proven. The share is by triangles, each classed by its corners' mean normal (sampled, except on planes). Each face's triangles are gathered once per body (the cost is the mesh's, not faces × triangles).
- `curvature {of, at}` (and `measure.curvature`): the face nearest to `at` by its mesh, the exact surface's nearest point on it, and there `K` (the number), `H`, `k1`, `k2`. Without `at` it is the largest |K| of the faces, as before.

**Tests.** `render/tests/analysis.rs`: zebra on a sphere against `φ = −2θ` at every pixel down the middle (except within 0.02 of a period of an edge) with the count of stripe edges, and mirror symmetric; on a cylinder every column of the side one colour; draft classes of a cylinder; the map's ramp; determinism, and no analysis is the plain picture. `gpu/tests/analysis.rs`: each mode against the CPU (sphere, cylinder in perspective, torus; the map with values) and determinism. `engine/tests/analysis.rs`: a sphere's map is `1/R²`, `1/R`, `1/R` at every vertex and a torus's `cos v / (r(R + r cos v))` at every vertex (three scales); `measure.curvature` at the torus's equators and top against closed forms; a block drafted 7° is 7° exactly and its assertions pass and fail as they should; a cone is sampled; **zebra across a seam**: two quarter cylinders made by `feature.surface.coons`, beside a quarter of the same cylinder (G2) or of one three times its radius tangent to it (G1 only), seen in perspective down the seam; every stripe edge is traced on both sides and its direction where it meets the seam fitted (a quadratic within 14 pixels), and the median kink is below 4° at the G2 seam and above 8° (and three times the G2's) at the G1 seam; `view.render` with each mode, deterministic, and every bad option an error. `ui-egui/tests/analysis.rs`: `ui.set`, `ui.state` and `ui.screenshot` with stripes, combs and the other modes; refusals change nothing; `null` is the plain picture again.

### Limits of stage 1

- The draft of a cone or a spline face is sampled, not proven (owed: a bound on the normal's turn over a patch from the surface's second derivative bounds, as `geom::offset::curvature_bound` gives for a rational Bézier patch).
- An iso-curve comb spans the face's parameter box, not only its trimmed region.
- The map's values are computed on the window's thread when the analysis, the scene or the theme changes (a projection per vertex: about a second for a million vertices), not in the background.
- Parity is not held where stripes are finer than the samples (a moiré): there the renderers differ by single samples that no shift of the phase, the grazing bound or the normal explains (highlight lines at 20 on a small torus, 2026-10-30; also at 640 × 480 seen closer); the parity scenes keep their stripes resolved. Owed: a stripe filtered over the sample's footprint (the phase's derivative), which would also take the moiré away.
- `continuity {edge}` (G0 to G3 along a seam, proven) is stage 3's measure (`r1-surf-ca3`), not repeated here.
