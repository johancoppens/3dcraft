# 3DCraft design: Freeform surfacing (curve networks)

Sheets through networks of curves: the Coons patch of four boundary curves, the Gordon surface of u- and v-curves that cross, lofts along guide curves, and edges held in position on a face or tangent (G1) to one. Code: `crates/geom/src/network.rs` (the geometry), `crates/regen/src/network.rs` (curves and faces from the history, the sheet body and its names), `crates/engine/src/surface_cmds.rs` (`feature.surface.network`, `feature.surface.coons`), `crates/regen/src/loft.rs` › `guided` (`feature.loft` with `guides`). The plan, with stages 1 (analysis), 3 (G2) and 4 (blends, n-sided corners), is `plan/class-a.md`; this is stage 2.

## Method

**Exact, never fitted.** Every result is a B-spline surface built from the curves by exact operations on homogeneous control points (`[x w, y w, z w, w]`): knot insertion, degree elevation, Bézier pieces, composition with a polynomial, Bernstein products, interpolation at given parameters. Nothing is sampled into being, so there is no approximation whose error would need a bound. Arcs and circles take part as rational curves (`network::arc`: quarter-turn rational quadratic pieces, end weights 1).

**Curves.** A network curve is a sketch's one open chain or edges of earlier bodies end to end (`doc::Rail`, as a developable's rails; `regen::developable::rail_of`). Its pieces become one spline (`network::chain`): every piece raised to one degree, its homogeneous points scaled so it meets the piece before in four dimensions, and where two pieces meet tangentially (within the angular tolerance) each piece gets the length of its control polygon as its share of the parameter and its end speeds brought to that rate by the exact Hermite reparametrisation of `refine::reparametrized` (three times its degree), so the joined curve is once differentiable in its parameter, as a single spline is. Corners stay corners.

**The Gordon surface** (`network::gordon`; Gordon 1969, Piegl & Tiller §10.5):
1. *Crossings.* Every u-curve must cross every v-curve within the tolerance: closest points by Gauss-Newton on `|C(s) − D(t)|²` from the best pair of samples (8 per knot span, at least 32). Each curve is turned to run from the other family's first curve to its last; it must cross the other family in order, and end on the first and last curve of the other family (those bound the sheet).
2. *Common crossing parameters.* A family's crossing parameters are the means over its curves. A curve crossed elsewhere is cut at its crossings and each piece composed with the increasing cubic taking the common parameters to its own; the slopes at the crossings are the harmonic means of the neighbouring secants (Fritsch and Butland), so every cubic's Bernstein slopes are at most 2 and it increases, and the curve stays once differentiable in its parameter. Exact: the same points at three times the degree.
3. *Weights.* A rational curve may be multiplied by a constant without changing. The constants that make crossing curves agree in their homogeneous points solve `ln rᵢ − ln sⱼ = ln w_v(v̄ᵢ) − ln w_u(ūⱼ)` in closed form (row and column means of a two-way table); a residual above 1e-9 means no constants exist and the network is refused (its rational curves are not one rational surface's). A curve that is a point (a pole) takes the weights of its neighbour in its family: a point's weight function is free.
4. *Boolean sum.* `S = A + B − T`: the u-curves interpolated across v at the common parameters (degree `min(3, count − 1)`, knots by averaging), the v-curves across u likewise, minus the tensor interpolant of the crossing points (the v-curves' points there, so the u-curves are reproduced exactly and the v-curves within the crossing gap, reported as `mismatch`). The three are brought to one degree and knot vector each way and added. The sum reproduces exactly any surface that lies in the spaces of both interpolations: a bilinear patch from its sides, a tensor B-spline from its iso-curves at parameters whose averages are its knots, the rational cylinder and sphere octant from their circles and lines.

**The Coons patch** is the Gordon surface of two curves each way (the bilinearly blended Coons patch); `feature.surface.coons` takes four curves round the patch.

**Edges held.** Each of the four edges (`uFirst`, `uLast`, `vFirst`, `vLast`) is free, in position on a face (its curve lies on the face: checked at 64 places per knot span and more, refused beyond the tolerance), or tangent to a face:
- *The crossing curves leave along the face.* Each curve crossing the edge gets its end tangent projected into the face's tangent plane there, keeping its speed, times the edge's weight: the control point beside the end moves (the curve raised to degree 3 at least, a knot inserted so that point's basis function ends before the next crossing, so nothing else of the curve changes). A curve that is itself a held edge keeps its shape and must already leave along the face (else the corner is refused).
- *The cross derivative put into the tangent planes.* After the sum, along the edge (the net turned so the edge is `v = 0`): the target cross derivative is `E(u) = Σ aₖ(u) Fₖ(u) + b(u) C′(u) + c(u) C(u)` in homogeneous terms, `C` the edge curve and `Fₖ` exact fields whose projections span the face's tangent planes along it: for a plane its two axes (constant); for a cylinder its axis and `d × (A − w o)`; for a cone `A − w·apex` and `d × (A − w o)`; for a sphere `eₖ × (A − w c)`; for a spline face the edge must run along one of its parameter lines, and the edge curve is replaced by that line (exactly, cut to the edge) with the face's homogeneous derivative across it as the field, both carried through every change of parameter. The scalars interpolate, at the crossings, the least-squares coefficients of the derivative the surface already has there (which lies in the plane, by the first step). Then `E − S_v(u, 0)` (an exact spline, by Bernstein products) is added to the second row of control points times `(t_{q+1} − t₁)/q`, with the degree across at least 3 and the knots across arranged so that row's basis function ends before the next u-curve: every curve of the network stays on the surface, every other edge keeps its position and cross derivative.
- *Checked.* The angle between the tangent planes is then found along every tangent edge (32 places per knot span, 64 at least; reported as `tangency`); above ten times the angular tolerance the feature is refused. That is where two held edges meeting at a corner show a twist conflict.

**Names** (rule 4). The face is `F9:side(k₁, k₂, k₃, k₄)` after the four edge curves (the first and last u-curve, the first and last v-curve), each by the id of its sketch, or of the feature of its first edge. Its edges are `F9:startEdge(f, k)` and `F9:endEdge(f, k)` (`f` 0 for the u family, 1 for v) after their curve; its corners `F9:startVertex(e, kᵤ, kᵥ)` on the first u-curve and `F9:endVertex(e, kᵤ, kᵥ)` on the last (`e` 0 at the first v-curve, 1 at the last). A pole is one vertex and no edge. Inserting a curve inside a family changes no name.

**A loft with guides** (`feature.loft {"guides": [...]}`): open sections as u-curves and the guides as v-curves of a network; the first and last guide are the sheet's edges.

**Thickening and stitching.** A network sheet is a sheet body of one face: `feature.plate` makes it a solid (held to a quarter tube's closed-form volume; `feature.thicken` takes faces of solids only), and booleans cut with it as with any sheet. Sewing several network sheets into one body is `topo::sew` (the STEP importer's), not yet reachable as a command (*Limits*).

**The seam measure** (`network::seam_gaps`): the largest distance between two surfaces along a curve and the largest angle between their tangent planes, at given places, with the largest change between neighbouring places. A minimal stand-in for stage 1's `continuity` measure, used by the tests; its bound is an estimate, not a proof.

## Limits

- No sweep along two rails yet. The method owed: copies of the profile placed by the similarity that takes its ends to the rails' points at stations along them, and the Gordon surface through the copies and the rails.
- A loft with guides takes open sections only (owed: a periodic Gordon surface for closed sections) and is not ruled or closed; `feature.edit` changes its sections, not its guides.
- Two held edges at one corner pass only where their faces ask the same twist there (their second fundamental forms agree along the corner's directions, as on one smooth face); otherwise the measured tangency refuses the feature. Owed: a corner blend (stage 4, Gregory or subdivided patches).
- Tangency to a torus, or to a spline face along a curve that is not one of its parameter lines, is refused (owed: a tangent ribbon fitted with a proven bound, held to half the tolerance).
- Networks of rational curves whose weights cannot be made to agree by constants are refused (owed: a common rational reparametrisation, or a polynomial approximation with a proven bound).
- The surface's degree grows: three times a curve's degree where its crossings need a new parameter, three times again across a smooth joint of a chain. Every curve reparametrised for alignment makes the whole family that degree.
- No new sewing command: several sheets are not stitched into a closed body by a feature yet.
- `seam_gaps` samples; stage 1 builds the proven `continuity` measure.

## What stage 3 (G2) builds on

`hold_tangent` is the place: a G2 condition adds the second-derivative row, solving the third row of control points for `S_vv` in the face's normal curvature along the edge, with the same exact fields (the second derivative of a spline face across its parameter line is as exact as the first), the same locality rule (the third row's basis function must end before the next curve: two more knots), and the same checking, with a curvature gap measured alongside the angle. The crossing curves then need their second derivative at the edge matched as well (the second control point beside the end), which `bend` does for the first.
