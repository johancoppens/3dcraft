# Origami: crease patterns, rigid folding, Kawasaki and Maekawa

A crease pattern on a flat sheet, folded rigidly by its fold angles: `feature.fold` (the document's `Fold`), `fold.edit`, `fold.inspect`, `fold.check`, and the crease pattern through `file.export` with `fold`. The kernel's part is `craft3d_kernel::origami` (`crates/kernel/src/origami/`), regeneration's `crates/regen/src/fold.rs`, the commands `crates/engine/src/fold_cmds.rs`. Built 2026-10-26 (R1 block *sheets*, item 4: `plan/r1-sheets.md`).

## Method

**The sheet and the creases.** A fold reads one sketch. The crease lines are lines of it (drawn or construction), each a *mountain*, a *valley* or a *cut*; the sheet is bounded by the closed profiles of the sketch's other curves (`detect_profiles`, so holes and several pieces come for free), which must be straight lines. Seen from the side the sketch's normal points to, a valley folds the paper towards the viewer; its fold angle counts positive, a mountain's negative (Tachi's convention). A given angle is the magnitude, 0 (flat) to 180 degrees (folded flat); the kind says which way.

**Facets: the planar arrangement** (`arrange.rs`). The boundary pieces and the creases are one set of segments; every segment is split where another crosses it (a proper crossing, both parameters further than the tolerance from the ends) or ends on it (an end within the tolerance of the other segment). Points within the tolerance of each other are one vertex (chained, sorted by x; a cluster wider than four tolerances is refused as finer than the tolerance). Two segments that touch the same stretch (two of the points where one touches the other more than a tolerance apart) lie along each other and are refused. The faces are traced on the half-edges: round each vertex the half-edges that leave it are sorted counter-clockwise, and after a half-edge comes the one before its twin, so every face is walked with its inside on the left. A counter-clockwise cycle bounds a face; each connected piece of the graph has one clockwise cycle, its outside, which is a hole of the smallest counter-clockwise cycle *of another piece* around it (asked of other pieces only: a cycle of the same piece shares its vertices), or the outside of everything. A face lies in the sheet when its boundary pieces run the way the sheet's loops run (the sheet on their left) and is dropped when they run against them; a face bounded by creases alone lies in the sheet, because every piece of a crease is checked first to lie inside the sheet (its middle, by even-odd). The checks a crease pattern must pass, each by name: inside the sheet; dividing what it crosses (a crease with the same facet on both sides ends inside a facet, *dangling*; a cut doing so is a *slit*); no cycle of zero area (a crease floating free); no facet thinner than the tolerance (area at most the tolerance times its perimeter). Each fold crease piece between two facets is a *hinge*.

**Rigid folding** (`solve.rs`). Each facet moves as a rigid body. A breadth-first forest from the fixed facet (the largest, or the one holding `fixed`), then from the first facet of each other piece of a cut sheet, places every facet by the product of the rotations along its path, `T_child = T_parent ∘ R(crease, ±ρ)`, each rotation about the crease as it lies in the flat sheet (−ρ when the parent is on the crease's left). Every hinge outside the forest closes a loop and holds only when its two facets agree: `T_right = T_left ∘ R(crease, −ρ)`. This is the loop-closure condition of rigid origami (the product of the fold rotations round a loop is the identity: Belcastro and Hull's condition, Demaine and O'Rourke 2007, §13.1), here for every fundamental cycle of the facet graph, so it holds for any sheet: one vertex or many, with holes, cuts and several pieces, without asking which loops go round a vertex. Each loop is written as three points of the right facet (the crease's ends and a point beside it) that must land in the same place both ways round: nine numbers per loop, lengths, all zero when the loop closes.

Angles that are given are kept; the others are solved by Levenberg–Marquardt on those numbers, with the exact derivative: turning crease `k` by `dρ` moves a point `X` beyond it by `s D × (X − P) dρ`, `P` and `D` the crease's place and direction as its parent facet has it, `s` its sign in the forest. The normal equations are gathered row by row from the rows' few non-zero entries (a row depends only on the creases on the paths of its two facets), and solved dense with partial pivoting; at most 400 unknowns (`MAX_FREE`). The solve works about the middle of the sheet's box and its stopping rule and penalty are scaled by the box's diagonal, so a sheet far from the sketch's origin folds as one at the origin. **The fold is the continuation from the flat sheet**, and that is part of what is accepted. The given angles are approached in steps, the first of 1 degree of the largest, then doubling up to 10 degrees. The first step starts at the flat sheet, where the branches of a vertex meet (a flat-foldable degree-4 vertex has two, the modes below) and there is no tangent: every unknown crease is turned its kind's way by 1, 3, 10, 30 and 100 times the step (at most 170 degrees) as guesses, and a solution is taken when it closes, keeps every kind and lies on a branch through the flat sheet: solved again at half the step it is half as far, within a quarter (a smooth branch through the flat sheet is straight near it, whatever its slope). Every guess is tried; when two different solutions both pass, the kinds do not tell the branches apart and the fold is refused as *ambiguous*, naming the creases that differ (give one of them an angle). A joint without a kind (sign 0, which the linkage allows) is guessed both ways, every pattern up to six such joints. No crease pattern with mountains and valleys assigned was found where two branches through the flat sheet keep every kind (a search of random degree-6 vertices with three angles given found none), so the test is a degree-4 vertex whose solved creases have no kind. Every later step is predicted along the tangent of the branch, `J_free dx = −J_given dg` at the last solution (least squares, the normal equations' free block), and corrected by Levenberg–Marquardt from the prediction; it is taken when the corrector closes, keeps every kind and lands within a quarter of the predicted motion (plus the angular tolerance). A step not taken is halved, at most 24 times in a row (4 for the first step: near the flat sheet the branches are straight, so a smaller first step finds no other), and then the fold fails as *could not be followed from the flat sheet* (`NotFollowed`). Where `J_free` is singular partway (a bifurcation, or a branch that stops) there is no tangent, and the fold fails as *undetermined*, naming the creases (more freedom than the given angles fix, or a bifurcation). Nothing compares how fast the solved creases move with the given ones: a vertex whose solved creases fold fifty times as fast near the flat sheet (mode B at sectors of 61 and 59 degrees) and a Miura sheet of 88-degree parallelograms (1 / cos γ ≈ 29) fold by their closed forms. A solved crease turned against its kind also costs the sheet's size per radian in the least squares (zero at every solution that keeps the kinds). The whole solve has a budget of work (`MAX_WORK`, 2·10¹⁰ multiplications of the factorisations and residuals, some 20 seconds at the worst): past it the fold fails (`TooMuchWork`), so regeneration is bounded in time; 60 iterations per step. At the end the solution must close within the tolerance (else *not rigidly foldable at these angles*, naming the worst loop, or *did not converge*); the derivative's columns, scaled to unit length, must have full rank (a symmetric pivoted elimination; a pivot below 1e-10 leaves its crease *undetermined*, named), **also at the flat sheet**: with no angle given, or every given angle 0, solved creases are undetermined (at the flat sheet the branches meet and nothing says which way the others go), and the fold fails rather than lie flat; every solved crease must turn its kind's way within the angular tolerance, its angle wrapped into (−180, 180] degrees first. A solved crease on no loop at all (a bridge of the facet graph) is refused before solving. A crease line with an angle folds every piece of it by that angle; without one, each piece is its own unknown (a line through several vertices need not fold alike along its length).

**One fold mechanism** (`solve.rs`: `Linkage`, `Joint`, `fold_linkage`). The solve knows bodies and joints, not crease patterns: each joint has its own axis in space (a point, a unit direction, a side to sample the loop at, a reach), and the motion of its right body relative to its left one at angle ρ, `Joint::motion` (today a turn about the axis by −ρ), with its velocity `Joint::velocity`; `origami::fold` builds the linkage of an arrangement (axes in the sheet's plane, about the box's middle) and maps the motions back. A bend of sheet metal plugs in as a joint whose motion is the turn followed by the offset of its neutral fibre (the arc of radius `r + k t` swept by ρ moves the right body along the bend's normal and tangent by closed forms in ρ), with that offset's derivative added to the velocity; the forest, the loop closure, the continuation, the rank and the kinds' rule stay as they are, so folding a crease pattern and bending a part are one mechanism. Owed to `r1-plates` beside it: (b) the input is a sketch whose sheet is bounded by straight lines (`regen/src/fold.rs`, the boundary and the creases read from one sketch); a plate's outline and bend lines from faces or from an unfolded developable need their own reader; (c) `folded_sheet` builds a zero-thickness sheet whose names are keyed to sketch curve ids; a plate needs the solid between two offset sheets, with the bend zones as cylindrical faces.


**Closed forms** (the oracles). A flat-foldable degree-4 vertex with sector angles α, β, π − α, π − β (counter-clockwise from crease 1) has two modes (Huffman 1976; Tachi 2009): mode A, ρ₃ = ρ₁, ρ₄ = −ρ₂, tan(ρ₂/2) = −(cos((α+β)/2) / cos((α−β)/2)) tan(ρ₁/2); mode B, ρ₃ = −ρ₁, ρ₄ = ρ₂, tan(ρ₂/2) = (sin((α+β)/2) / sin((α−β)/2)) tan(ρ₁/2). The Miura-ori vertex (sectors γ, π − γ, π − γ, γ: α + β = π) folds in mode B: the straight crease pieces by ±ρ, the zigzag ones by 2 atan(tan(ρ/2) / cos γ), γ the parallelograms' acute angle. Both were checked numerically before use (a closure product of rotation matrices solved by `scipy`, 2026-10-26, scratch).

**Contact** (`collide.rs`). No feature hands on a body that passes through itself or touches itself ([`regeneration.md`](regeneration.md) › *Never through itself*), so a fold whose facets meet other than where the pattern joins them fails, naming the two facets, and the existing `overlap` measure (a volume in common) has nothing to say about a sheet. Two facets in planes that cross meet only on the planes' common line `L`: each facet's part of `L` is a set of intervals (where its boundary crosses `L`, and between those points where the middle lies in the facet, closed), and the facets meet where their intervals overlap after what the pattern shares is taken out (the stretch of a shared edge, a tolerance round each shared vertex; the two sides of a cut share its edge and vertices). What is left is *through* where its middle lies inside both facets (further than the tolerance from their edges) and longer than the tolerance, *touch* otherwise. Two facets in one plane (normals within the angular tolerance, every corner within the tolerance of the other's plane) meet when a corner of one that is not shared lies in the other (inside or within the tolerance of its edges), or when their edges cross properly away from the shared vertices: a fold of 180 degrees lays facets on one another, which a sheet of no thickness cannot be, so it is refused as a touch. The pairs looked at are those whose boxes (grown by the tolerance) meet, found by the kernel's box tree (`boxtree.rs`), in index order; the first meeting is the error. A facet that cannot be placed is an error too, never "no meeting" (fail closed). Parallel means the sine of the angle between the normals is at most the angular tolerance.

**The body** (`sheet.rs`). One planar face per facet (its plane the sketch's plane carried by the facet's motion, facing the way the sketch's normal is carried), one straight edge per hinge shared by its two facets, one per piece of the boundary and one per side of a cut piece. Round a vertex the facets meet in *fans*, runs joined by fold creases and ended by the boundary or a cut; each fan is one vertex of the body, so the boundary passes each vertex once and the pieces of a cut sheet share nothing. One shell per piece. The body passes `topo::check` as a sheet. Names are a fill's (`extrude::fill`, [`names.md`](names.md)): a facet `F7:startCap(e1,e5,e8)` after the sorted curves of its outer boundary (a piece number when two facets have the same), an edge `F7:startEdge(e5)` with its piece counted along the curve's own direction (a cut's two sides are pieces 2i and 2i + 1), a vertex `F7:startVertex(e1,e5)` after the curves meeting there and its fan.

**Kawasaki and Maekawa** (`checks.rs`, `fold.check`). At every interior vertex (every edge there a fold crease; vertices on the boundary have no condition, vertices on a cut are listed apart and not checked), with its creases counter-clockwise: Kawasaki (also Justin), the sectors taken alternately add up to π each; it holds when the first sum is within the angular tolerance plus the angle the linear tolerance makes at the shortest crease there (a point may lie anywhere within the linear tolerance), and an odd number of creases never holds. Maekawa: |M − V| = 2. Each vertex reports its sums, how far off, M, V and the difference. **Both are necessary for a vertex to fold flat and neither is sufficient**: a vertex also needs a mountain–valley assignment that meets the big-little-big condition, and a pattern whose every vertex passes both may still not fold flat, because whether a crease pattern folds flat is NP-hard (Bern and Hayes 1996). The command's summary, its result (`note`, `message`) and the guide say so; nothing in 3DCraft claims a pattern folds flat.

**The crease pattern as a drawing.** `file.export` with `fold` writes the flat pattern in the sketch's axes: the outline on the layer `boundary` (black, continuous), mountains on `mountain` (red, dash-dot), valleys on `valley` (blue, dashed), cuts on `cut` (black), each crease line whole, dashes a sixtieth of the outline's diagonal. The drawing crate gained the dash-dot line (`Layer::dot`: DXF line type `DASHDOT`, dash, a quarter gap, a dot, a quarter gap; SVG `stroke-dasharray` of four, the dot a tenth of the dash); a drawing without one is written byte for byte as before.

## The rules this adds, and why each is general

- *Split where a segment crosses or ends on another* (crossing parameters further than the tolerance from the ends; ends within the tolerance of a segment): the definition of the arrangement, for any straight segments.
- *Points within the tolerance are one vertex; a chained cluster wider than four tolerances is refused*: the document's tolerance decides what coincides; a cluster that wide means the pattern is finer than the tolerance can tell, which no rule could resolve.
- *Two touch-points more than a tolerance apart means two segments lie along each other*: true for straight segments of any direction.
- *A cycle of area at most the tolerance times its perimeter* is a floating crease (no area) or a sliver thinner than the tolerance: the tolerance as a width.
- *Hole of another piece only*: a property of planar graphs (a connected piece's own cycles share its vertices).
- *A first step of 1 degree, then up to 10; the first step's guesses at 1, 3, 10, 30, 100 times the step and its half-step test (within a quarter); later steps accepted when the corrector lands within a quarter of the tangent's prediction; at most 24 halvings; the kinds' penalty weighted by the sheet's size; 60 iterations per step; a work budget of 2·10¹⁰*: these decide what is accepted: the fold is the continuation from the flat sheet along a branch, scale-free (fractions of the step and of the predicted motion, never a speed); a fold that cannot be followed, reaches a bifurcation or needs more work than the budget is refused with the reason, not guessed.
- *Rank pivot 1e-10 on unit-scaled columns*: numerical rank; a crease left free at the solution is refused, never guessed.
- *At most 400 unknowns* (dense solve), *4096 creases*, *4096 boundary pieces*: caps on the work, refused with the reason beyond.
- *Coplanar when the sine of the angle between the normals is within the angular tolerance and the corners within the linear one*: the document's tolerances.
- *The solve about the middle of the sheet's box, its stopping rule (10⁻¹³ of the box's diagonal, or a thousandth of the tolerance) and penalty by the box's diagonal*: scale and place do not change the answer.
- *Kawasaki's slack: the angular tolerance plus the linear tolerance over the shortest crease*: what the two tolerances allow at that vertex.

## Limits, refusals and what is owed

- **Straight creases only.** A curved crease bends the facets beside it, which rigid folding does not do; it needs the developable surfaces of `r1-develop` (curved-crease origami: the facets become developable strips).
- **A sheet bounded by straight lines.** An outline with arcs or splines is refused, naming the curve; owed: the arrangement of lines and curves (splitting a curved boundary by the creases).
- **No thickness.** The sheet has none, so folding to 180 degrees is a touch and is refused; thick origami (hinge offsets, `r1-plates`) is owed. A fold to just under 180 degrees is built.
- **A slit** (a cut that ends inside a facet) is refused: the facet's loop would run along both sides of it with two coincident vertices at its mouth; owed with a sheet check that allows it.
- **Dense solve**, at most 400 angles to solve at once and a work budget; a larger pattern is refused with the advice to give more angles; owed: a sparse factorisation (the normal equations are sparse along the paths; the solve crate's sparse factor is crate-private and on the same layer, so reusing it is a layering decision).
- **Pattern instances.** Creases are lines drawn in the sketch, not instances of a sketch pattern (their curves have no entity of their own); a Miura sheet is drawn line by line (or by a script).
- **The branch.** The solve follows the branch the mountain–valley assignment picks from the flat sheet; a configuration reached only through a flat-folded state (past a bifurcation) is not looked for; a bifurcation partway is refused as undetermined, by name.
- **Zero angles with solved creases** are undetermined (the flat sheet does not say which way the solved creases go): give every crease 0, or give the driving one a small angle.
- **Not checked:** global flat-foldability (NP-hard), the layer order of a flat fold, the big-little-big condition.

## Findings and history

### Built (2026-10-26)

The arrangement, the solve, the contact test, the body and the two checks as above. Found while building: the hole of a piece must be looked for among the *other* pieces' cycles (a sheet's outer cycle shares its vertices with the facets, and a point-in-polygon test on a shared vertex answers either way: every pattern was refused as a face on both sides of the boundary until then); and from the flat sheet a plain least-squares solve picked the wrong mode of a degree-4 vertex whose solved creases fold much further than the driving one (mode B at α = 70, β = 50 degrees: 47 degrees for a 10-degree drive), which the kinds' penalty and the first step's scaled guesses fixed.

### Review round (2026-10-26)

Fixed after review: a fold with solved creases and no non-zero given angle lay flat silently; it is undetermined now (the rank check runs at the flat sheet too). The first step's guesses at ten times the largest given angle could close on another configuration and later steps went on from it; the fold is now the continuation from a 1-degree first step, each step accepted only when it follows on from the last (the ratio above), halved otherwise. The stopping rule and penalty were scaled by the largest coordinate from the sketch's origin, so a small sheet far away stopped early; they are scaled by the sheet's box, and the solve runs about its middle (`a_small_sheet_far_from_the_origin_folds_as_one_at_the_origin`). Contact failed open when a facet could not be placed; it is an error. The contact prefilter is the box tree; parallel is the angular tolerance; the kinds' check wraps the angle first. The solve takes a linkage with an axis per joint (above). Smaller: `math::Transform` instead of an own rigid motion, one union-find, the pieces from the solve's forest, one ring distance, the profile's 2D helpers, `Meeting`/`FacetMeeting` (not to clash with the boolean's `ContactKind`), and `fold.check` and the export read the pattern only (`regen::fold_pattern`), without folding.

Follow-up (2026-10-26): the speed ratio of the first round (a solved angle could move at most 15 times the given step) was a per-configuration constant that refused valid linkages (mode B of a 61/59-degree vertex, a Miura sheet with γ above about 86 degrees); replaced by the tangent predictor-corrector and the half-step test above. Tests: `solved_creases_need_an_angle_that_folds_and_contact_fails_closed` (the 61/59 vertex's mode B at 1, 3 and 10 degrees to its closed form), `a_miura_ori_sheet_of_steep_parallelograms_folds_by_its_closed_form` (γ = 88 degrees at 2, 10 and 30 degrees).
