# 3DCraft design: STEP import

STEP files read into the document as bodies: typed reading (`craft3d-stepread`), sewing on topology only, the `import` feature and how the document keeps the file, names, and the oracles. The formats themselves: [`../file-formats.md`](../file-formats.md) › *STEP* › *Import*. Milestone M10 ([`../roadmap.md`](../roadmap.md)).

## Method

**Three layers.** `craft3d-step` (L0) reads the exchange structure of ISO 10303-21 into records and references and knows no entity. `craft3d-stepread` (L3, new 2026-10-21) knows which entities make a solid and what their attributes mean (ISO 10303-42, -41 for units), and turns one solid or sheet of a parsed file into a `craft3d-topo` body in the caller's unit, checked with the caller's tolerance. It sits at L3, beside the kernel, so that regeneration (L4) can build an import feature from the bytes the document holds; the export's mapping stays in `io` (L5), which uses it only in its tests. `regen` builds the feature; the engine's `file.import` adds the features.

**The reading follows the export's conventions** (`crates/io/src/step.rs`, module documentation): the elementary surfaces of Part 42 have the parametrisations of `craft3d-geom`, so frames and radii go over unchanged and only lengths are multiplied by the unit's factor; `same_sense` of a face is `!reversed`; a bound with `orientation` `.T.` is the loop as it runs; an `ORIENTED_EDGE` `.T.` uses its edge from start to end. What other writers do differently is read too: an `EDGE_CURVE` with `same_sense` `.F.` runs against its curve, and since an edge here always runs with its curve, it becomes the edge from the file's end vertex to its start vertex, used the other way by every loop. An `ORIENTED_FACE`, `ORIENTED_CLOSED_SHELL` or `ORIENTED_OPEN_SHELL` with `.F.` turns its faces over (normal and loops). A `VERTEX_LOOP` is dropped: a face here needs none for a whole sphere or torus or a cone's apex. A swept surface that is one of our kinds (a line or a circle extruded or turned) is read as that kind, and whether the file's normal (`∂u × ∂v` of the swept surface's own parametrisation) is ours or the opposite is measured at a point of the swept curve and folded into `reversed`. Any other swept curve (a B-spline, an ellipse, a circle out of every plane through the axis or one whose torus would reach the axis, a polyline) is the exact B-spline surface it is (slice 2): turned, the curve's control points each swept round the axis as four rational quadratic quarters (*The NURBS Book* §8.5; a control point on the axis gives a row that is one point, a pole), u round the axis, v the curve's own parameter, so the normal is the file's; extruded, two rows `P + v₀V` and `P + v₁V` of degree one, u along the curve, and since `SURFACE_OF_LINEAR_EXTRUSION` is unbounded the range `[v₀, v₁]` is found from the face's edges (the `v` of a point `X = C(u) + vV` lies within the hull bounds `(X·V − max P·V) / |V|²` to `(X·V − min P·V) / |V|²`, over 33 points per edge, widened by a quarter and one unit each way; past its ends the surface goes on as the straight lines it is). Uniform, quasi-uniform and piecewise Bézier B-splines (`UNIFORM_CURVE`, `QUASI_UNIFORM_SURFACE`, `BEZIER_CURVE`, …, simple or complex, rational too) get the knots ISO 10303-42 implies (`−d, …, n`; the ends `d + 1` times and every whole number between; pieces of `d` with inner knots `d` times). `OFFSET_SURFACE` of a plane, cylinder, cone, sphere or torus is that kind again (the plane moved along the file's normal, the radius changed by `±d`, a cone's by `±d / cos α` with its frame kept), refused where the offset passes the axis or centre; of a spline or swept surface it is refused naming it (no exact surface of that kind). An edge's parameter range is found by projecting its vertices onto its curve: a whole period for a closed edge on a circle or ellipse, the domain for one on a B-spline that closes at its ends.

**Bounded on hostile input.** `craft3d-step` already refuses missing references, nesting deeper than its limits and files over its size. Here nothing recurses through the file's references without a depth bound (a curve through another: four; a unit through another: four; an oriented face or shell of an oriented one: refused), so a cycle ends. Each face and edge of a body holds its own copy of its surface or curve, so a surface of a million control points shared by many faces is many copies: when a file is parsed, the control points every face and edge of every item would hold are counted from list lengths alone (`budget.rs`) and a file above four million is refused (review of 2026-10-21: 150 faces on one 1000 × 1000 surface, a 4 MB file, were killed at 4 GB before). Within one body each curve and surface is read once by instance id and cloned. A face without an edge loop is accepted only on a surface that closes all around. The multiplicities of a B-spline must add up to control points + degree + 1 and the degree be at most 32, checked before the control points are read; every number must be finite once converted; files over 128 MiB are refused before they are parsed; the files parsed for regeneration are kept by hash, at most four and 128 MiB of them.

**Sewing on topology only** (the tolerant-modelling decision, [`../decisions.md`](../decisions.md) 2026-10-18: "merges topology and measures gaps; it never moves geometry"). What the file shares (an `EDGE_CURVE`, a `VERTEX_POINT` referred to from several places) is shared. A `VERTEX_POINT` within the merge distance of an earlier vertex is that vertex (a grid of cells twice the distance wide, the nearest within it, in file order); then two edges used once each between the same two vertices, whose midpoints lie within the distance, become the first of them (for a closed edge, the way the curves run at the midpoint says whether the second is used reversed). The merge distance is the file's uncertainty, at least the document's tolerance and at most the document's cap on an entity's tolerance (`loosest`). Geometry stays where the file put it. Then, with tolerant modelling merged (2026-10-21, review): every edge is proven on the surfaces of all its faces over its whole length (`topo::record_edge`: `certify::curve_on_surface`, the least bound in steps of two) and every vertex measured against its edges' ends (`record_vertex`); an entity off by more than the tolerance gets the proven distance, an eighth more, as its own tolerance, and one beyond `loosest` refuses the body naming it by its instance id. A joined edge is proven on both faces this way, so sewing does not trust its midpoints. Since 2026-10-22 (slice 3) the edges still used once after that are sewn by `topo::sew` at the document's cap (`loosest`), whatever the file's uncertainty ([`topology.md`](topology.md) › *Sewing*): faces that share nothing and meet within a gap, as other kernels write them, read as one body with every gap recorded; the import reports `sewnEdges`, `sewnVertices` and `sewnGap`, and since slice 3 `splitEdges`: edges split where two or more shorter free edges of other faces run along them (a T-junction), each piece then sewn and carrying the file's id of the edge it is part of. A solid left with an edge used once is refused naming the edge's instance id and how far the nearest free edge is. Edges that start or end at a singular point of a surface's parametrisation (a sphere's pole, a cone's apex, a revolved spline's pole) and edges on the seam of a spline surface closed round are proven since slice 2 (`geom::certify`, below); should the prover still find no bound within the cap on an edge, 1025 places along it within the tolerance of each face accept it, counted as unproven (`unprovenEdges`, 0 on every test and on the showcase round trip); anything sampled further off is refused. Before this review the check was `topo::check`'s five samples per edge, which a polyline whose odd points left the plane passed. The body is then checked with `topo::check_capped`. A shell whose enclosed volume has the wrong sign is turned over (topology only) and counted.

**The feature and the data** (rule 2 and §8 of AGENTS.md). `doc::Import { file_name, solid, data: Blob }`: `data` is the file's bytes, `solid` the position of the item among the file's solids and sheets in instance order. A `Blob` is an `Arc<[u8]>` with its SHA-256 (FIPS 180-4, `doc::sha256`, no new dependency): a clone shares the bytes, so the undo history (a whole document per step) and the regeneration cache hold the file once, and equality is the same `Arc` at once, else the hash and the bytes. In JSON (`document.inspect`) a blob is its size and hash, never its bytes. The `.3dcraft` file (format 25) writes each distinct blob once as the stored entry `blobs/<sha256>.step` and the feature as `{"type": "import", "file_name", "solid", "sha256"}`; loading checks that every named blob is there and hashes to its name. So a document regenerates the same bodies without the original file, and the bytes are the whole truth: nothing derived (no body) is stored. The "share large blobs between undo snapshots" item of the roadmap is met for imported files by the `Arc`; it stays open for anything else large a document may hold later.

**One feature per placed solid.** `file.import` reads every placed solid with the document's units and tolerance before it changes anything (a file with one solid that cannot be read changes nothing and says which), then adds one `import` feature per placed solid or sheet in one undo step, named after its place in the assembly (the last step of its path, `bolt#2`; the same leaf in two places numbered on, `bolt#2-2`), else after the file's name for the solid, where the document can take it, with the solid's colour as the body's appearance in the same step. Each feature builds its body alone (a failed solid fails only its own feature), and the file is parsed once for all of them: `stepread::parse_shared` keeps the last parsed file, keyed by the blob's hash. The whole body is then looked at for places where it passes through or touches itself (`selfcheck::crossing`, as for a loft): a file is not a body this kernel made.

**Names.** The entities of an imported body are numbered as a primitive's are (`F5:face(0)`, `F5:edge(3)`, `F5:vertex(1)`): faces in the order of the file's shells and of their faces in each shell, edges and vertices in the order the faces reach them. The order comes from the file alone, so importing the same bytes again gives every entity the same name (`crates/engine/tests/step_import.rs` pins it); each placement of a part in an assembly is a feature of its own, so its entities are numbered the same way under its own feature, and the placements come in the file's order. Names are positional: a file another program writes again, even of the same part, may order its faces differently, and a reference to an imported face may then land on another face; that is the stated limit of numbered names ([`names.md`](names.md)). The file's instance ids of every face, edge and vertex are kept beside the body (`Imported::face_ids`, …) for messages; a name by STEP id would not survive a re-export by another program either, so it was not made a name.

**Assemblies** (slice 2, `stepread::assembly`). Every shape representation is a node; a relationship without a transformation (a part's shape and its B-rep) joins two nodes into one place; a `REPRESENTATION_RELATIONSHIP_WITH_TRANSFORMATION` or a `MAPPED_ITEM` places a child node in a parent, the child's frame onto the parent's (`ITEM_DEFINED_TRANSFORMATION`, `AXIS2_PLACEMENT_3D` frames; or a `CARTESIAN_TRANSFORMATION_OPERATOR_3D` without scale or mirror). Which representation is the child: the `NEXT_ASSEMBLY_USAGE_OCCURRENCE` a `CONTEXT_DEPENDENT_SHAPE_REPRESENTATION` ties to the relationship says (its related product definition, whose shape a `SHAPE_DEFINITION_REPRESENTATION` names), else `rep_1` (ISO 10303-43 relates `rep_1` to `rep_2`); which frame is the child's: the one the child's node lists, as writers differ in the order, else `transform_item_1` in `rep_1`. A solid is placed once per way up from its node to a root, the transformations `F_parent ∘ F_child⁻¹` composed from the solid up, each frame read in the units of the representation that lists it (a part in inches in an assembly in metres lands where it should). `Parsed::placed` lists the placements: the items in their order at their first placement, then the further placements in file order, so for a file without assemblies (and one whose transformations are the identity, which slice 1 read) the position of a solid is the one format 25 stored and the body is the same bit for bit (an identity within 1e-12 is not applied): the document model and the format did not change. The shared part is one blob and one parse; each placement is a feature and a body of its own, moved exactly (`Body::transformed`). Its path (`robot/arm#left/peg#2`: the root product, then per step down the child product and the occurrence's id, a mapped item's name, or the relationship's instance id) is in the import's result (`instance`) and names the feature. Hostile assemblies are refused before anything is built: a cycle (naming the relationship that closes it), more than 32 levels, more than 10 000 placed solids (a fan of 2¹⁶ by 16 levels of two is refused in milliseconds), a placement so far that rounding there exceeds the tolerance (`|t| ε 8 > tol`), one that scales or mirrors; the control-point budget counts a solid once per placement.

**Colours** (slice 2, `stepread::style`). `STYLED_ITEM` (`OVER_RIDING_STYLED_ITEM` overrides) → `PRESENTATION_STYLE_ASSIGNMENT` → `SURFACE_STYLE_USAGE` → `SURFACE_SIDE_STYLE` → the colour of `SURFACE_STYLE_FILL_AREA` → `FILL_AREA_STYLE` → `FILL_AREA_STYLE_COLOUR`, else of `SURFACE_STYLE_RENDERING(_WITH_PROPERTIES)`; `COLOUR_RGB` (0 to 1, rounded to sRGB bytes) or `DRAUGHTING_PRE_DEFINED_COLOUR` by name. A solid takes its own style as its body's colour, else one of its shells', else, when every face is styled, the colour most of its faces have (the first of equals); since 2026-10-22 each face styled in another colour than that keeps it as a face colour (`face.appearance` data, `F5:face(i)`, in the import's undo step; `Parsed::face_colors`), and a solid with some faces unstyled and no style of its own has no body colour (the unstyled faces have the solid's, which the file does not give). Held by `a_step_file_brings_its_face_colours_back_as_face_colours` (an export of a face-coloured part imported: the same colours per face) and `colours_read_back_per_solid`. A colour that cannot be read is left out, never a refusal; every chain is a fixed depth.

**Units.** The length and plane-angle units of the representation context the item is in (or the file's only context with units), an `SI_UNIT` with any prefix or a conversion-based unit of one; lengths are multiplied by (metres per file unit) / (metres per document unit), angles (a cone's semi-angle) by radians per file unit. No unit is assumed: a context without a length unit is refused. The file's uncertainty is reported in the document's unit (`uncertainty`) and used only as the merge distance.

## Oracles

- **Round trip** (`crates/io/tests/step_import.rs`): the 26 bodies the export's own tests write (the test kit's solids, trimmed solids, cavities, several lumps, rational splines, a sheet) at the scales 1e-3, 1 and 1e6, and in inches, metres and micrometres, read back with the same face, edge, vertex and shell counts, genus, volume and area (1e-7), nothing merged or turned. Faces with their seams as edges (cylinder, cone to its apex, torus with two seams) likewise.
- **Other writers' conventions**, made from our files by changing them the way other writers differ: every edge written against its curve (`same_sense` `.F.`, vertices swapped, every use flipped); every face turned over inside an `ORIENTED_FACE(.F.)`; every elementary surface written as the swept surface it is, both ways round (so `same_sense` must be read against the swept surface's own normal); every second use of an edge given its own `EDGE_CURVE` and vertices (sewing must join them back: as many merged edges as open edges, the gap 0); a cavity listed as a plain shell (turned, counted). Each reads as the same body.
- **A gap is recorded or refused, never healed**: a corner of a block moved by 1e-4 mm reads with that vertex's tolerance at least the gap and the vertex where the file put it; moved by 1e-2 mm (ten times the cap) it is refused naming the entity. A sheet on a plane bounded by a 9-point polyline whose odd points are 1 mm off the plane (which five samples per edge do not see) is refused naming the edge; 2e-4 mm off, it reads with the edge's proven gap as its tolerance; flat, exact.
- **Bounded copies**: the reviewer's probe (one 1000 × 1000 B-spline surface shared by 150 faces without bounds) is refused at parse within seconds; one such face is refused as a face without a loop on a surface that does not close.
- **Documents that open again**: a document whose imported files would make a file over 256 MiB is not saved, and the import that would make it so is refused (`crates/format/tests/format.rs`, `crates/engine/tests/step_import.rs` with three 90 MB files).
- **Hostile files** (`crates/stepread/src/tests.rs`, and `damaged_files_are_errors_never_panics` in the io test): kinds not read, named; a file without units; a chain of curves in a circle; wrong kinds of reference; absurd numbers, degrees and multiplicities; a mapped item of the wrong kinds; truncated text; plus, over the files of nine bodies, 39 truncations, 40 random byte damages and 60 random changes of references, reals, integers, flags and lists each: an error or a body, never a panic. `panic_hunt` runs `file.import` with adversarial parameters.
- **Through the engine** (`crates/engine/tests/step_import.rs`): import, the bodies of the exported document again; save, delete the STEP file, reopen: the same bodies, and saving again gives the same bytes; undo removes the whole import, redo brings it back; a document in inches; refusals change nothing. The format: `crates/format/tests/format.rs` (`an_import_is_written_like_this_and_round_trips`) and the version-25 fixture (written by the CLI: an export of a plate with a hole and a ball, imported, a feature on the import).
- **Swept, offset and implied B-splines** (`crates/io/tests/step_import.rs`, slice 2): every cylinder, sphere, cone and torus of exported bodies (with their seams; a cone to its apex; a sphere bounded by one meridian from pole to pole) written as a swept surface of a curve that is a spline in the file (a rational circle extruded, a rational half or whole circle turned, a quasi-uniform segment turned), both ways round: read as B-spline surfaces with the volume and area of the elementary bodies (1e-7), every edge proven, none loose; every elementary surface written as an `OFFSET_SURFACE` of one `d` nearer, both signs: the same bodies; an offset of a spline surface refused naming it. In `crates/stepread/src/tests.rs`: the implied knots of each type, a quasi-uniform polyline evaluated, Bézier, uniform and quasi-uniform surfaces, offset cycles ended, a hyperboloid and an offset through a sphere's centre named.
- **Poles and seams proven** (`crates/geom/src/certify.rs` › `curves_through_poles_and_apexes_are_proven`): meridians from, to and over a sphere's pole, rulings from and to a cone's apex, arcs over and from a revolved spline's pole proven within 1e-6; a curve 1e-2 off near the pole never proven within 1e-3. The round trips at three scales now require every edge proven (none sampled), and so does the showcase.
- **Assemblies and colours** (`crates/io/tests/step_assembly.rs`): a part placed three times by occurrences (both orders of the transformation's frames), a sub-assembly placed twice holding the part twice (four bodies, composed), mapped items, a part in millimetres in an assembly in metres: each body's centroid where the frames put the part's (1e-9), the paths, the same bytes giving bit-identical bodies; an identity placement giving the part bit for bit; hostile: a cycle through two assemblies, a chain 34 deep, a fan of 2¹⁶ (refused within the bound, in milliseconds), a placement at 1e308, a scale of 2, a transformation of the wrong kind. Colours the export writes read back (a body's; faces' only, the most common when every face has one, none when some have none, each face's own; none; one out of range left out). Through the engine (`crates/engine/tests/step_import.rs` › `an_assembly_imports_one_feature_per_placement_with_its_colour`): three features in one undo step, named `Peg#1…`, their `instance`, centroids and colour, undo and redo, saved and reopened without the file.
- **Opt-in, in release** (`cargo xtask freecad`, the Strix's gate): `every_body_of_the_showcase_reads_back` (every body of every showcase model exported and read back in three units, counts, volume and area to 1e-7); `freecad_files_read_with_freecads_volume`: FreeCAD (an independent kernel) makes solids of its own with its Python (box, cylinder, sphere, torus, cones with and without apex, a cut, a fusion, a common, filleted and chamfered boxes, a filleted cut, a revolved polygon, a revolved spline, a loft from a circle to a square, two solids in one file), writes each as STEP and measures it; each must read here with FreeCAD's volume and area to 1e-6 or be refused with its reason, never read wrong, and the plain ones must read; `freecad_assemblies_read_where_freecad_places_them` (slice 2): FreeCAD places a part three times by `App::Link`s in an `App::Part`, and a sub-assembly of two twice, writes them with its own STEP writer (`Import.export`, its assembly structure with the part shared) and measures the placed solids: ours must have as many bodies, the same total volume (1e-6) and a body at each of FreeCAD's centroids; where FreeCAD's file holds a colour (it may not, without its GUI: recorded), the bodies have it. No third-party file is committed.

## Findings and history

**First slice (2026-10-21), and its review the same day** (proven edges with tolerances recorded up to `loosest`, the control-point budget, the size cap on saving). What is above. Open for the next slice:

- Faces whose edges cannot be proven (the prover near poles) are accepted on dense samples: make the prover cover them.
- Assemblies: `MAPPED_ITEM`, `REPRESENTATION_RELATIONSHIP_WITH_TRANSFORMATION` with a transformation that is not the identity, `NEXT_ASSEMBLY_USAGE_OCCURRENCE` (a placed copy of a body per occurrence, named by the product).
- Colours and names of faces (`STYLED_ITEM`), as the export writes them; parameter-space curves are not needed (we project).
- More kinds: `OFFSET_SURFACE`, `DEGENERATE_TOROIDAL_SURFACE` (a spindle torus), swept surfaces of other curves (as NURBS), `HYPERBOLA` and `PARABOLA` (as rational B-splines), uniform and quasi-uniform B-splines, Bézier curves and surfaces, `TRIMMED_CURVE`, faceted B-reps (`POLY_LOOP`).
- Speed on large files: the whole file is parsed for the command and once per regeneration (shared by the features); the self-crossing check meshes each imported body whole. Measure on a corpus file of thousands of faces.
- A pinned public corpus (M10's next item), which is where the refusals above will be counted.

**Second slice (2026-10-21).** Swept surfaces of any curve as exact B-spline surfaces (the revolved spline FreeCAD wrote in slice 1 was the one file refused), the implied-knot B-splines, offsets of the elementary surfaces; every edge proven (the prover's feet at poles and on closed spline seams, below); assemblies with one feature per placement; colours per solid. What is above. Findings:

- *Feet stuck at a pole.* An edge that starts at a sphere's pole (a meridian from the pole, as a sphere bounded by one seam is written) was never proven: the foot of the pole has no longitude, Gauss-Newton steps from it have no direction and either stay (every later foot stuck at the pole) or fly off along the singular border (a foot at `u = −5752`), and a bound carries the whole jump in `u`. Fixed in `geom::certify`, so for every proof in the kernel: at a singular point the steps are damped (Levenberg-Marquardt, only where `|∂u × ∂v|² ≤ 1e-18 (|∂u|² + |∂v|²)²`), a foot is named by the parameters nearest its start round a periodic direction, a start at a singular point is taken from the foot of a point a little along the curve and followed back, and the path leaves a singular end straight (the projection of the tangent there is all rounding). An arc ending at a pole proved in 35 pieces before, 8 now. 35 edges of the showcase were accepted on samples before this.
- *Review (2026-10-21).* Colours were followed per styled item through shared style lists, n² in every parse (one assignment of n entries shared by n styled items: 6 s at n = 8000): each style assignment, side style and fill area is now read once, with a cap of 2²⁶ references past which colours are left out (n = 40 000 parses within seconds, `a_style_shared_by_many_styled_items_is_read_once`). A frame is read in the units of the representation that lists it; an identity placement is judged as slice 1 judged it (each step's two frames the same within 1e-12 in the file's units); an offset of a cone takes its side on the cone's own nappe; a leaf name met twice is numbered on (`bolt#1-2`).
- *Feet on the wrong end of a closed spline's seam.* A full circle on an extruded rational circle (closed in u, no period) was not proven: the projection of its start, on the seam, named `u = 1`, and past it the surface goes on as its last piece's polynomial. `curve_on_surface` without a start now takes a start on a spline's border the same way as a singular one (from a point a little along the curve).

**Third slice: sewing at the cap (2026-10-22, tolerant modelling slice 2).** Before, the file's vertices were joined within its uncertainty (at least the document's tolerance) and edges only between the same two vertices with their midpoints within it: a file whose faces share nothing and lie apart by more than its uncertainty was refused ("used 1 times"). Now `topo::sew` sews what is left at the document's cap (Method). Held by `crates/io/tests/step_import.rs` › `faces_that_share_nothing_and_meet_within_a_gap_are_sewn`: nine bodies (cavities, a NURBS can, a washer, an ellipse, an open box, a block, a cylinder, a cone) written with every face its own loops, edges, vertices, curves and surface, each face moved rigidly by up to 0, 30 or 300 tolerances: 27 of 27 read with the original counts, valid with their tolerances, 124 edges sewn, the largest gap 4.1e-4 mm; `a_face_apart_by_more_than_the_cap_is_refused_naming_its_edge` (a face 2e-3 mm off, twice the cap). The vertices are now measured in one pass over the edges (`topo::record_vertices`; one pass per vertex before). *Expectation changed:* `stepread::tests::a_closed_circle_reads_and_stays_a_circle` held a plane face bounded by one circle, in a solid's place, to the check's "used 1 times"; sewing refuses it first, naming the edge (`edge #16 bounds only one face`): the same refusal of the same body, in sewing's words.

Open for the next slice:

- Sewing an edge to several (a T-junction: one edge of a face against two of its neighbour's), which other writers leave in some files; refused today as an open edge.
- Names of faces and of solids from `STYLED_ITEM`s and product names beyond the feature's name; per-instance colours (`CONTEXT_DEPENDENT_OVER_RIDING_STYLED_ITEM` on an occurrence's path).
- Placements that mirror (`Body::transformed` takes them; the operator's handedness is refused today) or scale.
- More kinds: `DEGENERATE_TOROIDAL_SURFACE`, `HYPERBOLA` and `PARABOLA`, `TRIMMED_CURVE`, offsets of spline surfaces (no exact kind), a line turned about an axis it does not meet (a hyperboloid: a bounded generator from the face's edges, as for extrusions), faceted B-reps.
- Speed: a part placed N times is read and proven N times (one parse); a cache of the read body per item would make it once.
- Two faces on one `SURFACE_OF_LINEAR_EXTRUSION` of a spline get two B-spline surfaces, each over its own face's extent (the same point set, different `v` ranges): a later operation that compares the two faces' surfaces sees two.
- The FreeCAD assembly oracle runs on the Strix only; whether FreeCAD's headless writer puts colours in the file is recorded there, not assumed.

**Real-world models, the ABC dataset (2026-11-01).** `cargo xtask abc` ([`../testing.md`](../testing.md) › *Real-world models*) ran the 10 000 STEP files of chunk 0 on the Strix ([`../abc-scorecard.md`](../abc-scorecard.md)): 75.7 % imported, checked, meshed and measured; 23.9 % refused; 0.3 % accepted and then faulted by `document.check` (a face the measure cannot integrate); six timeouts at 60 s; no crash and no out-of-memory. The refusals are led by edges that cannot be proven on, or lie off, their faces' surfaces within the cap (1667 together), tori whose major radius is not larger than their minor (192: spindle and horn tori, `DEGENERATE_TOROIDAL_SURFACE` written as `TOROIDAL_SURFACE`), offsets of B-spline surfaces and of extruded curves (188), and closed edges on open curves (66). On the 7548 read as valid solids, a drill, a slab and a corner block were united, subtracted and intersected: 98.7 % all nine right, 1.2 % refused, three results the measure cannot integrate, four timeouts, no identity broken. FreeCAD agrees on the volume of 196 of a sample of 200 within 1e-3; the four others differ by 0.2 to 0.75 % (who is right is not settled yet). These are work owed, counted, not chased in the run that found them.
