# 3DCraft design: STEP import

STEP files (and, since 2026-11-01, FreeCAD documents: *FreeCAD documents* below) read into the document as bodies: typed reading (`craft3d-stepread`), sewing on topology only, the `import` feature and how the document keeps the file, names, and the oracles. The formats themselves: [`../file-formats.md`](../file-formats.md) › *STEP* › *Import*. Milestone M10 ([`../roadmap.md`](../roadmap.md)).

## Method

**Three layers.** `craft3d-step` (L0) reads the exchange structure of ISO 10303-21 into records and references and knows no entity. `craft3d-stepread` (L3, new 2026-10-21) knows which entities make a solid and what their attributes mean (ISO 10303-42, -41 for units), and turns one solid or sheet of a parsed file into a `craft3d-topo` body in the caller's unit, checked with the caller's tolerance. It sits at L3, beside the kernel, so that regeneration (L4) can build an import feature from the bytes the document holds; the export's mapping stays in `io` (L5), which uses it only in its tests. `regen` builds the feature; the engine's `file.import` adds the features.

**The reading follows the export's conventions** (`crates/io/src/step.rs`, module documentation): the elementary surfaces of Part 42 have the parametrisations of `craft3d-geom`, so frames and radii go over unchanged and only lengths are multiplied by the unit's factor; `same_sense` of a face is `!reversed`; a bound with `orientation` `.T.` is the loop as it runs; an `ORIENTED_EDGE` `.T.` uses its edge from start to end. What other writers do differently is read too: an `EDGE_CURVE` with `same_sense` `.F.` runs against its curve, and since an edge here always runs with its curve, it becomes the edge from the file's end vertex to its start vertex, used the other way by every loop. An `ORIENTED_FACE`, `ORIENTED_CLOSED_SHELL` or `ORIENTED_OPEN_SHELL` with `.F.` turns its faces over (normal and loops). A `VERTEX_LOOP` is dropped: a face here needs none for a whole sphere or torus or a cone's apex. A swept surface that is one of our kinds (a line or a circle extruded or turned) is read as that kind, and whether the file's normal (`∂u × ∂v` of the swept surface's own parametrisation) is ours or the opposite is measured at a point of the swept curve and folded into `reversed`. Any other swept curve (a B-spline, an ellipse, a circle out of every plane through the axis or one whose torus would reach the axis, a polyline) is the exact B-spline surface it is (slice 2): turned, the curve's control points each swept round the axis as four rational quadratic quarters (*The NURBS Book* §8.5; a control point on the axis gives a row that is one point, a pole), u round the axis, v the curve's own parameter, so the normal is the file's; extruded, two rows `P + v₀V` and `P + v₁V` of degree one, u along the curve, and since `SURFACE_OF_LINEAR_EXTRUSION` is unbounded the range `[v₀, v₁]` is found from the face's edges (the `v` of a point `X = C(u) + vV` lies within the hull bounds `(X·V − max P·V) / |V|²` to `(X·V − min P·V) / |V|²`, over 33 points per edge, widened by a quarter and one unit each way; past its ends the surface goes on as the straight lines it is). Uniform, quasi-uniform and piecewise Bézier B-splines (`UNIFORM_CURVE`, `QUASI_UNIFORM_SURFACE`, `BEZIER_CURVE`, …, simple or complex, rational too) get the knots ISO 10303-42 implies (`−d, …, n`; the ends `d + 1` times and every whole number between; pieces of `d` with inner knots `d` times). A torus whose tube reaches its axis (a spindle or horn torus: `TOROIDAL_SURFACE` with major radius ≤ minor, `DEGENERATE_TOROIDAL_SURFACE`, a circle turned about an axis that cuts or touches it, a ring torus offset past its axis) is read per face as the exact surface of revolution of the one sheet the face lies on (*Spindle and horn tori*, below). `OFFSET_SURFACE` of a plane, cylinder, cone, sphere or torus is that kind again (the plane moved along the file's normal, the radius changed by `±d`, a cone's by `±d / cos α` with its frame kept, or, where that radius would be negative, the same cone with its frame moved to its apex, 2026-11-15), refused where the offset passes a cylinder's axis or a sphere's or torus's centre; of a spline or swept surface it is refused naming it (no exact surface of that kind). An edge's parameter range is found by projecting its vertices onto its curve: a whole period for a closed edge on a circle or ellipse, the domain for one on a B-spline that closes at its ends. A hyperbola or a parabola, which `geom` has no kind for and which does not end, becomes the exact rational quadratic B-spline of the part its edge's vertices, a trimmed curve's trims or the faces on a swept surface of it bound; a trimmed curve of any other kind is its basis (turned round for `sense_agreement` `.F.`), a composite curve its segments joined into one exact B-spline (*Hyperbolas and parabolas*, below).

**Bounded on hostile input.** `craft3d-step` already refuses missing references, nesting deeper than its limits and files over its size. Here nothing recurses through the file's references without a depth bound (a curve through another: four; a unit through another: four; an oriented face or shell of an oriented one: refused), so a cycle ends. Each face and edge of a body holds its own copy of its surface or curve, so a surface of a million control points shared by many faces is many copies: when a file is parsed, the control points every face and edge of every item would hold are counted from list lengths alone (`budget.rs`) and a file above four million is refused (review of 2026-10-21: 150 faces on one 1000 × 1000 surface, a 4 MB file, were killed at 4 GB before). Within one body each curve and surface is read once by instance id and cloned. A face without an edge loop is accepted only on a surface that closes all around. The multiplicities of a B-spline must add up to control points + degree + 1 and the degree be at most 32, checked before the control points are read; every number must be finite once converted; files over 128 MiB are refused before they are parsed; the files parsed for regeneration are kept by hash, at most four and 128 MiB of them.

**Sewing on topology only** (the tolerant-modelling decision, [`../decisions.md`](../decisions.md) 2026-10-18: "merges topology and measures gaps; it never moves geometry"). What the file shares (an `EDGE_CURVE`, a `VERTEX_POINT` referred to from several places) is shared. A `VERTEX_POINT` within the merge distance of an earlier vertex is that vertex (a grid of cells twice the distance wide, the nearest within it, in file order); then two edges used once each between the same two vertices, whose midpoints lie within the distance, become the first of them (for a closed edge, the way the curves run at the midpoint says whether the second is used reversed). The merge distance is the file's uncertainty, at least the document's tolerance and at most the document's cap on an entity's tolerance (`loosest`). Geometry stays where the file put it. Then, with tolerant modelling merged (2026-10-21, review): every edge is proven on the surfaces of all its faces over its whole length (`topo::record_edge`: `certify::curve_on_surface`, the least bound in steps of two) and every vertex measured against its edges' ends (`record_vertex`); an entity off by more than the tolerance gets the proven distance, an eighth more, as its own tolerance, and one beyond `loosest` refuses the body naming it by its instance id. A joined edge is proven on both faces this way, so sewing does not trust its midpoints. Since 2026-10-22 (slice 3) the edges still used once after that are sewn by `topo::sew` at the document's cap (`loosest`), whatever the file's uncertainty ([`topology.md`](topology.md) › *Sewing*): faces that share nothing and meet within a gap, as other kernels write them, read as one body with every gap recorded; the import reports `sewnEdges`, `sewnVertices` and `sewnGap`, and since slice 3 `splitEdges`: edges split where two or more shorter free edges of other faces run along them (a T-junction), each piece then sewn and carrying the file's id of the edge it is part of. A solid left with an edge used once is refused naming the edge's instance id and how far the nearest free edge is. Edges that start or end at a singular point of a surface's parametrisation (a sphere's pole, a cone's apex, a revolved spline's pole) and edges on the seam of a spline surface closed round are proven since slice 2 (`geom::certify`, below); should the prover still find no bound within the cap on an edge, 1025 places along it within the tolerance of each face accept it, counted as unproven (`unprovenEdges`, 0 on every test and on the showcase round trip); anything sampled further off is refused. Before this review the check was `topo::check`'s five samples per edge, which a polyline whose odd points left the plane passed. The body is then checked with `topo::check_capped`. A shell whose enclosed volume has the wrong sign is turned over (topology only) and counted.

**The feature and the data** (rule 2 and §8 of AGENTS.md). `doc::Import { file_name, solid, data: Blob }`: `data` is the file's bytes, `solid` the position of the item among the file's solids and sheets in instance order. A `Blob` is an `Arc<[u8]>` with its SHA-256 (FIPS 180-4, `doc::sha256`, no new dependency): a clone shares the bytes, so the undo history (a whole document per step) and the regeneration cache hold the file once, and equality is the same `Arc` at once, else the hash and the bytes. In JSON (`document.inspect`) a blob is its size and hash, never its bytes. The `.3dcraft` file (format 25) writes each distinct blob once as the stored entry `blobs/<sha256>.step` and the feature as `{"type": "import", "file_name", "solid", "sha256"}`; loading checks that every named blob is there and hashes to its name. So a document regenerates the same bodies without the original file, and the bytes are the whole truth: nothing derived (no body) is stored. The "share large blobs between undo snapshots" item of the roadmap is met for imported files by the `Arc`; it stays open for anything else large a document may hold later.

**One feature per placed solid.** `file.import` reads every placed solid with the document's units and tolerance before it changes anything (a file with one solid that cannot be read changes nothing and says which), then adds one `import` feature per placed solid or sheet in one undo step, named after its place in the assembly (the last step of its path, `bolt#2`; the same leaf in two places numbered on, `bolt#2-2`), else after the file's name for the solid, where the document can take it, with the solid's colour as the body's appearance in the same step. Each feature builds its body alone (a failed solid fails only its own feature), and the file is parsed once for all of them: `stepread::parse_shared` keeps the last parsed file, keyed by the blob's hash. The whole body is then looked at for places where it passes through or touches itself (`selfcheck::crossing`, as for a loft): a file is not a body this kernel made.

**Names.** The entities of an imported body are numbered as a primitive's are (`F5:face(0)`, `F5:edge(3)`, `F5:vertex(1)`): faces in the order of the file's shells and of their faces in each shell, edges and vertices in the order the faces reach them. The order comes from the file alone, so importing the same bytes again gives every entity the same name (`crates/engine/tests/step_import.rs` pins it); each placement of a part in an assembly is a feature of its own, so its entities are numbered the same way under its own feature, and the placements come in the file's order. Names are positional: a file another program writes again, even of the same part, may order its faces differently, and a reference to an imported face may then land on another face; that is the stated limit of numbered names ([`names.md`](names.md)). The file's instance ids of every face, edge and vertex are kept beside the body (`Imported::face_ids`, …) for messages; a name by STEP id would not survive a re-export by another program either, so it was not made a name.

**Assemblies** (slice 2, `stepread::assembly`). Every shape representation is a node; a relationship without a transformation (a part's shape and its B-rep) joins two nodes into one place; a `REPRESENTATION_RELATIONSHIP_WITH_TRANSFORMATION` or a `MAPPED_ITEM` places a child node in a parent, the child's frame onto the parent's (`ITEM_DEFINED_TRANSFORMATION`, `AXIS2_PLACEMENT_3D` frames; or a `CARTESIAN_TRANSFORMATION_OPERATOR_3D` without scale or mirror). Which representation is the child: the `NEXT_ASSEMBLY_USAGE_OCCURRENCE` a `CONTEXT_DEPENDENT_SHAPE_REPRESENTATION` ties to the relationship says (its related product definition, whose shape a `SHAPE_DEFINITION_REPRESENTATION` names), else `rep_1` (ISO 10303-43 relates `rep_1` to `rep_2`); which frame is the child's: the one the child's node lists, as writers differ in the order, else `transform_item_1` in `rep_1`. A solid is placed once per way up from its node to a root, the transformations `F_parent ∘ F_child⁻¹` composed from the solid up, each frame read in the units of the representation that lists it (a part in inches in an assembly in metres lands where it should). `Parsed::placed` lists the placements: the items in their order at their first placement, then the further placements in file order, so for a file without assemblies (and one whose transformations are the identity, which slice 1 read) the position of a solid is the one format 25 stored and the body is the same bit for bit (an identity within 1e-12 is not applied): the document model and the format did not change. The shared part is one blob and one parse; each placement is a feature and a body of its own, moved exactly (`Body::transformed`). Its path (`robot/arm#left/peg#2`: the root product, then per step down the child product and the occurrence's id, a mapped item's name, or the relationship's instance id) is in the import's result (`instance`) and names the feature. Hostile assemblies are refused before anything is built: a cycle (naming the relationship that closes it), more than 32 levels, more than 10 000 placed solids (a fan of 2¹⁶ by 16 levels of two is refused in milliseconds), a placement so far that rounding there exceeds the tolerance (`|t| ε 8 > tol`), one that scales or mirrors; the control-point budget counts a solid once per placement.

**Colours** (slice 2, `stepread::style`). `STYLED_ITEM` (`OVER_RIDING_STYLED_ITEM` overrides) → `PRESENTATION_STYLE_ASSIGNMENT` → `SURFACE_STYLE_USAGE` → `SURFACE_SIDE_STYLE` → the colour of `SURFACE_STYLE_FILL_AREA` → `FILL_AREA_STYLE` → `FILL_AREA_STYLE_COLOUR`, else of `SURFACE_STYLE_RENDERING(_WITH_PROPERTIES)`; `COLOUR_RGB` (0 to 1, rounded to sRGB bytes) or `DRAUGHTING_PRE_DEFINED_COLOUR` by name. A solid takes its own style as its body's colour, else one of its shells', else, when every face is styled, the colour most of its faces have (the first of equals); since 2026-10-22 each face styled in another colour than that keeps it as a face colour (`face.appearance` data, `F5:face(i)`, in the import's undo step; `Parsed::face_colors`), and a solid with some faces unstyled and no style of its own has no body colour (the unstyled faces have the solid's, which the file does not give). Held by `a_step_file_brings_its_face_colours_back_as_face_colours` (an export of a face-coloured part imported: the same colours per face) and `colours_read_back_per_solid`. A colour that cannot be read is left out, never a refusal; every chain is a fixed depth.

**Units.** The length and plane-angle units of the representation context the item is in (or the file's only context with units), an `SI_UNIT` with any prefix or a conversion-based unit of one; lengths are multiplied by (metres per file unit) / (metres per document unit), angles (a cone's semi-angle) by radians per file unit. No unit is assumed: a context without a length unit is refused. The file's uncertainty is reported in the document's unit (`uncertainty`) and used only as the merge distance.

## FreeCAD documents

**Layers** (2026-11-01). `craft3d-brep` (L0, standalone like `craft3d-step`) reads Open CASCADE's text BRep format into its own records, from the format's published description; `craft3d-zipstore` (L0) gained a DEFLATE reader (RFC 1951) for FreeCAD's compressed entries, each decompressed only when asked for and never past the size the archive records; `craft3d-fcstdread` (L3) reads `Document.xml` and `GuiDocument.xml` (its own small XML reader), chooses the objects and maps the BRep shapes to bodies. It depends on `craft3d-stepread` in its own layer (a listed exception in `xtask/src/layers.rs`) for what both formats share: the options, report and result types, the swept and offset surfaces (`stepread::swept`, moved out of the STEP reader for this) and everything a body goes through once its faces are read (`stepread::finish`: the file's repeated edges joined, free edges sewn within the cap, shells turned the right way out, every edge proven and every vertex measured, the whole body checked). `regen` picks the reader by the bytes (`fcstdread::reads`), so the `import` feature and the `.3dcraft` format did not change.

**Which objects.** FreeCAD keeps every step of a model (a cut keeps its base and tool, a body its features); an import wants the result. An object is imported when it has a shape, is visible, and no visible object with a shape consumes it through one of the link properties that consume (`Base`, `Tool`, `Shapes`, `Links`, `Source`, `Group`, `BaseFeature`, `Tip`, `Profile`, `Sections`, `Spine`, `Originals`, …): that rule also holds for files FreeCAD wrote without its GUI, where nothing is hidden. Links that only refer (a sketch's external geometry, an attachment, `UpToFace`, `ReferenceAxis`) consume nothing (review of 2026-11-02: counting every link dropped a body another body's sketch referred to, silently); a hidden consumer consumes nothing, and whatever is left out is listed in `skipped` with why. Containers (`App::Part`) have no shape and use nothing, but place their children: the shape in the file holds only the object's own placement, so the parts' placements are composed outside it.

**Mapping.** Topology is shared as the file shares it: a vertex is one per vertex shape and place (within the tolerance), an edge one per edge shape and its two vertices, so an edge met through two faces is one edge and a seam is one edge used both ways. A face's material normal is its surface's `∂u × ∂v` (the format's parametrisations, which for the elementary surfaces are ours on a right-handed frame), turned where the face is used reversed and where the file's frame is left-handed; its wires' edges, with their orientations composed through wire and face, run with the face on their left, so they are this kernel's loops once put in order by their vertices (an Euler circuit per wire, never turning straight back along the edge just used while another way is open, so a cylinder's seam is crossed twice in one loop). Curves keep the file's parameter (a circle on the frame of its own x and y directions), so every edge's range is the file's. Degenerate edges are dropped and the edges and vertices only they reached removed. With several shells the one whose vertices span the largest box is the outside, the others cavities (Open CASCADE lists them in any order); `finish` turns whatever encloses the wrong sign.

**Periodic B-splines.** A periodic curve's poles cover one period, the knots of the period counting them; the basis function of the file's first pole starts `degree + 1 − m` knots before the first knot (`m` its multiplicity), which FreeCAD's own evaluation of periodic curves confirms (a committed one of degree 3 with `m` = 2, and on the Strix degrees 2 to 5 with `m` = 1 and 2, all 1e-9). It becomes the unclamped B-spline over one period (two for an edge that crosses its first knot) and is then clamped at both ends by knot insertion (Boehm), so a surface swept from it closes on itself where the file's does (over two periods the tessellator found the boundary jumping across the surface).

**Oracles.** FreeCAD's own volumes, areas, tight boxes and colours of the objects of six documents it made; where FreeCAD's integrator is the weaker (a surface of revolution, an extruded periodic spline: 1.6e-5 and 4e-5 off), the volume by Pappus along the profile and by Green's theorem over the section, 1e-9. The face, edge and vertex numbers are FreeCAD's (`Face1` to `Face6` of a box). **The generator runs FreeCAD's GUI** (colours live in `GuiDocument.xml`): with the developer's own FreeCAD settings its start page made thumbnails of his recent files with an external viewer that ran past 6 GB (2026-11-01), so it runs with an empty `FREECAD_USER_HOME`, away from a desktop session; `verify_fixtures.py` checks the numbers again headless.

**Bounded work** (review of 2026-11-02). Counts in a BRep file reserve room for at most 4096 records, poles or knots before they are read (a count of eight million curves in a 16 MB file reserved 960 MB, and some 40 MB overflowed the capacity on wasm32); B-spline multiplicities must add up to the poles before any knot is expanded; each archive entry is decompressed once and all of them within 256 MiB together, each shape parsed once and shared by the objects that use it, and the walks over all objects' shapes share one bound; a wire's edges are put in order through an index by start vertex (a wire of 100 000 edges in well under a second). Spindle and horn tori go through `stepread::spindle` and `swept::spindle_part`, the code STEP uses. The deflate decoder follows RFC 1951 §3.2.2's canonical codes (`next_code`), rewritten after the review found the first version close to Mark Adler's `puff.c`.

**Open.** Binary BRep (refused by name); `App::Link` objects (no shape of their own: their target's placed again, not read); parabolas, hyperbolas, offset curves (refused; the STEP reader's conic arcs, `stepread::conic`, are there to take them since 2026-11-15); per-face colours were taken from the view's material list by face number and are tested only through single-colour objects.

## Oracles

- **Round trip** (`crates/io/tests/step_import.rs`): the 26 bodies the export's own tests write (the test kit's solids, trimmed solids, cavities, several lumps, rational splines, a sheet) at the scales 1e-3, 1 and 1e6, and in inches, metres and micrometres, read back with the same face, edge, vertex and shell counts, genus, volume and area (1e-7), nothing merged or turned. Faces with their seams as edges (cylinder, cone to its apex, torus with two seams) likewise.
- **Other writers' conventions**, made from our files by changing them the way other writers differ: every edge written against its curve (`same_sense` `.F.`, vertices swapped, every use flipped); every face turned over inside an `ORIENTED_FACE(.F.)`; every elementary surface written as the swept surface it is, both ways round (so `same_sense` must be read against the swept surface's own normal); every second use of an edge given its own `EDGE_CURVE` and vertices (sewing must join them back: as many merged edges as open edges, the gap 0); a cavity listed as a plain shell (turned, counted). Each reads as the same body.
- **A gap is recorded or refused, never healed**: a corner of a block moved by 1e-4 mm reads with that vertex's tolerance at least the gap and the vertex where the file put it; moved by 1e-2 mm (ten times the cap) it is refused naming the entity. A sheet on a plane bounded by a 9-point polyline whose odd points are 1 mm off the plane (which five samples per edge do not see) is refused naming the edge; 2e-4 mm off, it reads with the edge's proven gap as its tolerance; flat, exact.
- **Bounded copies**: the reviewer's probe (one 1000 × 1000 B-spline surface shared by 150 faces without bounds) is refused at parse within seconds; one such face is refused as a face without a loop on a surface that does not close.
- **Documents that open again**: a document whose imported files would make a file over 256 MiB is not saved, and the import that would make it so is refused (`crates/format/tests/format.rs`, `crates/engine/tests/step_import.rs` with three 90 MB files).
- **Hostile files** (`crates/stepread/src/tests.rs`, and `damaged_files_are_errors_never_panics` in the io test): kinds not read, named; a file without units; a chain of curves in a circle; wrong kinds of reference; absurd numbers, degrees and multiplicities; a mapped item of the wrong kinds; truncated text; plus, over the files of nine bodies, 39 truncations, 40 random byte damages and 60 random changes of references, reals, integers, flags and lists each: an error or a body, never a panic. `panic_hunt` runs `file.import` with adversarial parameters.
- **Through the engine** (`crates/engine/tests/step_import.rs`): import, the bodies of the exported document again; save, delete the STEP file, reopen: the same bodies, and saving again gives the same bytes; undo removes the whole import, redo brings it back; a document in inches; refusals change nothing. The format: `crates/format/tests/format.rs` (`an_import_is_written_like_this_and_round_trips`) and the version-25 fixture (written by the CLI: an export of a plate with a hole and a ball, imported, a feature on the import).
- **Swept, offset and implied B-splines** (`crates/io/tests/step_import.rs`, slice 2): every cylinder, sphere, cone and torus of exported bodies (with their seams; a cone to its apex; a sphere bounded by one meridian from pole to pole) written as a swept surface of a curve that is a spline in the file (a rational circle extruded, a rational half or whole circle turned, a quasi-uniform segment turned), both ways round: read as B-spline surfaces with the volume and area of the elementary bodies (1e-7), every edge proven, none loose; every elementary surface written as an `OFFSET_SURFACE` of one `d` nearer, both signs: the same bodies; an offset of a spline surface refused naming it. In `crates/stepread/src/tests.rs`: the implied knots of each type, a quasi-uniform polyline evaluated, Bézier, uniform and quasi-uniform surfaces, offset cycles ended, a hyperboloid and an offset through a sphere's centre named.
- **Hyperbolas and parabolas** (`crates/io/tests/step_conics.rs`, files written by hand from ISO 10303-42, 2026-11-15): cones cut parallel to their axis and to a ruling, held to the closed form of the cut cone, to the same piece the kernel cuts, to `topo::check`, the mesh and an export read again; the conic edge in nine forms; extruded and turned conics against closed forms; a cone offset past its apex (*Hyperbolas and parabolas*, below). In `crates/stepread`: `conic.rs` (the arcs against the standard's points), `bounded.rs` (parts, reversals, joins), `conic_tests.rs` (trims, composites, swept conics, the offset cone, hostile entities, the budget).
- **Poles and seams proven** (`crates/geom/src/certify.rs` › `curves_through_poles_and_apexes_are_proven`): meridians from, to and over a sphere's pole, rulings from and to a cone's apex, arcs over and from a revolved spline's pole proven within 1e-6; a curve 1e-2 off near the pole never proven within 1e-3. The round trips at three scales now require every edge proven (none sampled), and so does the showcase.
- **Assemblies and colours** (`crates/io/tests/step_assembly.rs`): a part placed three times by occurrences (both orders of the transformation's frames), a sub-assembly placed twice holding the part twice (four bodies, composed), mapped items, a part in millimetres in an assembly in metres: each body's centroid where the frames put the part's (1e-9), the paths, the same bytes giving bit-identical bodies; an identity placement giving the part bit for bit; hostile: a cycle through two assemblies, a chain 34 deep, a fan of 2¹⁶ (refused within the bound, in milliseconds), a placement at 1e308, a scale of 2, a transformation of the wrong kind. Colours the export writes read back (a body's; faces' only, the most common when every face has one, none when some have none, each face's own; none; one out of range left out). Through the engine (`crates/engine/tests/step_import.rs` › `an_assembly_imports_one_feature_per_placement_with_its_colour`): three features in one undo step, named `Peg#1…`, their `instance`, centroids and colour, undo and redo, saved and reopened without the file.
- **Opt-in, in release** (`cargo xtask freecad`, the Strix's gate): `every_body_of_the_showcase_reads_back` (every body of every showcase model exported and read back in three units, counts, volume and area to 1e-7); `freecad_files_read_with_freecads_volume`: FreeCAD (an independent kernel) makes solids of its own with its Python (box, cylinder, sphere, torus, cones with and without apex, a cut, a fusion, a common, filleted and chamfered boxes, a filleted cut, a revolved polygon, a revolved spline, a loft from a circle to a square, two solids in one file), writes each as STEP and measures it; each must read here with FreeCAD's volume and area to 1e-6 or be refused with its reason, never read wrong, and the plain ones must read; `freecad_assemblies_read_where_freecad_places_them` (slice 2): FreeCAD places a part three times by `App::Link`s in an `App::Part`, and a sub-assembly of two twice, writes them with its own STEP writer (`Import.export`, its assembly structure with the part shared) and measures the placed solids: ours must have as many bodies, the same total volume (1e-6) and a body at each of FreeCAD's centroids; where FreeCAD's file holds a colour (it may not, without its GUI: recorded), the bodies have it. `freecad_cone_sections_read_and_go_back` (2026-11-15): FreeCAD cuts a frustum along its axis and along a ruling and cuts a plate's countersunk hole by a flat, and writes them (its files hold `HYPERBOLA` and `PARABOLA`, which the test checks); each must read here with FreeCAD's volume and area to 1e-6 (measured on the Strix: within 3e-10), and FreeCAD must read 3DCraft's export of it (the conics as rational B-splines) as one valid solid of the same volume within 1e-4 (measured: 9e-7 to 7.4e-6; FreeCAD interpolates its own parameter-space curves for such edges, the export harness's `APPROXIMATED`, while 3DCraft reads its own export back to 4e-16). **Spindle and horn tori (2026-11-01).** The ABC corpus (chunk 0, 10 000 models) refused 192 models with "TOROIDAL_SURFACE: torus major radius must be larger than its minor radius": `geom::Torus` is a ring torus and `Torus::new` refuses `major ≤ minor`, while ISO 10303-42 asks only positive radii (and has `DEGENERATE_TOROIDAL_SURFACE` with `select_outer` for the same surface), and real parts put ball ends, lemon-shaped knobs and fillets of small revolved features on such tori. *Method* (`crates/stepread/src/spindle.rs`): the tube's circle crosses (a horn torus: touches) the axis, so the surface is two sheets that meet only at the two cusps on the axis: the **apple**, swept by the arc on the centre's side of the axis (`|v| ≤ acos(−R/r)`), and the **lemon**, swept by the arc beyond it, which is what its mirror image sweeps (the circle of radius `r` about `−R`, `|w| ≤ acos(R/r)`; empty for a horn torus). A face lies on one; which, its edges tell (33 points per edge against both circles in the meridian plane, a point voting when it is beyond the document's tolerance and four times as far from the other circle, so points near the axis do not vote; edges on both sheets, or none that tells, are refused naming the surface); `select_outer` names it, and edges on the other sheet are refused. The face's surface is the exact rational B-spline of revolution of its sheet's arc alone (`geom::network::arc` turned by `revolved`, the arc's ends put exactly on the axis so they are poles): the self-intersecting part is in no face's surface, so meshing, measuring, projection and booleans see a surface of revolution with poles, which they already handle (`kernel::revolve` makes an arc whose circle reaches the axis the same way, [`sweeps.md`](sweeps.md)). `geom::Torus` stays a ring torus (no "all tori are rings" assumption leaks: a spindle never becomes a `Torus`). *The file's normal* follows ISO 10303-42, which defines a `toroidal_surface`'s normal explicitly as `N(u, v) = cos v (cos u x + sin u y) + sin v z`, away from the tube's centre circle at the same `u` on both sheets (`Normal::Iso`): `∂u × ∂v` of the parametrisation on the apple, its opposite on the lemon, so the normal points out of a lemon. A `SURFACE_OF_REVOLUTION` has no such definition; its normal is `∂u × ∂v` of its own parametrisation, which turns over between the sheets (`Normal::Swept`), so the two paths do not share a convention. The first version of this (same day) read the lemon of a `TOROIDAL_SURFACE` by `∂u × ∂v`; the review found an ISO-conformant half lemon read silently as the half above its rim (its mirror image, of the same volume, the shell turned over): the tests now hold the centroid below the rim and no shell turned, which that version fails. An offset moves each sheet along that normal, so the apple and the lemon of an offset of a swept surface lie on tori of different minor radii (`Sheet` per part; under ISO's normal both move the same way); a ring torus offset past its axis is a spindle, a spindle offset back below it a ring torus again (when only its apple is left, or for a face on the apple). A face without an edge loop on a spindle is refused by name. *Tests*: `crates/stepread/src/spindle_tests.rs` (hand-written files: half an apple and half a lemon closed by a disc, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e3 and three radius ratios, volume and area within 1e-9 of Pappus on the tube's disc beyond and before the axis, centroid below the rim and no shell turned, so the face is the part the file meant; a revolved lemon by `∂u × ∂v`; the refusals for edges on both sheets, none that tells, and an empty sheet named as empty; a horn torus `2π²Rr²`; offsets each way; a revolved circle with both orientations; edges on the wrong sheet; hostile radii (0, negative, both, equal, NaN, infinity, subnormal) refused, never a panic), `crates/engine/tests/step_spindle.rs` (`file.import` of an apple and a lemon half, the deep check, no shell turned and the centroid below the rim, a block through the pole subtracted and intersected: each half within 1e-7 and below the rim, adding up to the whole; a rod united on the disc).

**Hyperbolas and parabolas, trimmed and composite curves (2026-11-15).** A file with a `HYPERBOLA` or a `PARABOLA` was refused by name ("this kind of curve is not read"), and so was a `TRIMMED_CURVE` or a `COMPOSITE_CURVE`; other modellers write the conics for the section of a cone by a plane parallel to its axis or to a ruling (a countersunk hole cut by a flat, a chamfered hole meeting a wall), so such a part did not import at all.

*Method* (`stepread::conic`, `stepread::bounded`). The standard's parametrisations: `λ(u) = C + a cosh u x + b sinh u y`, one branch, on the `x` side of the centre, both semi-axes positive (the other branch is a placement turned about its normal); `λ(u) = C + F (u² x + 2u y)`, `F` signed and not zero. `geom` has no conic curve and none was added: a variant of the open curve enum touches every `match` of the kernel, and the kernel already holds these sections as B-splines (its booleans make the hyperbola of a plane through a cone as the rational quadratic below, `geom::intersect::clip`). The part a file bounds becomes the exact rational quadratic B-spline of the arc: pieces of one unit of `u` (at most 16, then 16 equal pieces), the knots at the piece ends the standard's `u` there; a parabola's middle control points the polar form `C + F (lo hi x + (lo + hi) y)` (weight 1: the B-spline is `λ` parameter for parameter), a hyperbola's `C + E(m) / cosh d` with weight `cosh d` (`E(m) = a cosh m x + b sinh m y`; a hyperbolic rotation, a linear map that keeps rational Bézier curves and their weights, takes each piece to the one about `m = 0`, whose point at `s` is `(a (1 + τ²), 2bτ) / (1 − τ²)` with `τ = tanh(d / 2)(2s − 1)`, that is `(a cosh u, b sinh u)` for `tanh(u / 2) = τ`, `s ↦ u` monotone). An edge on a conic is the arc between the feet of its vertices (the inverse `asinh(y / b)` or `y / 2F`, then Newton steps), with the arc's domain as its range; a closed edge on one is refused. A `TRIMMED_CURVE` takes its trims by `PARAMETER_VALUE` or by point (`master_representation` preferred, else the point, which needs no unit; a point within the file's uncertainty of its curve; on a curve that does not close, the order agreeing with `sense_agreement`); a trimmed conic keeps its trims, which bound a swept surface's profile, and any other trimmed curve is its basis turned round for `.F.` (exactly: a line's direction negated, a circle's or an ellipse's frame seen from the other side, a B-spline's knots mirrored), since an edge is bounded by its vertices; a trimmed curve of a trimmed curve is refused. A `COMPOSITE_CURVE`'s segments are each one exact B-spline (a trimmed line as a segment, an arc of a circle by `geom::network::arc`, of an ellipse that arc scaled, part of a B-spline cut by de Casteljau in homogeneous coordinates, a conic's arc), turned round for `same_sense` `.F.`, raised to the highest degree among them (degree elevation of each Bézier piece in homogeneous coordinates) and laid end to end, one unit of the joined parameter per segment; ends within the file's uncertainty become one control point at the first's, as two vertices do, ends farther apart are refused naming the piece. A swept surface of a trimmed conic is that of the B-spline between its trims. An untrimmed one waits for its faces (`Read::Conic`, `swept::conic_surface`): extruded, the points of the face's edges moved along the direction into the conic's plane give the range of `u` (widened by a quarter, at most a unit); turned, the distance from a point `L` of the axis bounds it (a turn keeps that distance: `a cosh u` and `b |sinh u|` at most `R + |C − L|`, `|F| u²` and `2 |F u|` likewise, `R` the farthest edge point a quarter larger). Refused: an extrusion along a direction in the conic's own plane (no surface), and an untrimmed conic turned about an axis in its plane that it crosses or touches (a paraboloid, a hyperboloid of two sheets: the surface swept twice with opposite normals, and which half a face is on is in its parameter-space curves, which are not read; the message says that a profile trimmed to one side, or the surface as a B-spline, reads). The control-point budget counts a conic as 33 points, a composite curve as 32 times its segments'.

*The offset through the axis.* An `OFFSET_SURFACE` of a `SURFACE_OF_REVOLUTION` of a line that meets the axis was refused for every offset towards the axis, as going "through the basis's axis": the line is read as a cone whose frame is at its apex with radius 0, so the offset's radius there, `±d / cos α`, is negative for every such offset (and so for a `CONICAL_SURFACE` offset inward by more than its radius at its placement). It is the same cone with its apex moved along the axis by `d / sin α`, and is read so, the frame moved to where the radius is 0 (`swept::offset`); the offsets of the basis's points within `d cot α` of its apex cross the axis onto the other nappe, which no face of a solid uses (an edge there is not proven). An offset through a cylinder's axis or a sphere's or torus's centre is still refused, the message saying that a file with the face's surface as the plain surface it is reads.
