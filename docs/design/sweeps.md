# 3DCraft design: Sweeps: extrude, revolve, loft, sweep, helix, thread

Solids made by moving a profile: profiles from sketches (`kernel::profile`, `spline2`), extrusions and their ends (`extrude`, `extrude_to`), revolutions (`revolve`), lofts (`loft`), sweeps along a path (`sweep`) and along a helix (`helix`), and the thread cut by one (`thread`); each with its `regen` module and command. Method 4 of the map ([`../architecture.md`](../architecture.md) §5).

## Method

**Profiles.** `kernel::profile` finds the closed regions of a sketch's curves (lines, arcs, circles, splines joined end to end; even–odd nesting gives holes and islands) and refuses open, branching, crossing or touching curves by name, deciding clearance of splines by subdivision against the tolerance. A spline with corners, or one that closes on itself, is cut into smooth pieces (knot insertion: the pieces are the spline exactly); a piece of degree 1 is a line. Each segment carries its source and a piece number, which become the names. The pairs that may meet (curve ends, touching curves, nested loops) come from a tree of boxes and are judged in the order an all-pairs sweep would judge them, so detection takes `n log n` time on drawn and patterned sketches up to 262 144 curves, with bounds on the pairs and on the splines' control points for hostile input (*Profile detection at scale*).

**Exact surfaces where the motion allows.** An extrusion sweeps lines into planes, arcs into cylinders, splines into B-spline surfaces; a revolution sweeps lines into planes, cylinders and cones, arcs into spheres and tori, splines into rational surfaces of revolution (a full turn in two halves, so no face meets itself across a seam). A loft through a row of closed sections matches their curves (by order from a chosen start, cutting sections with fewer corners at the same fractions of length) and makes planes, cylinders and cones where the sections allow, else B-spline surfaces that contain every section curve exactly (ruled, smooth, or closed into a ring); where every section is smooth at a vertex and the faces would meet there at more than the angular tolerance, they are made to meet tangentially (a piece's parameter run through a quadratic or a cubic so the ratio of the speeds across the vertex is the same in every section, and through three or more sections the weights of a column made level at its knots where they would crease it); a loft without such a crease is built as before. A sweep along a path in a plane carries the profile square to it: a plane, cylinder, cone or torus along lines and arcs, mitres at corners between lines, a polynomial surface within **half the linear tolerance** along a spline; small kinks within the tolerance are absorbed exactly. A sweep along a helix carries the profile by the helix's screw motion, its faces B-spline surfaces within half the tolerance (a line parallel to the axis sweeps an exact cylinder).

**Two rails.** A sweep with a second rail places the profile at every station so that the two points of its outline where the rails start follow them: stations by the same fraction of each rail's length (arc length along lines and arcs, the spline's parameter within a spline piece), the profile's x axis along the chord between the rails, its y axis the up direction (one given, or the normal of the first rail's sketch) made square to the chord, scaled uniformly (the default) or only along the chord. The surface through the placed profiles is `O*(v) + U*(v) x(u) + B*(v) y(u)` exactly, so its distance from the true sweep is a function of the station alone, bounded by samples and the second derivatives of both: **proven within half the linear tolerance**, not sampled; rails that meet anywhere are refused by a branch and bound, the fold by the crossing speed proven the same way. Two closed rails make a ring without caps, started at a joint (*Sweep along two rails*).

**Ends and threads.** An extrusion ends at a distance, symmetric, through all (from the bodies' boxes) or up to a face, by a tool solid with that face's surface and the boolean. A thread is an ISO metric groove (size and pitch found at every regeneration from the face, a designation or a length) swept along a helix and subtracted from the body, or only reported (cosmetic, the default); external on a shank, internal in a hole.

**Soundness.** Every sweep refuses before building what would fold or pass through the axis (a profile across the axis, a bend tighter than the profile's reach, sections whose planes cross within the profiles, a pitch shorter than the profile), then `topo::check`; what passes through itself without folding is found by regeneration's self-intersection check over the whole body, and the feature fails with the two faces.

**Names** are by role and source: `side(e7)`, `startCap(…)`, `endCap(…)`, `startEdge`, `lateralEdge(e5,e6)`, `startVertex`, with `piece(k)/` for further pieces of one source and the matched curves for a loft or a sweep (`side(e3,e12)`); a thread's own faces have the role `thread`. Details are in [`names.md`](names.md) and below.

## Owed, found by the coordinator's demo (2026-11-04)

- *A loft with a shared parametrisation*: two lofts through the same section vertices do not coincide between the sections (up to 3 µm apart), because each fits its own surface through its sections; a mould half and the part it is cut from then leave slivers. Owed: lofts whose sections and their parametrisation are the same give the same surface (one fit for the same input, or the shared surface taken by the second).

## Findings and history

> Moved verbatim from `docs/architecture.md` on 2026-10-15. Section numbers (§4.1, §5.3, …) in the text are those of that file before the split; [README.md](README.md) says where each one is now.

**Profiles and extrusions with splines (2026-10-13).** `kernel::profile` takes `Curve2::Spline` (degree, knots, control points; `kernel::spline2` holds the arithmetic). A spline's ends join like those of a line; where it meets an arc its end control point is moved onto the arc's end. Area and centroid are exact (Green's theorem by Gauss–Legendre on each Bézier piece, whose integrands are polynomials). Whether a spline keeps clear of another curve, and of itself, is decided by subdivision: curves are compared as chords of pieces flat within an eighth of the tolerance, so curves that come within three quarters of the tolerance of each other away from a shared end are always refused, curves that stay more than one and a quarter of it apart never are; a spline with a cusp, or one that crosses or touches itself, is refused and named (`e7 crosses or touches itself`). A spline with a corner and a spline that closes on itself are cut into pieces (the next paragraph; both were refused until 2026-10-14). `kernel::extrude` sweeps a spline into a face on a polynomial B-spline surface (the spline's degree by 1) between B-spline edges; the caps are planes bounded by them; the wall is named `side(e7)` like any other, so moving a control point renames nothing. `kernel::revolve` sweeps it into a rational surface (the next paragraph). Such a solid is a boolean operand like any body with polynomial B-spline faces. Known limits, each an error and not a wrong result: the exact integrals (`mass_properties`) and the tessellator can decline the wall of a spline with a hairpin just short of a cusp (measured on random profiles: 4 of 88 meshes declined, 0 of 30 integrals), and the integrals take seconds on a wall that wiggles.

**Revolved splines, closed splines and corners (2026-10-14).** Three things a spline could not do in a profile, built on one idea: *no face lies on a spline surface that closes on itself*. `craft3d-geom` knows no period for a spline surface, so the tessellator, the exact measure and the booleans could not follow such a face across its seam; a surface that ends where its face does is an ordinary patch to all three. (The tessellator follows such a face since 2026-10-14, §6, for what a STEP file will bring; the measure and the booleans do not, so the kernel goes on making halves.) So where one curve would sweep a surface that closes, or one with a crease, it sweeps several faces instead.

- *A revolved spline* (`kernel::revolve`) sweeps a surface of revolution that is a NURBS surface exactly: around the axis (u) the standard rational quadratic arcs, in equal pieces of at most a quarter turn (weights 1 and the cosine of half the piece's angle, so never below cos 45°), along the spline (v) its own degree, knots and control points, each control point turned into the control polygon of its circle. u runs over 0 to 1 and is not the angle. A partial turn is one face between the spline's edge at the start and at the end (polynomial B-spline curves) and the arcs its ends sweep, as for a line or an arc. **A full turn of a loop with a spline in it is made in two halves:** each spline sweeps two faces (0 to 180° and 180° to 360°, each on its own surface), with the spline's edge in the sketch plane (`startEdge`) and half a turn on (`endEdge`) between them; the circles at a spline's ends are two arcs between a `startVertex` and an `endVertex`, and a line or an arc next to a spline has a face whose loop there is those two arcs. Loops without a spline are built as before (no seam, whole circles).
- *At the axis.* A spline may end on the axis: the border of its surface is then one point (as a cone has its apex), a *pole*. The body is valid, meshes watertight and has the volume Pappus gives; since 2026-10-14 `topo::mass_properties` measures a boundary through such a point and the booleans take the face as an operand (§5.3 › *Freeform faces with a pole*), so `feature.revolve` builds it like any other profile: a dome between two points of the axis, a drop, a vase with a round bottom (`crates/engine/tests/spline_sweeps.rs`: Pappus to 1e-9 at three scales, the deep check, booleans, save and reopen). A spline that leaves the axis *along* it (a cusp) is built and measured too, and since 2026-10-15 a boolean operand (§5.3 › *Freeform faces with a pole*, *Cusps*), except against a face that holds the axis through the point. A spline that reaches the axis *between* its ends is refused by name (`UnsupportedCurve`: the surface would pinch into a point); one that crosses it is `CrossesAxis`, judged on the curve (its true extremes, `Spline2::extent`), not on its control points.
- *A closed spline* (its last control point on its first) is a loop on its own. `kernel::profile` cuts it in two at the inner knot nearest the middle of its domain (knot insertion: the two pieces are the spline exactly) and hands on a loop of two segments with the same `source` and `piece` 0 and 1. Extruded it is two walls and two caps; revolved, two faces, or four for a full turn. Where it closes it has a corner in general, since the curve leaves along its first leg and arrives along its last.
- *Corners.* A spline with an inner knot as often as its degree (which is every inner knot of a spline of degree 1, a polyline) is cut there the same way, into one segment per smooth piece; a piece of degree 1 is a `Shape::Line`, so a polyline sweeps planes, cylinders and cones like the lines it is. A knot repeated more often than the degree tears the curve and stays refused. A piece between two such knots shorter than the linear tolerance (its control polygon is) cannot be a curve of its own: its ends coincide, and until 2026-11-01 it failed as a branching profile, an error the user could do nothing with. `Spline2::profile_pieces_within` (used by profile detection, `open_chain` and a sweep's path, each with the document's whole tolerance) now handles it in one of two ways:
  - *Dropped.* This happens when the pieces on either side meet it in tangents within the angular tolerance (the joints of a formula curve that nearly meet), and its own control polygon runs on along the tangent it is entered by (no leg turns back). The neighbours are then joined at the middle of its ends into one piece. The curve moves there by at most half the linear tolerance, and the joint is smooth to the angular tolerance. Keeping the piece inside the joined one instead left control points within rounding of each other. Their curvature was rounding, and a sweep along the path refused it as a bend tighter than its profile.
  - *Refused by name* in every other case: a corner at the piece, a piece that turns back, or a piece at an end of the spline. The error names the piece and the knot it starts at (the start of the domain for the first piece): "a piece of spline e7 shorter than the tolerance at knot 0.5", with what to do. Regeneration gives the kernel's own text with the sketch's name for the curve.

  A closed spline whose corners all went is cut in two, as one without corners is. A polyline keeps its own refusal (two control points in a row coincide). Pieces at their old knots keep their numbers, so names are unchanged; only splines that failed before are now cut differently. Held by `crates/kernel/tests/narrow_spans.rs`, at three scales each:
  - a degree-12 wave with every joint knot repeated twelve times and a piece 1e-10 long, extruded and meshed within the tolerance;
  - a cubic whose piece 1e-10 long turns a corner, refused with its knot;
  - through `open_chain` and as a sweep's path: the tangent case kept (the sweep a valid solid), and a corner, a piece that turns back and a piece at the start refused with their knots;
  - a closed spline whose only corners are around such a piece, cut in two and extruded.
- *Names.* An origin has a `piece` number beside its role and sources (`kernel::extrude::Origin`): 0 for the first or only entity those sources give in that role, counted up for the others (the pieces of a curve; in a full turn made in halves `2 k` and `2 k + 1` for piece `k`; for a vertex or a lateral edge, the number of the curve the loop leaves by). In a persistent name the first keeps the plain name and the others get the role `piece` in front, written like a copy: `F2:side(e7)`, `F2:piece(1)/F2:side(e7)`. Names stay unique within a body and a wall of a spline keeps its name when a control point moves; the numbers of the pieces after a corner change when a corner is added before them (they are counted along the curve).
- *Measured (2026-10-14).* `crates/kernel/tests/spline_sweeps.rs`: vases, rings, a hollow ring, closed splines of degree 2, 3 and 5, cornered cubics and polylines at 1e-3, 1, 1e3 and 1e6, on both sides of the axis, in two frames, through a full turn, 120°, 90°, a symmetric 120°, 270° and 342°: `topo::check` clean, volume, surface and centroid within 1e-9 of Pappus's theorems on an oracle that integrates the profile itself (de Boor and Gauss–Legendre, independent of the kernel's Bézier pieces), meshes watertight at two tolerances, the surface on the turned spline to 1e-12; a straight spline gives the cone's and the tube's closed forms to 1e-10; a polyline gives the solid its polygon gives to 1e-12. `crates/engine/tests/spline_sweeps.rs`: the same through the commands at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6 with `document.check {"deep": true}`, save and reopen, a control point moved by a variable against a full regeneration, and booleans with a block and a cylinder that keep `V(A ∪ B) + V(A ∩ B) = V(A) + V(B)` and `V(A − B) = V(A) − V(A ∩ B)` to 1e-7.

**Closed splines are periodic (2026-11-04).** A closed spline was clamped at its first point: the fit through points (`sketch.spline {through, closed}`, and a live one at every regeneration) was the open fit from the first point back to it, and a closed control polygon the clamped spline on it, so the curve left and reached its seam in directions of their own. A fan blade lofted from closed splines through points had a kink of 57 to 100 degrees along its leading edge. Now a closed spline is the periodic uniform spline of its degree, `p − 1` times continuously differentiable at its seam as at every other knot, written as the clamped spline it is: `k + p` control points on `clamped_uniform_knots`, the first and last the seam point, so nothing that takes a sketch spline (profiles in two pieces, lofts, sweeps, offsets, the tessellator, STEP, DXF and SVG) sees another kind of curve. Through `k` points (`curve_fit::interpolate_closed`): point `i` at `i / k` on the periodic knots `(j − p) / k`, a circulant system (never singular for an odd degree, as the Euler–Frobenius polynomials of even order have no root on the unit circle; singular for an even degree through an even number of points, refused), then the knots 0 and 1 inserted `p − 1` times each (*The NURBS Book*, A5.1), which makes the curve pass through a control point there without changing it; the seam is the first point exactly. On a control polygon (`curve_fit::periodic_control`): the same insertion, the polygon turned so the seam lies across its first point; the polygon's points away from the seam stay the spline's own entities. `periodic: false` keeps the clamped closed spline for a corner on purpose. The solver has a closed live spline as that clamped spline with `SplineSeam` rows (`solve::Spline::periodic`): each derivative of order 1 to `p − 1` the same at both ends (weights from the derivatives' control points, eq. 3.8, one row per coordinate, scaled by the largest weight), so its unknowns and equations stay square, and a tangent at its seam has one direction. A periodic non-live spline is stored as such (`Geometry::Spline::periodic`, format 69), and the solve holds its seam with the same rows whenever its sketch is solved, so moving one of its points (`sketch.edit`) keeps it smooth; before the review of 2026-11-04 it was stored as a plain clamped spline and a move made a corner of 7°. Closedness is never read off coordinates: an open spline through points whose last lies on the first stays the open fit (`curve_fit::interpolate` is always open; `interpolated(…, closed)` is told by the caller, regeneration from the same point first and last). An even degree through an even number of places is refused by the command, live too; a point of the sketch given to a periodic spline on control points that would drop out of it is refused (`periodic: false` closes on it). Saved files: older ones load with every spline non-periodic, as saved (an identity step to 69); a saved closed live spline rebuilds smooth (pre-release, no meaning-preserving migration). Tests: `engine/tests/sketch_spline.rs` (seam angle and curvature jump below 1e-9, the clamped one above 0.5 rad; eight points on a circle within Hall and Meyer's `5/384 h⁴ |f⁗|`; extruded volume against Green's theorem at three scales; live equal to fitted once), `regen::curve_fit` (degrees 1 to 7 at three scales, even degrees, the cubic's seam at `(P[k−1] + 4P[0] + P[1]) / 6`), `solve::equations` (the seam's weights in closed form, its gradients).

**Profile detection at scale (2026-10-17, roadmap M5; the weld table's finding W4).** Profile detection took at most 4096 curves (`MAX_CURVES`) while a sketch pattern makes up to 100 000: a 70 × 70 perforated sheet and a 10' × 5' weld table plate (4675 curves) were refused. Its three searches for pairs were sweeps in x: ends sorted by x, each compared with the ends after it up to `tol` further on (step 1); curves sorted by the left side of their box grown by the tolerance, each compared with the curves after it whose box starts before its own ends (step 4); and every loop against every other whose box holds its first point (step 5, quadratic outright). A column of holes shares its x range, so the sweeps compared nearly every pair of it. *The method:* each search asks a static tree of boxes (`kernel/src/boxtree.rs`: split at the median of the centres along the longer side of their spread, leaves of 8, node boxes the hulls of the boxes under them, so no rounding) for the items whose box passes a test that holds for a box whenever it holds for one inside it; such a test finds exactly what testing every box finds. Each box found is taken from the step's budget as it is found, and the search stops when the budget runs out. The candidates come in no particular order and are judged with the old predicates as the old order would: of the ends near an end, the two first in the x order are kept as they come (they are the two listed in a branching error), the curves after a curve in the sweep are sorted by that place (so the first pair that fails, and the error, are the old ones), and of the loops round a point the parent is the smallest, the one of least index among equal areas (what a scan in index order keeps). A loop's winding number about a point is summed over a tree of its stretches (halves of halves down to 8 segments): a stretch whose box does not hold the point lies in a half-plane beside it and turns by the principal angle between its ends; other stretches are summed segment by segment as before. The winding number is a whole number, and the point is at least the tolerance from the loop (step 4 refuses anything nearer), so both sums round to it. *Conditions added, each general:* ends are searched within `2 tol` in x and in y (a distance is at least each of its components; the factor 2 leaves room for the rounding of `hypot` and of the subtractions, and every candidate is then judged by the exact old test, so a wider search only costs time); a step looks at no more than `MAX_PAIRS` = 4096² ordered pairs of curves or loops (each with itself), and four times that of ends, which is what an input under the old cap could need at most, so nothing accepted before is refused for it, and past it the step is refused with `CrowdedCurves`, which names the curve, end or loop searched about and curves found near it (hostile input: thousands of concentric circles, one line drawn many times, nested squares); the splines of one call may have `MAX_SPLINE_POINTS_IN_ALL` = 2¹⁸ = 262 144 control points in all, refused beyond with `TooManySplinePoints` naming the splines with the most. Deciding clearance costs 25 to 90 µs per control point in release on an idle machine (closed cubic splines of 8 to 4095 points, `profile_scale.rs` › `spline_work_timings`); at the bound it took 9 to 14 s for splines of 8 to 512 points and 53 s for 64 splines of 4096 points, measured at a load average of 30 (about 2.4 times slower than idle). A pattern of 100 000 splines of 4095 points would have taken about a day. **For the developer to judge:** this bound refuses inputs that `main` accepts (more than 262 144 control points in all, for example 65 or more splines of 4096 points), which before took minutes to hours; the cap is 2¹⁸ = 262 144 curves, which holds a pattern's 100 000 with its drawn curves and spline pieces, and keeps a sheet at the cap within 0.7 s and 250 MB. **Offset of profiles at scale (2026-10-17, roadmap M5; the laser kerf of patterned sheets).** The offset of a sketch's profiles (`kernel/src/offset_profile.rs`, method in [`document.md`](document.md) › *Offsets of profiles*) took at most 4096 pieces (`MAX_PIECES`) and 16 384 raw curves (`MAX_RAW`) after profile detection's cap rose: its cut step compared the raw offset's curves pairwise in a sweep in x (a column of holes shares its x range), and its keep step tested the middle of every piece against every curve of the input and every mitre wedge (quadratic outright). A sheet of 4900 holes offset by half a kerf was refused. *The method:* the cut step asks a [`BoxTree`] of the raw curves' boxes for the boxes that overlap a curve's within `tol` (the sweep's own overlap test, which passes for a node's hull whenever it passes for a box inside it, so exactly the pairs the sweep looks at), and judges the ones after it in the sweep's order by that place, with the exact intersections as before, so every cut is recorded in the sweep's order. The keep step asks two trees, of the input's curves and of the mitre wedges, whether any box holding the piece's middle has a curve or wedge that passes the old exact test (`BoxTree::any`, which stops at the first, as the old `any` did; `any` has one answer whatever the order). *Conditions added, each general:* a curve's box is grown by `2 keep_from + 64 ε S` (`S` the largest coordinate of the raw offset and the input; a point the distance test puts within `keep_from` of a curve is within `keep_from` plus the rounding of the test, at most a few units in the last place of `S`, of a point of the curve, and the nearest point of a line or circle lies in its box; the factor 2 covers the rounding of the distance itself); a wedge's box, the hull of its corners, is grown by `tol/4 + 64 ε S (1 + 4 / sin θ)` with `θ` the angle the corner turns through (a point the wedge test finds inside is inside every side up to the rounding, so within the wedge grown by the rounding over the sine of half its sharpest angle; the angles at the offset curves are right and those at the corner and the tip add up to half a turn, with the sine of the turn, at most twice the sine of half of either), and a turn whose sine is 0 grows it to everything, so that wedge is tested with every middle as before. Wider boxes cost only time: every candidate is judged by the old test. *Caps and bounds:* `MAX_PIECES` = `profile::MAX_CURVES` = 2¹⁸ (the pieces go through profile detection, which takes no more), `MAX_RAW` = 4 × that (a corner adds at most two curves, so input that detection takes stays below it). The work bounds are set so that nothing the old caps let through is refused where that can be said: the cut step looks at no more than `MAX_PAIRS` = 16 384² ordered pairs (what 16 384 raw curves can have), and the names may list no more than `MAX_NAMED` = 4096² curves in all (a piece of a curve that left several is named by the curves of its loop, which made that quadratic in memory: up to 4096 pieces of up to 4096 curves under the old cap). Two have no such argument, because the old steps had no bound: `MAX_CUT_POINTS` = 2²² points where raw curves cross (each kept on both curves and making a piece of each; a sheet of 2¹⁸ pieces needs about a million; the bound is reached in 3.5 s in release at a peak of 388 MB for a test whose 262 000-curve input alone takes about 200 MB; 2²⁴, the first choice, took 1.2 GB per try, too much for this machine) and `MAX_NEAR` = 2²⁸ curves and wedges found near the middles of pieces. **Revolved arcs on a spindle or horn torus (2026-10-15).** An arc whose circle reaches the axis without being centred on it was refused ("sweeps a spindle torus"), although the arc itself kept clear of the axis (the turbofan's spinner, an ogive whose second arc is centred 26 mm beyond the axis with a radius of 48 mm). The rule was about the wrong thing: `geom::Torus` has no spindle or horn form, but the face is only the part of that torus the arc reaches, and the profile keeps it on one side of the axis. Such an arc is now swept as a spline is: the arc as an exact rational quadratic B-spline (*The NURBS Book* §7.3, made in the sketch and mapped to the turning half-plane control point by control point, which an affine map allows), turned into a rational surface of revolution whose weights are the arc's times the ring's, and made in two halves in a full turn. Like a spline it may reach the axis at an end (a lens, a horn whose arc ends where its circle touches the axis); an arc that reaches the axis between its ends pinches and is refused by name (`UnsupportedCurve`, "the arc reaches the axis between its ends"), and a whole circle that touches the axis likewise ("the circle touches the axis"). The arc's edges stay circles. Measured: `crates/kernel/tests/revolve_shapes.rs` › `arcs_whose_circle_reaches_the_axis_match_pappus` (a D, a lens and a horn at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6, both sides, five sweeps: volume and area within 1e-10 of Pappus, `topo::check` clean, two rational faces in a full turn); the 4000 random profiles of `revolve_random.rs` (1151 rational faces, all within 1e-9 of Pappus, a sample meshed watertight); `crates/engine/tests/revolve.rs` › `an_ogive_whose_arc_lies_on_a_spindle_torus_is_revolved` (the finding itself; a variant with exact corners within 1e-9 of Green's theorem, the deep check, and a block subtracted and intersected adding up to the whole within 1e-9).

- *A pinch between two halved curves (found by the random run, also for splines).* Where two curves that are both made in halves in a full turn meet at a point of the axis (the tip of two cones, but curved), the body's boundary meets itself in that point. Both sides shared one vertex there, which no manifold boundary allows (Euler–Poincaré: `V − E + F − R` odd). Each side now has a vertex of its own at that point, as two cones meeting at their apex share none (they have no vertex there at all); the arriving side's is named `startVertex` by the arriving curve alone.

**Loft (2026-10-14, M9).** `doc::Loft { sections: Vec<LoftSection { sketch, start }>, ruled, closed }` is a solid through the closed profiles of a row of sketches, in order (`feature.loft`; `kernel::loft`, `regen/src/loft.rs`). Each sketch holds one closed loop without holes, on planes that follow each other (offset planes, datum planes; not necessarily parallel).
- *Matching.* Every loop is walked counter-clockwise as seen looking back along the loft, whichever way its sketch plane faces, and the curves are matched in that order, first with first. The first section starts at its curve with the smallest id; each later section is turned to lie nearest to the one before it (with as many curves: the turn for which the squared distances between matched corners, each seen from the centre of its section's area, add up to the least; otherwise the corner most nearly in the direction in which the section before starts; ties go to the smallest id). `start` names the curve a loop starts with instead: that is how a twist is given or taken out. A whole circle is walked as four quarter arcs from its point in that direction. Sections with different numbers of curves (a square to a circle, a triangle to a hexagon) are cut where the others have their corners, at the same fraction of the length of the loop from its start; corners within a thousandth of the loop of each other are one.
- *Sides.* One face per column of matched curves and per band. A band runs between two sections that follow each other when the loft is ruled, closed or has two sections, and through all sections otherwise. The surface is a plane where every control point lies in one (line to line in a plane: a box, the frustum of a pyramid), a cylinder or a cone for an arc to a coaxial arc over the same angles (whole circles about one axis, in straight bands, give one face without a seam, as an extrusion does), and otherwise a B-spline surface that contains every section curve exactly: the curves of a column are brought to one degree and one knot vector (`geom::refine`: knot insertion and degree elevation, which change the description of a curve and not the curve; arcs are exact rational quadratics), and each row of their control points, as homogeneous points, is joined by a straight line (ruled), by the spline of degree up to 3 that interpolates it at chord-length parameters (smooth), or by the closed cubic spline with continuous curvature (a smooth ring; one Bézier band per pair of sections). The parameters are the same for every column, so neighbouring faces share their edges exactly. The ends are planes on the first and the last sketch plane.
- *Soundness.* Refused in the kernel, with the sections named: planes that cross within the profiles (each profile lies wholly on one side of the plane of the next), sections in one place, a plane that lies along the loft, a side whose normal vanishes (it folds: half a turn of twist), and everything `topo::check` knows. What folds nowhere and still passes through itself (a bend that runs on into its own beginning) the kernel cannot see: regeneration runs `tess::body_self_intersections` on every loft with the parameters of `document.check {"deep": true}` and fails the feature with the two faces that cross and the advice to give a section another `start`. A loft that regenerates is one the deep check passes. The check's mesh of a loft side has no limit on segments along the sections, only on its cells (2026-10-31; before that a side was refused above 1024 grid segments either way, and knot spans a billionth of the range wide, where sections' joints nearly meet, made it ask for 2500): [`tessellator.md`](tessellator.md) › *A grid limited by its cells*. A smooth loft through slender twisted sections (a wind-turbine blade whose 0.55 m tip follows a 1.1 m section) could not be meshed for that check until 2026-10-14, because the chords along the aerofoil's nose lay beside the face; the tessellator now cuts such chords finer (§6, *A chord the triangles cannot follow*).
- *Names.* A side is `F9:side(e3,e7)`: the matched curves of its column, one per section, up to the section the face ends at (a face through all sections names all of them; in a ruled loft the face of the second band is `F9:side(e3,e7,e5)`; in a closed loft the face from the last section back to the first names all and the first again). The ends are `F9:startCap(…)` and `F9:endCap(…)` after the curves of the first and the last section. The parts a curve was cut in (a quartered circle, a side cut to match two) carry the curve's id, so their faces share a name: as after a boolean that cuts a face, the name then resolves to several faces. Edges and vertices are named by the faces they lie on (`F9:between[…]`, `F9:corner[…]`), as for a boolean. Names follow the matching: an edit that turns a section so far that another corner is nearest changes which curves are matched, and the old names then point at nothing rather than at another face.
- *Failures.* A section's sketch that is missing, suppressed, later in the history or failed fails the loft with `sections: section 2: …` and `causedBy`; so does a sketch with no closed profile (and no open chain: since 2026-10-21 an open chain is an open section, below), with several, or with a hole, and a `start` that is not a curve of the profile. `feature.edit` takes `sections`, `ruled` and `closed`.
- *Not built.* Guide curves, tangency at the ends, a section that is a single point (a tip), profiles with holes (loft the hole on its own and cut it out), and analytic recognition of a smooth loft that happens to be a surface of revolution (it is a B-spline surface).

**Open sections: a loft that is a sheet; 256 sections (2026-10-21, the Garabit valley's findings 1 and 2).** No command made a freeform sheet (`feature.fill` fills flat profiles; a loft refused an open profile), so a terrain could not be a B-spline sheet cutting a block, the use the sheet booleans were built for (the showcase lofted 64 *closed* sections, a spline top and three lines, instead). Of the two ways to a freeform sheet the task named, a fill of a closed non-planar boundary has no command to draw its boundary (a sketch is planar), so the loft it is.
- *Built so.* A sketch whose curves make one open chain (`kernel::profile::open_chain`: lines, arcs and splines end to end with two free ends, read as profile detection reads curves: ends within the tolerance are one junction with one representative, a spline cut at its corners, no two curves touching but where they join; a circle, a closing spline, a loop, a branch and several chains are refused) is an *open section*; regeneration tries it when a sketch has no closed profile (`regen/src/loft.rs`), and the kernel's loft takes all sections open or all closed (mixed is refused naming both, regeneration naming the open one with why it is no closed profile, as a profile left open by mistake reads; a closed loft, a ring, of open sections is refused as not built). An open loft is the closed loft without the caps and without the turn of a loop: each column of matched curves gives a side face as before (the same matching by length for different counts, the same tangency of columns where every section is smooth, except across the chain's two ends, which are no joint), every section has one vertex more (the chain's last end) and the rails are one more, and the body is `BodyKind::Sheet`.
- *Which way it is walked, and so which way it faces* (rule of 2026-10-21; before, the first section's chain was walked from the free end of its curve with the smaller id, so redrawing it, reversing it or the order of the sections flipped the sheet silently). The sheet faces *down in its sections' sketches*: for each section, of the two directions in its plane square to its chord (end to end), the one against the sketch's `y` axis (for a chord along `y` within the angular tolerance, the one along `x`) (`loft::sketch_down`), read off the plane's frame and the chain's two ends only. The facing is the sum of these over all sections, so the order of the sections does not change it either; sections whose sketches say opposite sides (two of them at a right angle or more: a sketch whose `y` axis points the other way) are refused naming both, since the sum would turn on how many say which. *Frames that turn with the loft* (2026-10-22, the m9-r1 review): only neighbouring sections are compared, so sketches turned round a bend by a right angle or more over all (each square to the bend, `y` toward its axis) build; the first section is held to the sum of the downs that agree with its own (all of them when every section agrees with every other, so nothing built before changes), and the walk by nearest ends carries the side to the last; two neighbours a right angle or more apart are still refused, naming both. Held to by `sections_whose_frames_turn_round_a_bend_build_facing_down_in_each_sketch` (120°, 180° and 270° in 6, 12 and 18 steps, either order, three scales: ruled to the closed form of its strips `(n − 1) · 2h · 2R sin(Δ/2)`, smooth within 1 % of the cylinder `2h R θ`, every face facing out from the bend's axis, which is down in each sketch; fails before) and `neighbours_turned_the_other_way_in_a_bend_are_refused`. The first section is then walked the way for which (its chord) × (the loft's direction, from its middle to the next section's) points that way, and every later one the way round for which its ends, seen from its middle, lie nearest to those of the section before (as before); a `start` on the first section overrides the rule (a chord along the loft, where the side cannot be told, asks for one). Sections across x drawn on `yz` planes, `y` up, face down, so a subtract keeps what is under the sheet (decided 2026-10-18: subtract keeps the side the sheet faces). Held to by `crates/kernel/tests/loft_sheet.rs` › `the_side_a_sheet_faces_follows_the_sketches_not_ids_order_or_drawing` (as drawn, in reverse order, the first chain drawn backwards, both backwards in reverse order, ids swapped: the same block under the sheet, at three scales and turned off every axis; fails before) and `sections_whose_sketches_disagree_on_down_are_refused`.
- *The cap.* A loft took at most 64 sections (a DEM of 71 rows fits 0.75 m rms where 64 fit 0.81 m). Measured on a terrain of sections with a spline of 51 control points (debug build, this machine under load): 64 sections loft and are checked in 1.2 s and 45 MB, 128 in 2.0 s and 95 MB, 256 in 6.2 s and 200 MB, and a block cut out of the 256 in 9 s more (224 MB in all); 512 are refused by `geom::refine::interpolation`, whose matrix is dense and which takes at most `MAX_INTERPOLATED` = 256 points (the closed loft's `periodic_tangents` the same). More would take a banded interpolation (as `refine::MAX_INTERPOLATED_BANDED` for curves) and a band of the surface per stretch of sections; not needed by the showcase.
- *Held to* (`crates/kernel/tests/loft_sheet.rs`, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6): the open chain of curves in any order and direction, and every refusal; a loft of two open polylines, two flat faces of closed-form area, one boundary loop; a block cut by a ruled loft of quadratic Bézier sections, the volume under it in closed form (linear along the loft, quadratic across, so Simpson's rule is exact; 1e-9 relative) and over it the rest; a smooth loft through three sections, `vol(below) + vol(above) = vol(block)` and `area(S ∩ A) + area(S ∖ A) = area(S)`; a section drawn backwards walked the right way; 256 sections lofted, 257 refused. `crates/engine/tests/garabit_findings.rs`: the showcase's reproducer as a sheet through three sections cutting a block, both sides, `document.check` deep clean. **Lofts tangent where their sections are (2026-10-15, M9; the bike's finding B1).** A tube lofted from an oval of four tangent arcs into a circle had its side faces meet at a crease between the sections (up to 1.6° with two sections, more with three), though every section is smooth at every vertex: a weld round it stopped at the first rail. Both side faces of a rail are Σₛ cₛ(v) Hₛ(u) in homogeneous coordinates (the sections' curves of the column, and the same functions of v for every column), with weight 1 at the curves' ends. Across the rail the face before has the tangent Σ cₛ (Dₛ + W′ₛ (Pₛ − S)) along u and the face after Σ cₛ (Eₛ + V′ₛ (Pₛ − S)), with D and E the sections' derivatives before and after the vertex Pₛ, W′ and V′ their weights' and S the point of the rail. With Eₛ = λₛ Dₛ (tangent sections) the two faces are tangent all along the rail exactly when λₛ is the same in every section and V′ₛ − λ W′ₛ is too (the second sum then vanishes, Σ cₛ (Pₛ − S) being zero; between two sections Pₛ − S lies along the rail anyway). The oval's arcs leave a vertex at 5.07 and 0.197 times the speed they arrive at, the circle's quarter arcs at 1: the crease. The same holds inside a face, at the knot of an arc of more than a quarter turn (two rational pieces whose weight falls into the knot and rises out of it): there D = E and W′ jumps, a crease of up to 3.7° through three sections.
- *Built so* (`loft::tangent_columns`), keeping every curve as it is, and only where the faces would crease: (1) a joint where two of the sections would make the faces meet at more than the angular tolerance (the angle between the faces' tangents (1 − t) Dₛ + t Dᵣ and (1 − t) Eₛ + t Eᵣ, the 31 values k / 32 of t inside (0, 1), every pair of sections) gets every section's λ made the geometric mean of theirs, by running a piece's parameter through a polynomial composed exactly: a quadratic (`geom::refine::reparametrized_at`, twice the degree) on the piece after or before the joint whose other end is a corner (its ratio there free), or the cubic keeping that end where the factor is 2 or more, otherwise half the factor on each side by Hermite's cubic keeping the other end (`reparametrized`, three times the degree; φ′ has to stay positive, a piece whose map would not is left as it is). (2) Through three or more sections, a column one of whose curves is rational and whose weights' slopes at its tangent joints or turns at its inner knots differ between the sections (beyond rounding, 1e-12) gets level weights in every curve (`geom::refine::level_weights`: each Bézier piece times the cubic that is 1 at its ends and cancels the weight's slope there, three degrees more), so V′ = W′ = 0. A column that is a cylinder or a cone (arcs about one axis in a band of two sections, the only bands `round` is asked about) is never changed, nor its joints. **Sweep along a path (2026-10-14, M9).** `doc::Sweep { sketch, path }` is the solid the one closed profile of a sketch sweeps out as it is carried along the curves of another sketch, staying square to them (`feature.sweep`; `kernel::sweep`, `regen/src/sweep.rs`). The profile may have holes (a pipe with its bore); the path is in a plane.
- *The path* is every curve of its sketch that is not construction: lines, arcs and splines that meet end to end into one chain, open or closed, or one circle. A spline with corners (a polyline) is the pieces between them. Where two curves meet they are tangent or, two straight lines only, at a corner, which is mitred: both sides end on the plane that bisects the corner. The order and the direction the curves were drawn in do not matter.
- *Kinks* (2026-10-15). Two curves that meet a little off tangent (a path from rounded coordinates) count as tangent when the profile can be turned through the kink within the tolerance; between two lines only within `sweep::SMOOTH` (1e-6 rad), since a mitre is exact. The exact sweep through a corner turns the profile about the path's binormal there, from square to the piece before to square to the piece after. Turning it through a small angle `a` moves each point of it along the path, which is along the faces either piece sweeps, to first order; off those faces it moves by `reach (1 - cos a)` at most (`reach`: how far the profile reaches to the side of the path in its plane). That second-order movement is the test: at most half the linear tolerance (`kink_movement`). The first-order `reach sin a` is not a distance any point of the body is off (the turbofan's tube has a 2.9e-6 rad kink across a profile of radius 5: 1.5e-5 first order, 2e-11 second). How the kink is absorbed, deterministically and with every piece an exact sweep: the profile stays square to the piece before the kink until a line lays it square again. A line before a kink ends square to the piece after it (its end ring is the profile slid along the line onto that plane and normalised, `reach (1 - cos)` in towards the path), so the next piece starts square. A line after a kink slides the profile as it arrives, which is an exact prism. An arc after a kink carries the profile round its own axis (the true centre, not one taken from the arriving station) turned as it arrives: its faces are surfaces of revolution of the turned curves, and a point `q` towards the axis is then `radius q (1 - cos) / (radius - q)` off the square sweep (`tilted_turn`; B-spline surfaces of revolution are that exactly, tori, cylinders and cones are put through the turned curve's centre or ends); its rails start at each vertex's own angle. Kinks before arcs add up until a line squares the profile; that sum is checked against half the tolerance at each arc and at each line's end. A spline after a kink starts from the turned profile and is held to half the tolerance of the true sweep by its own measure. A path that ends on an arc still turned by a kink is refused unless its end cap is within half the tolerance of square (`reach sin a`, first order, since the cap is a face of its own). A closed path whose last piece is a curve and whose first is a line starts with the profile square to the last piece, so it closes on itself. Beyond the tolerance a kink is a corner as before: mitred between two lines, refused otherwise, with the angle and the curves. *Measured against the exact sweep* (2026-10-15, `crates/kernel/tests/sweep_kinks_measured.rs`, independently of the construction): for line→arc, arc→line, arc→arc and arc→spline kinks, round and rectangular profiles, at scales 1e-3, 1 and 1e6, at the largest kink the sweep admits (found by bisection on `sweep` itself: the rule's own for three of them, 0.894 of it for arc→arc, where `tilted_turn` asks for less; a kink 1 % larger is refused), points of the B-rep faces (placed by the mesh, then projected onto the face's own surface and kept on the face's side of its edges) and of every edge are at most half the tolerance from the exact sweep (the profile carried square along the drawn pieces and turned about the vertex at each kink, in closed form: the points within `r` of the path for a circle, within `w` in plan and `h` in height for a rectangle), and every point of the exact sweep's boundary is at most half the tolerance from the body (an upper bound: along the exact normal, the farthest crossing with a face's surface that lies on that face). Both ways 0.500 tol to four digits, at every scale; the measure sees a mitre of 0.02 rad as the `reach (1/cos(a/2) - 1)` it is, to 2 %.
- *Where the profile sits.* The path has to pass through the profile's plane at a right angle: an open path at one of its ends (the one on the plane; of two, the one nearer the centre of the profile), which is where the sweep starts; a closed path anywhere (of several crossings the one nearest the centre of the profile). The profile need not be centred on the path nor contain it: each of its points keeps its place in the frame the path carries (the path's direction, its normal to the left in its plane, the normal of that plane; a path in a plane has no twist to choose). A profile whose plane leans off square is laid into the plane that is square (2026-10-15), by a rigid motion (the sketch's origin to its foot on that plane, its x axis to the direction of its shadow there), when that moves no point of the profile by more than the document's linear tolerance; the movement is measured exactly at the corners of the profile's bounding box, which bound it because the motion is affine. The body is then the exact sweep of the laid-in profile, valid as any other, and since every station carries the profile rigidly no point of it is farther than that movement from the sweep of the profile as drawn. A larger lean is refused with the angle and the movement (at the default tolerance of 1e-6 a profile of radius 5 may lean about 2e-7 rad). A lean whose cause is an arc of a `sketch.path` with an end off its circle no longer arises when that end is given as coordinates within 1 % of the radius: the command puts it on the circle (`snapped`); otherwise it reports it (`warnings`).
- *Pieces.* One body, not a union: along a line the profile slides, along an arc it turns about the arc's axis, along a spline it follows the spline's frames, and the pieces share the profile's edges where they meet. A line of the profile sweeps a plane along a line of the path, and a cylinder (square to the path's plane) or a cone (slanted) round an arc; an arc or a circle sweeps a cylinder and a torus (a rational surface of revolution when its circle reaches the arc's axis: a torus would be a spindle); a spline sweeps a B-spline surface along a line and a rational B-spline surface of revolution round an arc. All of that is exact. **A line of the profile that is parallel to the path's plane sweeps one plane along the whole path, whatever the path does: that is one face** (with two loops on a closed path), not a face per piece. A profile that is one circle has no seam along lines and arcs: its faces are bounded by the circles (ellipses at a mitre) between the pieces alone, as an extruded cylinder is. A circle that is the whole path is two half turns, and a spline that closes on itself two pieces, so that no face goes all the way round and meets itself.
- *Along a spline* the swept surface is not a B-spline surface, and it is approximated within the document's tolerance. Each polynomial span of the path (the true surface is only once differentiable where spans meet) is followed in one or more parts; along each part the surface is one polynomial of degree 5, 7 or 9 through the profile placed in the exact frames of the path at the ends of the part and at the Chebyshev points between them (`geom::refine::interpolation` and the loft's skinning). Degrees and parts are raised until every surface is within **half the linear tolerance** of the true sweep, measured at three places between each two stations in a row: a point of the surface there is compared with the point of the profile at the same parameter, placed in the exact frame of the path whose plane contains it. What was reached is on the feature (`FeatureResult::sweep`: `deviation`, `stations`, `length`, `closed`; `document.inspect` and the result of `feature.sweep` show it under `path`). The ends of a spline piece are exact frames, so caps and neighbouring pieces are exact. A whole circle of the profile is four quarter arcs when the path has a spline in it. (The first attempt was a cubic through evenly spaced stations, which is what a loft is: its faces had a knot line per station, and `topo::mass_properties` gave up on some of them, "a boundary integral that does not settle". The S-shaped spline of the tests, three spans, is followed through 43 stations at a tolerance of a millionth of its size: two parts of degree 7 per span.)
- *Soundness.* Refused in the kernel before anything is built, with the curves of the path named as the sketch names them: a bend whose axis the profile reaches (`the bend e6 is too tight for the profile: its radius is …, and the profile reaches … towards its centre`); a spline whose radius of curvature comes within 2 % of the profile's reach towards the centre of curvature (sampled at 64 places per span; the message has the place and the radius); a mitre so sharp on a line so short that the mitres at its two ends would cross within the profile (the corner is named); a corner at an arc or a spline; a path that turns straight back; a path that branches or is in several parts; a path that does not start on the profile's plane or crosses it at a slant; a closed path that does not close when it is followed. Then everything `topo::check` knows. A sweep whose path comes back near itself passes through itself without folding anywhere: regeneration runs the same self-intersection check as for a loft (`regen/src/selfcheck.rs`, shared by both) and fails the feature with the two faces that cross.
- *Names.* A side is `F9:side(e3,e12)`: the curve of the profile, then the curve of the path. The one face of a line that is parallel to the path's plane is `F9:side(e3)`. The ends of an open path are `F9:startCap(…)` and `F9:endCap(…)` after the curves of the profile's outer loop. Where the same two curves give several faces (a circle that is the whole path, a polyline's pieces, a quartered circle along a spline, a spline cut at its corners) the later ones are numbered in the order they are made, piece of the path by piece and round the profile within one: `F9:piece(1)/F9:side(e3,e12)`. Edges and vertices are named by the faces they lie on, as for a loft.
- *Failures.* A profile's sketch that is missing, suppressed, later in the history or failed fails the sweep with `sketch: …`, the path's with `path: …`, each with `causedBy`; so does a sketch with no closed profile or with several. `feature.edit` takes `sketch` and `path`.
- *Not built.* A path that is not in one plane other than a helix (below; a 3D polyline or spline needs a rule for how the profile turns about the path: rotation-minimising frames, [`decisions.md`](../decisions.md)), a profile that is not square to the path, several profiles at once, twist and scale along the path, guide curves, a corner at an arc (it would not be flat), and recognising a spline that happens to be a line or an arc.

**Helix, and the sweep along it (2026-10-14, M9).** `doc::DatumHelix { axis, start, pitch, extent, left_handed }` is a datum curve (`datum.helix`; `kernel::helix`, `regen/src/datum.rs`): about an axis (`AxisRef`: a world axis, an edge between two faces, a datum axis), starting at a radius level with the axis's own point (towards the first of the world's x, y and z axes that is least along the axis) or at a point off the axis (`HelixStart`), advancing `pitch` per turn for `turns` or a `height` (`HelixExtent`), counter-clockwise seen from the tip of the axis unless left-handed. Its result is `Datum::Helix(kernel::helix::Helix)` (origin on the axis level with the start, unit axis, unit start direction, radius, pitch, turns, handedness); `datum.helix` and `document.inspect` show it with its closed-form length `turns · √((2πr)² + p²)`. The viewport draws it as a curve, as a spline of a sketch (`scene.rs` › `scene_helix`, chords within the scene's tolerance). A helix is not a rational curve (a rational curve is algebraic, and a whole helix meets a plane through its axis infinitely often), and `geom` has no helix kind: the kinds are the boolean's, the tessellator's and STEP's to know, and none of them needs one. Where a curve is needed (`Helix::curve`) it is a polynomial B-spline in parts of at most a quarter turn, each a polynomial of degree 5, 7 or 9 through the helix at the part's ends and at the Chebyshev points between; the advance along the axis is linear in the angle and reproduced exactly, so the distance to the helix is the radius times the same polynomial's distance to the unit circle (`helix::circle_error`, measured once per plan: 2.0e-5 for quarter turns of degree 5, 5.5e-8 of degree 7, 9.5e-11 of degree 9), and the plan is the cheapest that keeps that within a quarter of the tolerance.
- *The sweep along a helix* is `feature.sweep` with the helix as its `path` (`doc::Sweep` unchanged: `path` is a sketch or a datum helix; `kernel::helix::sweep_helix`). **The profile is carried by the helix's screw motion:** it turns about the axis and advances along it, a pitch per turn, as a rigid body. That is the helix's own frame that keeps one axis pointing at the helix's axis (the Frenet frame of a helix does, and is carried by the same motion), not a rotation-minimising frame, which turns against it by the torsion times the length: a thread's tooth drawn in a plane through the axis would leave that plane and the thread would not be the same after a turn. The profile lies in any plane through the start of the helix that the motion crosses everywhere: through the axis (a thread), square to the helix (a spring's wire), square to the axis (a flight). Every turn is the first moved along the axis by the pitch.
- *Faces.* Each curve of the profile sweeps one face per half turn (a whole circle of the profile is four quarters first), the later ones numbered as pieces in the order they are made (`F9:side(e3)`, `F9:piece(1)/F9:side(e3)`, …); the ends are the profile at the start and where the motion leaves it, both flat (`F9:startCap(…)`, `F9:endCap(…)`). A line of the profile parallel to the axis sweeps a cylinder, exactly. Every other face is a B-spline surface through the profile placed at stations along the half turn by the screw motion, with the band of the sweep along a spline (`sweep::part_band`): two or more parts per half turn, of degree 5, 7 or 9, starting from the plan the profile's reach needs and raised until the surfaces lie within **half the linear tolerance** of the true sweep, measured at three places between each two stations and nine across the profile against the point the screw motion puts there. Since the motion is rigid and every column curve has the same knots and weights, the distance is the reach from the axis times the plan's error on the unit circle, so it is predicted and then checked: the spring of the tests (wire 1 on radius 10) is 1.0e-9 off at a tolerance of 1e-6, with degree 9 in quarter turns (91 stations over two and a half turns), and its volume is the closed form to 4e-13. `FeatureResult::sweep` has the deviation, the length of the helix and the stations.
- *Run-out.* `kernel::helix::RunOut { angle, rise }` carries the profile on past the end of the helix while it moves away from the axis by a rise that grows with the square of the angle (it leaves the helix tangentially); used by the thread. A negative rise moves it towards the axis (an internal thread's groove running out into the hole, 2026-10-15); one that would bring the profile to the axis is refused.
- *Soundness.* Refused in the kernel before anything is built: a profile that reaches the axis, or round it (more than a quarter turn from the start, seen along the axis: a profile across the axis or behind it); a profile whose plane the motion runs along somewhere (the speed of the motion across the plane is affine in the point, so its sign is checked on the profile's boundary; the sweep would fold); a pitch not longer than the profile is along the axis as the motion sees it (the extent of `z − (p/2π) φ` over the profile: one turn would run into the next); a helix that does not start on the profile's plane (within the tolerance). Then `topo::check`, and regeneration's self-intersection check as for every sweep.
- *Volume.* The swept volume is `θ · A · n·v(c)`: the speed of the motion across the profile's plane is affine in the point, so its mean over the profile is its value at the centre of area. For a profile in a plane through the axis that is Pappus (`A · 2π x̄ · turns`), for one square to the helix with its centre on it the area times the helix's length, exactly and for any size of wire as long as the sweep does not pass through itself. These are the tests' closed forms.

**Thread (2026-10-14, M9).** `doc::Thread { face, start, size: Option<ThreadSize>, pitch: Option<Expr>, length, left_handed, modeled }` puts an ISO metric thread on the cylindrical face `face` (external on a shank; internal in a hole since 2026-10-15, below) of the body of an earlier feature, which goes into the thread: cosmetic by default (the face is marked with it), cut into the body when `modeled` (since 2026-10-14; before, a thread was cut unless `cosmetic`) (`feature.thread`; `kernel::thread`, `regen/src/thread.rs`).
- *Where.* `face` and `start` may be named as an earlier body had them and are followed to its newest form (*References that follow a body*). On the axis of the face, from where the axis passes through the flat face `start` (square to the axis at an end of the cylinder: the end of a shank, with nothing of the body beyond it) along the cylinder for `length` at full depth, then a run-out of a quarter turn.
- *Size and pitch, found at every regeneration* (`regen/src/thread.rs` › `resolve`), so that a thread on a shank made from a variable follows it. `size` is one of three: `ThreadSize::Designation` (`M10`: ISO 261, `M` and the nominal diameter in millimetres, whatever the document's unit); `ThreadSize::Diameter` (the nominal diameter as an expression of a length, `d`; named by the table's designation when it is one of the table's sizes within the tolerance, else `M` and its millimetres, `M9`); or `None`, the size of the table (`kernel::thread::iso_261_sizes`, every size of ISO 261 from M1 to M68, those with fine pitches only included) that would take the face if it were given (the margins below), the nearest when two would; a face no size takes fails naming its diameter and the sizes of the table on either side (`size: F2:face(0) is 9 mm across, which is the nominal diameter of no ISO metric size of the table (the nearest: M8 (8 mm), M10 (10 mm)); give `size` …`). `pitch` is the expression given, which stays when the size changes, or `None`: the coarse pitch of the table for the size, looked up again each time (a size without one, `M15`, fails asking for `pitch` and naming its fine pitches, `kernel::thread::fine_pitches`; the coarse table, `iso_sizes`, has every size of ISO 261 that has a coarse pitch, M1.1, M2.2, M4.5, M7, M9, M11, … since 2026-10-15). The command reads `size` as a designation when the text is one (`M` and a number), refuses a text that starts like one but is none (`M10x1.25`), and takes any other text as an expression; a number is refused (it would be in the document's unit, a designation is in millimetres). `ThreadReport` has the designation found and `pitch_given`. The dependencies are the usual ones: the face's body, and the variables of `size` and `pitch` when they are expressions (`FeatureKind::exprs`).
- *The profile* is the basic one of ISO 68-1 for the size: 60° flanks, `H = √3/2 P`, the crest flat `P/8` at the major diameter `D`, the root flat `P/4` at the minor diameter `D − 5H/4 = D − 1.0825 P`. Its groove (the trapezoid between two teeth) is carried on past the major diameter by `H/16`, so that it breaks through the shank's surface and stays narrower than the pitch (`15P/16`). The face has to be of the nominal diameter, within half the tooth's depth below it and `H/32` above it.
- *How.* The groove is swept along a helix at the minor radius by the screw motion, starting a pitch before the start plane (outside the material) and lifting out of the material over the run-out (the root rises `5H/8 + H/8`), and subtracted from the body by the boolean. The root is a cylinder; the flanks are B-spline surfaces within half the tolerance; the boolean meets them with the shank's cylinder, the lead chamfer's cone and the end face. An M10 shank of 20 with 12 of thread: 55 faces, 4 s for the boolean.
- *Cosmetic, the default.* Without `modeled` the body is the source body, every face, edge and vertex a copy; the thread is only said: `FeatureResult::thread` (`ThreadReport`: size, major and minor diameter, pitch, whether the pitch was given, length, handedness, cosmetic, the groove's deviation), shown by `feature.thread`, `feature.edit` and `document.inspect` under `thread`. It is not exported to STEP (AP214 has no clean entity for it that FreeCAD reads; a property on the face would be a convention of our own).
- *Names.* The faces left of the body are copies, instance 0 (`F9:copy(0)/F2:side(e7)`); the groove's faces are the thread's own, by the new name role `thread`: `F9:thread(0)` the root, `F9:thread(1)` the flank that faces the start, `F9:thread(2)` the flank that faces away, one per half turn, the later ones pieces (`F9:piece(3)/F9:thread(1)`). The groove starts outside the material, so its first pieces are not left (an M10 × 12 thread starts at `F9:piece(1)/F9:thread(2)` and `F9:piece(2)/F9:thread(0)`). Edges and vertices are named by their faces, as after a boolean.
- *Checked.* The removed volume is the closed form to 8e-7 mm³ in 164 mm³, the minor diameter by a section to the bit; a screw whose sizes all follow a variable `d` is driven through d = 6, 8, 10, 12 mm with its thread following (`crates/engine/tests/thread_follows.rs`).
- *Failures.* A face that is not a cylinder, not of the size's diameter, a start that is not flat, not square to the axis or not at an end of the cylinder, a thread whose length and run-out are longer than the cylinder, a pitch of 0 or of the diameter or more, a size that is no designation and no length, a face of no size of the table when no size is given, a size without a coarse pitch when no pitch is given: each fails the feature with the parameter. `feature.edit` takes `size`, `pitch`, `length`, `leftHanded` and `modeled` (or `cosmetic`, its opposite, the name of before; both given and disagreeing is refused); `size: null` goes back to the face's size, `pitch: null` to the coarse pitch.
- *Internal threads* (2026-10-15). A cylindrical face whose material is outside it (the face's normal, with its `reversed`, points at the axis) is a hole, and its thread internal: no field of its own, so no format change. A hole takes a size from the minor diameter less half the groove's overshoot up to the tap drill's diameter `D − P` and an eighth of a pitch (M10: 8.33 to 8.69 mm); left without a size, the size it fits whose tap drill it is nearest. The groove is the nut's basic profile of ISO 68-1 (`IsoThread::internal_groove`): its root a flat `P/8` wide at the major diameter, its flanks `3P/4` apart at the minor diameter, carried `H/16` inside it to break through the wall; swept along a helix at the major radius, it runs out towards the axis (rise `−(5H/8 + H/8)`) and is subtracted from the body. `ThreadReport::internal`; `feature.thread` reports `internal` and `tapDrill`. Checked: the volume removed against the flux integral of the groove outside the hole (M10 in its tap drill, M6 in its minor diameter: to 2e-6 mm³ in 108 and 48), the root at the major diameter by a section, cosmetic and modeled through the commands with the deep check (`crates/kernel/tests/helix.rs`, `crates/engine/tests/thread.rs`).
- *Not built.* A hole feature (drill, thread, depth in one), other standards (UNC/UNF, pipe threads, trapezoidal), multi-start threads, a thread that ends against a shoulder without running out (the groove would need an end of its own), tolerance classes (6g: the basic profile is cut), the thread drawn as such in the viewport for a cosmetic thread.

**Extrude ends (2026-10-13, M8.5).** `doc::Extrude` has, besides `sketch`, `distance` and `symmetric`, an `end` (`ExtrudeEnd`: `Distance`, `ThroughAll`, `UpToFace { face: PersistentName }`) and `reversed` (against the sketch's normal; for a distance the same as negating it). `feature.extrude` takes one of `distance`, `throughAll` and `upTo`, with `symmetric` and `reversed`; `feature.edit` changes the end by giving the new one.

- *Symmetric:* `distance` is the whole length, half to each side of the sketch plane.
- *Through all* is decided in `regen` (`regen/src/extrude.rs`, `through_all`), from boxes, without a boolean. "All" is every body the features before this one have made that is still a body of the document at that point of the history (not taken by a boolean, a transform or a pattern before it) and that lies in the way: its box (`Body::bbox`, conservative), seen along the sketch's normal, overlaps the box of the profiles. The extrusion runs from the sketch plane to past the farthest such box by a margin (a hundredth of the way, at least a thousand tolerances), so that a cut never ends flush with the far side; `symmetric` runs from before the nearest box to past the farthest. With nothing in the way the feature fails and says so (`there is nothing to go through along the sketch's normal: none of the 2 bodies built before this feature lies in the way of the profile`). The length is not stored: it follows the bodies. The regeneration cache knows that such a feature depends on every feature before it (`FeatureKind::uses_all_earlier`).
- *Up to a face* is `kernel::extrude_to::extrude_up_to`. The profiles are extruded past the face's surface, a *tool* solid is built that has that surface as one of its faces (a block behind a plane; the solid cylinder, ball or ring of a cylinder, sphere or torus; for a polynomial Bézier or B-spline face the solid behind the whole patch, with a wall under each of its borders), and the kernel's boolean takes the tool away (or, from a sketch plane inside a ball or a cylinder, keeps what is inside it). What hangs on to the start caps is the result; what comes out on the other side of a cylinder is dropped. So the end lies on the face's *surface*: where the profile hangs over the face's edge it ends on the plane, cylinder, sphere or torus continued, and a freeform surface ends where its patch ends. The profile has to reach the face itself somewhere (looked at on nine points per edge of the end and of the face; the module documentation says what that cannot see). The reference is the face's persistent name, resolved against the body of the feature the name starts with, as it is at that point (a copy name such as `F3:copy(1)/F2:face(1)` against the boolean's body); pieces of one face count as the face, and a cap whose outline changed still names its surface, as for a sketch's plane. A name that points at nothing fails the feature with the name and the faces that are there (``upTo: `Part` no longer has a face F3:copy(1)/F2:face(1); its faces are: …``); it never lands on another face, and the features that do not depend on it are built. Pointing `upTo` at a face that is there repairs it.
- *Names.* The faces are named as a plain extrusion names them: `startCap` in the sketch plane, `side(e7)`, and `endCap` (with the outline of its profile) for the faces on the target surface, which need not be flat, and can be several faces with one name when the boolean left the end in pieces. Edges and vertices get their plain names too (`endEdge(e7)` where wall `e7` meets the surface) whenever each can be told; when one cannot, the body has no edge and vertex names, as after any boolean.
- *Refused, each with its reason:* a face parallel to the direction (or a freeform patch that turns away from it somewhere); a face behind the sketch plane (`reversed` turns the extrusion round; the direction is never guessed, so that an edit which moves the face to the other side fails instead of flipping the body); a surface that passes through the sketch plane inside the profile; a profile part of which passes beside the surface or beyond the patch; a profile that misses the face; `symmetric` with `upTo`. **Not built:** a cone face and a rational freeform face as the end (`Unsupported`); a second end on the other side (from a face up to a face); an offset from the face. Whatever the boolean refuses is the feature's failure, with the boolean's reason.

Measured (`crates/kernel/tests/extrude_to.rs`, `crates/engine/tests/extrude_ends.rs`, scales 1e-3, 1 and 1e6, world and tilted frames): a round profile up to an inclined plane has the volume of a cylinder of the mean height and an elliptic end of area πa²/cos θ; a rectangle up to a lying cylinder and a round profile up to a ball (from outside and from inside) have their closed forms to 1e-9; a rectangle up to a parabolic B-spline wall has the area under the parabola times its height, and the arc length in its area, to 1e-7.

**Sweep along two rails (2026-11-03, R1).** `doc::Sweep` has `rail: Option<FeatureId>` and `scale: SweepScale` (`Uniform`, the default, or `Width`; format 66, an identity migration): with a rail, `feature.sweep` carries the profile so that two of its points follow `path` and `rail` (`kernel::sweep2`, `regen/src/sweep.rs` › `two_rails`).
- *Rails and stations.* Each rail is one open chain of a sketch of its own (`profile::open_chain`; closed rails: *Rings* below), so the two may lie in different planes; each is walked from the end that touches the profile's outline (within seven eighths of the tolerance, by `spline2::distance_to`, an upper bound of the distance; else refused naming the rail's curve). A station is `σ` in [0, 1]: each piece of a rail takes the share of `σ` its length has (lines and arcs exactly, a spline piece by eight-point Gauss–Legendre in four panels per span, deterministic), and runs by arc length along a line or an arc, by its own parameter along a spline. That choice keeps every bound below free of a spline's speed from below: the point moves at most `speed` and turns at most `bend` per unit of `σ`, both bounded from the hodographs' control points. Legs are the stretches of `σ` along which each rail is on one piece; joints of the two rails within 1e-12 of `σ` are one (the piece continued analytically by that much), joints closer than four tolerances along the rails but not one are refused naming both.
- *Placement.* `a`, `b`: the rails' starts in the profile's sketch; the profile's axes have `a` at the origin and `b` on the x axis (a reflection when the up direction points the other way, as the helix's sweep does). At `σ`: `O = A(σ)`, `U = (B − A) / |b − a|`, `B = w s` (uniform; `s = |B − A| / |b − a|`) or `w` (width only), `w` the up direction (the first rail's sketch normal, turned round when the rails leave the profile against it) made square to the chord. The profile is laid into the plane of the chord and up at the start when that moves it by at most the tolerance (measured at the corners of its box, the movement being affine), as the one-rail sweep lays in a lean; more is refused.
- *Surfaces and the proof.* A leg along which both rails are lines whose ends differ by the same vector (within `tol/64` of what it moves the profile, with what the station before left over) and the profile is of its own size is a translation (`Motion::Slide`): planes and exact cylinders. Every other leg is `Motion::Follow` through stations at the Chebyshev points of parts of degree 5, 7 or 9 (the band of the sweep along a spline), plans tried in order up to 32 parts of degree 9. Since every column curve is the profile's curve under the affine map of its station, with the same knots and weights, the skinned surface is `O*(v) + U*(v) x(u) + B*(v) y(u)` with `O*`, `U*`, `B*` the band's polynomials through the stations' placements, and its distance from the true sweep is at most `|ΔO| + X|ΔU| + Y|ΔB|` (`X`, `Y` the largest `|x|`, `|y|` of the profile's control points, a convex hull bound). On each sample interval of width `h` a vector function with `|Δ''| ≤ M` is within `M h²/8` of the chord of its end values; `M` is the polynomial's (its Bézier second differences) plus the true placement's: `|A''| ≤ bend`, `|c''| ≤ bend_A + bend_B`, and through the chord's length `r ≥ r_lo` and the sine `g ≥ g_lo` between chord and up, `|e''| ≤ |c''|/r + 3|c'|²/r²`, `|w''| ≤ |g''|/g + 3|g'|²/g²` with `|g'| ≤ 2|e'|`, `|g''| ≤ 2|e''| + 2|e'|²`, the scale's `s'' ≤ (|c''| + |c'|²/r)/|b − a|` (`leg_bounds`). `r_lo` and `g_lo` are the least sampled values less their Lipschitz constant times half the step, on a grid refined by four up to 2¹⁸ points; `r_lo` is also at least the rails' least distance. `h` is chosen so that `M h²/8 ≤ tol/8`; the bound is the largest sampled value plus that plus `256 ε` times the size, and a plan is taken when it is at most `tol/2` (a quick look at three places between stations skips plans that are plainly too coarse). A plan whose proof would take more than 2²⁰ samples is passed over for the next; when every plan is too coarse or too costly the leg is refused (give a coarser tolerance), and the sweep spends at most 2²³ samples in all (proofs and the grids of `leg_bounds`), refused past that naming the leg's curves and where it starts. *Rounding:* the sums of the band and the surface's evaluation are a few dozen operations each off by half a unit in the last place of the sweep's size, bounded by `256 ε · size`; a sweep is refused when its tolerance is below `4096 ε · size` (`FINEST`; size: the rails' hull of ends, centres and radii and control points, plus the profile's reach times the largest scale), so that term is at most a sixteenth of the tolerance. Below that ratio rounding is not bounded, and nothing is built. What was reached is `Swept::deviation`, shown under `path`: the bound on the spline faces from the sweep of the profile as laid in. Two things are not in it: a face whose control points lie within an eighth of the tolerance of a plane is that plane, and a rail edge within an eighth of a line that line (`flat_or`, `straight`, as for every sweep), and a profile laid in moves by at most the tolerance (measured, refused beyond).
- *Refused, before anything is built, with the curves:* rails that meet or cross anywhere (`rails_apart`: a branch and bound over pairs of places, each box of pairs judged by the distance at its centre less `(rate_A w_σ + rate_B w_τ)/2`; a box is done when that lower bound is at least half the distance, a centre within four tolerances is a meeting, and more than 2²⁰ boxes is refused as undecided; the least distance less the tolerance, since curves' ends within the tolerance are one joint without being moved onto each other, bounds the chord from below), rails that start at one point, a profile off the plane of the chord and up, a chord that turns into the up direction, and a fold: the speed `V·f` of the profile's box corners across their station's plane (`V = O' + U'x + B'y` from the rails' exact velocities, `f = e × w`), proven positive with its Lipschitz constant `|V'| + |V| |f'|` on the same grid. A circle or a closed chain as a rail is refused (an open chain is a rail). Then `topo::check`, and regeneration's self-intersection check as for every sweep.
- *Names.* `F9:side(e2,e3,e7)`: the curve of the profile, then the curves of the path and of the rail the leg runs on (nested pattern numbers moved past each other as for the one-rail sweep); `startCap(…)`, `endCap(…)` after the profile's outer loop. A side's number (`piece(n)/`) says which pieces of the rails' curves it runs between, `n = k + 4 (b + 1024 a)` with `a`, `b` the pieces (`Segment::piece`) of the path's and the rail's curve (a spline with corners is several; a curve not cut is piece 0) and `k` the faces those give (the quarters of a circle), so a leg on pieces 0 and 0 is numbered 0 to 3 as before and the leg on the path's second piece from 4096. What holds: moving points of a rail renames nothing while every curve keeps its pieces; a corner added to one rail renumbers only the legs on the later pieces of that rail's curve (pieces are counted along the curve, as for the one-rail sweep, whose numbering is unchanged); a name whose pieces are gone points at nothing, an error downstream, not another face. An edit that moves a joint of one rail past a joint of the other re-pairs the legs: the name of a pair of pieces that no longer run together points at nothing (an error downstream, not another face), and the pairs that remain keep their names.

- *Up direction (2026-11-04, format 67).* `doc::Sweep::up: Option<AxisRef>` (a world axis, the straight edge between two faces, a datum axis; `regen::datum::axis_ref`'s direction, its sense irrelevant): `kernel::sweep2::sweep_two_rails_up` takes it in place of the first rail's sketch normal, normalised, and turns it round by the same rule. A constant up changes nothing in the proof (every bound above is for a constant `up`); a direction of no length is refused, one along the chord where the rails start is refused as before, and one along the rails lays the section into their course, which the crossing speed refuses as a fold (the message says the section stands in the plane of the chord and the up direction). A profile with an arc or a circle in a loop now slides (`Motion::Slide`) only when the rails move it square to its plane: at a slant a circle sweeps an elliptic cylinder, not the round one `Slide` makes (with the sketch normal as up that happened for parallel equal lines whose chord is not square to them, which `topo::check` then refused); such a leg is followed, within the bound. Lines (planes) and splines (ruled between the two stations) slide exactly at any slant, as before: a rectangle between rails (0, 0)–(0, 30) and (10, 4)–(10, 34) is still a slide, its deviation rounding only, every face a plane and `10 h · 30` its volume (`sweep2_ring.rs` › `a_rectangle_carried_at_a_slant_…`).
- *Two proofs (2026-11-04).* Each leg's bounds are first proven as format 66 proved them (`leg_bounds` with `sharp` false: the chord's speed bounded by the rails' speeds added, the crossing speed taken at the corners of the box of the profile's reach both ways), so every body that built before is built to the bit as it was (the fixtures of format 66 hold it). Where that proves nothing (a corner of that box that does not cross, no grid fine enough, or no plan within `MAX_SAMPLES`) the sharper proof is tried: `|c'|` at most its largest value on the grid plus `|c''| h / 2`, the crossing speed at the corners of the profile's own box (the speed is affine in the point, so least at a corner of any box round the profile). Concentric rings move the chord far slower than either rail (`|B' − A'|` against `|A'| + |B'|`: 25 against 503 per unit of `σ` for radii 38 and 42), and the cruder `M` was a hundred times too large: a band 4 wide and 12 tall round them with up leaning 27° asked for more than 2²⁰ samples, and a torus of minor radius 3 round rings 7 and 13 with up leaning 0.5 rad was refused as a fold at a corner of the box that lies inside the hole; both build with the sharper proof. What the sharper proof cannot show is refused as before. Both rounds draw on the one budget of 2²³ samples per sweep: a ring of 24 legs or more whose cruder proof refines its grid to `MAX_GRID` (4 · 2¹⁸ samples at the last step alone) before it gives up can spend the budget before the sharper round has run, and is refused as out of samples.
- *Rings (2026-11-04, format 67).* `profile::chain` is `open_chain` that also accepts one closed loop (walked from the start of the curve with the smallest id; a spline that closes on itself is cut in two), and one circle is a ring of two half turns, pieces 0 and 1. Both rails open or both closed, else refused. A ring is found where it passes through the profile's plane nearest the outline (`Course::crossings`: lines and arcs in closed form, splines by 32 sign samples per span and 60 bisections), within seven eighths of the tolerance, else refused with the distance; it is travelled the way the profile's sketch normal points (refused where it runs along the plane). Stations count each ring's length from `a` and `b`; the body starts at `σ = 0`, the first joint of either ring after the profile (the profile at `σ_p = 1 − c`), so the piece the profile is on is not cut; the other ring's piece there is cut in two (`Round::opened_at`, `Course::between`: each part keeps the piece's parameter and its share of `σ`, so `σ` maps to the curve as for the whole piece). That cut piece runs first and last, beside pieces of the first ring that differ (a ring has at least two), so the names stay distinct and the scheme is the open rails'. The last leg is followed with its last station the start's (`follow`'s `last`, its gap from the exact one sampled like any other) and ends on the first ring of edges; a slide that ends there adds its gap (at most `tol/4`, else refused as a bug) to the deviation. A shell per loop of the profile, no caps, `Swept::closed`. Every leg is an ordinary patch between two rings of edges, so no face meets itself and the tessellator sees no seam.
