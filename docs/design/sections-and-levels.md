# Sections and levels

What lies on one side of a plane, for any closed bodies: the volume and centroid of the part below a plane, the area, centroid and second moments of the section by it, and the offset of a plane at which the volume below it is a given volume. A tank's fill level and the centre of mass of its liquid, a beam's cross-section, the cut of a part at a height, the plane a floating object settles at. The kernel part is `crates/kernel/src/below.rs`; the commands and the assertion measures are `crates/engine/src/below_cmds.rs` (`measure.below`, `measure.section`, `measure.levelFor`; `volumeBelow`, `centroidBelow`, `sectionArea`, `sectionCentroid`, `sectionInertia`, `levelFor`); the user's view is the guide, [chapter 10](../guide/10-measure.md#below-a-plane-sections-and-levels).

## Method

**The plane.** `{p : p · normal = offset}` with a unit `normal`; *below* is the side the normal points away from. The commands take it as `measure.symmetry` takes a plane (a base plane, a datum plane, `{point, normal}`) or as a flat face by its persistent name (its exact surface's frame, with the outward normal), moved by `offset` along the normal; the offset they return is from the plane given. The section's axes are `xAxis` (in the plane: given, which must be square to the normal within 1e-9 of the cosine, or by default the world axis least parallel to the normal, x before y before z, made square to it) and `yAxis = normal × xAxis`.

**Below the plane, exactly.** For each body, a box whose top face lies in the plane and that holds all of the body below it (wider than the body's box by a quarter of its half-diagonal on every side, and deeper) is intersected with the body by the boolean kernel. What comes back is an exact B-rep of the part below:

- its volume and centroid are those of the exact integrator (`craft3d_topo::mass_properties`: the divergence theorem over the faces, each sum held to 1e-12 of what it sums; [`topology.md`](topology.md));
- the section is the faces of the result that are pieces of the box's top face (`BooleanResult::merged`), flat faces in the plane bounded by the curves in which the plane meets the body. A piece of any other face of the box in the result is an error (the box did not hold the body), never a silent number;
- the surface below is the result's area less the section's.

A body wholly above the plane (by its extent along the normal: the held box, `Body::held_bbox`, of the body turned so that the normal is z, tight for analytic faces, so a plane tangent to a sphere along any direction meets nothing) has nothing below; one wholly below has all of itself below and no section. Extents, boxes and whole volumes are measured once per call and kept across the steps of a level search. Where the boolean finds nothing below (an empty result, or one of no volume), that answer is checked against a bound that holds every point of the body, its *floor* along the normal: the least, over its faces, of a bound for each. An analytic face is bound by its held box (`Body::held_face_bbox`) in a frame whose z is the normal, tight where the face reaches its extreme, so a sphere or a torus (which have no vertices) is bound by its own lowest point. A Bézier or NURBS face is bound by what of its surface lies in its trim, with its edges: branch and bound over pieces of the surface (boxes of its parameters) and of its edges (ranges of theirs), each piece's least along the normal proven by `craft3d_geom::support` (the convex hull of its Bézier net, positive weights). A piece of the surface is connected, so one that meets none of the face's edges (its box grown by the tolerance and the body's largest entity tolerance, how far an edge may be from the surface: the outline by which the face tells in from out is its edges' curves seen on the surface) is wholly in the face or wholly out of it, told by its middle (`Probe::on_face`), and dropped when out; every other piece is kept in full, holding every point of the face it covers (the test of the proven distance, [`verification.md`](verification.md)). The region with the least bound is cut in two (a piece of the surface across the parameter along which five of its points spread more along the normal, a surface turned about the normal so cut only along its profile; an edge's range in half) until that bound reaches the plane less the tolerance, or comes within a hundredth of the tolerance of the least point of the face seen so far (points of its edges, middles of pieces in the face), or 16 384 splits are spent, where the bound left is still a true one. So a face cut from a far larger surface is bound near where it is, not where its surface reaches; the face's least is on its boundary or at a point inside it, and both are closed in on. Where that cannot be made (a net the support pieces refuse, a body the probe cannot take) the face is bound by its whole surface, by the same branch and bound over its rational Bézier patches without the trim: sound, not tight. Edges and vertices lie on the faces, so a closed solid's faces hold all of it. Nothing below is the answer only when the floor is within the tolerance of the plane; otherwise it is an error (*not measured*, with the depth the body may reach below the plane), never a silent zero. Several bodies are summed: volumes, first moments, and the section's integrals about one common point; bodies that overlap are counted once each.

**The section's moments.** Green's theorem in the plane, with `a` along `xAxis` and `b` along `yAxis` from a point `o` of the plane:

| Integral | Boundary form |
|---|---|
| A | ∮ a db |
| ∫a dA | ∮ a²/2 db |
| ∫b dA | −∮ b²/2 da |
| ∫a² dA | ∮ a³/3 db |
| ∫ab dA | ∮ a²b/2 db |
| ∫b² dA | −∮ b³/3 da |

in two passes: the area and the first moments about the middle of the section's own box (the boxes of its faces), which give the centroid, then the second moments about the centroid itself, so a small section far from the bodies' middle (a thin rod cut near one end) loses nothing to cancellation; along every coedge of the section's faces, in the direction the loops run about the surface's normal (counter-clockwise), signed by which way that normal faces against the plane's. Per edge the panels end where its curve is not smooth (the knots of a spline; along a circle or an ellipse the first panels are a sixteenth of a turn, only as a starting point), each panel is summed with the 8- and the 16-node Gauss–Legendre rule, and a panel whose two sums differ by more than 1e-13 of the section's own size (half its box's diagonal) to the integral's power (times the panel's share of the size, plus 64 ulps of its terms) is halved; at most 40 halvings of a panel and 65,536 panels per body, past which the section is an error ("do not settle"). On a line every integrand is a polynomial of degree 3 at most and both rules are exact; on the cubic Hermite splines the boolean fits to the curve where the plane meets a surface, degree 11 per span, exact for the 8-node rule already. About the centroid, with the parallel-axis rule taking out what rounding leaves of the first moments: `ix = ∫b² − A b̄²` (about the axis along `xAxis` through the centroid), `iy = ∫a² − A ā²`, `ixy = ∫ab − A ā b̄`; the principal moments `(ix + iy)/2 ± √(((ix − iy)/2)² + ixy²)`, the major one about the axis at `θ = ½ atan2(−ixy, (ix − iy)/2)` from `xAxis` (where the two are equal every axis is principal and that gives `xAxis`).

No tessellation is integrated anywhere. The error bound is that of the B-rep: the integrals are held to 1e-12 (volume) and 1e-13 per panel (section), and the curve in which the plane meets a freeform surface lies on both within the modelling tolerance, so a section bounded by such curves is exact to the tolerance times its perimeter, and the volume to the tolerance times the surface below.

**The level for a volume.** The offset at which the volume below is the volume asked for. The volume below grows with the offset and its derivative is the section's area, so it is Newton's method on the area, inside a bracket from the bodies' extent along the normal that every measured offset narrows: a step that would leave the bracket, a section of no area, or a step after one that did not halve the residual is a halving of the bracket instead. It stops when the volume is within 1e-10 of the one asked for (`SETTLED`); where the bracket is as narrow as an `f64` makes it, the offset there is the answer only within 1e-7 (`UNSETTLED`), and otherwise "did not converge". An offset the boolean refuses (a face lying in the plane where a step landed, a configuration it cannot cut) is stepped around: the next offset is the first of the bracket's middle, its quarters and its three-eighths point (`AROUND`, as shares of the bracket) not yet refused since the last offset measured (further than 1e-9 of the bracket from those); refused at all of them, the last refusal is the error; at most 64 steps (`MAX_STEPS`: halving alone takes the bracket below an `f64`'s resolution by then), after which "did not converge" is an error with the bracket and the residual. The first offset is where the volume would be for a prism of the bodies' extent. A volume not strictly between 0 and the bodies' whole volume is refused (*outside (0, total)*). Each step is one boolean and one exact integration; on the oracles it settles in 2 to 6 steps.

**For a Jig: a floating body.** With ρ the liquid's density, a body of mass m floats with V = m/ρ below the plane square to up: `measure.levelFor {volume: V}` gives the draft T (offset) and the centre of buoyancy B (centroid), and the section is the waterplane. Then KB is B's height above the keel, BM = ix / V (ix about the axis along the length), KM = KB + BM, and GM = KM − KG for the centre of gravity G the Jig knows; heeled by φ, the plane's normal turned by φ about the length and `levelFor` again gives B at the heel, and the righting arm is GZ = (B − G) · (normal × xAxis) (Biran & López-Pulido, *Ship Hydrostatics and Stability*). `crates/engine/tests/plane_measures.rs` does exactly that for a box (GM changing sign at B/D = √(6 s (1 − s))), and the kernel's tests hold the centroid below a turned plane to the wall-sided closed form. The naval quantities themselves belong in Jigs (rule 14).

- a tube: the annulus (the inner loop a hole), A = π (R² − r²), ix = iy = π (R⁴ − r⁴)/4;
- a U: one body, two section faces, iy by the parallel-axis rule;
- a stepped block: the level for a volume of 2 is the shoulder, where Newton lands on the step's flat face, and the measure exactly there;
- a sphere cut along (1, 1, 1): its extent is its own, the tangent plane cuts nothing, a plane a tenth inside it cuts the cap;
- nothing found below, checked: a sphere (no vertices) off the origin along z and along (1, 1, 1), half a radius and twice the tolerance into it, is lost by exactly that depth (`c · n − r`), and half the tolerance into it is nothing below; a torus with its axis tilted 0.4 rad, a quarter of its tube and twice the tolerance into it, lost by its depth from `c · n − (R |n − (n · a) a| + r)`; a ring swept from a cubic spline whose bottom (−1.5, a circle on its spline face with no vertex) lies above its control net (−2): lost by the face's depth, not the net's (to a hundredth of the tolerance above), and nothing below between the net and the face, also through `below`; a prism whose side is a spline face extruded 101 high and trimmed to its top 1 (its held box, and so the level search's bracket, reaching 100 below it): nothing below at 50, at a tenth and at half the tolerance below its bottom, lost by a tenth (to the slack of the in-or-out test, the tolerance) a tenth into it, and the level for 0.3 and 0.7 of its volume 0.3 and 0.7 of its height, the search's first step landing far below it;
- a sphere cut 1e-4, 1e-2 and 0.5 of its radius above its bottom (the cap, πh²(3r − h)/3, and its circle), and a torus 1e-2, 0.5 and 1.5 of its tube above its bottom (Pappus: 2πR times the tube's circular segment, the section the annulus 4πR√(2rh − h²));
- the level search with the measure given (a volume of x² on [0, 1]): refused at the first guess, it goes on from the middle of the bracket (0.3, then 0.5, 0.55, …) and finds √0.3; refused again at the Newton step after that, it halves again (0.75) and still finds it; refused where a halving lands (after a refused first guess, or after a Newton step that did not halve the residual) it goes on from a quarter of the bracket and still finds it; refused at the first guess and at each of the four points of `AROUND` after it, the last refusal is the error; a volume that jumps by 3e-9 where it is asked for is found within `UNSETTLED` (past `SETTLED`) once the bracket is as narrow as an `f64` makes it, and by 1e-4 it is "did not converge" there;
- a box L × B × D below a level T: volume L B T, centroid T/2, area L B, ix = L B³/12, iy = B L³/12, surface below L B + 2 (L + B) T; the level for that volume back to T in at most 8 steps;
- a sphere at three levels: the spherical cap's volume, centroid 3 (2R − s)² / (4 (3R − s)) from the centre, the circle's area and πa⁴/4, the cap's area 2πR s; ix / V + the centroid's height is the centre at every level;
- a horizontal cylinder at two levels: the circular segment's area R²(θ − sin θ)/2 and centroid 4R sin³(θ/2) / (3 (θ − sin θ)), the rectangle's moments, the surface below;
- a V prism (a square prism on an edge): volume L T², centroid 2T/3, ix / V = 2T/3;
- a spline cone apex down (a surface a cubic spline sweeps about an axis): the cone's volume, centroid 3h/4, the circle's moments, the lateral area, and the level for its volume back;
- a rectangle turned in the plane: its product of inertia mixes, its principal moments come back, the major axis square to the long side;
- the box moved and turned in space, measured in a plane turned with it: the same volume, centroid height, moments and surface;
- a box under a plane turned by ±5° and ±10° about x with the same volume below: the centroid's lever about a point on its axis by the wall-sided closed form;
- two bodies apart: the sum, and ix by the parallel-axis rule;
- the errors: outside (0, total), not finite, no bodies, a sheet, directions that are zero or not square.

## Limits

- **Bodies are solids.** A sheet has no inside and is refused; an open shape must be closed first.
- **Overlapping bodies** are counted once each.
- **Every offset is a boolean.** A plane on a flat face of a body is coincident faces, which the booleans handle ([`booleans.md`](booleans.md), the table of contact): the stepped block cut exactly at its shoulder, and the level search landing there, are measured (`a_level_on_a_flat_face_of_the_body_is_found`). A body the boolean refuses at some offset gives that error with the offset; the level search steps around such offsets (up to four tries in a row).
- **A trimmed freeform face is bound by its whole surface only where its trim cannot be used** (a net past the support pieces' degree or span caps, a body the probe refuses): there a plane between the face's least and its surface's is refused (*not measured*) rather than answered: sound, not tight. A face whose least along the normal lies within a hundredth of the tolerance of where it is only by a long chain of splits (its least on an edge that meets the surface's continuation steeply, at a scale far finer than the face) may spend the 16 384 splits first; the bound left is then true but lower, and the answer an error, not a number.
- **The level search brackets from the held box**, which for a freeform face is its surface's net: a first step may land far below a body cut from a larger surface, and is then decided as nothing below by the trim (a few halvings more, not a wrong answer).
- **One plane at a time.** A turned plane (a tank tilted, a heel) is another call with another normal; a level with two unknowns (offset and tilt for a given centroid) is a Jig's loop over `levelFor`.

### Every rule that is a condition, and why it is general

- *A body wholly above or below the plane by its extent* is measured without a boolean: the held box of the turned body certainly holds it (`Body::held_bbox`), so nothing is below or all is; a shortcut with the same answer, not another method.
- *Nothing found below with the body's floor more than the tolerance below the plane* is an error: the floor is a bound that holds every point of every body (the faces' held boxes, and a freeform face's surface by branch and bound), the tolerance is the document's, and the same rule holds with vertices or without; the hundredth of the tolerance to which a freeform face's least is closed in on is relative to the same tolerance at every scale.
- *A piece of a freeform face's surface is dropped when its middle is out of the face and its box, grown by the tolerance and the body's largest entity tolerance, meets none of the face's edges*: a connected piece that does not reach the boundary is all on one side of it; the margin is the document's tolerance and the body's own, the same for every face and scale; an edge is told apart from a box by halving its enclosure 14 times at most, past which it is taken to meet it (kept, never dropped).
- *A trimmed freeform face whose trim cannot be used is bound by its whole surface*: a superset of the face, so still a bound; looser, never wrong.
- *A level search steps around a refused offset by the next point of `AROUND` (shares 0.5, 0.25, 0.75, 0.375 of the bracket) not within 1e-9 of the bracket of one refused, at most four in a row*: shares of the bracket, so the same for every body and scale; a refusal is a configuration at isolated offsets (a face in the plane, a tangency), which points spread over the bracket step past; the cap keeps a body the boolean refuses everywhere an error after five booleans, not 64.
- *The first panels of a circle or an ellipse are a sixteenth of a turn*, and those of a spline end at its knots: starting points for the same adaptive check on every curve; no curve is trusted without it.
- *A panel settles when its 8- and 16-node sums agree to 1e-13 of the size to the integral's power, in proportion to its length, plus 64 ulps of its terms*: the same for every curve and at every scale (relative to the bodies' size).
- *The level stops at a volume within 1e-10 of the one asked for* (relative), or at a bracket no `f64` can narrow when it is within 1e-7: both relative, the same for every body.
- *The section's panels are held to the section's own size*, not the bodies': relative to what is summed, at every scale.
- *The x axis must be square to the normal within 1e-9 of the cosine*: refused, not projected. *The default x axis is the world axis least parallel to the normal, made square*: a choice of axes only; the principal moments do not depend on it.
