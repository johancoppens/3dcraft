# Developable surfaces and unfolding

Surfaces a flat sheet bends onto without stretching (plywood panels, sheet metal, paper), the developable connection of two curves, a measure of how far a face is from developable, and laying a developable face or sheet flat with the map between the flat pattern and the 3D part. Roadmap: [`../roadmap.md`](../roadmap.md) › *R1* (the sheets block, 2026-10-26). Code: `crates/geom/src/curvature.rs` (second derivatives and the Gaussian curvature of any surface), `crates/kernel/src/developable.rs` (the connection), `crates/kernel/src/unfold.rs` (the development and its map), `crates/regen/src/developable.rs` (both features in regeneration), `crates/engine/src/develop_cmds.rs` (commands). Guide: [`../guide/09-features.md`](../guide/09-features.md) › *Developable surfaces and unfolding*.

## Interface (for the plates, sheet-metal and kayak work that builds on it)

This section is the contract; it changes only with a dated note under *Findings and history*.

**Geometry (`craft3d_geom::curvature`, L0).**

- `second_derivatives(surface, u, v) -> (Vec3, Vec3, Vec3)`: `S_uu`, `S_uv`, `S_vv`, exact for every kind (closed forms for the analytic kinds, the NURBS Book's derivatives of the basis functions and the rational quotient rule for Bézier and NURBS surfaces).
- `gaussian_curvature(surface, u, v) -> Option<f64>` (and `curvatures` with the mean curvature): `K = (LN − M²)/(EG − F²)`; `None` where the surface has no normal (a cone's apex, a collapsed edge of a spline).

**Connection (`craft3d_kernel::developable`, L3).**

- `Rail { pieces: Vec<RailPiece> }`: a chain of 3D curves end to end (`RailPiece { curve: Curve3, range: (f64, f64), reversed: bool, source: u64 }`, each run from its start to its end in the chain's direction; `source` is the caller's id, a sketch curve or an edge, kept on the faces' edges).
- `connect(a: &Rail, b: &Rail, tolerance: &Tolerance) -> Result<Connected, KernelError>`: the developable sheet between the two rails, `a` run as given, `b` as given or, when only that gives a developable (rails drawn in opposite directions), the other way (`Rail::reversed`); with both ways possible, as given (so a sheet that builds keeps its shape and its faces' names; review of 2026-10-27, which undid a rule that took the way of fewer faces).
- Faces named by place (2026-10-28, develop-r2; `Piece::Inner(n)`, a count along the rails, until then): `Piece::Rulings` the first run of rulings, `StartFan` and `EndFan`, `Fan(mark)` a fan at a fold or from a corner, `After(mark)` a run of rulings after a corner or after a fold counted from the start, `Before(mark)` a run after a fold counted from the end (named by the mark that follows it), `Last` the run that reaches the end when it is not the first. `Mark::FoldFromStart(n)` is the `n`-th fold (from 0) in the first half of the sheet counted from the start, `FoldFromEnd(n)` the `n`-th in the second half counted from the end (the half by the mean of the unit rail parameters of the fan's first ruling; a fold up to a millionth of the sheet past the middle still counts from the start, 2026-10-29, so that a fold at the middle, where symmetric rails put one, keeps its name under rounding: `MIDDLE_MARGIN`), `Corner { rail, source }` the corner of `a` (0) or `b` (1) before its piece `source`. So a fold that appears or goes at one end of a sheet, or between, leaves the names at the other end as they are, and the faces that hold the sheet's ends keep theirs; a fold that moves across the middle, or one more fold nearer the same end, renumbers the folds counted from that end (the limit of a count: a reference to `Fan(FoldFromEnd(0))` then names the new fold's fan, without an error; names.md's rule 4 holds for folds only as far as their count from the nearer end stays). In the document (regen) a face's name is its words: `0`, `1`, `2`, a fold's `10¹⁰ + n` (from the start) or `2·10¹⁰ + n` (from the end), a corner's `3·10¹⁰` (of `a`) or `4·10¹⁰` (of `b`) followed by the piece's source as a second word (2026-10-29: packed into one word, a source of a sketch pattern's instance `e7[2]` and up, or of a nested pattern, was refused as too large), a run after a mark `10·10¹⁰` plus the mark's first word, before one `20·10¹⁰` plus it, the last run `30·10¹⁰`.
- Rails may have corners (2026-10-28): where two pieces meet with tangents that differ by more than the angular tolerance, the sheet turns the corner by a fan from it (Method, *Corners*); a cusp (the tangent turned back) is refused. Two closed rails give a tube (Method, *Tubes*); one closed rail with an open one is refused. For a tube, `b` is run from the seam (the end on it of the ruling from `A(0)` the tube starts from: the piece there split in two), and its parameters in `rulings` are of that run.
- `Connected { body, faces: Vec<Piece>, rulings: Vec<(f64, f64)>, deviation: f64, unresolved: f64, curvature: f64 }` (`unresolved` 2026-10-29, measured since develop-r4: the largest angle found between a stored face's normal at an end ruling and the tangent plane it was built with from the rails' tangents, times the ruling's length; an estimate of what the rounding of the control points leaves unresolved, not a bound, not counted in `deviation`, and reported by the feature as `unresolved`; *The faces*): a sheet body (`BodyKind::Sheet`, one shell) of one to three faces in order: `Piece::StartFan`, `Piece::Rulings`, `Piece::EndFan` (fans only where needed, below: none that lies within the tolerance of the strip beside it, 2026-10-27); the rulings found, as rail parameters (each rail's parameter runs from 0 at its first end to the number of its pieces at its last, piece `i` over `[i, i + 1]`); the largest distance found between the faces and the true developable; and the largest |K| found on the faces.

**Unfolding (`craft3d_kernel::unfold`, L3).**

- `develop(body: &Body, faces: &[FaceId], tolerance: &Tolerance) -> Result<Development, KernelError>`: the faces, which must be connected across shared edges, laid flat as one piece in the plane (2D coordinates; where it is placed is the caller's).
- `Development::to_flat(face, p: Vec3) -> Result<[f64; 2], _>` and `to_3d(p: [f64; 2]) -> Result<(FaceId, Vec3), _>`: the map between the part and its pattern, both ways, within the reported error.
- `Development::outline() -> &[FlatCurve]`: the boundary of the pattern: per boundary edge of the faces (an edge only one of them uses), its flat image as a 2D cubic spline (`FlatCurve { edge: Option<EdgeId>, face, spline: Spline2, length_3d, length_flat, deviation }`; `edge` is `None` for the cut along which a band round a cylinder or cone is opened), with its length on the part and in the pattern and how far the spline was found from the mapped edge.
- `Development::flat_body(frame: &Frame, tolerance) -> Result<(OpResult, Distortion), KernelError>`: the pattern as one flat sheet face in the plane of `frame` (its x, y; normal its z, the side the faces' outward normals were on), through the profile detection and fill of a sketch, with the distortion including the change of area.
- `face_curvature(body, face) -> Result<FaceCurvature, KernelError>`: the largest |K| on the face's region, where, `radius` and `stretch` (below).
- `Development::error() -> Distortion { length, area, map }`: what the development changed: the largest relative change of an edge's length, of the area (set by `flat_body`), and the estimated error of the map in model units (the layout's convergence, within half the tolerance, or the outline splines' deviation if larger).

**Document and commands (format 39).**

- `feature.developable {a, b}`: `a` and `b` are each a sketch (its curves make one open chain) or `{"edges": [edge names, in order]}` of earlier bodies. Faces named `F9:face(0)` (the rulings), `F9:face(1)` (the fan at the start) and `F9:face(2)` (the fan at the end), each only when made; edges and vertices by their faces (the role `face` with a number, as a primitive's faces, so no new name role).
- `feature.unfold {faces: [face names], at: [x, y, z] = origin, angle = 0 deg}`: a flat sheet in the plane z = `at.z`, its first face's first vertex at `at`, that vertex's first edge along +x turned by `angle` about +z. Its one face is `F12:face(0)`; the 3D faces stay where they are.
- `measure.developability {of: faces or bodies}`: the largest |K| per face and where, and whether each is developable within the tolerance (below).
- `unfold.map {unfold: feature, points: [[x, y, z], …], to: "flat" | "part"}`: points mapped through an unfold's development, in the document's frame (the pattern where the unfold placed it); each with the face it is on.
- Assertion measures `curvature` (the largest |K| of faces, unit length⁻²) and `developable` (a flag).

## Method

### Gaussian curvature

`K = (LN − M²)/(EG − F²)` with `E = S_u·S_u`, `F = S_u·S_v`, `G = S_v·S_v` and `L = S_uu·n`, `M = S_uv·n`, `N = S_vv·n` (do Carmo §3-3). The second derivatives are exact: closed forms for planes, cylinders, cones, spheres and tori; for Bézier and NURBS surfaces the derivatives of the B-spline basis functions to order two (Piegl & Tiller, *The NURBS Book*, algorithm A2.3) applied to the homogeneous control net, then the rational quotient rule (ibid. §4.5, eq. 4.20): with `A = Σ N_i N_j w_ij P_ij` and `w = Σ N_i N_j w_ij`, `S_u = (A_u − w_u S)/w`, `S_uu = (A_uu − 2 w_u S_u − w_uu S)/w`, `S_uv = (A_uv − w_u S_v − w_v S_u − w_uv S)/w`. The closed forms are the oracles: a sphere of radius `r` has `K = 1/r²` everywhere, a torus `K = cos v / (r (R + r cos v))`.

**The largest |K| of a face** is the largest found at the points of a grid over the face's region of its surface (the box, in the surface's parameters, of its boundary's points put on the surface; on a surface that closes on itself, the boundary's parameters unwrapped along each loop), 33 × 33 points (`unfold::face_curvature`); points of the grid outside the face are kept (the surface is the face's beyond its edges, and a curvature there is the surface's). It is a sampled maximum, not a proven bound: the measure says so (`proven: false`).

**Developable within tolerance (2026-10-27).** The stretch of a face is that of laying it out from a line through its middle, in Fermi coordinates about that line (the geodesics square to it keep their lengths): the metric is `dy² + G ds²` with `∂²√G/∂y² = −K √G` (the Jacobi equation, do Carmo §4-6), and the line developed flat with its own geodesic curvature has the same `√G` but with `K = 0`. So a curve at the offset `y` changes its length, to first order in `K`, by `∫∫ (y − η) K dη ds`: at most `∫ w(s)² κ(s) / 8 ds`, with `w(s)` the face's width across the line and `κ(s)` the largest |K| across it at `s`. Laid from the middle, each half adds its own; the stretch is the larger half's, taken along each direction of the face's parameters, the smaller of the two (each estimates the same stretch). For a face of constant `K` and sides `w ≤ L` this is `|K| w² L / 16`: within 6 % of the geodesic disc's `|K| r³ / 6` (Bertrand–Diguet–Puiseux, `r` half the diagonal) for a square, which it replaces, and far less for a long narrow strip, whose stretch does not grow with the cube of its length, and a face whose curvature is found at one end is charged for that end only. A face is *developable within tolerance* when its stretch is at most `DEVELOPABLE_SHARE` (a half) of the document's linear tolerance, the share the unfold's layout must converge to. It is an estimate, not a bound: first order in `K`, from a sampled curvature, and per half (the length change of a rim over the whole face can be twice it). It serves as the flag of `measure.developability` and as the unfold's first gate, nothing more. What refuses a face that is not developable past it is the unfold's own checks: the layouts at two grids must converge (each doubling at least halves their difference, down to half the tolerance), each edge developed along itself (its own speed and geodesic curvature) must lie within half the tolerance of the map's images of it, and each edge's flat length must be within the tolerance of its 3D length. A doubly curved face fails the second before its lengths change: its rims' geodesic curvature, which the flat pattern must keep, differs from what a pattern of the face's width allows by `|K|` times the offset, first order in the width, where the lengths change only at second order (`a_thin_sphere_band_that_passes_the_stretch_gate_is_still_refused`: a band of a sphere `0.0006 R` wide passes the gate at about 0.3 of the tolerance and is refused). The change of area is reported (`Distortion::area`), not gated: for a narrow face it is of third order in the width and says less than the rims do.

### The developable connection of two curves

A ruled surface `X(s, v) = (1 − v) A(s) + v B(t(s))` between the curves `A` and `B` is developable exactly when the tangent plane is the same all along each ruling, that is when `A'(s)`, `B'(t)` and `B(t) − A(s)` are coplanar (Pottmann & Wallner, *Computational Line Geometry*, §6.1; do Carmo §3-5): `f(s, t) = det[Â'(s), B̂'(t), (B(t) − A(s))/|B(t) − A(s)|] = 0`, with unit tangents so that `f` is the sine-like volume of three unit vectors and independent of how the curves are parametrized. The connection is a path through the rectangle of `(s, t)` from `(0, 0)` to the ends `(S, T)`, increasing in both (rulings that never cross), along the zero set of `f`. Beside the rulings that satisfy `f = 0`, a *fan* is developable too: the rulings from one point of one curve to a stretch of the other are a cone with its apex at that point (Pottmann & Wallner §6.1: a cone is developable), a straight piece of the path at one `s` (apex on `A`) or one `t` (apex on `B`). A fan that starts and ends on the zero set meets the rulings beside it with the same tangent plane, so the surface is smooth across it.

1. **The start.** The first ruling that satisfies `f = 0` is searched on the two sides of the rectangle at the start: along `B` from `A(0)` (`f(0, t) = 0`) and along `A` from `B(0)` (`f(s, 0) = 0`), each by sampling and bisection; the nearer (by the length of curve the fan covers) is taken. When that fan would cover no more than the tolerance there is no fan: the first ruling is `A(0)B(0)`, as the last is snapped to `A(S)B(T)` when what is left of both rails is within the tolerance (one test at both ends). Nor is there one when the fan lies within the tolerance of the strip that would run on to the corner in its place (2026-10-27): every ruling of the fan, the corner's included (17 along its stretch), strays from a developable by at most half the tolerance, by the measure the fit holds every ruling to, the turn of the tangent plane from one end of the ruling to the other times its length (`sin` of the turn `= |det[Â', B̂', D̂]| / (|Â' × D̂| |B̂' × D̂|)`, both planes holding `D̂`). The same test at the end, unless the path ends in a fan at a fold (whose parameter snapping would change). Ends of the two curves that coincide (a panel that ends in a point) are a vertex of both; the zero set then leaves that corner along a line (`α s² = β t²` to second order), found on an arc about a hundred tolerances long; without one the fan is a cone from that point.
2. **Tracing.** From there the zero set of `f` is followed by a predictor–corrector walk, with steps of at most 1/128 of the rectangle accepted only when the corrected point is forward, within one and a half steps and turns the zero set's tangent by at most 0.2 rad (else the step is halved, so a fold of the zero set is walked into and refused, not jumped over) (tangent `(f_t, −f_s)`, oriented to increase `s` and `t`; Newton back onto `f = 0` along the gradient), with steps that keep the change of the ruling within a fraction of the tolerance.
2b. **Branches that pass each other (2026-10-28; rewritten 2026-10-29).** Where the condition has, besides the developable's own branch, another one across it (rulings from near one end of A to farther along B and back, as near both ends of the sea kayak's sheer panel, or the two branches that cross at an inflection, split), the two cross at a saddle of `f`, or, generically, pass each other: the zero set splits into two arcs that turn away, a fold on each. A predictor step comparable to the arcs' distance can be corrected onto the other arc (branch jumping near a bifurcation, Allgower & Georg ch. 8), and the walk's own fold is then never seen. The walk's safety here is step control, not a proof: (i) at every step the quadratic model of `f` (the saddle of [`cross`]) is asked for a saddle ahead within the step; one that `cross` steps over (split within half the tolerance, 4) is left to it, otherwise the step is kept to half the saddle's distance, but not below twice the walk's least step (`saddle_ahead`, `approach`); (ii) a step accepted with such a saddle between its ends (the model at its middle, which sees what the model at its start may not) is retried shorter (`split_between`); (iii) a step whose tangent turned by more than `MAX_TURN` is retried shorter, as everywhere. A jump that gets past all three gives a refusal, not a wrong sheet: no ruling on the zero set lies between the two rulings it joins ("the rulings between two found ones cannot be followed"), or, where the model sees the saddle between them, the rulings interpolated across are held to the twist `φ L ≤ tol/2` every ruling is held to, refined or refused. Where the walk stays on its arc it meets the fold where it turns and closes it with a fan (4), as every fold: an exact developable, every ruling on the zero set. Pinned by synthetic rails as well as by the kayak's: `passing_branches` with B's profile scaled by 1.002 over 80 % of the rails, whose arcs pass about 0.0012 of the square apart, closer than the largest step, the model's value at the saddle some 80 tolerances over the ruling (`branches_that_pass_closer_than_a_step_are_followed_into_the_fold`); measured 2026-10-29 with the guards switched off: without (i) and (ii) the walk jumps and is refused, "the rulings between two found ones cannot be followed"; without (i) alone, (ii) catches every jump in the synthetic cases tried (scales 1.002 to 1.03, A bent out of its plane or not), so (i) is a saving of rejected steps there, not a guard on its own. On the kayak's sheer panel (its rails from the model's json, `rails_whose_condition_has_branches_that_pass_each_other_are_closed_by_fans`), measured 2026-10-29 with (i) alone off (runs 40823025519, 40823038272): the same six faces at 1e-3 (707 rulings instead of 693, deviation 0.49 of the tolerance), but its first run of rulings no longer lies flat (its layouts at two grids differ by 2.5 tolerances and do not converge), so there (i) is what keeps the path one the layout settles on, not what keeps the walk on its arc; the model itself (`kayak_findings`, the eight faces and the plates on them) passes with (i) off. A fan across a fold that lands where the other arc already turns back is closed by a second fan from its end (`fold_start`; two fans in a row with one apex, as the walk round such a fold can make when its first step meets the fan's line again within rounding, are one fan, `merged_fans`, and so are the start's or the end's fan and a fan of the path beside it with the same apex, the merged fan named as the end's, `merged_end_fans`, 2026-10-29; `a_fan_that_lands_where_the_other_branch_turns_back_is_followed_by_a_second`, A bent out of its plane by `2x²`). (develop-r1 stepped over such a saddle where the rulings interpolated across it lay flat by `φ² Δ / 8`, a bound some 24 000 times looser than the twist `φ L` every other ruling is held to; review of 2026-10-28: removed, *Findings*.)
3. **The end.** When the walk reaches `s = S` (or `t = T`), the rest of `B` (or of `A`) is the end fan from `A(S)` (or `B(T)`). Reaching `(S, T)` itself needs none.
4. **Folds: closed by a fan (2026-10-27).** Where the zero set turns back in one parameter (`dt` or `ds` changes sign: later rulings would cross earlier ones), the walk finds where it turned (the point between its last two steps where the zero set's tangent is square to that parameter's axis, by bisection) and closes the gap with a cone whose apex is the point of a rail there and whose two end rulings both satisfy `f`, so it shares its tangent planes with the rulings on either side (Frey & Bindschadler 1993 join developable pieces through cones the same way). At a fold where `t` would fall at `(s*, t*)` there are two: *across*, at `s = s*` (apex `A(s*)`) from `t*` up to the next zero of `f(s*, ·)` from which the zero set goes on forward (the bridge between two branches that pass each other: a crossing that one rail's inflection makes, split because the other does not inflect with it), and *along*, at `t = t*` (apex `B(t*)`) to where the zero set, followed round the fold without keeping its points, comes back to `t*` going forward (a dip); likewise with `s` and `t` swapped. Of those that exist, the one that covers less of its rail. Where the zero set comes back to the fan's line is searched along that line from the walk's last point before it; when that point is where the fold starts (the walk's first step went over the whole dip), the search starts on a zero of `f`, whose rounded value has either sign, so the sign beside it is taken from the first derivative of `f` along the line that does not vanish there: the first where the fold starts at a fan's end (a simple zero), the second where the zero set turned (a double zero, `turning_point` found it), trusted only when larger than what the rounding of `f` makes of its difference quotient (`2·16ε/h` and `4·16ε/h²`); a start found by neither, or a derivative below that, leaves the search as it was (`Fold::sign_past_start`, `FoldStart`, 2026-10-31). A crossing whose two branches pass within the tolerance (the model's value at the saddle of `f`'s quadratic model, times the ruling's length, within half the tolerance; Allgower & Georg, ch. 8, simple bifurcation points) is stepped over along the branch nearest the walk's heading instead.
5. **Refused, with where.** Where both parameters turn back at once (a curve that winds about the other), where a fold has neither fan (the zero set leaves the square before it comes back, with nothing across: a fan to the end would crease the sheet), when the gradient of `f` vanishes over a stretch (the curves lie in one plane there so that every ruling satisfies `f`), when a ruling's length falls below the tolerance away from the ends, and when no ruling satisfies `f` at either start side.
5b. **Corners (2026-10-28).** Where a rail's tangent turns at a joint of its pieces (by more than the angular tolerance), the condition jumps on that line of the square. The walk does not step across it: a step that reaches it ends on it, at the zero of the condition with the tangent of the side the walk comes from (the piece before the joint; `Rail::locate_side`), and the gradient beside the line is taken on the point's own side. From there the gap is a fan with its apex at the corner, from the walk's ruling along the line to the first ruling, forward, from which the condition with the tangent after the corner goes on forward (as the bridge at a fold): its two end rulings satisfy the condition of the face on their side, so it shares their tangent planes and the sheet is smooth while its rail has the corner (Frey & Bindschadler's cones between developable pieces). A fan that would cover no more than the tolerance is none: the faces part on that ruling (a rail's row cannot follow a corner) and the rulings go on. Where the ruling reaches a corner of the other rail within the tolerance (both rails turn on one ruling: a generalised cylinder over a profile with a corner) the faces part there too, the sheet creased along it, when the ruling also satisfies the condition after both corners within half the tolerance; else refused. A step that would reach both rails' corner lines goes to the ruling between the two corners when that ruling strays from a developable on the side the walk comes from by at most half the tolerance (2026-10-28, develop-r1b: at a generalised cylinder's corner the walk reaches both lines at once, and the corrector on one met the other's jump within rounding of the root and found none on either side). Refused too: a fan from a corner that would reach past a corner of the other rail, no ruling beyond the corner that goes on forward (the rulings would cross), and a corner met while going round a fold.
5c. **Tubes (2026-10-28).** For two closed rails the square of their parameters is a torus, and the developable is a path along the zero set that goes once round both, from a ruling `A(0) B(t*)` round to the same ruling. `B` is started at `t*` (its piece there split), so the walk starts at the corner of the square on the zero set, and the sheet's last ruling is its first: one edge, the seam, used by the faces on both sides of it (by the one face of a tube without fans), each rail's ends one vertex. Where the walk reaches the end of one rail first, the rest is a fan from that end, which closes the tube as smoothly as an end fan does an open sheet (its first ruling satisfies the condition, its last is the seam). Of the zeros of `f(0, ·)`, the shortest ruling is tried first, then the others in order of length (at most sixteen): each that goes round is a developable (two coaxial circles: the frustum, and the rulings across the axis), and the shortest is the one a tube between two loops is. `A` must be smooth where the seam starts: where it closes at a corner (a loop's start is where its curves were found to close, not the user's choice), it is started in the middle of its first piece instead, as `B` is at `t*` (2026-10-29; refused until then, "start the curve where it is smooth"), and the corner is then one like any other along it, as a corner of `B` where it closes is. When both ways round are refused, both reasons are told (2026-10-29: only the first was, and for loops drawn opposite ways round it said to draw them the same way round when the other way had failed for another reason). Two closed rails that are the same (each within the tolerance of the other, at 64 points per piece refined by Newton) are refused. Closed rails in one plane give the flat ring between them, cut along the shortest segment from `A(0)` to `B`, when the two loops do not touch or cross (from each nearest approach of their samples, the nearest points found by projecting from one curve onto the other in turn, which converges to a crossing as to a touch), one encloses the other (the winding number about a point of the other, summed over stretches shorter than half its distance from the point, along each of which the angle turned is that between its ends) and they run the same way round (the signs of their enclosed areas); refused otherwise, with which (2026-10-28, review: the plane was taken without these). A loop drawn the other way is then turned like any rail that only that way gives a developable (`connect`).
6. **Both curves in one plane** (every sampled point of both within half the tolerance of one plane): `f` vanishes everywhere and every ruled surface between them is the planar region they bound; the face is that plane (the rulings, matched by the curves' parameters, only shape the fit that is then replaced by the plane).

**The path before the fit (2026-10-27).** Points within the tolerance of their neighbours are one; a point that leaves a stretch shorter than half the one before it is dropped (and the point before the last while the last stretch is shorter than half its neighbour), the ends of a fan kept. Where the walk crept (steps halved at a turn or at a side) its points lie far closer than those beside them, and an interpolation through spacings that unequal overshoots by more than any refinement takes back; the fit puts rulings back at the middles where needed, which keeps neighbouring stretches within a factor of two. The ruling added at a middle is the zero of `f` on the line square to the chord of its two neighbours (the corrector of a pseudo-arclength continuation), else Newton's onto the zero set.

**The faces.** The traced rulings are a list of parameter pairs `(s_k, t_k)`. The path is split at its fans: each run of rulings and each fan is a face. The face of the rulings is a B-spline surface of degree 3 along the path and 1 across: its two rows of control points interpolate the points `A(s_k)` and `B(t_k)` at one common parameter (the path's length in `|dA| + |dB|`), so every ruling `A(s_k)B(t_k)` is a straight line of the surface. Rulings are added (at the middle of every interval where the surface is farther than half the tolerance from `A`, from `B` or from the true ruling at the middle of the interval) until the surface is within half the tolerance everywhere it is checked. At the face's two end rulings, where it meets the next face of the sheet, its tangent plane is held to the developable's too (2026-10-28): the angle between the fitted normal at the ruling's middle and the plane of the ruling and each moving rail's tangent (at a corner, on the face's side), times the ruling's length, within half the tolerance, refined in the end interval otherwise, whatever its length. The rows leave the face's end rulings along the rails' own tangents (2026-10-29, develop-r4; Hermite end conditions): each row is the cubic B-spline through its points whose end derivatives are given (`geom::refine::interpolate_with_end_derivatives`, the NURBS Book §9.2.2: the first and last parameter four times, the inner ones once, the control points beside each end fixed by the end's point and derivative, the inner ones a tridiagonal solve), the derivative the rail's unit tangent at the end ruling, on the face's side of a corner, times the row's rate there as the quadratic through its three end points has it (Bessel's end condition), never backward, and zero for a fan's apex row. Where a rail's derivative vanishes at the ruling (a NURBS whose first two control points coincide, a curve run where it stands still), its tangent is the limit from inside the piece (`Rail::tangent_side`: the unit derivative at `h` and `2h` into the piece, `h` = 1e-5 of it, extrapolated as `2 u(h) − u(2h)`, second order whichever derivative is the first that does not vanish), as everywhere a rail's direction is used (the condition, corners, cusps, a tube's closing corner, the end check); an end whose plane has no direction even so is refused with the reason, never skipped (final review of 2026-10-29). So the face's tangent plane along an end ruling is the plane of the ruling and `(D_A + D_B)/2`, built from the rails' tangents, which carry only their relative rounding; taken from those, it is held to the developable's with no allowance (`end_turn` against the planes of the ruling and each moving rail's tangent, times the ruling's length, within half the tolerance), and a face whose end ruling is twisted (its rails' tangents and the ruling not in one plane) is refused with the place, since no ruling added inside the face changes that plane. A neighbouring fan's moving row leaves the shared ruling along the same tangent, so the check bounds the kink between them. The stored face's normal there is the cross product of its first control leg (as short as a third of the end interval) and the ruling, and the control points' coordinates round by about `ε |p|` each, which turns it by about `ε |p| / leg`: that is measured at every face end (the angle between the stored normal and the plane built from the tangents, times the ruling's length) and reported as `unresolved`, an estimate of what the coordinates leave unresolved, not a bound and not counted in `deviation`; positions are not affected beyond `ε |p|`. Near the origin it is a small fraction of the tolerance; on the sea kayak's sheer panel, some 2.4e9 tolerances out, it reaches a few tolerances at its narrowest faces (*Findings*, 2026-10-29). (2026-10-28 to 2026-10-29 the end was held to half the tolerance plus an allowance `ε |p| L / span` computed from each round's end interval, which doubled with every halving of the interval, so far from the origin a twisted end passed once refined enough; and before that an end interval no longer than the tolerance was not checked at all: reviews of 2026-10-29.) An interpolation's natural end derivatives are its least accurate, and two faces that meet at a corner's fan were 2e-6 rad apart there before the end check (2026-10-28). Its edges are the curves of the rails themselves, not the fitted rows (within half the tolerance of them): two developables that share a curve meet edge to edge exactly, the condition for panels cut flat to close up. A fan is a B-spline surface of degree 3 along the other curve and 1 across with all control points of its apex row at the apex.

**Planes, cylinders, cones and tangent developables.** They are the developable surfaces (do Carmo §3-5: away from planar points a developable is a piece of a plane, a cylinder, a cone or a tangent developable), and no new surface kind is made for them: the analytic planes, cylinders and cones the kernel has are developed as they are (their curvature is exactly zero in closed form, `curvature::second_derivatives`), and a connection between two curves comes out as one of them in effect (two coaxial arcs give a cone's rulings, a curve and its translate a cylinder's, curves in one plane the plane itself, which is then the face's surface). A tangent developable (the tangents of one space curve) is a connection of a curve with a curve on its tangent surface, so it is built the same way; a feature that makes it from one curve alone is not built (no use for it yet). Unfolding and the measure take any surface kind, a B-spline face that is developable included: the method never looks at the kind.

### Unfolding

**One face.** The development is computed over a grid of the face's region in its surface's parameters (below): the grid points are laid in the plane one by one, the first two rows as a zig-zag strip and each later row from the one below, each point from the two already laid beside it that make the best-conditioned triangle with it, at their distances in the plane (below), on the side the face's outward normal gives; a point that coincides with a laid neighbour on the surface (an apex row of a fan) is laid there. The grid starts at four cells along the shorter side of the face and is doubled until the coarser layout, mapped (below), is within half the tolerance of the finer at every point of the finer grid, after the turn and shift that best lay one onto the other; that difference is the reported error of the map. A face whose stretch (above) exceeds the tolerance is refused with its curvature and where it is; an approximate flatten of a doubly curved face is not offered (its distortion would have to be chosen and bounded, which is a separate method, not a fallback of this one).

**A face with an apex (2026-10-28).** Where a side `v = v₀` (or `v₁`) of the face's region is one point of the part (nine points along it within a millionth of the tolerance, or within 64 ulps of the size of their coordinates where that is larger, as far from the origin the apex row's sum rounds by that, the normal lost there: the apex of a fan, a cone), the grid is not laid row by row: laid from neighbours a small angle apart, the rows from a collapsed row converge only at first order towards it (the fans of a folded sheet ran out of grid, r1-kayakplates). A developable is a cone about such a point, and laid flat a cone opens at its apex by the length of the curve its unit ruling directions draw on the unit sphere (an isometry keeps the angle between two curves; at the apex the angle between two rulings, measured on the cone, is that arc). So the iso-line from the apex at each `u` is laid from the apex's image in the direction `θ(u) = ∫ (T × T')·n du` (`T = S_v/|S_v|` at the apex, `T' = (S_uv − T (T·S_uv))/|S_v|`, `n` the outward normal beside the apex; Simpson's rule doubled to 1e-13), as its own planar image (below). The layout, and the map between nodes, are then exact up to the quadrature, and converge at the first doubling.

**Distances in the layout.** The distance two grid points have in the plane is not the length of the surface curve between them but the chord of that curve's image: an isometry keeps a curve's speed and its geodesic curvature (do Carmo §4-4), so the image of the curve `c(t) = S(p + t (q − p))` is the planar curve with the same speed `|c'|` and the turning `dθ/dt = c''·(n × c')/|c'|²` (with the exact second derivatives above), its angle and its end found together by Simpson's rule, step by step (the angle at a step's middle by the rule's own interpolant, so both are of the fourth order), from eight steps doubled until two results agree to 1e-12 of the segment's length (at most 256; 2026-10-27, before: the angle by the trapezoid rule and agreement to 1e-10, which along a 5 m face laid in rows of 400 cells added up to 5e-7 and the layouts stopped converging). On a developable surface this is the flat distance to well below the tolerance, so the layout is exact up to that quadrature and does not depend on the grid's step (a cone frustum's layout passes the convergence test at the first doubling); the grid only has to resolve where the face is. **Span by span (2026-11-02):** the line is split where it crosses the surface's knots (in u and in v), each piece integrated within its span and laid from the end of the one before in the direction its image has turned to (the angle held to 1e-12 as well), with every rate of a piece, its ends' too, taken in the spans of the piece's middle (`geom::curvature::spans_at` and `derivatives_in_spans`: a spline is evaluated at a knot in the span after it, and an end may round an ulp past the knot; where the spline is only once differentiable there its second derivative, and so the geodesic curvature, jumps), and a billionth of the piece inside only where the curve stands still at the end (its speed a billionth of the piece's middle's or less, a corner where a row stands still: the turning's limit instead of the rounding of a vanishing `c'`). The join assumes the parameter line's tangent continuous across the knot: a face whose surface is not once differentiable at an inner knot inside its region (one-sided first derivatives a millionth apart, at nine points along it; only a knot of full multiplicity can do this) is refused by name, a crease or a kinked parametrisation alike, rather than laid by a turn the join would miss. The work is bounded: a face whose surface has more than 16384 knot spans in u (a fitted developable has one per ruling, at most 8192) or 1024 in v (an image from an apex row crosses them all) is refused before it is measured or sampled, the first grid gets at least one cell per eight knots of its region, and an image crossing more spans than the two together (not reached) gives no number; every number of the layout, the walk's fits and the outline is checked as one, so such an image is refused, never a pattern with a map that is not a number (2026-11-02, review). The models of the tests, the showcases and the format fixtures have at most the spans `crates/engine/tests/knot_spans_census.rs` prints (ignored, run on the Strix). A rule across knots is of the second order at best, and a developable's fitted faces put their knots at its rulings, which crowd where the walk slows, towards a fold (*Findings*, 2026-11-02). The outline's edges were already developed span by span (below).

**The map.** A point `(u, v)` of the face goes to the plane from the corner of its grid cell where the surface is most regular (largest `|S_u × S_v|`, so never an apex): the planar image of the surface curve from that corner to `(u, v)`, started at the angle the isometry gives (the corner's grid edge in the layout, turned by the angle the two curves make on the surface, measured about the outward normal). A point of the face is found in parameters by projection; a point of the plane goes back by finding the grid cell around it and Newton's method on the map, then the surface at those parameters, kept only inside the face's region. A point mapped there and back returns within the reported error.

**The region.** The face's boundary is sampled (48 points per edge, put on the surface by projection, each from the one before), unwrapped along each loop on a surface that closes on itself. Loops that close in parameters bound the region; two loops that go once round (a band round a cylinder or cone) are cut open along an iso-line: through the first loop's start, or, for a band reached across a circle of a band already laid, where that band's cut meets the circle; each loop's edge that crosses it is split there (the crossing found by bisection on the edge's curve), and the two images of the iso-line are cut edges of the pattern. A boundary sample where the surface collapses (no normal) takes the parameter across of the nearest sample beside it only when that leaves it the same point of the part (a row that is one point: an apex row, a pole); at a corner where a row stands still but goes on (a cone's apex at a corner of a strip) the parameter beside it is another point, and the corner keeps its own (2026-11-02). A face without loops covers its surface's whole domain (a sphere). Anything else (a loop that goes round the other way, one round loop alone: a face that reaches an apex or a pole) is refused as not supported.

**A sheet of faces.** The faces must be connected across shared edges. A band reached across a loop of a band is cut open where that band's cut meets the loop, so both open along one line of the part; the shared edge's two images are paired by the parameter on its curve (both faces sample it alike), the point where a band's edge is cut (which has two images) left out. Seams (an edge a face uses twice) and shared edges the walk does not cross bound the pattern on both sides. The pattern must be one region (one profile, its holes kept); its area is compared with the part's (`Distortion::area`). The development is kept with the unfold's feature result (`regen::Developed`), so it is cached with the feature by the regeneration's own cache, and the unfold's report (`lengthChange`, `areaChange`, `mapError`) and `unfold.map` read it instead of laying the faces flat again. They are laid face by face, in order of a walk from the first face across shared edges (to the neighbour with the least index first): each face is developed alone, then placed by the rigid motion of the plane (turn and shift, no mirror) that best lays its image of the shared edge onto the image the face it is reached from gave (least squares over the edge's points). The two images must agree within the tolerance: two faces whose edge bends differently in the plane on either side (the chine between two kayak panels, the crease of a fold) cannot lie flat as one piece, and the unfold is refused, naming the edge and how far apart its images are (unfold the faces as separate pieces). An edge shared by faces of the set that is not crossed by the walk (a seam that closes a tube) is a cut: both its images are edges of the pattern.

**The outline's edges, developed along themselves.** Mapping each sample of an edge through the map gives points each within the map's error, but such an error, small at every point, need not cancel along an edge's length. So an edge's image is the planar curve with the edge's own speed and geodesic curvature (`dθ/dt = c''·(n × c')/|c'|²`, Simpson's rule span by span between the knots of a spline edge, with `c''` taken within the span, 8 steps per sample interval, doubled up to 256 until both the fit onto the map and its spline are within half the tolerance), laid by the turn and shift that best fits it onto the map's images of its samples (refused when they are more than half the tolerance apart); its length is then the edge's by construction, and the flat length of every edge is measured against its 3D length and refused when they differ by more than the tolerance. Cut lines are developed the same way.

**The flat body.** Each edge of each face's boundary is mapped to the plane at points along it (48 points per edge to start with, the middles between them added in up to six rounds until the spline through them is within half the tolerance of the mapped edge at the middles, refused otherwise), as a cubic spline; a face's seam on a surface that closes on itself (a cylinder or cone cut round) is mapped as the two iso-lines it is cut along. The profile detection and the fill of a sketch (`craft3d_kernel::profile`, `extrude::fill`) then make the flat sheet: one face per face of the part, its holes kept.

### What is checked (oracles)

- The curvature: a sphere `1/r²`, a torus `cos v / (r (R + r cos v))`, planes, cylinders and cones 0, at the scales 1e-3, 1 and 1e6; a spline surface against differences of its first derivatives.
- The connection: `f = 0` at every ruling and `|K|` below the tolerance's measure on the faces; two coaxial circles of different radius give a cone frustum, two parallel lines a plane, a curve and its translate a cylinder (generalised); the edges are the input curves; two developables on one curve share it exactly.
- The unfold: a cylinder of radius `r` and height `h` over the angle `θ` unrolls to the rectangle `rθ × h`; a cone frustum with slant radii `ρ₁ < ρ₂` (from the apex) and base radius `R` unrolls to an annular sector of radii `ρ₁, ρ₂` and angle `2π R / ρ₂`; lengths of every edge and the area of every face are kept; a point mapped there and back returns; a hole drawn flat lands where its 3D image says; three scales.

## Open

- A face whose parametrisation stands still at corners in both u and v (with no closure) is still integrated against `du` by `topo::mass` and may not settle there.
- A face with an apex whose iso-lines from the apex are not its rulings is laid by the same rule (the angle at the apex is the arc of the iso-lines' directions there), which holds for a cone; a face that collapses along a side that is not one point, or along a side in `u`, is laid by the grid as before.
- Of the two fans at a fold, the one that covers less of its rail is taken; any cone between two points of the zero set at one parameter is as developable, so which to take is a choice of shape the caller could make (an option owed).
- A connection split where the rulings would cross (owed; refused today with the place, Method 5). The method: where the zero set turns back and neither fan closes the fold, the sheet can still be built as developable pieces that meet at a crease: the fold's ruling ends the first piece, and the rest is joined as a connection of the remaining stretches of the rails, starting from the crease ruling with the fan to the first root of the condition beyond it (Method 1 at the crease as at a start, the shorter of the two fans). The pieces meet along the crease with tangent planes that differ by the turn there, a fold line of the pattern rather than a smooth sheet, so it must be asked for (an option of `feature.developable`, a document field) and the crease reported with its angle and named by its place (as the inner faces, *Open* below).
- An approximate flatten of a doubly curved face with its strain measured and reported (owed; such a face is refused by the unfold with its curvature). The method: the face's grid laid flat by least squares on its edges' lengths (the planar images' lengths, as the layout's distances), as springs, by Gauss–Newton from the layout about its middle line (the frame of the stretch estimate), on a sparse factor; the result's strain measured on every grid edge (the largest `|l_flat / l_3d − 1|`) and its change of area, returned with the pattern; offered only as its own feature (`feature.flatten`, never `feature.unfold`), named approximate in its result, and refused above a strain the caller gives.
- Marks drawn on a pattern carried onto the part as geometry (owed; the map is there, `unfold.map`). The method: each curve of a sketch on the pattern's plane mapped through the development (`to_3d` at points along it, the middles added until the curve through the mapped points is within half the tolerance of the map's images there, as the outline's edges are fitted), made a curve on the face it lands on (its points' parameters, a spline in the face's parameters), and imprinted on the face (split along it) or kept as a wire body; a feature `feature.wrap {unfold, sketch}` (a document field), and the imprint of a curve on a freeform face.
- The references of a developable to edges are not followed when the body they are of is taken by a later feature (as a fillet's are, `regen::follow`); a developable on edges of a body that a later feature changes keeps reading the body at the feature that made it.
- An edge of the outline that the spline cannot follow within half the tolerance in six rounds of refinement is refused, not approximated.
