# 3DCraft design: The tessellator

B-rep → triangle meshes and edge polylines (`craft3d-tess`), the qualities a scene, a picture and an export ask for (`engine/src/scene.rs`), and the mesh self-intersection test. The tessellator of the map ([`../architecture.md`](../architecture.md) §6).

## Method

**Entry points.** `tessellate_body(&Body, &TessParams)` with `TessParams { chordal_tolerance, max_segments, max_triangles, max_angle }`; `tessellate_body_and_edges` gives the mesh and the edges' polylines for the work of one; `tessellate_edges` the polylines alone; `tessellate_within_budget` coarsens the tolerance for a whole body until it fits a triangle budget. Every triangle carries its face, every polyline its edge.

**Edges first.** Each edge becomes points once, within the chordal tolerance (and the angle, when one is given), and every face that uses it uses those points, so a valid solid gives a closed mesh by construction. A face may ask for more: chords that cross another of its edges, lie beside its surface (a tight fillet, a slender nose), bulge past nearby points, are shared by two faces as a rung, or are long and straight beside points of a flat face, are cut in two on the curve, for every face that uses the edge, in rounds that are the same for any number of threads. Each face's mesh is then checked against its loops chord by chord before it is returned: a mesh that is not closed is an error, never a result.

**Faces in their parameter plane.** A face is triangulated in its surface's parameters by our own constrained Delaunay triangulation (holes by the even–odd rule), seeded with a grid whose cells are within the tolerance by the arc for analytic kinds and by proven bounds on the second derivatives for Bézier and NURBS surfaces (for a NURBS surface span by span, the lower of its Bézier patch's bound and its B-spline derivative nets', and across a span narrower than a cell how far the first derivative travels; the grid of a Bézier or NURBS face is held to as many cells as a square grid of `max_segments` a side, not to `max_segments` along each side: *A grid limited by its cells* below), then refined until every triangle is within the tolerance (and its sides within the angle), measured at its centroid and side middles. A face that goes round its surface is cut along a seam (straight where one exists, else in pieces from loop to loop); poles, apexes and spline surfaces that close on themselves are followed explicitly; with the angle, tori, spheres and spline faces are triangulated in a plane scaled or warped so that a cell is square in the angle. Planar faces get no interior points.

**No size of its own, deterministic, bounded.** Every threshold is a ratio, so a body and its tolerance *k* times larger give the same mesh *k* times larger (bit for bit for powers of two). Faces are meshed in parallel (`std::thread::scope`) and merged in face order. A triangulation has a work budget; a face and a body stop as soon as a count that is a function of the input exceeds the triangle budget, so a scene within the budget is the one built without it.

**Qualities** (`SceneQuality`): `Automatic` (1e-3 of the model's size: export and previews without a tolerance), `Tolerance(t)`, `Preview` (1e-3 of the bodies' box: the quick first picture), `Display` (2e-4 of the size and 12°, within a million triangles, coarsened by a ladder, never coarser than the automatic tolerance) and `Picture` (a picture's own: the display's floor, coarsened by powers of two to between a quarter and half a pixel for the picture's camera). Export refuses a tolerance it cannot mesh rather than coarsen it. A mesh export's `quality` (2026-10-23, `EXPORT_QUALITIES` in `engine/src/commands.rs`) is a multiple of the automatic tolerance and an angle: `coarse` 2× and 20°, `normal` 1× and 10°, `fine` ¼× and 5°; `tolerance` and `angle` set either one by hand.

**Self-intersections of a mesh** (`self_intersections`): triangles that cross or overlap without being neighbours, decided exactly by orientation predicates with an exact fallback, measured in depth, found through a bounding-volume hierarchy on several threads and merged in order; the body checks built on it are in [`topology.md`](topology.md).

**On its surface, within whose tolerance** (`tess::off_surfaces`, since 2026-10-22). A point of a face's mesh inside the face lies on the face's surface to rounding; a point on its boundary is a point of an edge's curve or a vertex, and on a loose body ([`topology.md`](topology.md) › *Tolerant entities*) that lies up to the entity's own tolerance off the surface. `off_surfaces(body, mesh, linear, rounding)` reports every mesh point further from its face's surface than rounding, unless it lies within rounding of an edge of the face (or a vertex of one), and then further than that entity's tolerance plus rounding. The tessellator itself needs nothing new: edge points are shared by the faces and projected into each face's parameters. Held by `crates/tess/tests/loose.rs`: 30 loose bodies (the reference solids and a block with a NURBS roof, every other face's surface moved by 30 or 300 tolerances off its edges, the gaps recorded, at three scales) mesh closed and pass; the same meshes against the same bodies without their tables fail, and exact bodies pass at rounding. Since slice 3 (2026-10-22) `document.check` deep meshes every body with loose entities at a thousandth of its size and reports the points beyond their entity's tolerance as a problem (`looseMeshesChecked` counts the bodies; rounding a billionth of the size, at least a hundredth of the document's tolerance); held by `engine::commands::loose_mesh_tests` (a sewn block at three scales passes, the same without its table is reported) and `crates/engine/tests/step_import.rs` › `the_deep_check_meshes_a_loose_import_on_its_tolerances`.

## Findings and history

> Moved verbatim from `docs/architecture.md` on 2026-10-15. Section numbers (§4.1, §5.3, …) in the text are those of that file before the split; [README.md](README.md) says where each one is now.

### From *Current state (2026-10-12)*

- **Tessellation:** two entry points. `tessellate_patches` meshes untrimmed Bézier patches on one uniform grid (the teapot). `tessellate_body` meshes any B-rep body edges first: every edge (line, circle, ellipse, NURBS) becomes points once, within the chordal tolerance, and every face that uses it uses those points, so the welded mesh of a valid solid is a closed manifold. Each face is triangulated in its surface's parameter space by our own constrained Delaunay triangulation (holes by the even–odd rule), with interior points added until every triangle is within tolerance of the surface; loops that wind around an axis are cut along a seam whose two sides share their 3D points; poles, apexes and collapsed patch edges become one point. Faces are tessellated in parallel (`std::thread::scope`) and merged in face order: 1, 2 and 8 threads give byte-identical meshes. Planar faces get no interior points (a block is 12 triangles). A face around an axis whose other loops leave no room for a straight seam is cut along a seam in pieces, from loop to loop (§6). A face on a spline surface that closes on itself (a NURBS surface of revolution, with or without its seam as an edge) is followed across the seam like one on a cylinder, and every face's mesh is held against the chords of its edges before it is returned, so a mesh that is not closed is an error, never a result (2026-10-14, §6). Still `Unsupported`: a loop winding more than once, or around both the axis and the tube of a torus; refused as well: a face across the seam of a spline surface that closes on knots that are not clamped.

### 6. Tessellator (`tess`)

*(Planned, not built: a cancel token, `angular_tolerance`, `max_edge_length`.)* `tessellate(&Body, &TessParams, cancel) -> Result<Mesh, TessError>` with `TessParams { chordal_tolerance, angular_tolerance, max_edge_length }`. *As built:* `tessellate_body(&Body, &TessParams)` with `TessParams { chordal_tolerance, max_segments, max_triangles, max_angle }` (no cancel token yet), `tessellate_body_and_edges`, `tessellate_edges` and `tessellate_within_budget`.

- **Edges first, then faces.** Each edge is discretised once; both faces sharing it use the same points, so the mesh of a solid is **watertight by construction**: no cracks, no T-junctions.
- **An edge is as fine as its faces need** (as built 2026-10-13). The points of an edge are first chosen for its curve alone, within the chordal tolerance. A face may need more: where another of its edges comes nearer than the tolerance, the chords of a curved edge cross it and no longer bound the face (a hole close to a rim, a notch that stops just short of an arc); and close to a pole of a sphere a chord is far from the straight segment the triangulation takes it for in parameter space. And a face may be narrower than the tolerance altogether: between a line and a flat arc from the same vertex to the same vertex the arc is one chord and the loop a line there and back, which encloses nothing; such a face asks for every chord of its curved edges. Before it is triangulated, each face names the chords it cannot do with; they are cut in two on the curve, for every face that uses the edge, and the faces concerned are done again. At most 12 rounds, an edge growing to at most four times its points and 32 more; each round is the same for any number of threads. What is left after that is the error it always was (edges that really cross). `tessellate_edges` goes through the same rounds, so its polylines are the borders of the mesh; `tessellate_body_and_edges` gives both for the work of one.
- **A chord the triangles cannot follow** (2026-10-14). A chord within the tolerance of its edge can still be useless to the face: where the surface is curved across the edge far more tightly than the tolerance (the nose of a slender aerofoil, a few millimetres round, along a loft whose tip of 0.55 m follows a section of 1.1 m directly), the chord sags off the surface by more than that radius and lies beside the face, over the next one. A triangle standing on it then faces sideways whatever its third corner, and the refinement, cutting the longest free side each time it fails to face the way the surface does, moved that corner towards the chord until there was no room left between them; the face was refused with a misleading "needs about 2050 segments per side" (`CdtError::TooMany`), at any tolerance the self-intersection check tried, so a loft whose ruled twin built could not be checked and failed. Now, when the refinement of a face can go no further (no room for a point, or 200 rounds), the chords that its stuck triangles stand on, or lie flat against (all three corners within a millionth of their longest side of the chord: cut after cut, the triangle can end one row away from it), are asked for like the chords of the rounds above (`mesh_face`'s `ask`, in `face.rs`), cut in two on the curve, and the faces using the edge are done again; a chord cut in two lies four times nearer to the surface. Failing that (the last round, or no such chord) the error is a `TessError::Face` that names the edge, which the self-intersection check answers with a mesh four times finer, as for any face it cannot mesh. *Within a chord's bulge* (2026-10-14): a chord can also lie beside the surface along it, by its bulge (`chord_bulge`: a chord of the circle where a fillet of a few tenths of a millimetre meets a plane bulges out of the fillet by its sag, in the plane), and a point of the face nearer to it than that lies beyond it in space; every triangle between the two faces backwards, and the refinement, cutting the side nearest the chord each time, walked towards it until there was no room for a point, a hundred-millionth of a millimetre from it (the turbofan's stripes and flange roots, with the angle criterion of the display at every tolerance). Such a chord is asked for too: the nearest one whose bulge, times `NEAR_BULGE`, a stuck triangle lies within; and when the refinement stops at a triangle without room, the other triangles still failing are looked at as well, so that all such chords are cut in one round. *When no chord is to blame*, the error says what happened instead of the old `TooMany` ("needs about 2050 segments per side", a count never made): a triangle smaller than the tolerance that still fails is a `TessError::Face` ("its refinement cannot go on" or "did not settle: a triangle 1e-8 across, far within the tolerance, is still off the surface or faces against it"), which no coarser tolerance cures; one as large as the tolerance means a tolerance finer than the coordinates resolve, `TooFine` without a count (`needed_segments` infinite, "asks for a tolerance finer than its coordinates can resolve"), which the display rule and `tessellate_within_budget` answer with a coarser tolerance (four times). Covered by `crates/engine/tests/display_quality.rs` (the stripe with fillets of 0.25 and 0.5 mm at ten tolerances from 0.01 to 1 mm: closed, the same on one thread and on eight; and, in release, every body of the showcase and of the models in `CRAFT3D_DISPLAY_MODELS`, the turbofan and the engine block, at the display scene's tolerance and at the first picture's with the angle, and at the automatic tolerance and four times finer without it, every solid closed, strictly since 2026-10-15: the showcase hull did not close until its bow got a wall, since its inner and outer walls touched along a line there, two vertices at one point and four triangles on one segment once welded). Only faces that failed before are affected: every mesh that was made is made bit for bit as it was. `tessellate_edges`, which does not triangulate, does not see these chords; `tessellate_body_and_edges` does. Held to by `crates/engine/tests/loft.rs` (`slender_twisted_blade_sections_loft_smoothly`, from an aerofoil and from a circle; `a_smooth_blade_whose_sides_cross_is_refused`: the same tip from a circle of 1 m, which overshoots so that the upper and lower sides cross, is still refused by the two faces that cross, where it was "cannot be meshed" before).
- **No size of its own** (2026-10-13). Nothing in the tessellator is a length: every threshold is a ratio (of the chordal tolerance, of an edge's parameter range, of a face's own extent). A body and its tolerance made *k* times as large give the same mesh *k* times as large: bit for bit when *k* is a power of two, where scaling does not round (`crates/tess/tests/scale.rs`, and for boolean results `crates/testkit/tests/self_intersection.rs`). For other *k* the edges get the same points to rounding; the triangles between them may differ where two triangulations are equally good (four points of a grid on a cylinder lie on one circle, and the last bit chooses the diagonal), so those meshes are the same surface, not the same triangles. A knot of a spline edge is a point of the edge only when it is inside the edge's range by more than 1e-9 of that range: an edge cut from a spline at a knot may start a rounding error before it.
- **Closed, and checked** (2026-10-14). That the mesh of a closed solid is closed is not left to the construction alone: it is checked on every face before its mesh is returned (`body.rs`, `closes_along_edges`). The sides of the face's triangles that lie on the boundary of its region are compared with what the face's loops say must be there: every chord of every edge, once for each use of the edge by a loop and in that direction, point for point and bit for bit; what the mesh has beyond its edges (the two sides of a seam the tessellator cut itself) must be there once each way. A face that fails is a `TessError::Face` that names the edge, the chord and the face on the other side. Faces that pass and share an edge use its chords in opposite directions, so the mesh of a body whose every edge is used once each way is closed without being welded: the sides inside a face are shared by two triangles by the making of the triangulation. The cost is that of sorting the boundary of each face, not its triangles: on the timing fixtures (`crates/tess/tests/timing.rs`, up to 396,270 triangles, best of three rounds on a machine busy with other work) it is not to be told from the noise between runs, where welding the mesh to check the same thing (`Mesh::weld` and `Welded::is_closed`, the table's last column) takes as long as making it: 190 ms for a sphere of 246,512 triangles that takes 150 to 180 ms to tessellate. A sheet is checked the same way: its border is the edges that are used once.
- **A manifold, where the body is thinner than the tolerance** (2026-10-14). The check above finds a mesh that is open; a mesh can also be closed and still no manifold. A rung is a side of a triangle that joins two points of a face's boundary across the face. Two faces that share an edge can both have the rung between the same two points of it: across a neck of a face between two stretches of one edge, with the neighbouring face just behind it, both take the same chord for their surface, and four triangles meet in it (a ball's face and a torus's around the curve they meet in; found on boolean results cut around a pole). `Welded::is_closed` and every program that reads the mesh call that open. Every face reports its rungs; where two faces have the same one, the curved face of the two (of two curved ones the first in face order) gets a point of its own surface in the middle of it and is done again, which takes its mesh off the chord by less than the tolerance. Three rounds, each the same for any number of threads; a rung still shared then is an error at the face. Two plane faces with a side in common overlap in one plane, which is the body's to answer for, and are left. The same can happen on the edges themselves: an arc flatter than the tolerance is one chord, and where a line joins the same two points (a lens-shaped profile 0.05 thick swept at a chord of 0.25, at the joint between two stretches of the sweep, where no face lies between the two edges to ask for more) four faces meet in that chord. A chord of a curved edge that is the chord of another edge as well is therefore cut in two, like the chords a face asks for (`body.rs`, `coincident_chords`).
- **Poles of spline surfaces** (2026-10-14). A face may run into a row of control points that is one point, with an edge that ends there or passes through. Two things are different from a sphere's pole, whose parameters are in closed form. The parameter around the pole of a boundary point *near* the pole is found by the search that projects it, to rounding over how slowly the surface moves with it there, which a few millionths of the surface's size from the pole is no digits at all (and the curves in which a boolean cuts such a face have points that near); a boundary that zigzags in that parameter bounds triangles that stand inside out. So a boundary point where the surface moves a thousand times slower with a parameter than it does elsewhere along the loop is projected again by descent from its neighbour along the loop, on the side away from the pole (`Surface::project_from`), which moves the parameter only as far as the point asks. And between such points lie triangles whose area is within the rounding of their own corners: they face no way that can be told, were cut in two for facing the wrong way until the triangulation gave up (the error read "needs about 2050 segments"), and are now left alone on spline surfaces. Held to by `crates/kernel/tests/boolean_poles.rs`: an octant's patch and a dome cut through and around their poles, eight results that were refused or meshed wrongly before.
- **Closed surfaces** (as built 2026-10-13). A face is to the left of its loops. On a sphere, loops that all run clockwise bound everything outside them: the face is the whole sphere with those loops as holes (a ball with a hole drilled across the sphere's axis), cut open along a meridian that no hole lies on. The same on a torus is not handled yet. A whole closed surface may also be given with its seam as an edge from pole to pole that the one loop walks there and back, as a STEP file has a sphere (2026-10-14): a loop that comes back from a pole the way it went there turns all the way around the pole, and the face is everything about it.
- **The seam of a face that goes around its surface** (as built 2026-10-13). Such a face (a wall between two circles, a zone of a sphere, a cap up to a pole, a band of a torus around its axis or around its tube) is cut open in parameter space along a seam from its lower winding loop, or the lower end of the surface, to the upper one; the two sides of the cut are the same curve in space and get the same points. A straight seam is tried first, at sixteen places, and is what an ordinary face gets. When the other loops are in the way of every one of them (seven blade roots that together cover the circumference of a turbine hub; two rows of holes, the second row half a pitch further), the seam is made of straight pieces from loop to loop: it enters a hole at one of its vertices and goes on from another, and the two stretches of the hole between those vertices become part of the two sides of the cut, one a period further than the other. The pieces are found by triangulating the loops, laid out three times side by side, with our own constrained Delaunay triangulation: no edge of it crosses a loop, so every edge between two different loops is a piece a seam can use, and the edges join the lower side to the upper because the face is connected. The seam is the chain with the fewest pieces (breadth first, short edges first); every piece is tested once more against all loops and against the pieces before it and their copies a period away. It costs nothing where a straight seam exists. Covered: cylinder, cone (also from its apex), sphere (zone, cap to a pole, whole sphere with holes), torus bands both ways, as the surface has them and reversed (`crates/tess/tests/winding_seam.rs`, closed-form areas at three sizes; `crates/testkit/tests/winding_seam_solids.rs`, a pipe drilled by the boolean kernel). *A seam along a line of the loop* (2026-10-15): a straight seam that runs along a straight edge of the loop it starts on (a bore with a stepped bottom: half a circle at one depth, a line along the wall, half a circle deeper and a line back at `a = ±π`, where the surface's parameter wraps) passes through the vertex where that edge ends; computed a period on, it can lean a last bit away from it, so that the exact test saw neither a crossing nor a touch, the seam was taken and swallowed the edge, and the face's mesh had no side on its chord: the engine block's bored mounts were refused as "not closed along edge 1068" at every tolerance finer than about 1 mm. A seam that passes within rounding of a loop's vertex (rounding per parameter, so the same at any scale) is refused like one that crosses it (`seam_grazes`; `crates/tess/tests/stepped_bore.rs`, the face as the engine block has it). A face for which no seam is found asks for finer chords first (chords too coarse can close the way between two loops) and is `Unsupported` after that.
- **A seam that starts where a loop passes twice** (2026-10-18, the fuzzer's seed 21635). A loop may pass twice through one vertex: a pocket of what is not the face hangs from the rest of the loop at a point (a mirrored body whose sphere face had a lens cut out of it under its rim, the lens touching the rim at one vertex). The ring then has that vertex twice, and the two passes face different ways: one has the face between the rim and the pocket on one side, the other on the other. A straight seam started from the pass whose corner of the face does not hold the seam's way (into the pocket's side of the vertex, rather than into the face) and the region's outline crossed itself there: the mesh lay the wrong way along the pocket's edge and the face was refused as "not closed along edge 12" (the body before the mirror, the same layout upside down, happened to start from the other pass). A seam now leaves its loop, and reaches the upper loop, only where it goes into the face: its way lies in the corner the face has at that vertex, between the edge that leaves it and the one that comes in, on the face's side (`Ring::opens_towards`, an orientation test in the parameter plane, unchanged by scaling it); along an edge or where an edge has no length it is not ruled out. Of the vertices the upper end may be, the nearest that opens is taken. Covered by `crates/tess/tests/pinched_loop.rs` (a zone with a pocket hanging aslant from its rim, both faces on the sphere, their areas summed against `2π R (R + c)`, the rim above and below, the sphere's own frame either way up, either pass first in the loop, at three sizes; the pass that does not open first failed before). The seam in pieces (`Routes`) chooses its vertices by triangulating the loops and has not met such a loop.
- **A face that winds around its axis along a thread, and the triangulation's work budget** (2026-10-14). The wall of a shank with a cut thread is one face whose lower loop winds once around the axis on its way up one edge of the groove and back down the other, sixteen turns for an M10 thread 24 mm long. Three things made such a face sit for over thirteen minutes at the display tolerance, fixed together. *(a) The straight seam was refused by rounding:* its end on the upper loop (which runs clockwise) is computed as "a period back, then so many periods on", which can be a bit away from that loop's own vertex moved by whole periods at once, and the loop's segments that end there then crossed the seam they only touch. A segment's end within 10⁻¹³ (relative, and of the period) of an end of the seam is now taken for that end (`snapped`). The end itself is left as it was computed: made exact, it let a seam through that runs along the side of a window on the surface's own seam within 10⁻¹⁷, which the rounding had refused as collinear (`crates/tess/tests/periodic.rs`). *(b) A seam from the far side:* when neither the sixteen vertices spread along the loop nor the search in pieces (whose layout is a turn either way, too narrow for a loop sixteen turns long) find a seam, it is tried straight up from the highest vertices of the lower loop (down from the lowest of an upper one), where nothing of the loop lies across the way. *(c) Point location that does not cost the square of the points:* a walk from the last point that runs out of steps (on a long, thin region in parameter space, which a thread's land is, a walk crosses a number of triangles that grows with all of them) starts again from a vertex near its point, found in a grid of about one cell per point made the first time it is needed and made again when the points double; the triangle it finds is put through the same choice as the scan of all triangles it replaces (of a point on an edge, the triangle made first), so the triangulation is the same bit for bit. And every triangulation (the face's and the one the search for a seam in pieces lays out) has a work budget: walk steps, triangles looked at, flips and crossed edges together may not exceed 65,536 plus, per point so far, 4,096 plus 64 times the square root of the number of points. Measured on the showcase and on threads down to 0.0003 mm, the most any triangulation used is about 1,200 per point (7 times the root); beyond the budget the face fails with *its triangulation did not converge*, in time of the order of n^1.5 instead of n². That error is not one the display rule retries at coarser tolerances: it retries only a triangle budget exceeded or a patch too fine for the tessellator, each of which ends as soon as it is certain (`crates/engine/src/scene.rs`, a test there). Covered: `crates/tess/tests/thread_land.rs` (a land forty turns long on a cylinder, closed-form area: 141 s and then refused before, 2 s now), `crates/engine/tests/thread_mesh.rs` (the M10 thread at 0.0015 mm and at the display rule), and two tests in `crates/tess/src/cdt.rs`. Still slow, though proportional: a thread's B-spline flanks cost about 20 µs per triangle to refine (the M10 screw with its head: 200,000 triangles at 0.0015 mm in about 3 s, a million at 0.0003 mm in about 20 s).
- **Spline surfaces that close on themselves** (2026-10-14). `craft3d-geom` reports a period for a cylinder, a cone, a sphere and a torus, and none for a Bézier or NURBS surface. The tessellator finds it: a spline surface closes in u (or in v, or in both, as a torus does) when its control net ends there on the row it starts with (the same points to 10⁻⁹ of the net's size, weights in one proportion, so that the two rows are one curve) and its knots are clamped; the period is the width of its domain. From there on such a face is a face on a closed surface like any other: its boundary is followed across the seam, a face that goes around is cut open along a seam whose two sides share their points, a face may reach a row of control points that is one point (a pole), and the seam may be an edge of the body that the loop walks there and back (then the face is an ordinary patch one period wide) or no edge at all. The surface itself is defined over one period only, so parameters beyond it are brought back into it wherever the surface is evaluated. Covered (`crates/tess/tests/closed_freeform.rs`, against closed forms, at three sizes): a full turn of a spline with a seam edge and between two circles, with the surface's seam anywhere and the turn in either parameter, a window across the seam and the wall around it, a NURBS torus with and without its two seams as edges, and a surface that closes to a point at both ends. **Not recognised, and refused:** a surface that closes on knots that are not clamped (the control net does not show it). Its seam is not followed, the boundary jumps from one end of the parameter range to the other, and that is what the face is refused for: a chord of an edge whose straight segment in parameter space strays further from the chord than the chord is long (and than twice the tolerance) is a boundary that is not closed in parameter space, an error at the face (`mesh_face`), on every kind of surface. (The first face on a closed spline surface, on 2026-10-13, came back as a mesh that was not closed, without an error: the points of its seam edge were projected now to one end of the range and now to the other.) A boundary whose extent on a spline surface is less than 10⁻⁹ of the surface's range is rounding of the projection and bounds no region.
- **Interior points keep their distance from the boundary** (2026-10-13). A grid point is left out when it is nearer than 0.3 of a grid step to any constraint, also one that is not an edge of the triangle the point falls in (the fan around a corner of a hole; found by walking the triangles within that distance, 64 at most). A point just above a chord of a hole, between the chord and its arc, makes a triangle that stands inside out, which refinement then never gets right.
- **Faces** are triangulated in parameter space (constrained Delaunay over the trimmed domain) with refinement until the surface deviation is within the chordal tolerance, then mapped to 3D. Planar faces need no refinement (but see *Long straight chords of flat faces* below).
- **The grid a curved face is seeded with** is sized so that a cell of it, cut in two triangles, is within the tolerance of the surface: by the arc for the analytic kinds, and for freeform surfaces by bounds on the second derivatives (Filip, Magedson & Markot 1986: over a right triangle with legs `hu` and `hv` along the parameters the deviation is at most `(Muu hu² + 2 Muv hu hv + Mvv hv²) / 8`). For a Bézier patch the bounds are those of its control net. **For a NURBS surface they are proven too since 2026-10-14** (until then: second differences on a lattice of 8 by 8 samples, times 1.5, which promised nothing): the surface is a rational Bézier patch on each knot span, each patch the face's box reaches is cut down to the part the box covers (across the seam too, for a surface that closes), and that part is bounded by `RationalBezierPatch::derivative_bounds` after four cuts in halves, each across the parameter that brings the bound down most. A spline face also gets a step of its own per parameter, `(Muu + Muv) hu² = (Mvv + Muv) hv² = 4 × tolerance`, which keeps the same bound and never has more cells than one step for both: the wall of an extruded spline, straight along the sweep, is one cell that way. Where one parameter would need more cells than the limit (`TessParams::max_segments`) and the other has room, it is held at the limit and the other made finer. The grid's promise is for its own cells; the triangles along the boundary are not cells of it and grid points too near the boundary are left out, so every triangle is measured afterwards all the same, and the measure at a triangle's centroid and the middles of its free sides remains a sample. *Measured* (2026-10-14, `crates/testkit/tests/mesh_hashes.rs`, 441 meshes: the reference solids straight and askew, boolean results, extruded, lofted and revolved splines, at three sizes and three tolerances): the 333 meshes of bodies without NURBS faces are what they were, bit for bit; the 108 of the twelve bodies with NURBS faces changed. Triangles at a tolerance of 10⁻³ of the size, before and after: a domed block with a NURBS roof 9,750 and 8,416 (now the mesh of its Bézier twin); an extruded leaf of two splines 10,966 and 324; a loft through three leaves 62,672 and 49,580; revolved vases 245,112 and 247,916 (degree 2), 269,380 and 318,048 (degree 3), 227,524 and 279,348 (degree 5), and up to a third more for three quarters of a turn: on rational surfaces the proven bounds are looser than 1.5 times what was sampled. The largest distance from the meshes to their surfaces, on seven points per triangle, is 0.889 of the tolerance before and after (8/9: a third along a boundary chord that sags by the whole tolerance); the sampled grid broke no promise that was measured, it made none. One body of the set is refused as too fine at 10⁻⁵ of its size where it was meshed before (a three-quarter turn of a cubic spline, 1.8 million triangles in one face): the proven grid needs 1,097 cells a side there against a limit of 1,024. The showcase documents: the six with splines changed (the housing from 5,244 to 1,868 triangles at the automatic tolerance, the vase from 17,300 to 20,980), the six without did not, bit for bit.
- **Output** keeps, per triangle, the face it came from (and per polyline, the edge), so the renderers can colour, select and pick by entity, and exporters can group.
- **Parallel per face** (`std::thread::scope`; the plan said rayon), merged in face order, so the result is identical for any thread count.
- **Our own triangulator.** No external triangulation or meshing library; this is part of "custom tessellator".
- **What a viewer draws: the display quality** (2026-10-13). The engine's scene (`Session::scene_with(SceneQuality)`, §8.5) is sampled at one of five qualities (`Preview`, the quick first picture below, and `Picture`, a picture's own, *A picture's own tolerance* below, besides these three): `Automatic`, the chordal tolerance previews and `file.export` use when none is given (`view.render` until 2026-10-15, see *A picture's own tolerance* below) (1e-3 of the model's size, the side of a square with the bodies' total area; without bodies, the diagonal of the sketches); `Tolerance(t)`, a tolerance in document units; and `Display { relative, max_triangles }`, what a viewport shows: `relative` times the model's size (2e-4 by default since 2026-10-14, a chord error of a fifth of a pixel on a model that fills a 1020-pixel viewport, with the tessellator's angle criterion at 12 degrees (`max_angle`, `DISPLAY_MAX_ANGLE`), so that a small hole stays round when the developer zooms in on it; 3e-5 without an angle before, see *An angle criterion* below), coarsened until the scene has at most `max_triangles` (a million) triangles and every body is within the tessellator's reach, but never beyond the automatic tolerance, so the display is never coarser than the export (since 2026-10-14 the size is the area of the quick first picture's mesh and the floor that picture's tolerance; see *Coarse first, then sharp* below). The coarsening is a ladder: a build with a budget stops at the first body after which the budget is exceeded, or that is too fine for the tessellator (`TessError::TooFine`), and the next tolerance is coarser by the ratio of the estimated total (the count so far over the share of the model's faces done) to the budget, at least twice and at most 64 times, six attempts at most; the scene says whether it was coarsened. The budget is the scene's, and it stops a body inside the tessellator (2026-10-13): the bodies are tessellated in feature order and each is given what the ones before it left (`TessParams::max_triangles`); the tessellator ends a body with `TessError::OverBudget` at the end of the first round of its faces in which they have more triangles than that, and ends that round as soon as the faces done show it (faces are done in parallel, each adds its triangles to a count, the ones not started are skipped). What it reports, and what the ladder's estimate takes, is the shortest run of faces from the first, in face order, whose triangles exceed the budget, as a single thread finds them: a function of the body and the parameters, not of the number of threads or of which finished first. So a scene within the budget is the scene built without one, bit for bit, and a scene over it is discarded; nothing in between. The granularity was a face until 2026-10-14 (one face that alone exceeded the budget, a fine sphere or the spring, was tessellated in full first); now a face stops too, see *A budget inside a face* below. Export and previews pass no budget (pictures have their own tolerance since 2026-10-15, see below). Measured on the plate with 1024 holes, asked for at 1e-6 of its size (647,180 triangles in full; processor time on a machine busy with other builds, relative to that full tessellation in the same run): a budget of 100,000 triangles took 0.79 of it before and takes 0.43 now, one of 30,000 took 0.78 and takes 0.24 (2.0 s against 6.7 s on the clock); the ladder ends at the same tolerance in 9 of 13 budgets on the part of `crates/engine/tests/scene.rs` and at a neighbouring one in the others (finer in 3, coarser in 1), because the estimate is now taken at the face that crossed the budget, not at the end of its body. Measured (release, 2026-10-13, `crates/engine/tests/scene_bench.rs`): a flange with six bolt holes, 820 triangles at the automatic tolerance and 4328 at the display quality, about 10 ms either way; a plate with 1024 holes (1030 faces), 24,588 triangles in 666 ms and 122,892 in 776 ms; the tessellator's cost beyond about 0.6 ms per face is about 1.5 µs per triangle, which is what the budget bounds.
- **Coarse first, then sharp** (2026-10-14). A viewer that keeps drawing while the display scene is built (`Session::set_refine_elsewhere`, the window) is answered for `Display` with a quick first picture until then: the scene at `PREVIEW_RELATIVE_TOLERANCE` (1e-3) of the diagonal of the box around the bodies (`Body::bbox`, from the geometry: nothing is measured), marked `Scene::preview` (`SceneQuality::Preview` asks for it directly). `Session::refinement` hands out the display scene's build with the document state and its regeneration (both `Arc`s), `Refinement::run` builds it on any thread and stops before its next body or attempt once cancelled, and `Session::accept_refined` keeps the result for the state it was built for, which replaces the first picture at the same revision. A host that does not refine elsewhere gets the display scene from `scene_with` as before, built from the same first picture. The display rule's size is the side of a square with the area of the first picture's mesh (within the first picture's tolerance of the exact area, which takes seconds to measure on spline faces), and its coarsest tolerance is the first picture's (the scene is then the first picture, bit for bit). *Measured* (release, a load of 10 on 14 cores, `crates/engine/tests/display_speed.rs`): regeneration, first picture after it, display scene after that, against the old display scene (regeneration, measure and ladder): the spring 0.8 s + 0.25 s (52,684 triangles) + 4.5 s (644,252) against 4.1 to 5.6 s; the screw 3.1 s + 1.1 s + 1.1 s (214,732) against 4.4 to 6.1 s; the plane 1.5 s + 0.08 s + 0.45 s (412,152) against 3.4 s; the villa 0.43 s + 0.04 s + 0.11 s (46,486) against 1.0 s. Where it goes: the spring's display scene is two attempts, the first (1.67 million triangles at 3e-5, over the budget) tessellated in full before it is given up, because the budget stops a body only between rounds of faces and the spring is one face; the screw's first picture costs more than its display scene, because the thread's faces cost the tessellator more at the coarse tolerance than at a finer one. Both are the tessellator's to improve. The display tolerance stayed 3e-5 then: at 1e-4 a pin hole a fiftieth of the model across is visibly faceted when zoomed onto, and only an angle criterion in the tessellator would keep it round at a coarser distance ([`decisions.md`](../decisions.md), 2026-10-14, *What a viewport shows*); that criterion came the same day (below).
- **An angle criterion, a budget inside a face, a body kept within a budget** (2026-10-14). *The angle* (`TessParams::max_angle`, radians, `None` by default so export and `view.render` mesh bit for bit as before, which `crates/testkit/tests/mesh_hashes.rs` holds): a chord of an edge may not turn the curve's tangent by more than the angle between its ends (nor between an end and its middle; tangents taken a millionth of the chord inside it, so a kink at a knot is not the chord's), a free side of a triangle may not turn the surface's normal by more than the angle between its ends (normals kept per vertex while the face is refined), and a face asks for a chord of its edges to be cut where the surface's normal turns further along it than the angle allows (a cylinder cut by an oblique plane, whose ellipse turns less than the cylinder's normal along it). A circle gets the larger of its chordal and its angular count; the grid a cylinder, cone, sphere or torus is seeded with is sized by both. Below `ANGLE_FLOOR` (one chordal tolerance) a chord or a side is held to the tolerance alone, so the angle never asks for more than a fine tolerance would, has no length of its own (a body and its tolerance twice as large give the mesh twice as large, bit for bit), and refinement for it ends. A turn within a millionth of the angle is rounding (`turns_beyond`): an arc cut in chords of exactly the angle is not cut again. *A budget inside a face:* a face stops as soon as a floor of its own count exceeds `TessParams::max_triangles`: its region in parameter space is a polygon with holes, so with `i` points inside it has `2i + b + 2h - 2` triangles, of which at most `b` lose their area at a pole, so at least `2i - 2`; every point added (seed or refinement) is inside, and the count is checked as each is added. The count it reports is that floor, a function of the face and the parameters, so a face that alone exceeds the budget (a fine sphere, the spring) is no longer made in full first, and a mesh within the budget is still the very mesh made without one. *A body within a budget:* `tessellate_within_budget` tries coarser tolerances for the whole body (its edges are the edges of two faces, so one tolerance for the body keeps it closed by the same construction as always), by the estimated excess, at least twice and at most 64 times, sixteen attempts and the body's own size at most, and otherwise is the last `OverBudget`. The quick first picture keeps every body within `DISPLAY_MAX_TRIANGLES` this way, and so does the display rule's fall-back at the first picture's tolerance. *The display quality* is now 2e-4 of the size and 12 degrees (`DISPLAY_RELATIVE_TOLERANCE`, `DISPLAY_MAX_ANGLE`; `ui.set` `quality.angle` in degrees, 0 for none; `ui.get` `scene.angle`); the quick first picture has the chordal tolerance alone. *The angle is a wish* (2026-10-14): a body the tessellator cannot mesh with it (any error but a budget exceeded, or a patch too fine that the ladder is about to answer with a coarser tolerance) is drawn without it at the same tolerance, as `view.render` draws it, rather than left out of the scene (`build` in `crates/engine/src/scene.rs`; `a_body_that_cannot_be_meshed_with_the_angle_is_drawn_without_it`). Covered: `crates/tess/tests/display.rs` (the angle on every chord and on every triangle above the floor of the reference solids and trimmed fixtures, closed meshes, volume within the tolerance times the area, the same mesh on 1, 2 and 8 threads and at twice the size, a hole of radius 5 in a plate of 100 at a tolerance of 0.5: 7 chords without the angle, 36 with 10 degrees; one sphere face stopped near the budget; bodies coarsened to a third, a twentieth and 500 triangles of their mesh: within it, closed, the mesh at the tolerance found, the same twice), `crates/ui-egui/tests/gpu_viewport.rs` (the flange's bolt hole: its rim's sag under a third of a pixel, and under one at four times the zoom, on 760 × 679 and 2300 × 1325 viewports: 0.105 and 0.216 px). *Measured* (release, in one run on a machine at a load of 9 to 12 on 14 cores, two rounds each; the old display quality, 3e-5 without an angle, against the new one; the display scene's build after the first picture, regeneration not counted): the spring 5.4 to 6.2 s (644,252 triangles, coarsened, two attempts) against 0.91 to 0.98 s (194,028); the showcase screw 1.2 s (214,690) against 0.30 s (44,292); the M10 screw of `pbolt` 1.3 to 1.7 s (203,006) against 0.36 to 0.78 s (41,882); the aircraft 0.45 to 0.62 s (412,152) against 0.18 s (68,004); the villa 0.14 s (46,486) against 0.17 s (20,888: many small curved faces, where the angle's normals cost more than the triangles saved). The first picture, meshed after regeneration (`crates/engine/tests/display_speed.rs`, same load): 0.33, 0.26, 0.39, 0.14 and 0.06 s, within half a second on all five; what is left before a first picture is regeneration (0.9 to 5.6 s at that load; the screws' thread and booleans). - **The angle on a thin fillet** (2026-10-15). On a fillet a hundred times thinner than the ring it runs around (the turbofan's stripes and flange roots: a tube of 0.6 mm on a ring of 50 mm) the surface's normal turns slowly over a long way around the axis and fast over a short way across the tube, so the cells of a grid that meet the angle are a hundred times longer than they are wide. The triangulation's plane was scaled to lengths in space (*Faces* above), where such a cell is a sliver; every diagonal of the grid turned the normal by both of its sides at once (about `√(tu² + tv²)`, beyond the angle when each side is at it), and every point the refinement put on one was joined by Delaunay's rule to points rows away, whose sides turned too far again. The turbofan at its display tolerance had 579,314 triangles, 9.1 times its 63,958 without the angle, 501,630 of them on tori (one fillet: 41,077 where about a thousand do). Three changes, all only with the angle (export meshes bit for bit as they were, `mesh_hashes`): *(a) the plane of the grid:* a torus or a sphere is triangulated with each parameter scaled so that a cell of its seed grid is square (`face_boundary`; the reach of a chord's bulge in that plane is scaled with it, `Boundary::stretch`), so a point added stays among its neighbours; *(b) diagonals within the angle:* the grid gives `v` what `u` leaves of the angle, `tv = √(angle² − tu²)`, with `u` held to `angle / √2` where it needs more (`both_ways` in `face.rs`), so the grid passes the angle with no refinement but along the boundary; *(c) the floor across a tube:* a side shorter than the floor is not held to the angle, and a fillet thinner than the tolerance got two cells across its tube, but the grid's diagonals are as long as its sides around the axis, and where those are not below the floor the refinement cut them until they were (a dozen points per cell, and more triangles at a coarser tolerance than at a finer one); there `v` gets the cells the angle asks for whatever the floor (at most a whole turn's worth at `tv`, and the grid's limit): a count of the angle alone, which keeps the tessellator free of a length of its own. *Measured* (release, one thread per face as usual, on a machine shared with other builds; before, one of its bodies could not be meshed at all, see the seam bullet); the stripe of `display_quality.rs` with a fillet of 0.25 mm 2,168 triangles at every tolerance from 0.17 to 1 mm (14,000 at 0.1 before); the showcase screw 36,432 (44,292 before, plain 37,078), the aircraft 68,004 and the spring 194,028 (unchanged: spline faces), the villa 20,938 (20,888). Covered by `crates/tess/tests/thin_torus.rs`: a torus and a pipe elbow of 50 by 0.6 at 0.17, 0.05 and 0.01 mm, every side not below the floor within the angle on the mesh's own normals (at most 12.000 degrees for 12), closed, the volume within the tolerance times the area, the same mesh on one and eight threads, and at most three times the triangles of a grid that meets both criteria unrefined (165,050 triangles for the torus at 0.17 before, 92 such grids; 3,960 now, 2.2). - **Long straight chords of flat faces** (2026-10-15). A plane face gets no points inside, and a straight edge is one chord however long, so a plate 4010 mm long with a row of 400 holes along it had, between each long edge and the holes, no point but the edge's two ends: its top and bottom were fans of slivers from the plate's corners across the whole plate (sides of 4010 mm), poor to draw and to export, and the self-intersection check, which prunes by boxes, found every one of them near every other (the first cut of all 400 holes: 140 ms of checking on a boolean of 200 ms, §5.5). Before any face is triangulated (`body.rs`, `cut_long_chords`; `long.rs`), each plane face looks at its straight chords: a piece of a chord in front of which two or more points of the face's boundary lie (projected inside the piece, on the face's side) nearer than an eighth of the piece's length (`LONG_CHORD`) is cut in two and its halves looked at again, down to the chordal tolerance. One point in front makes one triangle, not a fan; the points of the edges before and after the chord in its loop meet it at a corner (a half disc's arc over its diameter, a wedge) and are not counted: a long block, a thin wedge, a half disc are meshed as before. The points are the edge's, for every face that uses it (the edge's own count from then on, `EdgeSamples::cut_long`); a face across a thin wall may ask for its other edge in turn, six rounds at most, each with the faces that use an edge just cut. Points near a chord are found in a grid of about one cell per point of the face, in face order: the same for any number of threads; ratios only, the same cuts at any scale. *Measured* (release, 2026-10-15): the plate's first cut checked in 27 to 31 ms instead of 115 to 147 (its whole check 33 to 68 ms instead of 430 to 860; the mesh alone 11 to 17 ms either way, 7,064 triangles instead of 6,428); a new hole's check near it 2.4 ms instead of 1.3 to 2.1; the engine block 28,928 triangles at its display tolerance without the angle instead of 28,438 (67,576 with it instead of 66,998), the turbofan 64,022 instead of 63,958, the villa 2 more. - **The angle on a spline face** (2026-10-15). With the display's angle the turbofan's spline faces (its fan blades) had 35,302 triangles where the chordal tolerance alone gives 19,760. Measured against a grid that meets both criteria with nothing refined (whole columns and rows cut in two where a cell fails, `crates/testkit/tests/spline_angle.rs`), one side of a blade had 830 where such a grid has 750 (the curvature's cost), the other 936 where it has 336: a side of an aerofoil turns half a turn around its nose in a few hundredths of its chord, its parameter across runs 45 times slower near the nose than at the root of the side (|∂S/∂u| 102.6 to 2.3), and one scale per parameter fits such a face nowhere near the nose, so the refinement placed the points the angle asks for one by one, joined to points the plane said were near and the surface did not. A sample, not a bound: the refinement still holds every side to the angle. - **The normal at a pole** (2026-10-15, lamp T1). A vertex of the mesh is shaded with the surface's normal there, `∂u × ∂v`; at a pole of a spline surface (a border of its parameters that is one point, `geom::pole`) that product has no length and its direction is rounding, so the vertices there had a zero or arbitrary normal and a revolved spline dome was shaded with a starburst at its pole where a sphere is clean. Every vertex on a pole (`Pole::holds`, to a billionth of the parameter range) now takes the limit normal along the meridian that leaves the pole at its own parameter along the border (`Pole::normal_along`, taken a millionth of the range off the pole): the axis where the surface closes up smoothly, whatever the meridian, and the normal of that ruling at a cone-like point, so each fan triangle there is shaded as its own ruling is. It is the one rule the kernel already uses for the side of a surface a point at a pole is on, not a case of the dome; analytic surfaces have their normal in closed form there (a cone's apex keeps none). Held by `crates/tess/tests/closed_freeform.rs` › `the_normal_at_a_smooth_pole_is_the_axis` (a profile from the axis back to the axis, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e3, with and without a seam edge: every vertex at either pole within 1e-4 rad of the axis) and `crates/engine/tests/revolve.rs` › `a_revolved_spline_dome_is_shaded_with_the_axis_at_its_pole` (the lamp's dome through the commands, three scales).
- **Cuts along the axis of a cusp and of a spline cone** (2026-10-17, `face.rs` › `build_ring`, `Work::assess`). Two rules, for any freeform surface with a pole of any order and any number of edges ending there. *A parameter is free only at the pole.* A point of a loop took the parameter along the pole's border from the point before it wherever the surface moved with that parameter a billionth as fast as elsewhere on the loop. At a pole of the first order that is a billionth of the size from the pole; at a cusp, where the iso-curves shrink as the square of the distance, it is 3e-5 of it (the needle's iso-circle there is 2.4e-9 across, and the point's own meridian is known to seven digits). So the points of a cut edge next to the needle's point were put on the meridian of the edge before, the stretch of the pole was laid where they were and not at the pole, and the face's chords there were not sides of its triangles ("is not closed along edge"). On a spline surface with poles (`geom::pole::poles`) a parameter is now free only where the point is a pole: on its border to rounding (`Pole::holds`) or its point within a billionth of the surface's size (as `CornerPole` takes it); points short of it go to the projection again by descent from their neighbour (`project_from`), as near-pole points did before. Surfaces without poles keep the rule by the speed. *A triangle without area that is not rounding is cut.* Triangles whose area is within the rounding of their corners were kept on spline faces, because next to a pole cutting them only makes smaller ones. The cone a straight spline sweeps has straight rulings: its apex and two points of one ruling (of the cut edge, or of a column of the grid) are three points on a line, a triangle of no area (an error at output, "folds three distinct points onto one line") or of an area of rounding that faces any way. Such a triangle is now cut, as one that faces the wrong way is, on any surface, when it has no area at all or when it is longer than the tolerance (no rounding next to a pole is: those were a millionth of the size across); shorter ones are kept as before. Measured: the nine cuts of `boolean_poles.rs` › `UNMESHED` (the needle halved by three planes and a cylinder through its axis, quartered by a block edge and turned; the spline cone halved and quartered at 1 and 1e6) mesh closed and within their volume, and `cuts_through_a_cusp_and_an_apex_mesh_closed_and_converge`: the halves of the needle and of the spline cone at 1 and 1e6, chords 2e-2, 5e-3, 1.25e-3 of the scale: closed, every triangle with an area, facing as the surface does at the foot of its centroid and within the tolerance of it, the volume converging to `π ∫ ρ² dz / 2` (needle) and `π r² h / 6` (cone) within the area times the tolerance. (Across the needle next to its point, thinner than the tolerance, the normals at a triangle's corners turn by up to a right angle: a triangle there faces the surface at its centroid but not the mean of its corners' normals.) The uncut spline cone, refused at most tolerances before ("folds three distinct points" at 1e-1 to 1e-4 of its size at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6), now meshes closed within its volume (`crates/tess/tests/spline_cone.rs`); the meshes that it did make before change slightly (446 to 462 and 4494 to 4518 triangles). FreeCAD reads the STL and 3MF of the needle's half as a closed solid of the half's volume (`crates/io/tests/freecad.rs`). The export meshes (`mesh_hashes.rs`), the benchmark's triangle counts and the boolean corpus's digest are unchanged.
- **A picture's own tolerance** (2026-10-15, impeller T1 and lamp T2). `view.render`, `view.pick`, `view.replay` and MCP's `doc_render_preview` meshed every body at the automatic tolerance alone (1e-3 of the model's size, no angle), so a bore and a fillet's nose that the window draws round came out as polygons, and a body that needed more segments on a patch than the tessellator allows at that tolerance (the lamp: about 1203 per side against 1024) made the picture fail. The window's display scene is no answer for a picture: for the lamp it took minutes, and whether the window had finished it would decide what a picture shows. Without a tolerance a picture now has its own (`Session::picture_scene`, `scene::picture_tolerance`, `SceneQuality::Picture`), a function of the document and the picture's parameters alone. The floor is the display rule's 2e-4 of the diagonal of the box around the bodies (from the B-rep, `Scene::bodies_box`); above it the tolerance is `floor · 2^⌊log2(f / floor)⌋`, the coarsest rung at most `f`, half the model units a pixel covers at the point of that box nearest the eye, for the camera the picture is taken with (`scene::pixel_footprint`): between a quarter and half a pixel. Pictures are framed on that same box, so `view.render`, `view.pick` and a pick with the camera it passed back choose the same tolerance; the rungs make nearly equal framings one scene. The display's 12 degrees hold, and the scene goes through the display rule's ladder (`scene::picture_scene_of`, `coarser_by`) with a budget of a million triangles for the whole scene, starting at that tolerance and without a cap; out of attempts, the last rung with each body within the budget on its own. So a close-up (`box`, `zoom`) or a larger picture is finer, down to the floor, a small picture of a large model costs what its pixels can show, and no picture costs more than the budget. A state keeps one picture scene, the latest, and a full cache lets the oldest scene go that is neither the display scene nor the quick first picture, so pictures do not push the viewer's scenes out. With a tolerance, the scene of that tolerance; no scene without the display rule's budget refuses a body that is too fine for the tessellator any more: `tessellate_within_budget` meshes it at the finest coarser tolerance it can do, and the scene is `coarsened`. `view.render` reports `tolerance`, `maxAngle` and `coarsened`, `view.pick` `tolerance`. Export keeps refusing such a tolerance: a file is made at the tolerance asked for or not at all. `view.replay` meshes every step at the tolerance the picture's ladder settles on for the state at `to` and its own camera (or at the tolerance given), so without an orbit its last frame is `view.render`'s picture.
- **Self-intersections of a mesh** (`tess::self_intersections(&Mesh, tolerance, limit)`, as built 2026-10-13): the pairs of triangles that cross or overlap although they are not neighbours. *Whether* two triangles meet is decided exactly: orientation predicates evaluated in `f64` with an error bound and, where that cannot tell, again without rounding as sums of non-overlapping `f64` (`exact.rs`, after Shewchuk 1997; our own code, no dependency), because the triangles of a planar face are coplanar to the last bit or a rounding error from it. Neighbours (bit-identical corners) meet only beyond what they share. *How deep* is a measure in `f64`: how far the surface around one triangle reaches on both sides of the other inside the column over it, the smaller of the two directions, so that contact has depth zero and a tip that pricks a surface has the depth of the prick (the column is a hair wider than its triangle only across an edge beyond which the surface goes on, so that what stands exactly on a seam of the triangulation passes through; where the surface turns away square, as around an edge along which two parts of a body meet, what stands on the edge touches); for triangles in one plane, the width of their common area. Candidates come from a bounding-volume hierarchy, searched on several threads (`std::thread::scope`, as the tessellator does) and merged in triangle order.

Mesh invariants are tested, not hoped for: closed 2-manifold for a solid, consistent outward orientation, no degenerate triangles, deviation from the surface within tolerance, mesh volume converging to the B-rep volume as the tolerance shrinks.

- **The self-check's work bound, a tree of boxes, and work counted the same on any number of threads** (2026-10-17, fuzzing). *A face that cannot be meshed*: a chamfer of a union of tori and spheres left a face whose loops cross in parameter space; the check meshed it, each round splitting every curved chord of the starved face (16 384 edge points, eight rounds, each a few seconds of projections onto a NURBS of hundreds of spans), then tried twice more four times finer, the same: 30 to 90 s before it said it could not mesh the body. `refined` is now held to `SELF_CHECK_WORK` (20 million `geom::allowance` units, about one and a half times the dearest check of the tests) and the tessellator asks after each round; past it the check ends with `TessError::Spent`, which the caller refuses as a body it could not look at (the outcome it gave before, now in 2 to 10 s). *Ten thousand boxes per triangle*: near what changed in a pattern of 10 000 overlapping balls, every triangle asked every box of the zone whether it meets it (and every face every box of the zone, through a prefix sorted along x), quadratic in the instances: 2 minutes of the 2-minute regeneration. The boxes are now a tree (`SpanTree`: halved at the middle of the longest side down to eight a leaf), which answers what asking every box answers. *Work across threads*: `in_order` and the search for intersecting pairs give each worker's count (`geom::allowance::spent`) to the waiting thread when it is joined, so the count after a pool is the same for any number of threads and a bound asked after it decides the same (`crates/tess/tests/budget.rs`).

### Meshes of faces remembered (2026-10-18)

`tess::remembered` keeps the meshes of faces made lately, by a key that is everything a face's mesh is made from, written out bit for bit: the mesh's parameters and whether the round is the last, the face's surface and sense, and per loop per coedge its direction, its edge's curve and the points along the edge, and whether the loop closes. ### A scene rebuild meshes only what changed (2026-10-19)

**The problem, measured.** On the developer's Garabit pier (860 features, 307 bodies, 57k faces, 419k display triangles; `crates/engine/examples/step_profile.rs`, release), one `history.rollback` step took 2.6 to 3.5 s and one `feature.edit` near the end of the history 2.3 s, of which the command itself (regeneration through its cache: 1 feature rebuilt, 859 reused) took 3 ms. The rest was the scene: every body meshed again for the quick first picture (1.3 to 1.8 s) and again for the display scene (1.3 to 1.8 s), although 306 of the 307 bodies were the very `Arc<Body>` of the state before. Two causes: the scene was built from nothing every time, and the display rule's tolerance (2e-4 of the side of a square with the area of the first picture's mesh) moved in its fifth digit with every body rolled back (0.014709, 0.014702, 0.014701, …), so even a mesh kept by its tolerance would not have been found.

**Meshes kept by body** (`crates/engine/src/meshes.rs`, `MeshStore`, one per session, shared by every scene it builds, also the display scene refined on the window's own thread). A body is immutable once regeneration made it, and regeneration hands on the same `Arc<Body>` for every feature it did not rebuild; the tessellator is a function of the body and the parameters. So the mesh and the edges of a body are kept by the `Arc`'s address (held by a `Weak`, so no other body can be where it was while the entry exists) and the sampling (tolerance, angle and the segment limit as bits, and the budget of the body's own for a mesh coarsened under one), within a byte budget of its own of the same size as the scenes' (1.5 GiB by default; every entry weighs at least itself and an error its text, so a body refused at many samplings is held to it too), the least recently used first out and the meshes of bodies that are gone at once. A body the tessellator could not finish within the work allowance of the operation that asked (`TessError::Spent`) is not kept: that is the caller's state, not the body's. **The bound on memory:** the kept scenes within their budget, the store within its own, and as a kept scene holds its meshes from the store the two overlap: at most their sum, twice the budget, in practice the scenes' plus the meshes of bodies no kept scene holds. The plain mesh at a tolerance serves every budget it is within, whether the scene's (the display rule's ladder stops a body over what the bodies before it left) or the body's own (the first picture keeps each body within a million triangles), because a mesh within a budget is the very mesh made without one (the tessellator's promise, `TessParams::max_triangles`); a mesh coarsened for a budget of its own, and an error, serve only the limit they were made under; a budgeted build keeps only a mesh that was within its budget. With the meshes go the *facts* of a body a scene also says (`BodyFacts`: the names of its faces, edges and vertices as text, which faces each edge lies between and meet in each vertex, its vertices), worked out once per body and names and shared the same way; `SceneBody` holds its mesh, edges and facts by `Arc` (it derefs to the facts, so `body.face_names` reads as before). Nothing in a scene depends on whether a mesh was found or made: the scene is the same, bit for bit, whichever states were shown before and in whatever order (`crates/engine/tests/scene_reuse.rs`: rollback step by step, forward again, an edit, at five qualities, each scene against the one a fresh session builds, and every body that stays with the very `Arc<Mesh>` it had; `meshes::tests` for the budgets against each other on a cylinder).

**The display rule's and the first picture's tolerances stand on a ladder** (`scene::on_rung`): the rung `2^(k/8)` document units nearest the tolerance the rule computes, within 4.5 % either way (the rungs are 9.05 % apart), found from the exponent and the mantissa of the number, so the same bits on every platform. An edit that moves the model's size a little leaves the tolerance, and the meshes of the bodies it did not touch, as they were; the size has to move by about a rung for the tolerance to move. The rungs the display rule's and a picture's ladder climb when the scene is over its triangle budget stand on the same ladder (the next attempt's tolerance is `on_rung` of the estimate), so a model over the budget (the lamp, the turbofan) keeps its meshes across an edit too (the estimate from an attempt that stopped moves a little with every edit; before, every step re-meshed such a model whole: `crates/engine/tests/scene_reuse.rs` › `a_climbed_ladder_keeps_the_meshes_of_the_bodies_that_stay`). The display rule's chordal tolerance is a fifth of a pixel on a model that fills the viewport; a twentieth of that is below anything a viewer can see, and the angle criterion, which keeps small circles round, is not on the ladder. The picture's own tolerance (`view.render`, above) keeps its own rungs (whole octaves above its floor), and the automatic tolerance (export) stays exact. An expectation change: `crates/engine/tests/scene.rs` › `the_display_quality_is_a_finer_fraction_of_the_same_size` and `crates/ui-egui/tests/shell.rs` held the display tolerance to 1e-12 and 2e-3 of `size × relative`; they hold it to half a rung now, and to a rung; `crates/ui-egui/tests/gpu_viewport.rs` › `a_change_of_display_quality_reaches_the_direct_path` held the display at chordal 1e-3 to the automatic scene's triangle count exactly, now to within a quarter of it; `crates/engine/tests/view_render.rs` › `a_small_picture_of_a_whole_model_costs_what_its_pixels_show` held a small picture of a ball to eight times fewer triangles than the window's scene, seven now (7.9 at the smallest scale with the window's tolerance on its rung; the tolerance ratio of at least twelve is held as before).

**Two more costs of building a scene, per body, that were paid for every body every time** (seen once the meshes were kept: a rebuild of the pier's scene from kept meshes still took 500 to 850 ms): the names of 300k faces, edges and vertices written out as text (200 to 300 ms; now kept with the facts above); the colour of every face (155 to 315 ms: the walk of `appearance.rs` back through the features a face came through asked the regeneration and the document for a feature's result and definition at every step, each a search through 860 features, for every one of 57k faces; now the walk remembers per call what it asked (`Memo`), and a face whose name does not say it came in through an instance of its feature has the body's colour without walking, which is what the walk found at its first step); the first face id of each body (20 to 40 ms: a scan of the scene's face ids so far, per body; now carried along). Results identical (the same tests).

**Measured** (release, the pier, `step_profile`; the machine busy with other builds, so the before numbers are the quiet ones above): a rollback step 2.6–3.5 s → 75–128 ms (command 3–5 ms, first picture 35–65 ms, display scene 35–65 ms, no body meshed); `feature.edit` of a cylinder at the end of the history 2.3 s → 83 ms (2 bodies meshed: the preview's and the display's); rolling forward past ten rolled-back features 2.4 s → 140 ms (20 bodies meshed). Peak memory of the run (open, 10 steps, an edit, 11 states' scenes kept) 4.29 GB → 2.36 GB: the scenes of the states share the meshes of their common bodies (the kept scenes' byte count still counts a shared mesh once per scene, so the budget is met early rather than late). Opening the pier is unchanged: 6.4 s regeneration (18 of the 860 features over 50 ms, all boolean unions of a girder with its bracing), 1.2 s meshing the first picture, 1.3 s the display scene, 1.1 s measuring the exact area for the automatic tolerance when `view.render` or an export asks; the meshes are made once there, and what makes opening slow is regeneration ([`regeneration.md`](regeneration.md)).

**Why the empty window held 1.9 GB.** Not the window: a fresh window with an empty document holds 78 MB (22 MB of it anonymous memory; the rest the Vulkan driver's and LLVM's shared libraries). It is what the pier leaves behind: `craft3d-cli serve` is at 3 MB fresh, 1.67 GB with the pier open and a scene built, 1.77 GB after `file.close`, 1.80 GB after `file.new`, and 1.84 GB after opening the pier again (not 3.4 GB). So the memory is freed by the engine and kept by glibc's allocator (a million small allocations spread over its arenas, which it does not return to the system while any allocation in a heap lives); its trim tunables (`GLIBC_TUNABLES=glibc.malloc.trim_threshold=0:glibc.malloc.top_pad=0:glibc.malloc.mmap_threshold=131072`, `MALLOC_ARENA_MAX=2`) give back 100 MB of it. It is reused by the next document, so the process does not grow beyond its high-water mark. Returning it would take another allocator (`mimalloc` or `jemalloc` as the global allocator, a dependency: a developer decision), or `malloc_trim`, which is `unsafe` FFI. The mesh store now lets go of the meshes of bodies that are gone as soon as the active document changes, not only when the next scene is kept.

**Still open.** The merged mesh of a scene (every body's positions, normals, triangles and face ids in one `Mesh` for the renderer, 20 MB for the pier) is copied together for every scene, 15–20 ms; a renderer that took the bodies' meshes as they are would save it. `Scene::bytes` counts a shared mesh once per scene that has it, so the scenes' budget holds fewer states than it could.

### The self-check's three findings of the fuzz run of 2026-10-20 (seeds 20754684, 20811107, 20792240)

The one-hour run of 2026-10-20 (94,697 seeds on the Strix) reported two bodies as passing through themselves and one that could not be meshed for the check. Each was measured and found to be the check's own fault, not the kernel's: the bodies are valid, and so they end in `crates/engine/tests/fuzz_findings/` as designs that must end valid (`fuzz_wrong.rs`). Three rules were wrong, each put right in general terms; what was left open is named. (Checked again on 2026-10-21 by an oracle outside the code: the three bodies exported as STL at a chord below their thinnest wall and read by a separate script, which welds them and sums the solid angles of every triangle at a grid of up to 30³ points: no open edge and winding numbers 0 and 1 only, within 6e-13 of whole numbers, where a body that passes through itself has 2 or −1; mesh volumes within the chord of the exact ones.)

**An overlap in one plane that no approximation brings about (20754684, `selfcheck.rs`).** *Finding.* A pattern of a body in which a cylinder wall, slotted by a block, keeps a skin 0.034 thick beyond the block's far face: the wall's face narrows there to an isthmus between two lines in that face's plane, bulging out of it by 0.034, and the pattern body's first mesh (a chord of 0.095, a thousandth of a diagonal four times the body's) gives the isthmus no point inside, so its two triangles lie in the plane exactly, as the flat face's do. The check held that "only triangles that are in one plane exactly count, which no approximation brings about" and called the pair a certain overlap 0.46 deep against a margin of 0.095. The body alone, with a chord of 0.033 under the skin's 0.034, passed. *Method.* An overlap of meshes in one plane is an overlap of faces only where the faces are at the same place: for a pair deeper than its margin with a curved face in it, the two surfaces are asked at the centroid of the triangles' common area and halfway from there to each of its corners (`faces_apart`: each surface's nearest point to the sample); apart by more than twice the tolerance anywhere, the faces have no point in common there (each lies within the tolerance of its surface), and the finding counts as within the margin, which sends the check on to a finer mesh, where the isthmus gets its points and nothing is left. (This lowers a finding to a doubt and does not settle it: one still within the margin after the last refinement ends as marginal, which `document.check` counts but does not call a problem. A real overlap of faces has an area, and on a finer mesh the pairs inside it are certain again; one narrower than the finest mesh's triangles stays a doubt. Corrected 2026-10-21 after review.) A pair whose surfaces are one (a pin in a hole of its size, `self_intersection.rs`) stays certain, since their nearest points coincide; a surface that cannot be asked leaves the finding as it was. General: it is the margin's own reasoning (the mesh is within the chord of the face, the face within the tolerance of its surface) applied to the plane's normal, which the depth measured in the plane says nothing about. *Held to* `crates/testkit/tests/fuzz_selfcheck.rs › a_skin_of_a_cylinder_meshed_flat_is_no_overlap` (radius 25, 1500 long, a skin 1 thick behind a 7-24-25 chord, so that the lines come out exact and the rims' chords miss the isthmus) at 1e-3, 1 and 1e3: with one mesh a finding within the margin and nothing certain, refined spotless.

**A touch measured as a crossing by a walk over the touched face (20811107, `intersections.rs`).** *Finding.* A groove cut by a quarter of a barrel whose ceiling is tangent to the block's top face along a line, so that the groove's start wall ends on that line inside the top face (the body is a wall that thins to nothing, accepted since 2026-10-18; see `booleans.md`). The top face is a plane of a few large triangles; the depth of the pair (top triangle, a triangle of the start wall's annulus at the line's end) came out 2500, the body's reach, where the two only touch. The measure follows the surface around the one triangle inside the column over the other to see how far it reaches on either side; from the annulus it went down the start wall, round the barrel's side and *across the top face's own other triangles* (at height zero, inside the column) to the cylinder standing on it, 10000 high. *Method.* The surface around `x` is never followed through the face `y` belongs to (unless it is `x`'s own face), nor through a triangle lying in `y`'s plane to rounding *and facing the way `y` does*: a side of the plane reached through them was reached through `y`'s oriented surface going on, not past it. A triangle in the plane facing the other way is another sheet lying against `y` and is walked across. (Until the review of 2026-10-21 the rule skipped every triangle in the plane, which also stopped the walk at the tread of a top hat sunk through a lid to its tread, a real crossing then found by nothing; `self_intersections.rs › a_top_hat_sunk_to_its_tread_is_found` and `testkit/tests/self_intersection.rs › a_top_hat_sunk_to_its_tread_is_caught`, treads of 1.5 and 3 tolerances, three scales, tilted.) *Held to* `crates/tess/tests/self_intersections.rs › a_touch_is_not_a_crossing_by_what_lies_beyond_the_face_itself` (a plate of four triangles with a wall on an inner edge and a sheet touching one triangle at a corner, hanging from another inner edge: depth 0; sunk a tenth: depth a tenth) at three scales, with the plate's far triangles flat in its face, flat in another face of its plane (fails without the second rule) and lifted out of the plane as a curved face's are (fails without the first); the body's design in `fuzz_findings/20811107.json`. *Open.* The other direction of the same pair (the top face through the annulus's column) also measured 2500, by the column's hair across the annulus's edge to the start wall: the top face passes through that hair at the one corner where it touches. The pair's depth is the smaller of the two, so this stands only when both directions are polluted; a clipped part whose extent in the plane is no more than a few hairs is a contact at a point and should not count, which is the rule to add if a case shows it.

**A seam that runs past a vertex on its other side in space (20792240, `face.rs`).** *Finding.* Three tori stacked by their tube radius and united, a ball taken out where two inner rims meet, and the body mirrored (any plane): the mirrored torus face with the bite could not be meshed, "a triangle 3.6e-11 across, far within the tolerance of 2.5e-6, is still off the surface or faces against it". The face is a band round the tube that the bite's loop touches; the seam the band is cut along runs from the bite's end vertex C to the nearest vertex of the other loop that opens into the face, which here leaves C 0.04° from the bite's last chord C–B in the parameter plane. The mirror only turned the loop round so that the seam starts there. In space the seam's first piece (a chord of the seam's curve, 0.05 rad of the tube long) and the chord C–B leave C the other way round: B lies, seen along the surface's normal, on the other side of the seam's piece than in the plane. Every triangle in the corner between them faces against the surface, and no point inside mends it: a point in the corner splits it into two whose turns add up to the corner's, so one still turns the wrong way. The refinement can only cut free sides, cut the free side over and over, and ended in triangles of 1e-11. (A first fix, 2026-10-20, kept such triangles when thinner than the tolerance; that kept triangles facing against the surface, and broke `boolean_poles.rs › cuts_through_a_cusp_and_an_apex_mesh_closed_and_converge`, which holds every triangle, however thin, to face the way the surface does at the foot of its centroid. Dropped on 2026-10-21.) *Method* (`finish_boundary`, `passed_on_the_wrong_side`). The seam is the tessellator's own: its pieces are chords of a line in the parameter plane, cut until within half the tolerance of its curve. They are now also cut in two while a vertex of the region lies within a piece's diametral circle on the surface (in the tangent plane at the piece's middle, through the derivatives there: the vertices a triangulation joins to the piece in a triangle that stands on it, as in Ruppert's encroachment) on the other side of it in space than in the plane (`(q − p) × (v − p) · (∂a × ∂b)` against the plane's cross product: strictly opposite signs). Each halving brings the piece four times nearer its curve and halves its circle, so a vertex not on the seam's curve leaves the circle or comes to its own side; at most 24 rounds (`SEAM_HALVINGS`), after which a vertex still on the wrong side lies on the seam's curve and the refinement says so. General: it is the condition every triangle standing on a side the refinement may not cut must meet to face the way the surface does, applied to the one kind of such side the face itself chooses; no angle, no configuration, and nothing from the tolerance (the circle is the piece's own, so a body and its tolerance scaled together give the same mesh). At a pole (no cross product) nothing is told. A vertex far round the surface is out of the circle, which is measured on the surface, not in space (a 3D ball took in a vertex across a cylinder of radius 1 from a ruling 3 long, a first try). For an edge's chord the same is already done by asking for finer chords (`ask`). In the tests of `tess` and `testkit` the rule fires in this one face only. *Held to* `fuzz_selfcheck.rs › a_mirrored_torus_with_a_bite_is_meshed` (the body before the mirror and its images in four planes, at three scales, each at chords from 5e-2, the export's, to 2.5e-3, the self-check's: every mesh closed and every triangle facing the way its surface does at the foot of its centroid; fails without the rule at 1e-3, chord 5e-2, mirror plane (1, 1, 0)), and the cusp test unchanged. *Open.* The seam's choice itself: a seam 0.04° from a chord is legal but costs points; a choice that leaves its ends well inside the corners would spare them.

### Three faces the tessellator could not mesh (2026-10-21: the Perseverance's export and drill tip, a torus with holes)

Three faces were refused where a mesh exists; each cause was measured and mended by a rule that applies to any surface and placement. Each rule runs only where the face was refused before (when the refinement is stuck, when the rounds of halving end with the face refused, on a whole torus with only holes), so every mesh that was made before is made bit for bit as before (`mesh_hashes`: all 440 hashes and the freeform deviations the same as on 93899cb).

**A chord no triangle can stand on (the Perseverance's grouser, `face.rs`, `Work::carries`).** *Finding.* The STL and 3MF of the rover failed at its default tolerance (6.2): a fillet of 2.5 on the outer arc (radius 704) of a grouser, a quarter of a tube around a ring of 701.5, "its refinement cannot go on: a triangle 4.4e-7 across"; at chords of 4 to 6.2, at turns of 7.5°, 30° and −7.5° about y and not at 0°, 3° or 15° (and at 0° at a scale of 1e-3). *Cause, measured.* At a chord of 4 the fillet's rim on the wall (the tube's outer equator) is three chords of 0.145 rad, each 3.7 off its arc: within the tolerance, but more than the tube is thick, so the chord passes through the tube beyond its axis. Its sag is along the surface's normal, not beside it, so neither the bulge test of `chords_to_refine` nor the stuck triangles' (`flat_against`, `in_bulge`) saw anything. A triangle on such a chord faces sideways or backwards whatever its third corner (in the tube's section the chord's middle lies past the centre, and the side from it to any point of the quarter turns the wrong way), and the refinement cut towards the chord until there was no room. Whether a stuck triangle happened to lie flat against the chord (and so had it cut) was rounding: hence the turns. *Method.* When the refinement of a face cannot go on and no chord is to blame by the rules before, every chord of the face is tried: a third corner straight across from its middle, square to the chord on the surface (`(∂u a + ∂v b) · (q − p) = 0`), at the chord's length and at halves of it down to 2⁻²⁹ of it, on either side, within the face's box in parameter space; the triangle is held to the facing test of the refinement itself (`faces_the_surface`: clearly by the normal at the mean parameters, else by the normal at the centroid's nearest point). A chord on which none of these faces the way the surface does is asked for, cut in two (a quarter of the sag) and the face done again. General: it is the refinement's own acceptance test applied to the triangles a chord must carry; no length, angle or surface kind; a chord on which nothing can be tried is not blamed. *Held to* `crates/testkit/tests/tess_faces.rs › a_fillet_thinner_than_its_rims_sag_is_meshed_at_any_turn` (turns from −45° to 45° by 5° and by 0.25° around 7.5°, 30° and −7.5°, chords 2 to 8, three scales: closed, every triangle facing its surface and within the chord of it at the foot of its centroid, the volume within the chord times the area; failed before at 1e-3, 0°, chord 4) and `crates/engine/tests/tess_findings.rs` (the grouser's commands exported to STL and 3MF at 4, 6.2 and the automatic tolerance). The whole rover (55 bodies) exports to STL and 3MF at its default tolerance of 6.19: 75,264 triangles, every body closed, 4.7 s.

**Chords cut across from the points they cross (the rover's drill tip, `body.rs`, `edge.rs` `split_across`).** *Finding.* The union of a cone and a tilted drill, right since the tangent-order fix of the boolean, was refused by the self-check's mesh ("the boundary crosses itself in parameter space"): the crescents between the cone's base circle and the curves where it meets the drill's wall are 3.9e-4 and 7.8e-4 across, 94 long, with tips at 2.6e-5 rad. *Cause, measured.* Every chord of the two edges crosses the other edge's polyline: two arcs of radius 30 that far apart need chords of 0.3 where they are independent, and near a tip the two first chords must agree in length to within `60 θ` = 1.6e-3 (the sag of the longer one at the shorter one's end is more than the tip's angle leaves there). Halving gets there from chords of 8 in thirteen rounds; there are twelve, and the edges reach their most points (16 times their own and 32) in five (at a chord of 0.27; 0.017, the finest pass, ran out of rounds with a few chords left). *Method* (`tessellate_some`, `chords_to_refine` with `aligning`, `EdgeSamples::split_across`). When the rounds of halving end with a face refused for its chords (two of them cross in parameter space, which no triangulation survives, or its mesh failed in the last round on chords it asked for), up to twelve more rounds follow (`ALIGN_ROUNDS`). In them a chord that crosses a segment of the boundary, or passes a strip narrower than its bulge (the rule of `NEAR_BULGE`), is also cut at the point of its curve across from each end of that segment whose foot lies inside it (more than a millionth of it from its ends): the parameter whose point has the same foot on the chord, by 64 halvings (the foot moves along the chord with the parameter). The two edges' points then pair up, and two chords between paired points of curves that bend alike are copies of each other a crescent apart and do not cross, however long; near a tip the pairs are as long as each other to rounding. The chords beside a strip are halved as well. In those rounds an edge may grow to twice the cap of its own curve's points (`most_across`), or of those of an edge it was cut across from, when more (a short edge paired with a long one needs as many points as it); all edges together by no more than the sum of those caps of their own curves (`rescue_budget`), and a round that reaches it ends the rounds. Twelve rounds (`ALIGN_ROUNDS` = `MAX_ROUNDS`): what is left near a crescent's tip is what the halving could not finish, about a dozen halvings for the drill tip's. So a body no cut cures costs a bounded multiple of the work of its own curves, whatever its widest edge: a first version that let every edge grow to `max_segments` made `invalid.rs › damaged_bodies_never_panic` take 195 s and more than 3 GB (OOM-killed under the 3 GB cap) instead of 2.4 s and 63 MB, and a second that let it grow to twice the cap of the body's widest edge was bounded by edges × widest (review of 2026-10-21); `near_edges.rs › a_body_no_cutting_cures_is_refused_within_a_bound` (40 bow ties of arcs beside a disc whose rim has thousands of points: refused as before, edges those of the halving, 0.3 s, 9 MB) holds it. The rounds are kept only where they mesh the body; otherwise its points and errors are those the rounds of halving ended with (`near_edges.rs › curved_edges_that_really_cross_are_still_refused` still says "crosses itself"). They run only when meshing: `tessellate_edges` gives the polylines of the rounds of halving (for the drill tip at a chord of 6e-3, 840 points where the mesh has 1,886; `tessellate_body_and_edges`, which the engine uses, always gives the mesh's). General: a pairing of the points of two edges that lie closer than their chords sag, whatever their curves; nothing before it changes. *Held to* `tess_faces.rs › the_crescents_of_a_cone_on_a_tilted_drill_are_meshed` (the union in place and turned two ways at three scales: the self-check meshes it and finds nothing; meshes at the self-check's three chords and the export's: closed, facing, within the chord, the volume) and `engine/tests/boolean_findings.rs › the_rover_drill_tip_on_a_tilted_drill`: the feature is no longer refused for a mesh; the self-check, which can now look, found a vertex 5.6e-9 mm from an edge that is not its own and refused the result as touching itself, a sharp corner the touch rule reads as one since 2026-10-21 (the feature builds: [`booleans.md`](booleans.md) › *The rover's drill tip, measured*, 2026-10-22).

**A whole torus but for holes (`face.rs`, `torus_region`, `clear_of`).** *Finding* (the body-split agent, 2026-10-20). A torus (10, 2) less a rod of radius 1 through its middle across its axis (four holes through the tube) could not be meshed: "its loops do not bound a region". A rod of radius 3 cuts the tube in two and was meshed. *Cause.* Loops that all run clockwise bound everything outside them only on a surface that closes to points (a sphere, 2026-10-13); on a torus they were taken for a patch whose outer loop runs the wrong way. *Method.* On a surface closed both ways (a torus, analytic or spline), such loops are holes in the whole surface, opened along a circle of each parameter that no hole reaches: the middle of the widest gap between the stretches of that parameter the holes span (a loop that does not wind lies within its stretch, and so does every segment between its points); each hole is moved by whole periods into the square the two seams bound. Where the holes leave no such circle the face is refused as unsupported and says so. *Held to* `tess_faces.rs › a_torus_with_holes_through_its_tube_is_meshed` (rods of radii 0.5, 0.7, 1 and 1.5, square to the axis and tilted, three scales, chords 0.5 to 0.005: closed, facing, within the chord, the volume; failed before) and `face.rs › a_circle_clear_of_the_holes_is_the_middle_of_the_widest_gap`.

*Open.* A whole torus whose holes leave no clear circle either way (a seam in pieces, as `Routes` finds on a band, would do). The pairing runs only after halving has failed; used from the first round it would spare rounds on every sliver, at the price of changing meshes that are right now. The drill tip at a chord of 6e-3 turned needs all twelve of its rounds; cutting a chord beside a strip into as many pieces as its bulge asks at once (tried: up to 64) spent the edges' room on the long middle before the tips.

### Two faces a field test could not mesh (2026-10-25: a band between two spirals, a thread of more than three turns on a turned neck)

Both rules run only where the face was refused before, so every mesh made before is made bit for bit as before. *Pinned* on the Strix (2026-10-25), main 681d5e72 against the branch with both rules: `crates/testkit/tests/mesh_hashes.rs` (441 mesh hashes, and the 144 freeform lines of triangles and deviations) and `crates/engine/tests/mesh_digests.rs` (the STL bytes of the 10 examples, the 4 benchmark models and the 25 showcase files at coarse, normal and fine: 117 digests) are identical. Both tests are `--ignored`; run them on two commits and `diff` the output.

**A spline face that winds round more finely than the search for a foot (`face.rs`, `ring_jumps`, `follow_along_the_loop`).** *Finding* (field test, a CubeSat's wound tape spring). A band between two `sketch.curve` spirals of five and a half turns 0.5 mm apart, 0.15 mm thick, closed by two short lines and extruded: the feature built and its exact volume was right, but its side faces could not be meshed, "its boundary is not closed in parameter space: between two neighbouring points of edge 0 it jumps across the surface"; the body was not drawn, and the thumbnail of a save failed with that error. *Cause.* The boundary's points are placed on a spline surface by `Surface::project`, which starts its descent from the samples of a grid of 16 by 16 that are nearer than their neighbours. A side of the band winds round five and a half times within those 16 samples across it, so a point of its rim may lie between samples of the turns beside its own, and the descent ends on one of those, a turn away (geom's promise covers surfaces whose features are larger than a sixteenth of the domain, which this one is not). *Method.* Each loop is placed by the search as before. Only a loop on a spline surface that then jumps across its surface (`ring_jumps`: the test `mesh_face_in` refuses a region by, on the loop's own segments, so a loop that jumps would have had its face refused) is placed again: each point the search did not find on the surface (further than 1e-9 of the loop's size) is found by walking downhill from where the point before it along the loop landed (`Surface::project_from`), round the loop from the point found nearest. The walk's foot is taken when it is less than half as far as the search's *and* within the chordal tolerance of the point; a foot further off is another dip of the distance, not the point's place, and the search's stays, so the face fails as before rather than give a plausible but wrong mesh. Neighbours along a loop are near each other on the surface, so the walk stays on the point's own turn. The search itself is unchanged (it serves the kernel too, where a denser grid would change results and cost). The cost on faces that mesh: one surface evaluation per boundary point of a spline face (the jump test). *Held to* `crates/engine/tests/spiral_and_thread_faces.rs` (the band at three scales and moved by a transform as in the field: closed-form volume, deep check, a picture, STL welded watertight (each side once each way) with the volume to its area times the chordal tolerance, 3MF, STEP, a save with its thumbnail; refused before) and `face.rs` unit tests: a spiral side of 40 turns whose loop the search misplaces and the walk places on the surface; a loop 0.01 off the surface keeps the search's feet within a tolerance of 1e-3; on a closed spline cylinder no foot across the seam (where the walk stops at the border, a chord away) is taken.

**A hole that winds round the axis turns on its way up (`face.rs`, `Routes::cut_wide`).** *Finding* (a Jig agent's screw jar, 2026-10-24). A trapezoid swept along a helix of more than three turns and joined to a revolved neck: the join failed, "the result cannot be meshed to see whether it passes through itself (face 7 cannot be tessellated yet: a face around the axis of its surface with no way for a seam between its loops)"; three turns and fewer were meshed, and the jar's author found the same thread meshed on `feature.cylinder` (not measured here; where the rims start decides how far the layout below reaches). *Cause.* The neck's wall has its two rims and the thread's foot as a hole that winds round the axis as many times as the thread; every straight seam crosses the foot, and the seam in pieces must go up to the foot where it starts (its first turn faces the lower rim) and on from where it ends (its last turn faces the upper one), turns further round. The search for that seam lays the loops out one turn either way (`ROUTE_TURNS`), which reaches the foot's ends for at most about four turns, depending on where the rims start. *Method.* When every seam tried before fails (straight, in pieces, straight from the far side), and a loop spans more than a period round the axis, the search in pieces is run once more with the loops laid out as many turns either way as the longest of them spans, and one more. In that wide layout only: a piece longer round than a period is not used (`crosses_a_loop` looks at the loops' copies a period either side of a piece's start); a new piece is tested against the copies of the pieces before it that lie where it does, from the extents of both in `a` (a handful, since no piece is longer than a period); and the search's segment tests are counted. The ordinary search (`cut`, one turn either way) is as it was. *Caps, and why:* at most 64 turns either way (a thread of 64 turns: an M10 thread 96 mm long, a lead screw; each turn is one more copy of every loop); at most 262,144 points laid out (a triangulation of that size takes some tens of MB and well under a second, the order the face's own triangulations are held to); at most 2^26 segment tests (about a second; every piece is tested against every loop, so the work grows as the square of the points). Beyond a cap the face is refused with the reason ("a loop that winds round the axis 70 times on its way (more than 64 …)", "… would lay out N, more than 262144 (a coarser tolerance has fewer)", "… took more work than it is allowed"), and asks for no finer chords, which would only make it larger; a wide search that runs and finds no way leaves the face to ask for finer chords as before. *Held to* the same test file (the thread of four turns at three scales, and of 3, 3.25, 5.5 and 8 turns: closed-form volume by Pappus for the screw motion, deep check, picture, watertight STL with its volume, 3MF, STEP, thumbnail; above three turns refused before) and `face.rs › a_seam_past_a_hole_that_winds_turns_round_is_found_laid_out_wide` (a foot of six turns on a cylinder is passed through when laid out wide; 70 turns and 36,000 points are refused with their reasons).

*Open.* In the ordinary search a piece is tested against the loops' copies a period either side of its start only; a piece that runs several periods round (an edge of the layout's triangulation across an empty corner of it) could pass a copy further on unseen. Not met in a face so far; left as it is, since changing it could change seams of faces that mesh now (the wide search refuses such pieces).

**A thumbnail that cannot be drawn** is not a failed save: `file.save` and `jig.export` write the file without it and say why (`thumbnailError`, which names the body and the face). The window's status line now says so first ("no thumbnail (…); saved …") rather than at the end of the answer, where it was cut off (`apps/craft3d/src/files.rs`, `a_save_without_its_thumbnail_says_so_in_words`).

**A grid limited by its cells, and slim spans bounded by what keeps its digits (`face.rs`, `spline_grid`, `net_second_bounds`, `bounds_for_step`, `grid_within`).** *Finding* (the wavy-vase Jig, 2026-10-31). Two of its 81 range points were refused by the loft's self-intersection check: "patch 0 needs about 2515 segments per side, above the limit of 1024". The surface needs about 60. A loft through sections drawn as formula curves gets knot spans 1e-9 to 1e-10 wide where two sections' joints nearly meet (cubic, 721 control points; and, where the joints are made tangent by a change of parameter, degree 12 with every knot repeated twelve times). The control points of such a span's Bézier patch lie within rounding of each other, so their second differences are rounding, and divided by the square of the span's width they bounded the second derivative at 1.4e6 and 4.2e6 where sampling sees 7.6e3 and 1.9e3. One span sets the uniform seed grid of the whole face. Three changes, all in the seed grid of a Bézier or spline face (the triangles are measured afterwards as before):
- *Slim spans* (`SLIM`): a knot span narrower than a millionth of the surface's range in a parameter. Its Bézier net's second differences are about `w²` times the second derivative, against rounding of the coordinates. Only slim spans take the two bounds below; every other span keeps its Bézier bound, and surfaces without slim spans keep the grids they had (the mesh digests below).
- *The B-spline's own derivative nets* (*The NURBS Book* eq. 3.17): for a polynomial surface the second derivative over a span is in the hull of `R_ij = (p − 1)(Q_{i+1,j} − Q_ij)/(u_{i+p+1} − u_{i+2})`, `Q_ij = p (P_{i+1,j} − P_ij)/(u_{i+p+1} − u_{i+1})`, whose knot differences reach over `p − 1` spans, so a slim span between wide ones is bounded like its neighbours. A slim span takes the lower of this and its Bézier patch's bound. The span is found by its ends exactly (`knot_span`), never by a point inside it, which on a span an ulp wide could be the neighbour's. The cubic case: 1.4e6 → 7717.
- *How far the derivative travels across a slim span*: where every knot is repeated (degree 12) the nets are the Bézier nets and that does not help. Along any segment of a cell (a side, the diagonal, or a line from a corner through a triangle to the side across, which is how the Filip–Magedson–Markot bound reaches a triangle's inside), the surface is within a quarter of the segment's parameter length times `∫ |e''|` of the chord (Green's function at most `h/4`). The `du² S_uu` part integrates, across a part, to at most `|du|` times the part's travel `Σ_k max_j |ΔQ_kj|` over the differences of its first-derivative Bézier net (`∂²S/∂u² = (m − 1) Σ_k B_k(u) Σ_j B_j(v) ΔQ_kj` and `∫ B_k = 1/(m − 1)`, whatever `v` does on the way). That is a difference of first differences and keeps its digits. Across parts at least as wide as the cell it is what `Muu` gives. So a step `h` may go by `2 Muu(wide) + 2 Σ travel / h`, the sum over the narrow parts (narrower than the cell, of a slim span) that start within `2h` of each other (every row of `v` together, both sides of a seam), when that is lower than the largest bound. A grid is taken only when the bounds for its own step ask no more cells than it has; a few rounds look for the coarsest. The degree-12 face: 2515 → 63 cells along it.
- *The limit is on the cells, not on the sides*: `nu × nv ≤ max_segments²`, as many as a square grid at the limit (the vertex limit `4 (max_segments + 1)²` was already that), each count within a `u32`. A face long one way and narrow the other (a loft through sections of a few hundred waves, three sections high) needs thousands of cells along it and a few dozen across. The cells keep `a hu² + b hv² ≤ 8 × tolerance` with each parameter its half, which a face held at the limit one way did not need before (it got `max_segments` that way and the rest of the tolerance the other). A finer request is still refused as too fine, from the counts alone, with the side of a square grid of as many cells. The angle warp of a display or export mesh (`angle_warp`) keeps its limit of `max_segments` per side: a face whose chordal grid is longer gets no warp and keeps the chordal grid, which the refinement holds to the angle as on any face without one (moving the warp to the cell limit changed the rotor's export from 20 000 to 29 000 triangles).

*Bounded work.* A face the per-side limit refused at once may now be meshed, up to about a million cells (`max_segments²` at the default 1024; two million triangles) before anything else stops it: the triangle budget of a display mesh (`max_triangles`), and in the self-check `SELF_CHECK_WORK` (then `Spent`, which every caller refuses). A face beyond the cells is refused before its grid is made.

*Meshes that changed.* Release runs of `mesh_digests` (engine, STL bytes of the examples, showcase and benchmark models at coarse, normal and fine) and `mesh_hashes` (testkit, the reference bodies at three scales and three tolerances) on b2f793bd (base) and the branch, compared with `diff`. Only the vases of revolution of `mesh_hashes` at a tolerance of 1e-4 of their size change (degrees 2 and 5: 1 893 860 → 1 893 812 and 2 077 082 → 2 076 018 triangles, at all three scales; degree 3 still too fine, its message now giving 1089 instead of 1097). Those are rational faces held at 1024 cells along one parameter before, and now get the counts the tolerance asks for both ways: the grid that keeps the bound by construction (`the_cells_of_a_spline_face_keep_the_bound_within_the_tolerance` checks the deviation of the cells), and the triangles measured afterwards as on every face. Measured over every triangle against the surface (`mesh_hashes` › `vases_near_the_cell_limit_against_their_surfaces`, release, at scale 1): 0.8863 of the tolerance for degrees 2 and 5, before and after. Every digest of `mesh_digests` is the same. Before the slim-span rule and with the warp on the cell limit, the gear's three exports (positions; the same triangle counts), the rotor's coarse and fine and the villa's normal and fine had changed too.

### Helper threads only for work that lasts (2026-11-01, `body.rs` › `in_order`)
