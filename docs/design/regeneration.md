# 3DCraft design: Regeneration

> Moved verbatim from `docs/architecture.md` on 2026-10-15. Section numbers (§4.1, §5.3, …) in the text are those of that file before the split; [README.md](README.md) says where each one is now.

## From *Current state (2026-10-12)*

- **Regeneration:** `regen::regenerate` evaluates variables and builds each feature's body, with a status per feature (`Ok`, `Suppressed`, `RolledBack`, `Failed(reason)`); every body is run through `topo::check`. A bare number in a length parameter is taken in the document's unit. A sketch feature has no body: its result is the solved sketch (degrees of freedom, a state per constraint, solved coordinates, its frame in space), and `regen::settle_sketches` writes a solution back into the document, which the engine does at the end of every edit. No caching: a full rebuild per document revision, every sketch solved again.

**A feature that fails (2026-10-14, M8.5).** Every reference a feature holds is found again at each regeneration: the sketch an extrusion or a revolution sweeps, the body a transform, a mirror, a pattern or a boolean is made from, the face a sketch lies on or an extrusion ends on, the datum a sketch, a mirror, a revolution or a pattern is placed by, what a datum is placed by, and the variables in every expression. What holds for all of them, and is held to it by `crates/engine/tests/failed_features.rs` (every reference of every feature kind, broken in every way a command can break it):

- *The reason* is the feature's status `Failed(reason)`. It starts with the parameter as `document.inspect` shows it under `params` (`source`, `upTo`, `plane`, `through.0` for the first of the points a datum goes through, `size.x` for an expression) and names the feature it referred to and the name it lost: ``target: `Plate` is suppressed``, ``plane: there is no feature F2 for the face F2:endCap(e5,e6,e7,e8) (was it deleted?)``, ``about: `Plate` no longer has a face F2:side(e5); its faces are: F2:side(e6), …`` (what is there instead, up to twelve names), ``sketch: `Disc` comes after this feature in the history; move it before (feature.move)``. A reference made of two names (an edge between two faces) names both. A cap is the exception that does not fail: a reference to `endCap` or `startCap` survives a change of the outline it was named after, because all caps of one role lie in one plane.
- *What is made from a failed feature* fails with ``… `X` failed; fix it first``, never with an error of its own, and so on down the chain. `regen::root_cause` walks such a chain to its start, the one feature that failed for a reason of its own; `document.inspect`, `document.check` and the result of every feature command give it as `causedBy`, so an agent reads the features to fix without following the chain.
- *Everything else is untouched.* A failed feature has no body, no names and takes no body: the body a failed boolean or transform would have taken is its source's again. The test's oracle is a second computation: the document with the failed features deleted has exactly the remaining features and bodies (status, parameters, volume, area, centroid, counts, names, who took which body).
- *A circle* (a datum placed on a face of a body that is sketched on that datum; a feature pointed at itself) can be built in no order. The reason says so (``… comes after this feature in the history and is itself made from this feature, directly or through others: the two cannot both be built; point one of them at something else``) instead of sending the user to `feature.move`, which would only turn the failure round. Regeneration ends on any circle: a feature is built from the results before it and from nothing else.
- *Repair* is pointing the reference at another entity with the command that edits the feature, which keeps its id, its place in the history and everything that refers to it: `feature.edit` (`sketch` of an extrusion or a revolution; `axis` of a revolution: `x`, `y`, a line of the sketch or a datum axis; `source` of a moved, mirrored or patterned body; `target`, `tool` and `op` of a boolean; `upTo`; `plane` of a mirror; `axis` of a circular pattern), `sketch.edit` (`face`, `plane`) and `datum.plane`, `datum.axis`, `datum.point` with `replace`. After it the document is the one built that way from the start: the same features, parameters, bodies and names, which the test compares for every reference after every way of breaking it. A variable that was deleted is defined again. `feature.edit` refuses a feature pointed at itself, a `sketch` that is no sketch and a boolean whose target is its tool; whether what it is pointed at can be used (it comes earlier, it has a body nothing else took) is for regeneration to say, as when the feature was added.
- *Reorder.* `feature.move` takes any position and never refuses an order: a feature that then comes before something it is made from fails as above, with the remedy in the reason, and what is made from it fails naming it. Refusing would forbid the steps in between when several features are moved one after the other, and deleting and suppressing do not refuse to break a reference either; undo restores exactly. Which features are built depends only on what comes before what: the test moves each of sixteen features to each position and compares the failed set with one computed from the part's description. A move to where the feature is already changes nothing and is no undo step.
- *Roll back.* `history.rollback` puts the marker on a feature; the features after it are `RolledBack`, have no reason and no body and take none. The document is then, for everything up to the marker and for every body, the document that ends there (the test deletes the later features from a copy and compares). A feature before the marker that needs one after it fails with "comes after". Deleting the feature the marker is on leaves the marker on the one before, so what was rolled back stays rolled back; only when it was the first feature is the marker cleared. A feature added while rolled back goes in right after the marker, and the marker moves onto it (since 2026-10-14): it is built, and what was rolled back stays rolled back after it. Every command that adds a feature goes through one function, `commands::insert_feature`; a step that adds two (an extrusion cut into a body at once) puts both there in order. A feature put there that needs one still rolled back fails with "comes after", like any feature out of order. Undo removes it and puts the marker back.
- *Saved and reopened,* failed, suppressed and rolled-back features have the same states, reasons and bodies: none of it is stored, all of it is regenerated from the same history.
- *Incremental regeneration* gives the same as a full one through all of this. What a reason quotes is part of what the cache compares: the name and kind of every feature it is made from, also one that comes later, who took its body, and whether a later one is made from the feature itself.

### 5.5 Regeneration (`regen`)

`regen` walks `Document::features` in order and produces, per feature, a status (`Ok`, `Suppressed`, `Failed { reason }`, `Skipped` because an input failed) and the bodies that exist after it.

- **Incremental.** Each feature's result is cached under a key made of its own parameters (after expression evaluation) and the keys of its inputs. Editing feature *k* reuses 0..k-1 untouched and recomputes only the features that depend on what changed. *As built (M6, `regen/src/cache.rs`):* the key is the feature's definition, the values of its expressions, the document's units and tolerance, and for every feature it is made from (directly or through others) the stamp of that feature's result and who had taken its body. A rebuilt result that equals the old one keeps its stamp, so a change that makes no difference stops there. Sketch solves are cached on their own (a sketch solves the same wherever it lies), which also spares the solve that stores a sketch's solution after an edit. The cache lives with the open document; `regenerate_cached` returns exactly what `regenerate` returns, and the test suites hold it to that after every command. Measured (2026-10-13): one edit of one part of forty took 64 ms before the cache and 2 ms after.
- **Long rows (2026-10-15).** The context a feature's result is compared under lists every feature it is made from, directly or through others: in a row of cuts each made from the body the one before left, most of the history. Put together for every feature of every regeneration, that is the square of the length of the history per command (a thousand holes cut one command at a time: 593 s, of which building was 18 s). So the full context is put together only for a feature that may have a new one. The cache keeps, besides each feature's entry, the *trail* of the last regeneration: per feature in history order its `Step` (name and kind, the stamp of its result, the bodies it took). As regeneration goes, a feature that stands at the same place as the last time and whose step differs is marked, with the bodies it takes or took, and so is everything made from a marked feature, directly or through others (`Changed`: reverse edges of `FeatureKind::inputs`, plus every feature after it that is made from all earlier ones; each feature is marked at most once, so the marks of one regeneration cost the length of the history). A feature at the same place, made only from features before it, none of them marked, has the inputs it had: only its definition, the values of its expressions, units, tolerance, rollback and followed names are compared. Anything else (a feature moved, inserted or deleted before it, a reference to a later or missing feature, an id twice in the history) takes the full comparison as before. Either way what is reused is exactly what the full comparison reuses, and the rebuilt counts are the same. The names a fillet or a chamfer gives are followed to the newest body (`follow::feature`) before its context is known, for every feature of every regeneration; on the engine block that was most of the time of a command that only added a circle to a sketch. While no feature has been marked yet, a feature at the same place made only from earlier features follows its names as it did the last time (they depend on the results before it and the names and kinds of earlier features, all as they were), so the followed feature is taken from its entry. `RegenCache::last_run_work` (`Session::last_regeneration_work`) counts the entries of what features are made from that a regeneration looked at; `crates/engine/tests/long_chains.rs` holds adding a hole, setting a variable used at the end of the row and undoing to at most four per feature, the same per feature at 30 and 90 holes (about 2; the full comparison looked at 1425 for 91 features at 30 holes, growing with the square). `crates/engine/examples/regen_rows.rs` times rows of holes and steps files command by command. Measured (release, 2026-10-15, `craft3d-cli run` of the steps file, on a machine other agents loaded): 1000 holes cut one command at a time 593 s before, 29 s after; the engine block (330 steps) 13 to 14 s before, 2.4 s after; the turbofan (248 steps) unchanged at 16 to 23 s, nearly all of it its patterns (the fan's twenty blades 11.7 s) in the kernel; setting the variable of the turbofan's last pattern rebuilds one feature of 156 in 2 ms. `document.inspect` of the session and `craft3d-cli info` of the saved file are the same before and after for the four models measured, and the boolean corpus and the random runs give the same digests.
- **Variables first.** The variable table is evaluated in its stored order; a cycle or a dimension error fails only the features that use the affected variables.
- **Partial success is normal.** A failed feature does not discard the model: bodies up to the failure stay available and renderable, and the failure is data an agent can read (`document.inspect`). *As built (2026-10-14):* there is no `Skipped` status; a feature whose input failed is `Failed` with a reason that names the input, and `regen::root_cause` gives the feature to fix (§4, *A feature that fails*).
- **Background jobs.** Regeneration runs on the job pool from a snapshot and commits its result if the document has not changed in a conflicting way. *As built (2026-10-14):* no job pool; the desktop app runs whole commands, regeneration and meshing included, on one engine thread that owns the session while it works (§8.4), and regeneration reports each feature it rebuilds (`regen::progress::report`; the engine adds `measuring` and `meshing`) to a sink installed on that thread, which the window shows. Nothing reported changes what is built. Not cancellable yet.
- **Never through itself** (*as built 2026-10-15*, `regen/src/selfcheck.rs`). No feature hands on a body that passes through itself, whose shells overlap or lie inside each other the wrong way, or that touches itself; each operation either cannot make one, by the reasoning below, or is checked with the parameters of `document.check {"deep": true}` (`tess::body_self_intersections`, §5.2), and a finding fails the feature with two faces by their names. A body that cannot be meshed to look is refused too, with the tessellator's reason (`Crossing::Unknown`), as it was for lofts and sweeps.

  | Operation | Checked how | Why that is enough |
  |---|---|---|
  | block, cylinder, sphere, cone, torus | not | closed forms of valid sizes (a torus's tube may not reach its axis) |
  | extrude by a distance or through all, revolve | not | profiles do not touch or cross (`detect_profiles`) and nest even-odd; prisms along one direction, and solids of revolution of regions on one side of the axis turned by at most a full turn, do not either |
  | extrude up to a face | near the end caps | it is the prism cut by a tool with the target's surface: prism faces one group, end caps the other |
  | transform, mirror, a pattern whose instances are apart | not | an isometry of a body that does not; instances whose boxes (`Body::bbox`, conservative) are apart by more than the tolerance neither cross nor nest, and for a linear or circular pattern instance *i* and *j* are instance 0 and *j − i* moved |
  | boolean (also the one of extrude or revolve with `cut`, `join`, `intersect`), a pattern whose instances meet | near where the operands meet | every face is a piece of a face of one operand, which was checked or proven when it was made, or merged from several: pieces of one operand are one group, merged faces new |
  | chamfer, fillet, their chains and meets along freeform edges | near the new faces | made by booleans with tools: kept faces are pieces of the body's (one group); blend and corner faces new, and so is a kept face next to one or merged (it may have taken a tool's face in its plane); a blend face without a closed form (`rolling`) is new like any other, so a radius too large for what lies across the body is found |
  | thread (modeled) | near the groove | the groove is a helical sweep no one checked on its own: its faces are new |
  | loft, sweep, sweep along a helix | the whole body | new everywhere |
  | offset face, shell, delete face | the whole body | as before (2026-10-14); not yet made local |
  | move face, draft | near the faces that changed | every entity keeps its number (`kernel::tweak`): a face whose surface or one of whose vertices moved is new, every other face is the source's own, unchanged, one group (`face_ops::not_through_itself_where_changed`; 2026-10-15) |
  | thicken | the whole body | the layer is two offsets and a boolean, as a shell; not yet made local (2026-10-15) |
  | sketch, datum | — | no body |

  *Near what changed* is `tess::body_self_intersections_near`, which takes a role per face, `Known(group)` or `New`. A pair of faces of one known group is never looked at; the zone where pairs can meet is the box of every new face and the common part of the boxes of every two groups (per face, `Body::face_bbox`, grown by the first chordal tolerance and the tolerance); only faces whose box meets the zone are meshed, a shell whose box meets it is meshed whole when the body has several (so that where it lies can be told), the holes of flat faces that are far from the zone are filled before meshing (the face is the same face near everything looked at; a plate with a thousand holes is meshed with the few near the new one), and only triangles within ten chordal tolerances of the zone are searched. With every face new it is the whole check, finding for finding (`crates/testkit/tests/self_intersection.rs`). Measured (2026-10-15, release, a plate 4010 mm long with 400 holes): a new hole's boolean 10 ms, its check 1 to 3 ms (the whole check of that body 190 to 240 ms); the first cut of all 400 holes at once is checked over the whole plate: 140 ms on a boolean of 200 ms while the plate's long straight edges were one chord each and its top face's triangles fans across the plate, which the search cannot prune; 27 to 31 ms since the tessellator cuts such chords (2026-10-15, §6 *Long straight chords of flat faces*).

  *Touching itself* is a finding of its own (`tess::body_touches_itself`, on the B-rep and not the mesh, a sort along x): a vertex of a shell within twice the tolerance of another vertex of that shell, or of an edge of it that it is not an end of. Such a shell meets itself in a point or along a line (two walls whose outlines end in one point, as a hollowed hull whose inner and outer plans ended in one bow point did); `topo::check` passes it, no closed manifold mesh can be made of it, and its faces there do not say which side is inside. Two shells that touch each other (blocks that share an edge, which the random boolean runs make by the hundred) are each closed and are not this.

  *What it costs on a body of freeform faces (2026-10-15).* The impeller of the showcase (40 features, one body of 99 faces, 62 of them lofted, filleted or chamfered freeform faces) took about 90 s to build, and the check near what changed was suspected. Sampled (`eu-stack`, every busy thread) and built at 9a4970f, before the check, at db1d44b, which brought it, and after: the check is 1 to 2 % of the samples of every slow step but the two unions of blades and splitters with the hub, where its meshing is about 15 % (the two groups' boxes overlap nearly everywhere there, so most faces are meshed), and the build before the check was as slow (95 to 119 s of CPU against 96 to 141 s; the machine was loaded by other agents throughout, and the same build varies by half). The time was the booleans' own: every boolean on the finished body built the `PatchTree` of each of its freeform faces again, the shelf of trees being too small for them (§5.3, *Speed*), and `body.material` measured the body's mass and inertia exactly (§4.2). Both fixed; the run is 62 to 77 s of CPU and 32 to 40 s on the clock where it was 96 to 141 s and 53 to 100 s, the rest the unions and the patterns themselves (intersection curves of freeform faces, their classification) and `document.measure`.
