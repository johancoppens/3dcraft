# 3DCraft design: Blends: chamfers and fillets

Chamfers and fillets: the features (`doc::Chamfer`, `doc::Fillet`, `regen::blend`) and the kernel (`kernel::blend`, its front ends `kernel::chamfer` and `kernel::fillet`, and `blend::rolling`). Method 3 of the map ([`../architecture.md`](../architecture.md) §5). The Method says how blends are made today; *Findings* keeps, per finding, only what still describes live code; *History* has one dated line for everything else.

## Method

**What a feature holds.** A list of edges, each as the names of the two faces it lies between (the order says which face a chamfer's first distance or angle is measured on), a size (a chamfer's one or two distances, or a distance and an angle; a fillet's radius) and `chain`. At each regeneration every pair is found again (`regen::edges_between`), followed to the newest body of its chain; with `chain` an edge stands for the edges that continue it smoothly (`blend::tangent_chain`). New faces are `F9:chamfer(k)` / `F9:fillet(k)` (numbered by place in space when a reference gives several), corners `chamferCorner` / `filletCorner`; kept faces are copies, instance 0.

**Two constructions, chosen per edge**, being merged into one (*One blend construction* below). `blend::blend_inner` plans every edge (`plan`); an edge no closed form covers goes in place with every edge that shares a vertex with it (`mixed`); of the others, the routed class (`in_place`: a straight edge between two planes, or next to a cylinder along it, whose ends are vertices of three edges where no other requested edge ends, every face there with one loop) goes in place too and the rest by the tools (`construct`). Sources are carried back to the body the feature started from (`carry_back`).

- *Closed form: a tool and the boolean.* Where the section across the edge is the same all along (a straight edge between planes or along a cylinder, a circular edge between a plane, a cylinder or a cone about its axis), the section (two lines or arcs and the fillet's arc or the chord) is extruded or revolved into a tool solid (`tool`), subtracted at a convex edge and added at a concave one (`combine`). The fillet face is a cylinder or a torus, the chamfer face a plane or a cone. Where an edge ends the tool runs past the vertex when the other edges there turn like it, ends square at the vertex otherwise, or is cut in the plane of a slanted face it runs into (`lengthen`, `end_plane`, `trim`). Two blends that meet run out into each other; three of one kind and one radius meet in a corner (a flat triangle for chamfers, a piece of the ball for fillets), made by one more boolean (`corners`, `ball_corners`). The tools of a chain are combined one by one; the boolean takes the contact where one goes on in the other.
- *In place: the rolling ball* (`blend::rolling`). Balls of the radius touching both faces are found in planes across the edge (for a chain and round a ring, square to a cubic with continuous curvature through the centres found); the arcs between their feet (a chamfer: the lines between points at the distances) are interpolated into one B-spline surface, refined until it is within a quarter of the tolerance of the exact section between samples and tangent to both faces within the bound `bend` (the angular tolerance, or the angle the linear tolerance makes over the size); each pass halves every step over those bounds and every step the halving can push over them (`to_split`). The borders are the surface's own iso-curves, proven on their faces between the samples (`geom::certify`). Every open edge on its own is one construction (`blend_open`): sections from the middle out past both ends, against the edge's faces, or piece by piece where a border crosses a smooth edge of its face (*Borders carried across a smooth edge*); the surface exact where a translation carries the edge and both faces into themselves (`exact`); cut where it crosses the third face at each end; spliced in as a chain of one piece (`chain::splice_one`). A ring is the closed surface between the edge and a new border (`ring`, `splice_ring`). Chains share a section at each joint; two fillets that meet at an angle are trimmed against each other (`chain::meet`). At an end the corner is followed along the boundary with the third face (`walk_on`), the neighbours are shortened, and a face beside it may be grown (`NurbsSurface::extended`). A border may not come within ten tolerances of another edge of its face, proven (`stays_on`); no loop of a face may be left in what the blend takes (`no_stranded_loop`, `no_loop_behind`).

**Never a wrong solid.** Whether a size fits is not estimated: the blend is built and the result held against what a blend is (every blend face present, in one piece, bordering exactly its neighbours and the faces at the ends of its edge: `finish`; the boolean's own checks), a fillet's tangency is measured along its borders, `topo::check` runs, and regeneration checks near the new faces for self-intersection. A blend that does not build is searched for the largest size that does (`blend::fit`, *What fits*), every edge of that size together; refused as too large with nothing that fits only where every failure is evidence of no room from the result itself; every search is bounded (48 builds). The two constructions and the checks still sampled (*Fitted curves proven*) are structural debt (the map's §9).

**A fillet's offset is the fillet rebuilt** (`blend::rebuild`, 2026-10-18): a face operation that moves a rolling-ball fillet with the two faces it rolls along rolls a ball of the radius moved by the distance between their offsets, in planes across the old fillet's border started from its old centres, refined as above, in the old face's parameters ([`face-operations.md`](face-operations.md) › *Offset of a fillet: rebuilt*).

**Refused, with the reason**: vertex blends of three or more fillets in other arrangements, outside and inside blends that meet, two blends of one kind that meet where the third edge is of the other (the ball would roll over that edge), edges that end at four or more faces, a freeform edge that ends where it goes on straight into another edge (fillet the chain), blends whose border runs over a sharp edge of its face, or over a smooth one in a chain, a ring or a meet, or over more than one smooth edge from one end, or whose end runs over more than one face, creased joints, and the rows of *Remaining special cases*.

## Remaining special cases, with the general method owed

Conditions that exist for one configuration and are not (yet) a general method. Each is behaviour today: removing it changes results, so it waits for the general method named (most are steps of *One blend construction*). Iteration caps and the tolerances every fitted curve is held to are not listed: they are the general method's own.

## Findings

> The findings were moved from `docs/architecture.md` on 2026-10-15 (§ numbers in older text are that file's before the split; [README.md](README.md) says where each is now) and shortened to what still describes live code on 2026-10-17.

### A fillet along an earlier round of its own radius (2026-10-21, an R1 blocker)

**Finding.** "Round all edges of a box in two steps", the upright edges first and then the rim of the top as a chain, was refused for every radius from half the rounds' on: along each arc of the rim (the top of an upright round, radius `R`) the ball of radius `ρ` has its centre `R − ρ` from the round's axis, so for `ρ ≥ R/2` its torus reaches its own axis (`Reason::Spindle`), and at `ρ = R` the search said "too large" (the border on the top reaches the axis).

**Method.** At `ρ = R` (the centre within the tolerance of the axis) the ball no longer turns about the axis: the fillet along the arc is a piece of that one ball, which `revolve` already makes a sphere for a section centred on the axis. Two changes make it a blend: the spindle refusal takes only a centre off the axis (`from_axis > tol`), and `finish` accepts a border drawn into a point on the axis (`Plan::border_on_axis`: the foot on that face as far toward the axis as the rim is from it) as meeting its face there, at a vertex, when that face is still in the result (a rod's top filleted with the rod's radius loses its top, which stays "too large"). The result is the box rounded all round in one step, ball for ball. **Past the axis** (`ρ > R`) no ball of the size touches the face across the rim; the plan records the radius that puts the centre on the axis (`Plan::past_axis`: `own = ρ R / (R − from_axis)`, `from_axis` being linear in `ρ`), `blend_inner` builds at it, and when that builds says "too large" with it as what fits (between it and half of it nothing is built, so it is the largest); when it does not, the search runs as before.

**Conditions and why they are general.** The centre on the axis within the linear tolerance is the test `revolve` makes a sphere by, so plan and tool agree; the border on the axis is measured from the plan's own feet and the rim's radius, for either face and any rim (convex or concave, a rod's or a round's); the face that loses its border must still have a piece, as `LostNeighbour` asks of every blend.

**Tests** (fail before): `crates/kernel/tests/fillet_along_round.rs` (the two-step box against the closed form `wdh − (4 − π) r² h − 2 (w + d − 4r)(1 − π/4) r² − 4 (π r³/4 − π r³/6)` and against the one-step body, volume and area, four balls about points on the rounds' axes, at three scales and turned; shelled as thick as its rounds; 1.5 refused as spindle, 2.5 and 3 too large with 2 as what fits, 0.5 four tori as before), `crates/engine/tests/fillet.rs` › `a_box_rounded_in_two_steps_of_one_radius` (checked deep, an edit of the box's width followed, shelled 2 open at the bottom, 3 too large saying 2). **Still refused:** `R/2 ≤ ρ < R` (a spindle torus: its spline twin from `revolve` builds the tool, but the boolean refuses its tangent contact with the next fillet's cylinder over too large an area).

**Within the tolerance of the round's radius (2026-10-22, the m9-r1 review).** A radius `ρ = R ± δ` with `δ` up to the tolerance puts the ball's centre within the tolerance of the axis, so the plan takes it as on the axis and builds it; the build fails for most such `δ` (the boolean: the ends of the straight fillets of radius `ρ` and the round of radius `R` are `δ` apart, "at the edge of tolerance"), and the search for what fits scaled every size down by one multiple and landed below `R/2` (0.9977 for `R = 2`), past the spindle band, though `R` builds. Now `Plan::past_axis` records the radius on the axis for a centre within the tolerance of it as well (`PastAxis::within`), and when the build fails, `on_axis` builds at that radius (once, no retry of the retry): if it builds and is smaller than `ρ`, "too large" with it as what fits; if it is larger (the centre short of the axis), the spindle refusal, which names the rim's own radius as one that builds, as for the sizes below. A centre past the axis by more than the tolerance is tried before the build, as before. What the band does now, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6 with `δ` from a few ulps to 1.1 tolerances: `R + δ` builds (a few ulps, where the booleans cannot tell the sizes apart) or is too large with `R`, which builds; `R − δ` builds or is a spindle, and `R` builds. *Test:* `fillet_along_round.rs` › `radii_within_the_tolerance_of_the_rounds_build_or_say_what_fits` (before: the false largest at all three scales). Building the band (snapping `ρ` to `R`) would be a snap of the kind D-open-16 asks about, so it is not done.

### Chamfers as built (M9, 2026-10-14)

`doc::Chamfer` has `edges` (references), `size` (`ChamferSize`: `Equal { distance }`, `TwoDistances { distance, second }`, `DistanceAngle { distance, angle }`, all expressions) and `chain`; it makes its body from the body of an earlier feature and takes it, like a transform. `kernel::chamfer::chamfer(body, edges, tolerance)` takes per edge the edge, its first face and a size, and returns the body with a source for every face (`Kept`, `Chamfer` of which requested edges, `Corner` at which vertex), edge and vertex.

- *An edge is the names of its two faces, in order* (`[F1:face(1), F1:face(2)]`): the order says which face the first distance is measured along and which the angle is taken from; after a boolean the edge's own name is that pair; when a later edit cuts the edge in two both pieces stay chamfered; no numbered name is stored. The price: two faces that meet in several edges are chamfered along all of them, and one of several cannot be picked (a reference by the edge's own name, for that case: not built, owed). The chamfered body is the body of the feature the first face is of, after the names are followed to the newest body of their chain.
- *Resolution.* Each pair is found again with `regen::edges_between`; a face that is gone, or faces that no longer meet, fail the feature with the parameter and the names; what is made from a failed chamfer fails naming it (`causedBy`). With `chain` each edge stands for the edges that continue it smoothly (`tangent_chain`: at each end the one other edge that leaves the vertex straight on, `straight_on`); along the chain the first face is the one the edges share, or what continues it.
- *Which face gets which distance.* `distance` along the first face, `second` along the second, each along the face at right angles to the edge; `angle` between the first face and the chamfer face inside the corner, so the second face is cut `distance · sin(angle) / sin(angle + θ)` back (θ the angle between the faces across the edge; the sine rule in `distances`). Turning the pair round turns the distances round.
- *Names.* A face that is still there is a copy, instance 0, with its aliases. The face of reference `k` is `F9:chamfer(k)`, or `F9:chamfer(k,0)`, `F9:chamfer(k,1)`, … in the order of where the edges' middles lie (x, then y, then z) when it gave several; the flat corner is `F9:chamferCorner(i,j,k)`; chamfer faces in one plane that became one face are named after the first, the others aliases. Edges and vertices are named by their faces (`Names::name_entities`).
- *The frame of a flat chamfer face* (`reframe`): z out of the body; x along the edge, in the direction of the cross product of the directions into the first and the second face; the origin where the cut line on the first face starts in that direction. The in-place construction gives its plane the same frame.
- **The tool** (`tool`). A straight edge between two planes: `kernel::extrude` of the triangle (edge, edge + d₁ along the first face, edge + d₂ along the second). A circular edge between a plane, a cylinder or a cone about its axis: the triangle in the half-plane through the axis, turned by `kernel::revolve`; the face is a cone. Convex or concave is read from the solid (`across`), never from the direction of the edge. Every tool is made from the body as given, so the order of the edges does not change the solid.
- **Where an edge ends.** Every other edge at the vertex convex like it (or every one concave): the tool goes on past the vertex (past where the chamfer plane cuts the other edges, with a margin; by an angle for an arc). Otherwise it ends across the edge at the vertex, which is right against a wall square to the edge and where the next tool of a chain begins; against one plane at a slant it is cut in that plane (*Blend findings of the engine block*). Any other end leaves a face nobody asked for, which `finish` refuses.
- **Corners.** Two chamfers that meet: a mitre (the line both planes share). Three of one kind that meet in a vertex with nothing else: the flat face through the three points where the cut lines cross, made by one more boolean with a block against that plane; when one chamfer is larger than the other two reach together it takes the corner whole and there is no corner face. It is what OpenCascade gives (a 4 × 3 × 2 block with all twelve edges by 0.25: volume, area and 26/48/24 counts to the last digits, through FreeCAD) and Fusion's corner type "chamfer"; the pointed mitre corner is not offered. More than three edges, or convex and concave chamfers, meet as their half-spaces.
- **Never a wrong solid.** After the last boolean the result is held against what a chamfer is (`finish`): every chamfer face there, in one piece, bordering both neighbours and nothing but those, the faces at the ends of its edge and the blends and corners there; no other face of a tool left; no chamfer face part of a face of the body. Anything else is `KernelError::Chamfer` with the edge; the boolean's refusals are `ChamferBoolean`; a size that does not build is searched (*What fits*) and refused as `ChamferTooLarge` with the largest that fits.

### Fillets as built (M9, 2026-10-14)

`doc::Fillet` has `edges` (references, as a chamfer's), `radius` and `chain`; everything a chamfer does with its references a fillet does with the same code (`regen::blend`). `kernel::fillet::fillet(body, edges, tolerance)` takes per edge the edge and a radius and returns the body with its sources (`Kept`, `Fillet`, `Corner`).

- *An edge* is its two faces; their order says nothing: turning a pair round gives the same body and names. Names as a chamfer's: `F9:fillet(k)`, `F9:fillet(k,0)`, …, the corner `F9:filletCorner(i,j,k)`.
- *What can be put on a fillet face:* a datum axis (`datum.axis` with `face`: the cylinder's or the torus's own axis) and its edges with its neighbours (a later blend takes them by the pair of faces). Not a sketch: the face is not flat. A face next to a fillet keeps its frame.
- *After a fillet the neighbouring edge is part of a chain:* the top left edge of a block whose top front edge is rounded runs on smoothly in the arc where the fillet meets the left face; a blend of that edge alone stops in the middle of a chain and is refused ("does not end cleanly … fillet the edges it continues in as well"); with `chain` it runs round the corner.
- **The section and the tool.** The ball touches each face `r / tan(θ/2)` from the edge (`half_tan`), its centre on the bisector; the tool's section is the kite between the edge and the two tangent points less the sector of the ball, extruded along a straight edge (a **cylinder**) or revolved about a circular one (a **torus**). Every edge's tool is combined on its own, also in a chain: the boolean takes the contact of a cylinder that goes on in a torus since 2026-10-14 (`a_rod_that_goes_on_in_a_ring` in `boolean_surfaces.rs`) and of a torus that goes on in another torus (the closed form of geometry §5.1; `fillet_contact.rs` › `a_chain_of_arcs_that_go_on_in_each_other`, Pappus to 1e-13 at three scales). **`join` removed (2026-10-18, consolidation round):** until then the tools of a chain were joined into one body before the boolean (the two ends that lay against each other taken out and their edges and vertices made one), the one topological step of the tool path, written for a contact the boolean refused in 2026-10-14. Measured first with a switch (the filletcontact experiment, not in the tree): the kernel's blend, chamfer, fillet, offset-fillet and fillet-contact tests pass both ways and all 59 chained blends among their 1083 calls are the same bit for bit; the engine's chained blends differed in their last bits. Landed (`rm-join`, 61faa6e) and measured bit for bit on every `build` result: faces, edges, vertices and names the same everywhere; of the engine blend tests' 12 distinct chained results 4 moved by one ulp of volume or area (all in `fillet.rs` › `a_boss_on_the_face_next_to_a_fillet_and_blends_on_neighbouring_edges`: the chamfer chain and the fillet chain round a rounded corner, at two of three scales), and of the engine block's 15 chained builds 2 (one ulp of volume; one area by three ulp): an expectation change logged in the dev log.
- **Where an edge ends** is the chamfer's rule.
- **Corners.** Two fillets that meet with the other edges sharp run out into each other in the curve their surfaces share (two cylinders at a square corner: an arc of an ellipse; OpenCascade's volume to 1e-11 and counts). Three straight fillets of one kind and one radius that meet in a vertex with nothing else: the piece of the ball that touches the three faces between the three planes through its centre across the edges (`ball_corners`, `ball_tool`: the cell those planes cut out, less the ball; why that piece is argued at `ball_corners`). Any other arrangement of three or more filleted edges at a vertex is refused (`CornerKind`, `CornerRadii`).
- **Never a wrong solid.** The chamfer's checks, and the tangency of every edge a fillet face shares with a neighbour or a ball corner at five points (`smooth_seams`, to the angular tolerance and never closer than 1e-9); anything else is `KernelError::Fillet` and no body.

| a radius too large for a neighbour; fillets that meet in the middle of a face | `FilletTooLarge` with what fits, or `Fillet` from the final check |

*Tests:* `crates/engine/tests/fillet.rs` (closed forms at three scales, deep check, the axis through `datum.axis`, names, upstream edits, repair, a boss on the face next to a fillet, blends after blends on neighbouring edges, save and reopen, undo and redo, everything refused), `crates/kernel/tests/fillet.rs`. *Cost:* one boolean per edge or chain and four per ball corner (a block's twelve edges and eight corners: 44 booleans, about 0.16 s in the test profile).

### Blends next to a cylinder (M9, 2026-10-14)

A straight edge on a cylinder is one of its rulings, and a plane through it holds the axis' direction: the two faces look the same in every plane across the edge, and so does the blend (`blend::section`). In that plane each face is a line or a circle (`Trace`). A fillet is the circle of the radius tangent to both: its centre where the two curves moved by the radius cross (line and line, line and circle, circle and circle, in closed form; of two crossings the one nearest the ball between the tangent planes). A chamfer is the chord between the points the distances away along each curve (along the arc on a cylinder); with an angle the chord leaves the first point at the angle to the first curve. The tool is the extrusion of the section; the fillet face is a cylinder, the chamfer face a plane. Since step 3 (*Borders carried across*) an isolated such edge goes in place, with the routed chamfer's distances the section's chords. Corners of three such blends are not made. Tests: `crates/kernel/tests/blend_cylinders.rs` (a halved rod with one and both cut edges rounded, a lens's tip, the foot of a half-round boss, a halved rod chamfered along the diameter and the arc; closed forms by Green's theorem, three scales, a tilted frame).

**Two tip fillets of a lens (F1).** Both rims of the end of an extruded lens make two tori that run out into each other at the sharp tips. Two changes to the boolean (`kernel/src/boolean/section.rs`) make it: (1) a stretch of an intersection curve that is out of either face at its middle is dropped before it is held against the other face's boundary; (2) an approximated (spline) edge that leaves a surface tangentially at one of its ends meets the surface at its end and not where it crosses it again within the tolerance (an exact edge that lies on the surface up to a point is not touched). Test: `both_tip_edges_of_a_cambered_blade` (radii 0.05 to 0.3, both orders, three scales, tilted).

### Blends along freeform edges as built (M9, 2026-10-14)

`kernel::blend::rolling`: **a local operation**, built in place, the borders are edges of both faces from the start. (It was made local because the fillet face is tangent to both neighbours along curves with no closed form, a contact the boolean refused; since 2026-10-18 the boolean takes it, and the body less the tool a rolling fillet takes away is that fillet: [`booleans.md`](booleans.md) › *Tangent contact along an edge*.)

- **The face.** Each ball is found in a plane across the edge (past the ends on the edge's curve continued, a spline's end spans as the polynomials they are): its centre moves to where the two tangent planes at the nearest points, moved by the radius, cross the plane (`Solver::ball`: Newton steps on the two distances, never more than half the radius at once) until it settles; then it has to be the radius from both surfaces along their normals, on the right side. The face meets the ball in the short arc between its feet, a rational quadratic (`Section::net`). Which planes pick the balls does not change the surface. A chamfer is, in each plane, the line between the points the distances away from the edge's point (`Solver::cut`).
- **The approximation.** The arcs of a row of planes are joined by interpolating their homogeneous control points with a cubic (`surface_through`, `homogeneous`), so the surface passes through every sample; between two samples it is held against the exact section to a quarter of the linear tolerance, and a fillet's normal along its borders and across it to `bend`; samples are added until both hold (`refine`, at most 4096, laid through by a banded solve). The borders are the surface's iso-curves.
- **Where it ends.** Each end has to be a vertex of exactly three faces (`end_at`): the blend is continued past it until a whole section lies beyond the third face (`march_out`) and cut where it crosses that face's surface, along a cubic fitted to points found exactly (`cut_curve`, `sign_change`), proven within a quarter of the tolerance of both surfaces. The two other edges are shortened to the corners (`cut_back`), the third face gets the cut in place of its corner (`insert_cut`), the edge becomes the first border; every other entity keeps its number.
- **A closed edge** (one vertex that no other edge passes through: the rim of a slanted cut of a rod, the mouth of a slanted hole) has no ends: the sections go round it, a fillet's in the planes square to the closed path of the centres (`closed_path`), joined by the closed cubic with continuous curvature (`ring_surface`), refined the same way; the edge becomes the first border, the second border is new (`splice_ring`).
- **Never a wrong solid.** A ball that does not settle or does not fit (a face curves more tightly than the radius under it, as the fan blade's suction side at its leading edge for a radius of 0.049 mm: `FreeSurface`), an arc of half a turn, a cut not found, or an approximation that does not converge is refused with the edge and the reason; the body passes `topo::check` and the tangency is measured again along the borders at seven points (`tangent_along`, ten times `bend`). A large blend running into a face across the body is found by regeneration on a mesh (`tess::body_self_intersections`).
- **Tests** (`crates/kernel/tests/blend_freeform.rs`): a straight edge between a plane and a cone held point by point against the definition of a rolling-ball fillet (a point is kept exactly when a ball of the radius behind both faces holds it; Dykstra's projections) at three scales; the rim of a slanted cut of a rod; the same edge chamfered; a straight edge between two spline walls against the 2D fillet of the two Béziers times the height. Engine (`crates/engine/tests/blend_freeform.rs`, `chamfer.rs`): the turbofan's lofted fan blade's leading and trailing edges, a conical casing's cut edge, the rings of a slanted cut and a slanted hole, a spline wall chamfered against an independent integral.

### Chains and meets of freeform blends (M9, 2026-10-15)

`kernel::blend::rolling::chain` and `chain::meet`. **Grouping** (`chain::group`): where two requested edges end in one vertex and go on into each other (`continues`) they are a *joint* of a chain (walked from either end, or round to a closed chain); where they meet at an angle a *meet* (`chain::meet`); three or more at a vertex are refused. A closed-form edge that touches a freeform one joins it and is built in place too (`mixed`). A single open edge with no joint is a chain of one piece (*Method*).

- **One family of balls, a face per edge** (`along_spine`). A fillet chain is followed along the path of the balls' centres (`walk`: from a ball, a step ahead along n₁ × n₂, the next ball in the plane square to it; the step halves where the ball cannot be found or the path turns by more than a quarter radian, grows to a 24th of the edge where it runs straight). Each edge's face uses the planes square to a clamped cubic with continuous curvature through its balls (`path_through`, `clamped_tangents`), turned at the joints into the joint ball's plane by a correction that fades out over half the edge, flat to the third derivative at both ends (`Spine::at`; a family only C1 stalls the refinement), so the faces on both sides of a joint end in the same arc, their shared edge. A chamfer chain keeps the planes across the edges, which its lines depend on (`compute`). Both start from the same prelude and joint planes (`lay`, `joint_planes`).
- **Joints.** Both faces go on, or one goes on and the other changes into a face tangent to it across a smooth edge that leaves the vertex (cone into torus into plane along the casing's cut edge), or a seam of one face passes through the vertex (crossed as a smooth edge between the face and itself). Where a face changes, the shared ball is the one whose foot on the changing side lies on the smooth edge (`switch_ball`: bisection along that edge); the smooth edge is shortened to it, the vertex moves to the other foot, a new vertex and the arc are added (`splice_chain`). Tangency across every joint is measured (`smooth_joints`, ten times `bend`).
- **Meets** (two fillets of one radius ending in one vertex at an angle; three edges and three faces there, the third edge sharp, all convex or all concave). What is kept is what both fillets keep: each fillet's face where it lies inside the other's rounded corner and inside its far face. Both faces run on past the vertex and are trimmed per point across them by bisection on the larger of the two distances (`work_out`, `trim`); the two run out into each other in the curve they share, from where their borders on the shared face cross (`cross_borders`); the one whose border on its own face reaches the far face first is also cut by the far face; the third edge is shortened. Both reaching it in one point gives a vertex of four edges. Where the two run into each other there is a crease, as between two closed-form fillets that meet.
- **Tests** (`crates/kernel/tests/blend_chains.rs`): the rims of the cap of an extruded smooth outline against the swept volume (Pappus-Guldinus along a plane curve), three scales, tilted; a casing's cut edge through its root fillet against the rolling-ball definition; a chain with a closed-form edge apart; E6's boss foot; a half cone cut by a tilted plane, both top edges meeting at the cut edges, convex and concave, every point of a grid against the definition (Dykstra's projections onto the eroded convex sets); a lofted blade's tip rounded all round. Engine (`crates/engine/tests/blend_chains.rs`): the turbofan's core casing cut by its block, refilleted after an edit; the lofted blade's tip; E6 refused at 3 mm and built at 1 mm.
- **Not built** (refused with the reason): a border that runs over a smooth edge in the middle of an edge of a chain (E6 in the full engine block); concave fillets that meet round a sharp convex edge (a blade's root loop: the roll-over patch); a chain whose two ends meet at one vertex; chamfers whose face changes at a joint, or that meet.

### What fits, decided by building (M9, 2026-10-15)

`blend::fit::explain`, when a build fails in a way a smaller size can mend (`sized`): the sizes of every edge of the feature's size change together (`group`). First a quarter of the size at a time until one builds; then the sizes at which a border can first reach something of its face (`events`: a vertex, the nearest point of an edge, what another blend takes of the face, the axis of a circular edge, where a fillet's torus reaches its own axis), searched with builds just past (64 tolerances) and just before (4, 64, 1024) each; the event found is reported; where none explains the failure the size is bisected on builds alone, to a few tolerances. Too large with nothing that fits only where every failure is evidence of no room (`no_room`: the blend reaches a face, takes a neighbour away, comes out in pieces, runs past an edge or off its face); a refusal of the boolean or the sweep at every size is reported as it is (the boolean's saying that smaller sizes fail the same way, `smaller_too`). 48 builds at most; a spent budget ends the search with the original refusal. `fit` builds through the same router as the feature (`construct`), so the search builds whichever construction the edges take.

The rules of the estimate it replaced, and how building covers them: a boundary that runs on smoothly past its first piece (E2, E5) and an edge beyond an arc's sector (E3): the build sees the face as it is; a cut line exactly into a corner (E7): the boolean builds it, as three chamfers in one feature; a face that pinches to nothing beside the edge (E8): no size builds, refused with the boolean's own reason; the axis: one more event. *Cost of a refusal:* a few builds (in the kernel tests about 2 ms for a simple one; E8's chain, where nothing builds down to the smallest size, about 230 ms); a refused `feature.edit` on a model costs about what its regeneration does. *Still open:* events along a curved edge are found among 64 samples; a size between events is reported to a few tolerances.

### Fitted curves proven on their surfaces (consolidation, 2026-10-16)

Each fitted curve of a rolling blend is accepted by a proof (`geom::certify`, method in [`geometry.md`](geometry.md)), not by samples: the cut at an end on the blend face and the third face (`cut_curve`); a meet's trim on the fillet face it trims (`meet::trim`; its other side, a maximum of two signed distances, is held at the middles); the edge going on to a corner on both faces (`going_on`); each border on its face between every two samples, at most 1024 pieces a step (`refine`); a border staying on its face (`stays_on`: `curves_apart` to ten tolerances, a pair not told apart refused; its test, a border bowing towards another edge, pins the behaviour but does not tell this from the old chords and Gauss-Newton, which caught it too: what changed is that nothing rests on Gauss-Newton converging). A proof says a curve is within the tolerance of its face's *surface*, continued past its domain; staying within the face is `stays_on`'s. A spline face that cannot be split into Bézier patches refuses the blend as not approximated. Tests in `blend/rolling.rs` and `rolling/chain/meet.rs`: one per site, a face that waves between the points the old checks looked at; the cut, the trim, the edge to a corner and the borders fail with the proof switched off, the `stays_on` test does not (above). **Left sampled:** the blend surface's interior against the exact sections (no derivative bounds to prove against; 9 points across, at the middles); a fillet's tangency along its borders (`tangent_along`, seven points); the events of `fit` (64 samples); the sweep along a helix.

### One blend construction: design (2026-10-16)

The map's debt row "two blend systems": the target is **one construction, the in-place one, with closed forms as exact surfaces, not a separate pipeline.** A tool needs the boolean to take the tangent contact between the blend face and its neighbours, which it can only where that contact has a closed form; the in-place construction makes the borders edges of both faces from the start, and its cost does not grow with the body.

- **The section is the same for every edge** (a ball's arc, a chamfer's chord); ends, chains, joints, meets and corners are the same steps for every edge.
- **The surface is chosen by a symmetry**: where one motion carries the edge and both faces into themselves, the blend face is the first section swept by it, in closed form (a translation: cylinder, plane; a rotation: torus, cone). Otherwise the fitted B-spline.
- **Edges in closed form where their two surfaces meet in closed form** (`exact::make_exact`): a line, a circle or an ellipse through the edge's vertices and its fitted curve's middle; otherwise the fitted curve, proven on both.
- **Corners are vertex patches of the same construction**: a sphere where three fillets' balls come to rest in one, a plane for chamfers; the general patch where not.

| The tool path does | The in-place path | Covered by |
|---|---|---|
| Exact cylinders, tori, cones, planes, spheres; exact edges | exact by translation (`exact`), fitted otherwise | the rotation (step 4) |
| Chamfers by a distance and an angle | the routed class by the plan's distances; refused elsewhere (`FreeAngle`) | the chord at the angle in every plane |
| Ends: past the vertex, square at it, cut in a slanted plane | the end cut against the third face of a vertex of three faces | the end cut carried round the faces in turn (F9, step 7) |
| Two blends that meet with the third edge sharp | fillets: `chain::meet`; chamfers refused | the meet's trim in closed form, chamfer meets (step 5) |
| Three blends in a vertex: ball corner, flat chamfer corner | refused | the corner patch (step 7) |
| Chains of lines and arcs: a tool per edge and the boolean | joints ending in a shared section | exact surfaces per piece (step 6) |
| Blend faces in one surface merged into one face | never made | faces of one feature on one exact surface merged (step 8) |
| The whole body seen: a blend that reaches a face elsewhere is found (`Reaches`) | the faces at the edge (`stays_on`); regeneration on a mesh | a loop wholly in the strip refused (done: *Holes*); the blend face held against the other faces (step 8) |
| A border across a smooth edge or a seam of its face | done for single edges (2026-10-16, *Borders carried across*) | chains, rings, meets |
| What fits by building | the same router (`fit` through `construct`); `FreeTooLarge`, `FreeRunsOver` count as no room | done (step 1) |

**What changes for users.** The same solids within the tolerance; a blend face is a cylinder, torus, cone, plane or sphere wherever it is one now (a datum axis on a fillet face, a chamfer face's frame and the STEP export keep what they have); names from the sources, which both constructions report alike, so **persistent names stay as they are**; an edge the blend only shortens keeps its number. A step that would change a name (a merged face, a corner) is a format or expectation matter, logged before it lands.

**Migration, in small steps, each keeping every test green** (kernel `fillet`, `chamfer`, `blend_*`; engine `fillet`, `chamfer`, `blend_*`, `benchmark`, `panic_hunt`; the engine block and the turbofan with no failed feature, the same volumes and names): 1. exact surfaces in place, a straight edge between two planes whose ends are lone (done 2026-10-16); 2. a chamfer by a distance and an angle in that class (done 2026-10-16); 3. a straight edge next to a cylinder along it (done 2026-10-16); 4. the rotation (torus, cone), arcs first, then rings; 5. meets of closed-form edges (mitres); 6. chains of lines and arcs (`join` already removed, 2026-10-18); 7. corners and ends at vertices of four or more faces; 8. faces merged in one surface, the whole-body reach check, then the tool path (`tool`, `combine`, `lengthen`, `end_plane`, `trim`, `corners`, `ball_corners`, `mixed`) is removed and `section.rs` remains as the closed form of the section.

**Step 1 as built.** `blend::in_place` and `construct` route the class; `rolling::exact` (`motion`, `Moved`: the march past the ends asks no ball of the solver; `swept`, `make_exact`) gives the face its cylinder or plane and each edge its closed-form curve where it runs through the vertices; a chamfer face gets the tools' frame (`reframe`). A border that runs exactly into the corner where the next edge turns (E7) takes that edge whole (`cut_back`, `take_whole`), and `compact` drops the edges no loop uses and the vertices no edge has, keeping the order of the rest. The engine block's 24 such edges went in place (338 to 314 booleans); every volume and area to 2.3e-16 relative, every edge and vertex name the same. Tests: `crates/kernel/tests/fillet.rs` › `an_edge_built_in_place_has_an_exact_face_and_exact_edges`, `a_hole_wholly_inside_the_strip_a_fillet_takes_is_refused`.

### Holes in what a blend takes (merge review, 2026-10-16)

A hole lying wholly in the material a blend built in place removes was left outside its face and passed `topo::check`. The general methods: **`topo::check` finds a loop that lies outside its face** (`Defect::StrandedLoop`, `topo::stranded`, [`topology.md`](topology.md); on planes), for every operation; **after every splice in place** (a single edge, a ring, a chain, the meets) no loop may be stranded (`no_stranded_loop`), else the blend is refused as reaching that face (`Reaches`, which `fit` reads as no room); and **faces of any surface** next to the blend face are held by `no_loop_behind`: every loop that shares nothing with the blend face is held, at its vertices and the middles of its edges, against the blend's fitted surface (a point whose nearest point lies between the borders, no further than the section's diameter, the largest side of the triangle of the two border points and the edge's point there, and on the side that was removed or filled, is in what the blend took; a point near the blend that cannot be projected refuses it). **Routing:** an edge goes in place only when every face at its ends has one loop; a hole in what a tool takes is taken whole by its boolean. Tests: `crates/kernel/tests/blend_holes.rs` (holes beside a freeform edge, in the end face, in a ring's cap, in a cone at 0.06 to 0.14 from the edge, beside sharp edges of 50°, 35° and 20°; fillet and chamfer; three scales; each fails with its check switched off), `crates/topo/tests/defects.rs` › `a_hole_left_outside_its_face_or_across_its_border_is_found`.

### Borders carried across a smooth edge, and step 3 (2026-10-16)

- **Ends walked along the boundary** (`walk_on`, used by `cut_back` for single edges, chains and meets): where a border's corner is not on the edge that leaves the vertex along the boundary with the third face, that boundary is followed across every vertex at which it goes on between the same two faces and nothing else meets (a split of one curve of contact, such as a cylinder's seam, which bounds nothing), and across the vertex where a crossed smooth edge leaves it; the edges passed are taken whole.
- **A border carried across a smooth edge** (`rolling::cross`, single open edges). A smooth edge of a face beside the edge that enters what the blend takes does so at a vertex on the boundary with a third face (`branch`; that boundary inside what the blend takes all along, `inside_along`, 17 points per edge, and ahead of the edge, `Blend::ahead`), or through the border in the middle (refused as running over). Along a candidate a point is inside when the ball touching the face there is nearer the other face than the radius (a chamfer: nearer the edge than the distance); one change from inside to outside, found by bisection (64 samples, 80 halvings), is the *switch*, and the plane across the edge through the ball's centre gives its place along the blend; inside all along, the face before it goes. The edge has to lead to another face tangent at the vertex and at the crossing (ten times `bend`), else no crossing. Anything else is refused as running over (`FreeRunsOver`).
- **The surface across a switch** (`blend_open`): on each side the sections are found against the faces there; the section at the switch, found against the faces before it, is moved onto the faces after it (`moved_onto`, a quarter of the tolerance) and has to be the ball those faces give there (else `NotTangent`); the pieces are refined each on their own faces (at least four samples each) and joined (`cross::join`). A border that cannot be carried is refused as running off its face (which `fit` reads as no room).
- **The faces after** (`cross::carry`): the border is cut at each switch into pieces, each an edge of the face it lies on; the crossed edge is shortened to the switch, or taken whole when it lay inside all along; the loops of the faces touched are linked again from their coedges (`relink`: refused, as running over, where a vertex has two ways on, a loop does not close, or the loops come out different in number); a face left with nothing goes (`compact_faces`). Each piece and the crossed edge are proven ten tolerances apart beyond the stretch near the switch where crossing alone brings them that close (`apart_beyond`, rho = 20 tol / sin θ); a tangent crossing, or that stretch over half an edge, is not shown apart and refused.
- **Step 3**: a straight edge between two planes or next to a cylinder along it goes in place when each end is a vertex of three edges where no other requested edge ends, with one loop on every face there; a chamfer next to a cylinder by the straight distances in the plane across the edge (the section's chords).
- **Tests**: `crates/kernel/tests/blend_across.rs` (a prism's front top edge rounded across an earlier round's tangent line, every point of a grid against the definition; chamfered; the top's strip narrower than the radius; a concave corner whose floor goes; a hole past the switch refused; three scales, tilted), `crates/kernel/src/blend/tests.rs` (the boss root at five seam angles, filleted and chamfered, in place against the tools), `blend_cylinders.rs` › `a_boss_root_fillet_does_not_depend_on_the_seam`, `crates/engine/tests/fillet.rs` › `a_fillet_whose_border_crosses_onto_an_earlier_round`. The turbofan's stator blade edge rounds and disc blade-root fillets go in place: volumes and areas within 9e-10 relative of the tools, every face, edge and vertex name the same, nine blade bodies listing their edges and vertices in another order (in place keeps the numbers of what it does not replace, the boolean renumbers).
- **Still refused:** a border crossing a smooth edge in a chain, a ring or a meet (E6, B2); more than one smooth edge crossed from one end; an edge that enters through the border and leaves through it again, or ends inside; an edge between a face and itself.

### Consolidation of the blends (2026-10-17)

A round with no new behaviour, to fewer special cases and less code; every output bit-identical (below).

- **One construction for an open edge.** `blend_one` had its own path for an edge whose borders cross nothing, a copy of the crossing path (`blend_across`) with one piece: with no crossing that path has one piece on the edge's own faces (`cross::face_at`), the march to each end gives at least twelve sections a side (steps of at most 1/24), so its rule of four samples a piece never applies, and `cross::carry` leaves the borders as they are. It is now the only path (`blend_open`), given the seed.
- **A single face is a chain of one piece.** `splice` did for one face what `splice_chain` does for a chain of one piece cut at both ends: the same corners, borders, cut edges and loop, the entities added in the same order (`insert_cut` and the borders' coedges touch different faces). `blend_one` splices through `chain::splice_one`.

### Requests after a blend in place, renumbered (review, 2026-10-17)

**Found** (review; there since in-place blends could take an edge or a face whole): `blend::construct` planned the edges left for the tools on the body the in-place blends left, and `blend::mixed` blended the closed-form edges on the body the freeform ones left, both with the edge and face numbers of the body the feature started from. When a blend in place takes an edge whole (E7 in `rolling::cut_back`, a crossed edge in `cross::carry`), `rolling::compact` and `compact_faces` drop it and every later edge (face) moves down by one, so a request after it named the next edge: on a block whose two long top edges an earlier chamfer took, the top edge across the end (in place, takes the chamfer's end edges 13 and 16 whole) with the chamfer's end edge 17 and the vertical edge 11 (both by the tools) was refused, as a chamfer, because "the face is not one of the two faces of the edge", and as a fillet because edge 17 of the new body was an elliptic border; where the edge it named had the right kind, the wrong edge would have been blended without a word.

**Fixed:** the requests are renamed through where the entities of that body came from (`blend::renamed`, the inverse of `Blended::edges` and of the `Kept` faces) before they are planned and built; a request of an edge or a face the blend in place took whole is refused naming it (`Reason::TakenWhole`: the blend that took it is not built together with its own); and a refusal met on that body names the entities of the body the feature started from (`named_before`, `failed_before`), so `fit::explain` finds the plan the failure belongs to and the user is told about an edge of the body they picked from. An edge or a face the blend in place made has no name in that body and is never named by its number in the body it left (another entity there): a refusal leaves it out, a size too large for such a face names the request's own face (its first face, else the first face of its edge), a boolean that failed at such an edge reports only the boolean's error. A kernel error (an invalid body's defects) keeps the numbers of the body the blend in place left. No other place compacts a body and uses its old numbers after: `rolling::compact` and `compact_faces` are only called at the end of `rolling::blend`, and within it nothing is numbered anew.

**Tests:** `crates/kernel/tests/chamfer.rs` › `edges_after_one_a_chamfer_takes_whole_keep_their_requests` (the case above at three scales: the convex volume of all the planes in closed form, every chamfer face in the plane of the edge it names), `fillet.rs` › `edges_after_one_a_fillet_takes_whole_keep_their_requests` (the same as fillets: every fillet face a cylinder of its request's radius along its request's edge, the solid the same as in two features), `blend/tests.rs` › `requests_after_a_blend_in_place_follow_the_renumbering` (the renaming, the refusal and the names of a refusal).

### A blend face that folds through itself (fuzzer seed 14211, 2026-10-17)

**Found** (the random-design fuzzer, `wrong-unmeasurable/14211`): a ball of radius 7.5 united with a block it pokes out of by 0.016, then a chamfer of 0.625 on the rim of that cap (a concave circle between the sphere and the block's underside, built by `rolling`): accepted, valid by `check`, but `mass_properties` could not measure the chamfer face ("a boundary integral that does not settle"). **Which side was wrong: the chamfer.** The cap is sqrt(rim² + h²) ≈ 0.49 from rim to top along each section; a cut line 0.625 from the rim lies past the top on the far side, every chamfer line goes through one point of the cap's axis, and the face is a double cone through itself, its normal zero at the apex. The integrator was right to give up.

**Fixed** (`folds` in `blend/rolling.rs`, a new refusal `Reason::FreeFolds`, which `fit` counts as no room). The rule: *along every sample section of a blend face, and halfway between two, the surface normal keeps the side it has in the middle of that section* (a chamfer's against its normal there, a fillet's against the radius from the ball's centre); *a normal that vanishes is a fold.* General because the sections are a family of curves across the edge, one per plane across it; where those planes are not parallel they meet in a line (the axis of a circular edge), and a section that reaches past that line crosses its neighbours, so the surface folds with its normal passing through zero. Only the sections over the edge itself are held (those past the ends are cut away). Both rolling paths, the closed ring and the open edge, and fillets as well as chamfers.

**Tests:** `crates/kernel/tests/chamfer.rs` › `a_chamfer_wider_than_the_cap_it_cuts_into_is_refused` (the finding's ball and block at three scales: 0.5 and 0.625 refused naming the edge and the sphere, 0.2 and 0.45 built, valid, measured, and within the groove they fill); `crates/engine/tests/fuzz_findings.rs` replays the reproducer.

**Open.** The same cap in a plate 20 wide on all sides is refused at every distance as not approximable within the tolerance; a fillet on such a cap is not pinned by a test.

### A fillet's offset: rebuilt (2026-10-18)

What the face operations ask of the rolling ball to rebuild a fillet between offset faces (`blend::rolling::rebuild`; the operation's side in [`face-operations.md`](face-operations.md) › *Offset of a fillet: rebuilt*):
- **Reading a fillet off its surface** (`fillet_of`): the shape `refine` gives (three rows across, rational quadratic in `v`), the radius and its side from the exact end rows, each of 17 rows an arc of the exact ball between the two faces in its own plane, to the tolerance. Not point for point against a ball in another plane: two fits of one blend take their sections a little apart along it (the saddle's: 1.8e-6 mm at an end), which moves no point off the surface.
- **The same `Solver`, `Spine`, `follow` and `refine`**, the spine the old border, the joints' planes the old end rows' at a chain's joints.
- **`Solver::settle`**: how little a centre may still move per step and be settled. Zero everywhere else (rounding, as before); a hundredth of the tolerance for a rebuild, whose feet on fitted offsets of high degree are found only to about 1e-12 of the part (the saddle's rear rim jittered by 1e-8 mm).
- **Feet on the faces** (`on_face`): past the part of the old fillet its face's edges reach, a ball is kept only while its feet lie within a thousandth of a freeform face's parameters past its border; past that the fillet would roll along a fitted offset extrapolated, or, past a smooth edge, along the wrong face.
