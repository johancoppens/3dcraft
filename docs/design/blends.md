# 3DCraft design: Blends: chamfers and fillets

Chamfers and fillets: the features (`doc::Chamfer`, `doc::Fillet`, `regen::blend`) and the kernel (`kernel::blend`, its front ends `kernel::chamfer` and `kernel::fillet`, and `blend::rolling`). Method 3 of the map ([`../architecture.md`](../architecture.md) §5).

## Method

**What a feature holds.** A list of edges, each as the names of the two faces it lies between (the order says which face a chamfer's first distance or angle is measured on), a size (a chamfer's one or two distances, or a distance and an angle; a fillet's radius) and `chain`. At each regeneration every pair is found again (`regen::edges_between`), followed to the newest body of its chain; with `chain` an edge stands for the edges that continue it smoothly. New faces are `F9:chamfer(k)` / `F9:fillet(k)` (numbered by place in space when a reference gives several), corners `chamferCorner` / `filletCorner`; kept faces are copies, instance 0.

**Two constructions, chosen per edge** (`blend::mixed` runs both in one feature):

- *Closed form: a tool and the boolean.* Where the section across the edge is the same all along (a straight edge between planes or along a cylinder, a circular edge between a plane, a cylinder or a cone about its axis), the section (two lines and the fillet's arc or the chord) is extruded or revolved into a tool solid, subtracted at a convex edge and added at a concave one. The fillet face is a cylinder or a torus, the chamfer face a plane or a cone. Where an edge ends: the tool runs past the vertex when the other edges there are convex like it, ends square at the vertex otherwise, or is cut in the plane of a slanted face it runs into (`end_plane`). Two blends that meet run out into each other; three of one kind and one radius meet in a corner (a flat triangle for chamfers, a piece of the ball for fillets), made by one more boolean. Tools of a chain are joined into one (`blend::join`).
- *Freeform: a rolling ball built in place* (`blend::rolling`), for every other edge. Balls of the radius touching both faces are found in planes across the edge (for a chain, and round a closed edge, square to a cubic with continuous curvature through the centres found: the planes across an edge turn only as smoothly as its curve, and a fitted edge's turning jumps at every knot), the arcs between their feet (a chamfer: the lines between points at the distances) are interpolated into one B-spline surface, refined until it is within a quarter of the tolerance of the exact section between samples and tangent to both faces within the angular bound; each pass halves every step over those bounds and every step the halving can push over them (a change at one sample of the interpolating cubic dies away by 2 − √3 per sample). The borders are the surface's own iso-curves. The blend is continued past each end and cut where it crosses the third face, the neighbours are shortened, and a face beside it may be grown (`NurbsSurface::extended`). Joints of a chain end in a shared arc; two fillets that meet at an angle are trimmed against each other (`chain::meet`). A border has to stay on its face: it may not come within ten tolerances of another edge of it, proven (`geom::certify::curves_apart`; a pair not told apart is refused). The fitted curves are proven on their surfaces between their points, not only held at the middles (`geom::certify`, since 2026-10-16): the borders on the faces beside them, a cut on the blend face and the third face, a meet's trim on the face it trims, an edge going on to a corner on both its faces; a step the proof does not cover is halved.

**Never a wrong solid.** Whether a size fits is not estimated: the blend is built exactly and the result is held against what a blend is (every blend face present, in one piece, bordering exactly its neighbours and the faces at the ends of its edge, the boolean's own checks), a fillet's tangency is measured along its borders, `topo::check` runs, and regeneration checks near the new faces for self-intersection. A closed-form blend that does not build is searched for the largest size that does (`blend::fit`), changing the size of every edge of that size together (a feature's one size): a quarter of the size at a time until one builds, then the sizes at which a border can first reach something of its face (a vertex, the nearest point of an edge, what another blend takes of the face; the axis of a circular edge; where a fillet's torus reaches its own axis), worked out from the faces, searched with builds just past and just before each; the event found is reported, to within those builds (it builds 4, 64 or 1024 tolerances before it and fails 64 past it), and where none explains the failure the size is bisected on builds alone, to a few tolerances. Refused as too large with nothing that fits only where every failure is evidence of no room from the result itself (the blend reaches a face it should not, takes a neighbour away, comes out in pieces); a refusal of the boolean or the sweep at every size tried is reported as it is (a boolean's saying that smaller sizes tried fail the same way). Every search is bounded (48 builds; a spent budget ends the search with the original refusal, never "nothing fits" nor a size). The two constructions, the checks still sampled (the surface's interior against the exact sections, at the middles between samples; a fillet's tangency, at seven points per border) and the leftover `join` are structural debt (the map's §9).

**Refused, with the reason**: vertex blends of three or more fillets in other arrangements, outside and inside blends that meet, two blends of one kind that meet where the third edge is of the other (the ball would roll over that edge), edges that end at four or more faces, a freeform edge that ends where it goes on straight into another edge (fillet the chain), blends whose border runs over an edge of its face (a smooth one, or the end of the face) or whose end runs over more than one face, creased joints, and the cases listed under *Still refused* below.

## Findings and history

> Moved verbatim from `docs/architecture.md` on 2026-10-15. Section numbers (§4.1, §5.3, …) in the text are those of that file before the split; [README.md](README.md) says where each one is now.

**Chamfers (2026-10-14, M9: the first blend, and the first feature that consumes edges).** `doc::Chamfer` has `edges` (a list of references), `size` (`ChamferSize`: `Equal { distance }`, `TwoDistances { distance, second }`, `DistanceAngle { distance, angle }`, all expressions) and `chain`. It makes its body from the body of an earlier feature and takes it, like a transform.

- *What a chamfer holds of an edge* is **the names of the two faces the edge lies between, in order**: `[F1:face(1), F1:face(2)]`. Why the pair and not the edge's own name: (1) the order of the pair is what says which face the first distance is measured along and which face the angle is taken from, and a name of an edge has no first face; (2) after a boolean the edge's own name *is* that pair (`F7:between[a,b]`), so nothing is lost there, and on a sweep or a primitive `document.edges` lists the pair beside the name; (3) the pair survives what the own name does not: when a later edit cuts the edge in two (a slot through it), the own name is gone and two longer names are there, while "the edges between the top and the front" are both pieces, and both stay chamfered, which is what the user meant; (4) no numbered name is ever stored, so limit (1) of *Where stability cannot be had* cannot retarget a chamfer. The price: two faces that meet in several edges are chamfered along all of them, and one of several cannot be picked (not built: a reference by the edge's own name, for that case). Which body is chamfered is not stored apart: it is the body of the feature the first face is of, and every other face has to be of that feature too, after the names are followed to the newest body of their chain (since 2026-10-14, *References that follow a body* below).
- *Resolution.* At every regeneration each pair is found again with `regen::edges_between`. A face that is gone fails the feature with the parameter, the feature and the name (``edges.0: `Notched` no longer has a face F3:copy(1)/F2:face(3); its faces are: …``); faces that no longer meet fail it with both names (``edges.1: the faces … and … of `Base` do not meet in an edge (any more)``); what is made from a failed chamfer fails naming it and carries `causedBy`, and the body the chamfer would have taken is its source's again, as for every feature (*A feature that fails*). Repair is `feature.edit` with `edges`, which gives the document built that way from the start. With `chain` each edge found stands for the edges that continue it smoothly as well (`kernel::chamfer::tangent_chain`: at each end the one other edge that leaves the vertex straight on, both ways, until none does or the chain closes); along the chain the "first" face is the one the edges share, or what continues the first face.
- *Which face gets which distance.* `distance` is measured along the first face of the pair, `second` along the second, each along the face and at right angles to the edge; `angle` is the angle between the first face and the chamfer face, inside the corner that is cut away or filled, so against a square corner the second face is cut `distance · tan(angle)` back and in general `distance · sin(angle) / sin(angle + θ)` with θ the angle between the two faces across the edge. Turning the pair round turns the distances round.
- *Names of what a chamfer makes.* A face of the body that is still there is a copy, instance 0, of the face it was (`F9:copy(0)/F2:side(e7)`), with the aliases it had, as in every feature that makes its body from another; a sketch on it lies where it lay. The face in the place of the edge of reference `k` is `F9:chamfer(k)` (role `chamfer`, the place of the reference in the chamfer's list). When that reference gave several edges (pieces between the same two faces, or a chain) their faces are `F9:chamfer(k,0)`, `F9:chamfer(k,1)`, … in the order of where the edges' middles lie in space (x, then y, then z, as numbered edge names are counted, with the same limit: an edit that changes that order moves the numbers). The flat face where three chamfers meet is `F9:chamferCorner(i,j,k)`, by the references of the three edges. Chamfer faces in one plane that became one face are named after the first and answer to the others as aliases. Why a number and not the two faces, as an edge of a boolean has them: the edges and vertices of the chamfered body are named by the faces they lie on, and a name in such a list lists no faces itself, so a face's name has to be flat. The number is the place in this feature's own list, which no upstream edit changes; taking an edge out of the list renumbers the ones after it. The edges and vertices of the result are named by their faces (`F9:between[F9:chamfer(0),F9:copy(0)/F1:face(1)]`), and one the body already had also answers to a copy of the name it had there, exactly as after a boolean (one implementation: `Names::name_entities`).
- *The frame of a flat chamfer face* (what a sketch on it gets): z out of the body; x along the edge, in the direction of the cross product of the directions into the first and the second face (each along its face, away from the edge); the origin where the cut line on the first face starts in that direction. It depends on the pair and the geometry, not on which way the kernel runs the edge.
- *Tests:* `crates/engine/tests/chamfer.rs` (closed forms at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6, `document.check` with `deep`, names, upstream edits that move and remove the edge, repair, a sketch on the chamfer face placed by its frame, a chamfer on an edge of a chamfer, save and reopen, everything refused), `crates/kernel/tests/chamfer.rs`, `crates/format/tests/format.rs`, and FreeCAD on the STEP export (`crates/engine/tests/step_freecad/chamfers.rs`).

**Fillets (2026-10-14, M9: the second blend).** `doc::Fillet` has `edges` (references, as a chamfer's), `radius` (an expression) and `chain`. Everything a chamfer does with its references a fillet does with the same code (`regen::blend`: finding the edges of a pair of faces again, the chain, the numbering, the names; `regen::chamfer` and `regen::fillet` are the two sizes and the two wordings of what the kernel refuses).

- *An edge* is the two faces it lies between; their order says nothing, since a fillet treats both faces alike, and turning a pair round gives the same body and the same names.
- *Names of what a fillet makes.* The face in the place of the edge of reference `k` is `F9:fillet(k)` (role `fillet`), `F9:fillet(k,0)`, `F9:fillet(k,1)`, … when the reference gave several edges or a chain, in the order of where the edges' middles lie in space; the piece of a ball where three fillets meet is `F9:filletCorner(i,j,k)` (role `filletCorner`), by the references of the three edges. Faces that are still the body's are copies, instance 0, with their aliases; edges and vertices are named by their faces, so a seam is `F9:between[F9:copy(0)/F1:face(1),F9:fillet(0)]`.
- *What can be put on a fillet face.* A datum axis (`datum.axis` with `face`: the cylinder's or the torus's own axis, which is how the tests read where the face lies) and the edges it has with its neighbours (a later chamfer or fillet takes them by the pair of faces). Not a sketch: the face is not flat, and `sketch.create` on it is refused as on any curved face. A sketch on a face next to a fillet lies where it lay: the face keeps its frame.
- *After a fillet, the neighbouring edge is part of a chain.* The edge between the top and the left of a block whose top front edge is rounded now runs on smoothly in the arc where the fillet meets the left, and that in the upright edge between the front and the left. A chamfer or a fillet of that edge alone would stop in the middle of the chain and is refused ("does not end cleanly … fillet the edges it continues in as well"); with `chain` it runs round the corner (two cylinders and a quarter of a torus, or two flat faces and a cone). Two fillets on edges that meet, in one feature, run out into each other instead.
- *Tests:* `crates/engine/tests/fillet.rs` (closed forms at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6, `document.check` with `deep`, the axis of the new face through `datum.axis`, names, upstream edits that move the edge and remove a face, repair, a boss on the face next to a fillet, a chamfer after a fillet and a fillet after a chamfer and after a fillet on neighbouring edges, save and reopen, undo and redo, everything refused), the row `fillet: edges` of the failed-features table.

### Chamfers as built (M9, 2026-10-14)

`kernel::chamfer::chamfer(body, edges, tolerance)` takes, per edge, the edge, which of its two faces is the first, and a size (two distances, or a distance and an angle), and returns the body with a source for every face (`Kept` faces of the body, `Chamfer` of which requested edges, `Corner` at which vertex), edge and vertex.

**Route: a tool solid per edge and the boolean, not a local operation.** Two routes were open. (a) A local operation: cut the two neighbours at the offset curves, put in the new face, sew. It is what fillets will need in the end, and it keeps every untouched entity as it is; but it needs its own handling of every way an edge can end (the neighbours' other edges, a vertex of any valence, a mix of convex and concave edges, seams, a chamfer that eats a whole neighbour), and each of those is a place to produce a body that is valid in its counts and wrong in space. (b) A tool whose cross-section is the triangle between the edge and the two cut lines, subtracted from the body at a convex edge and added at a concave one. It leaves all of that to a kernel that is measured against a reference kernel (11549 of 11988 corpus outcomes agree, none wrong), whose result is checked or refused, and that names every entity of its result. Its price is the contact it produces by design: two faces of every tool lie in the two neighbours (coincident faces, same normals in a subtraction and opposite in a union), tools of edges that meet overlap each other, a chain's tools end against each other, and a corner plane goes through three existing vertices. All of that is what the boolean kernel handles today (`Contact without overlap`, the coincident rows of its table, merging): of the cases built for this feature it refused none. (b) was taken for the first version, with what it cannot do stated below; (a) remains the route for chamfers on edges the boolean route cannot make a tool for. (Fillets, built the same day, took (b) as well: the boolean built their tangent tools; see *Fillets as built*, also for the one small local step they added.)

**The tool.** *A straight edge between two planes:* a prism, `kernel::extrude` of the triangle (edge, edge + d₁ along the first face, edge + d₂ along the second) along the edge. *A circular edge between a plane, a cylinder or a cone about the circle's axis* (the end of a rod, the mouth of a hole, the foot of a boss, the base of a cone): the same triangle in the half-plane through the axis, turned by `kernel::revolve` through the whole circle or through the arc; the chamfer face is a cone. Convex or concave is read from the solid, never from the direction of the edge: with the faces to the left of their loops seen from outside, an edge is convex when the direction into one face points behind the other. Every tool is made from the body as it was given, so the order of the edges does not change the solid.

**Where an edge ends** (the part a local operation would have to do topologically). When every other edge at the end vertex is convex like the chamfered one (or every one concave), the body near the vertex is what its faces leave of space and the chamfer is one more such face: the tool is made longer than the edge (past where the chamfer plane cuts the other edges there, with a margin; by an angle for an arc), and what sticks out meets nothing, or only material when it is added. Otherwise the tool ends at the vertex, across the edge. That is right when the face the chamfer runs into lies in that plane (the top edge of a step that ends against a wall: the wall grows by the triangle and the boolean merges it into one face), and when the edge continues smoothly in another chamfered edge (a chain: the two tools end against each other and the cut between them leaves no trace). In every other case the end of the tool would stay in the result as a face nobody asked for, which the final check finds.

**Corners.** Two chamfered edges that meet: each chamfer is the whole half-space of its face near the vertex, so their faces meet in the line the two planes share (a mitre), for equal and unequal distances alike. Three chamfered edges of one kind (all convex or all concave) that meet in a vertex with nothing else: the corner is **the flat face through the three points in which the cut lines cross on the three faces**, a triangle; three points are always in a plane, so the rule is the same for unequal distances, as long as the three points are points of the chamfered body: when one chamfer is larger than the other two reach together (on a square corner: d₁ ≥ d₂ + d₃) it takes the corner whole, the other two never meet, and there is no corner face. It is what OpenCascade gives (measured through FreeCAD: all twelve edges of a 4 × 3 × 2 block by 0.25 have its volume, area and 26/48/24 counts to the last digits; three edges, and two, likewise), and what Fusion calls the corner type "chamfer"; the pointed corner where the three planes meet (a "mitre" corner) is not offered. It is made by one more boolean, with a block lying against that plane on the vertex's side. Where more than three edges meet, or convex and concave chamfers meet, there is no corner face: the chamfers are their half-spaces.

**Never a wrong solid.** (1) Before anything is built, a distance for which a cut line would leave its face is refused with the edge, the face and the largest distance that fits (`KernelError::ChamferTooLarge`): the measure is how far the rest of the face's boundary is from the edge along the face (for a straight edge the part of the boundary beside it; for a circle the part in its sector, and the axis), less the distance of another chamfer that cuts the face from the other side. (2) After the last boolean the result is held against what a chamfer is: every chamfer face is there, in one piece, borders both neighbours of its edge, and borders nothing but those, the faces at the ends of its edge, and the chamfers and corners of edges that end where it does; no other face of a tool is left; no chamfer face has become part of a face of the body. Anything else is `KernelError::Chamfer` with the edge (and the face), so a case the estimate in (1) lets through is still refused, with a less exact message. The boolean's own refusals come back as `KernelError::ChamferBoolean` with the edge.

**Supported and not** (tests: `crates/kernel/tests/chamfer.rs`, closed forms at three scales and in tilted frames):

| Configuration | Result |
|---|---|
| a straight edge between two planes at any angle, convex or concave; ending in corners of the part, against a wall across it, or in the next edge of a chain | built |
| a circle or an arc between a plane, a cylinder and a cone about its axis, convex or concave (rod, hole, blind hole, boss, cone, half a rod) | built: the face is a cone |
| one distance, two distances, a distance and an angle | built |
| several edges; two that meet (mitre); three that meet (flat corner); all twelve of a block | built |

| any other edge: a straight edge next to a cone or a spline wall, an elliptic or freeform edge, a circle between other faces (2026-10-14) | built as a ruled spline surface when each end is a corner of three faces (or the edge is closed) and no other blended edge ends there (*Blends along freeform edges* below); otherwise `Chamfer`, with the reason |
| an edge that ends against a plane that is neither square to it nor convex like it (a ledge that runs into a slanted wall, a root that ends on a chamfer; 2026-10-15) | built: the chamfer ends in the plane of that face (*Blend findings of the engine block* below); against a face that is not a plane `Chamfer`: "does not end cleanly" |

| two faces tangent along the edge; a seam; a sheet | `Chamfer`, with the reason |
| a distance too large for a neighbour; chamfers that meet in the middle of a face | `ChamferTooLarge`, or `Chamfer` from the final check |

**Cost.** One boolean of the body with a five-face tool per edge, and one per corner: the twelve edges and eight corners of a block take 20 booleans, a few milliseconds together in a debug build. On a body with thousands of faces each of those booleans looks at the whole body; a local operation would not. Not measured on a large body yet.

### Fillets as built (M9, 2026-10-14)

`kernel::fillet::fillet(body, edges, tolerance)` takes, per edge, the edge and a radius, and returns the body with a source for every face (`Kept` faces of the body, `Fillet` of which requested edges, `Corner` at which vertex), edge and vertex. The face in the place of an edge is what a ball of the radius touches as it rolls along the edge against both faces: tangent to both.

**Route: the chamfer's, a tool solid and the boolean, with one addition.** The plain case was tried first: a block less the prism whose section is the corner between the two tangent lines and the arc. The boolean builds it (the tool's curved face is tangent to both neighbours along lines that are edges of the tool, and two of its faces lie in the neighbours), and it built every single-edge and corner case asked of it. So fillets are made by the code chamfers are made by (`kernel::blend`, of which `kernel::chamfer` and `kernel::fillet` are the two front ends): how an edge is read from the solid, convex or concave, the path (a line, a circle, an arc), where the tool ends, the estimate of what fits, the booleans, the bookkeeping of where every face came from, and the check at the end. What differs is the section's third side and the corner. **The one contact the boolean refused** (it takes it since 2026-10-14, see below) was a cylinder that goes on in a torus across the border they share, as the fillets of a line and of the arc it continues in do along the rim of a slot: the two surfaces are tangent along that circle and cross in a second curve that leaves it, a pair `meet_surfaces` had no closed form for ("surfaces that touch along a curve … without a closed form"). A fillet does not ask it: the tools of edges that continue each other are **joined into one tool** first (`blend::join`: the two ends that lie against each other are taken out and their three edges and three vertices made one, a change of topology only; the joined tool is checked), so the cylinder and the torus are neighbours in one operand and the boolean never intersects them. That is the first local operation in the kernel, and the only one. **Since 2026-10-14 the boolean takes the contact** (§5.1: the circle of contact and the second curve in closed form; `a_cylinder_that_goes_on_in_a_torus_touches_it_along_their_border` in `crates/kernel/tests/fillet.rs`, which was the ignored reproducer, and `a_rod_that_goes_on_in_a_ring` in `boolean_surfaces.rs`), so the join can go; it has not been taken out yet. Measured once with the join switched off (an experiment, not in the tree): the twenty tests of `crates/kernel/tests/fillet.rs` pass with a separate tool per edge, the rim of the slot included; the engine's fillet tests were not run that way. A torus that goes on in another torus (two arcs of a rim that continue each other) is the same contact and has no closed form yet: the join still covers it.

**The section and the tool.** Across the edge the two faces leave it in two directions with the angle θ between them. The ball touches each face `r / tan(θ/2)` from the edge, and its centre lies on the bisector, `r / sin(θ/2)` from the edge. The tool's section is the kite between the edge and the two tangent points, less the sector of the ball: two lines and an arc, which `kernel::extrude` takes along a straight edge (the face is a **cylinder** of radius r, its axis parallel to the edge) and `kernel::revolve` about the axis of a circular edge (a **torus** whose tube has radius r), for an edge between a plane, a cylinder or a cone about that axis. The tool is taken out at a convex edge and added at a concave one.

**Where an edge ends** is the chamfer's rule, unchanged: longer than the edge where every other edge at the vertex is convex like it (or concave like it), ending at the vertex otherwise, which is right against a wall across the edge and where the next tool of a chain begins.

**Corners.** *Two fillets that meet, the other edges at the vertex sharp:* each tool is its whole section near the vertex, so the two faces run out into each other in the curve their surfaces share (two cylinders of one radius at a square corner: an arc of an ellipse), for equal and unequal radii. It is what OpenCascade gives (same volume to 1e-11, same counts). *Three straight filleted edges of one kind and one radius that meet in a vertex with nothing else:* the ball comes to rest in the corner touching all three faces, each of the three cylinders has its axis through the ball's centre, and the corner is **the piece of that ball between the three planes through its centre across the three edges**: an eighth of a sphere on a block, a spherical triangle on any other corner (the top corners of a hexagonal prism). It is made by one more boolean with the tool "the cell those three planes cut out on the vertex's side, less the ball"; the ball is tangent to each cylinder along the great circle in which the cylinder ends. That this is the surface a rolling ball leaves (the union of all balls of the radius that fit behind the three faces) is argued in `kernel::blend` and measured: see the tests. *Refused, with the reason:* three or more filleted edges at a vertex in any other arrangement (different radii, which a kernel call can ask for and a feature with its one radius cannot; a circular edge among them; a fourth edge) need a vertex blend that is not built; an outside and an inside fillet that meet end against each other and are refused by the rule for ends.

**Never a wrong solid.** The chamfer's two checks, and a third. (1) A radius for which a tangent line would leave its face is refused before anything is built, with the edge, the face and the largest radius that fits (`KernelError::FilletTooLarge`): the same measure as for a chamfer's distance, turned into a radius by `tan(θ/2)`; the ball that does not fit in a hole is the tangent circle that runs into the axis. (2) The result is held against what a blend is, as a chamfer's. (3) **Tangency:** at five points along every edge a fillet face shares with a neighbour of its edge, and with the ball of a corner, the two outward normals have to agree within the angular tolerance (and never closer than 1e-9, which is what rounding leaves); anything else is `KernelError::Fillet` and no body.

**Supported and not** (tests: `crates/kernel/tests/fillet.rs`, closed forms at three scales and in tilted frames):

| a circle or an arc between a plane, a cylinder and a cone about its axis, convex or concave (rod, hole, blind hole, boss, cone, half a rod) | built: a torus |

| an edge that ends against a plane at a slant (a ledge into a slanted wall, a flange root that ends on a chamfer or at a corner of the outline; 2026-10-15) | built: the fillet ends in the plane of that face (*Blend findings of the engine block* below) |
| an outside and an inside fillet that meet; an edge that ends against a face at a slant that is not a plane; one edge out of the middle of a chain | `Fillet`: "does not end cleanly" |
| a straight edge between a plane and a cylinder along it, or two such cylinders (the cut edges of a halved rod or tube, the leading edge of a lens of two arcs; 2026-10-14) | built: a cylinder (*Blends next to a cylinder* below) |
| both rims of the end of a lens of two arcs (two tori that run out into each other at its sharp tips; 2026-10-14) | built since two surgical changes to the boolean (below) |
| any other edge: a straight edge next to a cone or a spline wall, the edge where two lofted faces meet, an elliptic or freeform edge, a circle between other faces (2026-10-14) | built as a spline surface within the tolerance when each end is a corner of three faces (or the edge is closed) (*Blends along freeform edges* below), goes on smoothly into another blended edge (a chain), or meets another such fillet at a vertex whose third edge stays sharp (2026-10-15, *Chains and meets of freeform blends*); otherwise `Fillet`, with the reason |
| two faces tangent along the edge; a seam; a sheet | `Fillet`, with the reason |
| a radius too large for a neighbour; fillets that meet in the middle of a face | `FilletTooLarge`, or `Fillet` from the final check |

**Limits that are the shared code's.** The estimate of what fits takes an edge that leaves from the end of the blended edge as the end of the face there (followed on round tangent arcs since 2026-10-15). That is right for the faces of a block and too careful where that edge is short and the face goes on behind it (the top front edge of a block whose top left edge is chamfered by less than the radius: refused as too large, though it would fit). Both blends have it; it refuses, it never builds wrong.

**Cost.** One boolean of the body per edge or per chain, and four per corner (two to cut the cell out of three blocks, one to take the ball out of it, one with the body): the twelve edges and eight corners of a block take 44 booleans, measured at about 0.16 s in the test profile (the chamfers of the same block: a few milliseconds; where the time goes was not profiled). As for chamfers, each boolean looks at the whole body.

### Blends next to a cylinder (M9, 2026-10-14)

A straight edge on a cylinder is one of its rulings, parallel to its axis, and a plane through that line holds the axis' direction: the two faces look the same in every plane across the edge, and so does the blend (`kernel::blend::section`). In that plane each face is a line or a circle. A fillet is the circle of the radius tangent to both: its centre is where the two curves moved by the radius towards the ball cross (line and line, line and circle, circle and circle, in closed form; of two crossings the one nearest where the ball would sit between the tangent planes). A chamfer is the chord between the points the distances away along each curve (along the arc on a cylinder); with a distance and an angle the chord leaves the first point at the angle to the first curve. The tool is then the extrusion of the section (two sides along the faces, a line or an arc each, and the fillet's arc or the chord), exactly as between two planes, and goes through the boolean as every other tool: the fillet face is a cylinder, the chamfer face a plane. How far a face reaches across the edge, which the estimate of what fits measures, is measured along its circle. Corners of three such blends are not made (a fillet refuses them, a chamfer leaves the chamfers to meet). Tests: `crates/kernel/tests/blend_cylinders.rs` (a halved rod with one and both cut edges rounded, also from a boolean as in the turbofan's finding F5, a lens's tip, the foot of a half-round boss, a halved rod chamfered along the diameter and the arc; closed forms by Green's theorem over the section, at three scales and in a tilted frame).

**Two tip fillets of a lens (finding F1).** Rounding both rims of the end of an extruded lens of two arcs makes two tori that run out into each other at the sharp tips. The tools are right; the boolean refused at the edge of tolerance twice, and was changed in two places (`kernel/src/boolean/section.rs`): (1) a stretch of an intersection curve that is out of either face at its middle is dropped before it is held against the other face's boundary (a curve that leaves a face's edge tangentially, where a fillet's face meets the other side of the sharp edge, runs within the tolerance of that edge for a while, beyond the other face too, and was refused as "partly on and partly off a face boundary"); (2) an approximated (spline) edge that leaves a surface tangentially at one of its ends, staying within the tolerance of it up to a point where it crosses it again, meets the surface at its end and not there (it split the edge where the re-marched curve of the same two surfaces was not split: "an imprinted curve that does not fit the edges it should coincide with"). An exact edge that lies on the surface up to a point (a ruling of a cone up to its apex) is not touched by (2). The boolean corpus and every other boolean test give the same outcomes. Test: `both_tip_edges_of_a_cambered_blade` (radii 0.05 to 0.3 on the compressor blade's section, both orders, three scales, tilted).

### Blends along freeform edges as built (M9, 2026-10-14)

`kernel::blend::rolling`: every edge the closed forms above do not cover (a straight edge next to a cone or a spline wall, the edge where two lofted faces meet, an elliptic or a spline edge, a circle between other faces). **Route: a local operation, not a tool and the boolean**: the fillet face is tangent to both neighbours along curves that have no closed form, and a boolean between a spline face and a face it is tangent to along such a curve is what the boolean refuses ("surfaces that touch along a curve … without a closed form"). Built directly, the borders of the blend face are edges of both faces from the start and nothing has to be found where two surfaces touch.

- **The face.** A fillet is the surface a ball of the radius sweeps rolling along the edge against both faces. Each ball is found in a plane across the edge (through a point of it, square to it; past the ends of the edge on the edge's curve continued, a spline's end spans as the polynomials they are): its centre moves to where the two tangent planes at the nearest points of the surfaces, moved by the radius, cross the plane, until it stops moving (in a few steps); then it has to be the radius from both surfaces, along their normals, on the right side. The face meets the ball in the short arc between the two points where it touches the faces, a rational quadratic, exact. Which planes pick the balls does not change the surface: the balls of the radius that touch both faces are one family. A chamfer is, in each plane, the line between the points of the faces the distances away from the edge's point (straight distances in the plane; given by an angle it is refused).
- **The approximation.** The arcs (or lines) of a row of planes, evenly spaced along the edge and past its ends, are joined into one B-spline surface by interpolating their homogeneous control points with a cubic, so the surface passes through every sample exactly. Between two samples it is held against the exact section there, point for point in the same parameter (which bounds its distance from the true surface from above), to a quarter of the linear tolerance; and a fillet's normal along its two borders against the faces' normals, to the angular tolerance or the angle the linear tolerance makes over the radius, whichever is more. Samples are added until both hold (at most 4096, laid through by a banded solve since 2026-10-15). The borders are the surface's own iso-curves, so the blend face and the faces beside it share their edges exactly.
- **Where it ends.** Each end of the edge has to be a vertex of exactly three faces: the blend is continued past it until a whole section lies outside the third face, and cut where it crosses that face's surface, along a cubic fitted to points found exactly (each by bisection along the surface), held to a quarter of the tolerance between them. The two other edges at the vertex are shortened to where the borders reach them, the third face gets the cut in place of its corner, and the edge becomes the blend face; every other entity keeps its number. That is the cut edge of a conical casing ending at its end faces, the leading or trailing edge of a lofted blade ending at the root and the tip. **A closed edge** (one vertex that no other edge passes through: the rim of a slanted cut of a rod, the mouth of a slanted hole, a closed spline root) has no ends: the sections go evenly round it, joined by the closed cubic with continuous curvature through them (`refine::periodic_tangents`, one Bézier piece per pair), refined the same way; the edge becomes the first border, the second border is new, and the blend face is the ring between the two loops.
- **Never a wrong solid.** A ball that does not settle or does not touch both faces, an arc of half a turn, a border that leaves the edges at an end, a cut that is not found, or an approximation that does not converge is refused with the edge and the reason; the body passes `topo::check`, and the fillet's tangency is measured again along its borders (to ten times the bound above). What the kernel cannot see, a large blend running into a face across the body, regeneration looks for on a mesh (`tess::body_self_intersections`, as for a loft) before the body is handed on.
- **Refused, with the reason** (work owed): an edge that ends at a vertex of four or more faces; a closed edge whose vertex other edges pass through; a chamfer by distance and angle; a ball that does not fit (a face curves more tightly than the radius under it, as the fan blade's suction side does at its leading edge, radius 0.049 mm); a border that runs off a face beside the edge along the way (checked on the body: the border may not come within the tolerance of another edge of its face; `Reason::FreeRunsOver`). Chains of tangent edges, closed-form edges in the same feature and two fillets that meet at a vertex are built since 2026-10-15 (*Chains and meets of freeform blends* below).
- **Tests** (`crates/kernel/tests/blend_freeform.rs`): a straight edge between a plane and a cone, held point by point against the definition of a rolling-ball fillet (a point of the body is kept exactly when a ball of the radius behind both faces holds it: the distance to the set of such centres, an intersection of two convex sets, by Dykstra's projections), at three scales, with the borders tangent to within the angle the tolerance makes over the radius; the rim of a slanted cut of a rod (a whole ellipse, a ring) by the same definition; the same edge chamfered (every point of the borders the distance from the edge's line); a straight edge between two spline walls (an extruded blade section), whose removed volume is the 2D fillet of the two Béziers worked out on its own (Green's theorem with Gauss–Legendre) times the height. In the engine (`crates/engine/tests/blend_freeform.rs`, `chamfer.rs`): the leading and trailing edges of the turbofan's lofted fan blade, a conical casing's cut edge, the rings of a slanted cut and a slanted hole, a spline wall chamfered against an independent integral over the strip it cuts (to 1e-7).

### Blend findings of the engine block (M9, 2026-10-15)

An engine-block showcase found eight refusals of blends a part like it needs (the reproducers in its findings, E1–E8). The causes and what changed, all in `kernel::blend` except the last:

- **The estimate of what fits** (`fits`, which refuses a size whose cut or tangent line would leave a face before anything is built) read the face's boundary too narrowly. (1) An edge that leaves an end of the blended edge bounded the strip "up to its own far end": where the boundary runs on smoothly (a tangent sketch arc that turns by 38.7° next to a rim, "below 2.63" on a 176 mm cap, E5; a circle split at the cylinder's seam, so that a boss's root fillet depended on where the seam lay, E2), the far end of that one piece was taken as the end of the face. The boundary is now followed along the edges that go on straight from it (`smooth_run`) up to a corner, where the face can turn back. (2) Along an arc, an edge that goes on from its end along the circle's tangent lies beyond the arc's sector and bounds nothing of it (a short ledge after an inside round of the bellhousing's rail, E3). (3) A cut line that runs exactly into the corner where that boundary turns fits: a second chamfer of the same distance next to an earlier one (E7) is the solid the three make in one feature. (4) The other way round: a boundary edge that leaves an end the way the blended edge does (a rib's foot whose arc touches the line where the wall goes over into a rail's round, E8) means the face pinches to nothing there; that is refused as too large with nothing that fits, where the boolean used to refuse it ("an imprinted curve that does not fit"). What a blend would need there, running over the tangent edge onto the round, is not built.
- **Where a blend ends against a plane at a slant** (a flange root that ends on the deck's chamfer, E4, or at a corner of the block's outline where the next root is not rounded, E3): the tool used to end square across the edge, which leaves a face nobody asked for. When exactly one other face meets the end vertex and it is a plane, the tool is now made long enough to reach that plane and cut there by one more boolean (`end_plane`, `trim`): its end lies in the plane of the face, and the boolean merges it into that face, as modellers end a blend on the face it runs into. Both ways the plane can lean, convex and concave edges; the ledge that runs into a slanted wall, which the tables above listed as refused, is built the same way. A face that is not a plane there is still refused ("does not end cleanly").
- **Chamfer chains** with one distance are joined into one tool as fillet chains are (`joints`): the plane of a line's chamfer and the cone of the arc it goes on in touch along their common ruling, which the boolean refused at the finding's tolerance ("two points closer together than the tolerance can tell apart", E1).
- **The boolean** (`boolean/section.rs`, `edge_cuts`): a hit of an edge on a surface within twice the tolerance of an end of the edge that lies on the surface is taken as that end. Where an ellipse or a circle runs onto a face at a shallow angle the hit is found only to a fraction of the tolerance, while the end, which the other edges there share, is exact; two such hits of edges that meet there were two places closer together than the tolerance can tell apart (a ball corner of the crankcase bay where the outline turns by 51°, at the document's tolerance, E5b). The boolean corpus gives the same outcomes (11573 agreeing, none wrong).
- **Still refused:** all edges of a prism rounded in two steps with one radius (the rims after the long edges): along the earlier round's arc the rim's torus would reach its own axis, a sphere the closed forms do not make (one feature with ball corners builds it).
- **Tests:** `crates/kernel/tests/fillet.rs`, `chamfer.rs`, `blend_cylinders.rs`: closed forms (Pappus over the outline offset by the section's centroid, prisms between oblique planes, convex solids from their planes), against the same chamfers in one feature, against the definition of a rolling ball near a corner, at three scales and in a tilted frame, several at the finding's finer tolerance; the boss root with the seam at 0, 45, 90, 180 and 270° gives one volume. The engine block's full script builds with the same volumes as before; FreeCAD reads the reproducers' STEP as valid solids with our volumes to 2e-10.

### Chains and meets of freeform blends (M9, 2026-10-15)

`kernel::blend::rolling::chain` and `chain::meet`. **Grouping.** The requests of one feature are grouped by their vertices (`chain::group`): where two requested edges end in one vertex and go on into each other within the angular tolerance they are a *joint* of a chain (walked from either end, or round to a closed chain); where they meet at an angle they are a *meet*; three or more at a vertex are refused. A closed-form edge that touches a freeform one (a line between two planes that continues a cone's cut edge) joins it and is built by `rolling` too; closed-form edges apart from every freeform one are blended after them by the tools and the boolean, on the body the freeform blends left (every entity of which keeps its number), and the sources are carried back (`blend::mixed`).

- **One family of balls, a face per edge.** A fillet chain is followed along the path of the balls' centres rather than in planes across the edges (the edge's planes pick balls badly where the blend lies far from its edge, as at a concave foot that crosses a fillet's tangent line at a shallow angle, nine millimetres from the vertex): from a ball, a step ahead along the path's direction (square to both normals at the feet, n₁ × n₂), the next ball in the plane square to it there, by a damped Newton step on the two distances (never more than half the radius at once); the step halves where the ball cannot be found or the path turns by more than a quarter radian, grows to a 24th of the edge where it runs straight. Each edge's face then uses the planes square to a clamped cubic spline with continuous curvature through all its balls (exact tangents at its ends), turned at the joints into the joint ball's own plane by a correction that fades out over half the edge (flat to the third derivative at both ends; a plane family that is only C1, as with a smoothstep or a Hermite path, stalls the refinement), so the faces on both sides of a joint end in exactly the same arc, which is their shared edge. A chamfer chain (no face may change at its joints) keeps the planes across the edges, which its lines depend on.
- **Joints.** Both faces go on (a vertex of the two edges only), or one goes on and the other changes into a face tangent to it across a smooth edge that leaves the vertex (cone into torus into plane along the casing's cut edge; flare, root fillet, boss along E6's boss foot), or a seam of one face passes through the vertex (crossed as a smooth edge between the face and itself). Where a face changes, the shared ball is the one whose foot on the changing side lies on the smooth edge (bisection along that edge for the point whose ball, the radius in along the common normal, is the radius from the face that goes on); the smooth edge is shortened to it, the vertex moves to the other foot, and a new vertex and the arc are added. Tangency across every joint is measured (ten times the refinement's bound).
- **Meets** (two fillets of one radius that end in one vertex at an angle; the vertex has three edges and three faces, the third edge stays sharp, all three convex or all concave). What is kept near the vertex is what both fillets keep: each fillet's face where it lies inside the other fillet's rounded corner and inside its far face (the other fillet's own face). Both faces run on past the vertex (until wholly beyond the far face, outside it for a convex meet, inside it for a concave one) and are trimmed per point across them by bisection on the larger of the two distances (beyond the far face; inside the other's corner: beyond its surface where the nearest point of it lies within its borders, beyond the face past its border on the shared face otherwise, and not the other's corner at all past its border on its own face). The two run out into each other in the curve they share, from where their borders on the shared face cross; of the two, the one whose border on its own face reaches the far face first is also cut by the far face, up to where the other fillet's border on that face meets it (taken on the other fillet's border, which crosses it, rather than along the trim, whose two pieces touch there); the third edge is shortened to where its border crosses it. Both reaching it in one point gives a vertex of four edges. Where the two run into each other there is a crease, as between two closed-form fillets that meet (§ *Fillets as built*).
- **Tests** (`crates/kernel/tests/blend_chains.rs`): the rims of the cap of an extruded smooth outline of a line, a half circle and a Bézier, closed and open (square ends), against the swept volume (Pappus-Guldinus along a plane curve: the corner's area times the length less its first moment times the turning) to 0.05 of the tolerance times the fillet's area, three scales and tilted; a casing's cut edge through its root fillet, every point of the three faces' surfaces against the rolling-ball definition (the centre the radius from the cut plane and from the wall in the meridian), eight tangent seams; a chain with a closed-form edge apart in one fillet (the volume of the two blends apart); E6's boss foot (five faces, fifteen tangent seams, the definition in the cross-section of the base); a half cone cut by a tilted plane, both top edges meeting at the cut edges, and the same as a pocket in a block (concave), every point of a grid held against the definition (kept exactly when a ball behind the top and the cone and one behind the top and the cut plane hold it, by Dykstra's projections onto the eroded convex sets), self-intersection check; a lofted blade's tip rounded all round, every mesh point of the fillets against the definition, three scales. In the engine (`crates/engine/tests/blend_chains.rs`): the turbofan's core casing cut by its block, both cut faces' chains in one feature with `chain`, refilleted after an edit; the lofted blade's tip; E6 refused at 3 mm (border off the flare's strip) and built at 1 mm.
- **Not built** (refused with the reason): a fillet whose border runs over a smooth edge of a face beside it in the middle of an edge (E6 in the full engine block: the boss's foot passes within about a millimetre of the 12 mm waist round's tangent line on the flare); concave fillets that meet round a sharp convex edge (a blade's root loop: the ball would have to roll round the edge's foot, a pinched patch); a chain whose two ends meet at one vertex; chamfers whose face changes at a joint, or that meet. A vertex patch with tangency (a setback, an n-sided patch) in place of the crease where two fillets meet is the next step for meets: the trimmed faces give its boundary.

### Blend findings of the impeller and the lamp (M9, 2026-10-15)

Two showcases (a compressor wheel's lofted blades, a lamp shade's cells cut through a revolved shell) found freeform blend refusals and one invalid body. Each change below is a method for any geometry, not a rule for the configuration that found it.

- **Meets, where the two fillets touch the face they share** (`chain::meet`; F3, F4). Both fillets are tangent to the shared face along their borders there, so the distance from a point of one border to the other fillet's surface is nearly flat, and where it changes sign was found only to about √(tolerance × radius) along the face: the shared curve started up to 1e-3 mm off the other fillet's border (refused, or caught by the check as an invalid body). The crossing of the two borders, two curves on one face that cross at the angle θ the edges meet at, is now found directly (`cross_borders`: the nearest points of the two curves by Gauss-Newton; refused where tol / sin θ, how well the crossing is known along them, is not less than their length), and the trim curve is held to lying on both surfaces (to a quarter of the tolerance) where its positions along them cannot be told closer. Which fillet also runs on along the other's face is told by where the two borders on their own faces leave what is kept: one point when the two places are closer than each is known (half the tolerance over the sine at which a border meets the third edge, the surfaces being within a quarter of it), no longer by the size of the nearly flat gaps. Two meets at both ends of one third edge (both rims of a thin face rounded where they reach a third face) may not take more than the whole edge: refused as too large, naming the face.
- **A blend that runs into a wall** (`rolling::End::facing`; F2). The blend is continued past the end of its edge until a whole section lies beyond the third face and cut where it crosses it; beyond means out of the third face's material where the edge runs out of it (an end face, met at convex edges) and into it where the edge runs into it (a wall it ends in, met at concave edges), told by the edge's direction past its end against the third face's normal wherever that is known beyond the bound the blend's normals are refined to; nearer to along the face it is taken as out of it and the end cut decides. The blend is followed past the end the further the more nearly the edge runs along the third face (the allowance for a square end over the sine at which the edge meets it), never for more samples than a blend face takes (4096): an edge that runs along the face is refused there.
- **A blend that ends past the corner** (`rolling::cut_back`, `going_on`, `widen_to`; F1). A root fillet that adds material ends where the faces beside it meet the third face at convex edges: its border on such a face reaches that face's edge along the third face past the vertex. That edge keeps all it had; a new edge goes on from its end to the corner along both its faces (a line where both are planes, exact; otherwise a cubic through points where the two surfaces cross, found by alternating projections onto each surface continued past its patch, refined until every point halfway between two of them is within a quarter of the tolerance of both), and the vertex between the two stays where it was. A spline face whose patch the new edge leaves is grown to take it, as far as the edge reaches along it and no further: `NurbsSurface::extended` clamps each end it grows (knot insertion to full multiplicity, the knots and points past the domain dropped), makes the end span a Bézier piece (Boehm's knot insertion) and takes the same piece over the longer interval (de Casteljau's subdivision at a parameter past 1, on the weighted points), so every point of the face keeps its parameters and nothing of it moves; it refuses to grow a surface past a seam where it closes on itself (its clamped first and last rows of control points the same curve).
- **More samples** (`refine::interpolate`; F6). A fillet at a fine tolerance along an edge whose faces have many knots (a lofted blade's tip at the default 1e-6 mm) needed more samples than the dense interpolation took (256); the refinement converged, the matrix was the limit. The collocation system is banded and totally positive, so it is solved within the band without pivoting (de Boor), in time linear in the samples; blend faces, borders and cuts take up to 4096 samples.
- **Joints where a face changes** (`chain::Around::joint`, `blend::sine_at`; B2). Whether the faces before and after a smooth edge at a joint are tangent was judged halfway along that edge, with a fixed sine of 1e-6; a closed rim then built or not depending on where its seam lay (pieces of the shade tangent to about 2e-6 rad). It is judged at the joint's vertex, to ten times what the blend's faces are refined to (the angular tolerance, or the angle the linear tolerance makes over the size: the bound the tangency across every joint is measured to afterwards). Each piece then ends in the ball its own faces hold in the joint's plane, which has to lie within three quarters of the tolerance of the shared arc (which stays the edge, so it is within the tolerance of a face held to a quarter of it; further off the faces are creased by more than the tolerance allows at the size, and the joint is refused), so its approximation is not asked to jump.
- **Tests**: `crates/kernel/tests/blend_chains.rs` (two fillets that meet at the corner of a spline wall at 80°, 89.5°, 90° and 100°; a rim across a joint tangent to 3e-6 rad, against the swept corner), `blend_ends.rs` (a convex fillet into a wall; a concave root fillet out at the end of a wall whose bowed spline side is grown, at 80° and 100°; a small fillet along a wavy rim at 1e-7 needing 406 samples), all at three scales in a tilted frame, every point of a grid against the rolling-ball definition and every mesh point of the fillets on it within the tolerance (0.005 to 0.03 of it seen); a fillet whose edge runs out at a slant of 20° and 30° to its end face; `crates/geom/src/extend.rs`, `refine.rs` (the continued surface is the same function inside and past its domain, also over unclamped knots with rational weights, and is not continued past a seam; the banded solve is the dense one). In the engine (`crates/engine/tests/blend_findings.rs`): the reproducers, built and checked deep.
- **Still refused** (work owed, with the general method):
  - A blend whose end runs over more than one face (the splitter's tip edge after its leading-edge chamfer, F9: the border on the tip face reaches past the chamfer into the end face). The end cut would follow the faces round the end in turn.
  - A blend ending at a face tangent to its side across a smooth edge (the tip edge after the leading edge was rounded, F2). That is a chain across the smooth edge; `chain` does not pick the next edge because the fillet's own seam is tangent only to its refinement bound, and chain picking asks 1e-9.
  - A joint whose faces are creased by more than the tolerance allows at the radius (lamp cells 10, 52, 84, 87; the rounded polygon cell, B4). The two pieces' balls differ by more than the tolerance; the general method is a crease joint, each face ending in its own arc and the gap closed like a meet at a small angle.
  - A ball that cannot be followed (lamp cells 32, 107, 108): not looked into.
  - A chamfer chain whose face changes at a joint (B4, B5). The joint section has to be found where the cut's point on the changing side lies on the smooth edge, as for fillets.
  - The full blade's leading edge and tip at 0.12 mm: the two meets would take more than the 0.6 mm tip edge. This is refused as too large and builds at 0.08 mm.

### What fits, decided by building (M9, 2026-10-15)

The estimate `fits` (before anything was built, it measured how far each face's boundary lay from the edge and refused a size whose cut or tangent line would leave the face) is gone, with the rules it grew one finding at a time: the boundary followed round tangent arcs and across seams (`smooth_run`, E2 and E5), edges beyond an arc's sector skipped (E3), a cut line that runs exactly into a corner let through (E7), a boundary that leaves an end the way the edge does taken as no room (E8). The closed-form blend is built for the size asked for and its result decides (`blend::fit`, the Method above). How each rule is covered:

- **E2, E5** (a boundary that runs on smoothly past the end of its first piece) and **E3** (an edge beyond an arc's sector): the build sees the face as it is, whatever its edges are cut into or wherever its seam lies; nothing has to be followed or skipped. The events list every vertex and every nearest point of every edge of the face, and only the builds say which one stops the blend.
- **E7** (a cut line exactly into a corner): the boolean builds it, and the result is the one three chamfers make in one feature (the test holds it against that).
- **E8** (a face that pinches to nothing beside the edge): no size builds, down to a thousandth of the radius; every failure is the boolean refusing the tool, which is no evidence of room, so it is refused with the boolean's own reason ("an imprinted curve that does not fit"), saying that smaller radii tried fail the same way. Until today it was called too large with nothing that fits, which nothing in the result showed.
- **The axis** (a circular edge's cut line that would cross it): one more event, and the sweep's own refusal (`CrossesAxis`) is what fails past it.

**Outcomes and expectation changes.** Every blend that built builds the same: the engine block (both scripts) and the turbofan give the same results line for line, measure and check included, and as fast. The hints changed where the estimate was wrong:

- A fillet far too large for the end of a rod of radius R, or for the bottom rim of a blind hole of radius rh, was said to fit below R (rh), where the end's tangent circle reaches the axis. Every radius from R / 2 (rh / 2) up is refused, because the torus would reach its own axis (a spindle torus face, work owed in the roadmap: R / 2 is the kernel's limit, not the geometric one), so the hint is R / 2 (rh / 2) now; held to 1e-9, and just below it builds, against Pappus's volume (kernel and engine tests).
- Two blends of one size that would meet in the middle of a face (two chamfers of 1.6 across a face 3 wide): the estimate held the other one at its size and said 1.4; the feature has one size, both shrink with it, and they fit up to 1.5 each, which is the hint now; just below it builds, against the closed-form volume (kernel and engine tests).
- The engine block's rail root (`RailRoot`, 8 mm) edited to 200 mm: the estimate said "below 97.98 mm"; the build says 12 mm, and it is right: 11.9 mm builds, 13 mm, 50 mm and 97 mm do not.
- The turbofan's last turbine disc rims (`T3DiscRims`, 1 mm) edited to 40 mm: the estimate said "below 8" on one face; the build says 4.5 mm on the other, and it is right: 4.4 mm builds, 4.6 mm does not.
- E8: the boolean's reason with "smaller radii tried fail the same way" instead of too large with nothing that fits (above).

**Cost of a refusal.** A few builds instead of none; what builds costs nothing more (no estimate is made). In the kernel tests (release, best of three, one thread) a simple refusal goes from well under a millisecond to about 2 ms, and E8's chain of four edges, where nothing builds down to the smallest radius, from about 15 ms to about 230 ms. On the models, a refused `feature.edit` costs about as much as before, the edit's regeneration being most of it: the engine block's rail root at 200 mm, five refusals in a row, 0.8 s each against 1.4 s with the estimate (measured under a load of about 12 on 14 cores, so the difference is noise); the turbofan's last turbine disc rims (`T3DiscRims`, 1 mm) at 40 mm, three refusals in a row, cost less than the run's own spread (about ±5 s in 120 s) with and without the estimate. No bound beyond the 48 builds was needed.

**Still open:** the events along a curved edge are found among 64 samples and refined (sampled, not proven, as elsewhere); a size between events is reported to a few tolerances.

### Blend findings of the bike and the turbofan (M9, 2026-10-15)

A bicycle frame (tubes welded into tubes, lofted oval tubes, dropouts) and the turbofan found six kinds of freeform blend refusal, most of them welds. Each change is a method for any geometry; the reproducers are in `crates/engine/tests/blend_findings/` (`bike-*`, `turbofan-*`).

- **A weld of a tube into a tube** (B7: the closed edge where two cylinders meet, at any angle, diameter ratio and radius; "could not be approximated"). The edge is the boolean's fitted curve, a cubic with continuous tangents only, about a hundred pieces round; the planes across it turn with a jump at every knot, and the ring's sections jittered by about 1e-5 mm (its tangent is only as good as the fit over a piece), which no number of samples could follow. A ring's balls are now taken in the planes square to the closed path of their centres (`rolling::closed_path`: the closed cubic with continuous curvature through the centres found in the edge's planes; which planes pick the balls does not change the surface, as for chains). Two more things the ring needed: (1) the refinement crept, a pass at a time, as halving a step pushed its neighbours just over the bound (errors at 0.8 of it went to 1.1 to 2 after the neighbour was halved, and the passes ran out); one refinement now serves open and closed faces (`refine_samples`) and halves, with every step over the bound, the steps the halving can push over it (`to_split`: the interpolating cubic's response to a change at one sample dies away by 2 − √3 per sample, the root of its recurrence for even steps). (2) Closed cubics through more than 256 samples: `geom::refine::periodic_derivatives` solves the cyclic tridiagonal system in linear time (Thomas, with the corners by Sherman–Morrison) where `periodic_tangents` inverted a dense matrix. The same creep was what refused the stay's weld to the dropout plate (B5, "could not be approximated" at three bows); it builds, and the weld on a plain cylinder stay now names what stops it, its border running off the stay's wall.
- **A loft that is not tangent where its sections are** (B1: a weld round a tube lofted from an oval of tangent arcs into a circle stopped at the loft's columns, "does not end cleanly"). The chain test was right: the lofted faces met at a crease of up to 1.6° (two sections) and more (three) between the sections, though both sections are smooth there. The cause and the fix are the loft's ([`sweeps.md`](sweeps.md) › *Lofts tangent where their sections are*). With it the welds of the tapered oval, the down tube, the top tube and the down tube into the head tube's cone build.
- **A weld whose ball runs past the end of a face** (B2: an oval tube welded to a conical head tube 1 mm from its end; the turbofan's fan blade root fillet): the body could not be meshed ("loop 2 lies outside the region its other loops bound"). The border on the cone crossed the end's rim, which the check that a border stays on its face (`stays_on`) did not see: it held 64 chords of the border against 64 chords of every other edge of the face, and on a curved face chords cut inside the surface by their sagittas, so where two curves cross their chords pass each other by up to a few hundredths of a millimetre. Chords that come within half their lengths of each other (every point of a piece of curve lies within half its length of one of its ends) are now followed to the curves' own nearest points (Gauss–Newton, `nearest_points`); a border that comes within ten tolerances of another edge is refused as running off the face. Refused so now: B2's head tube at four placements, the seat stay pushed back 25 mm, the fan blade's root.
- **One half of a rim** (turbofan F8: the rim between a spinner's top and its revolved spline side, which a full turn makes in two faces, each half of the rim an edge; "the rolling ball could not be followed"). The edge goes on straight into the other half at both ends; a blend cannot stop there. Refused saying so (`Reason::FreeGoesOn`), the closed-form blends' rule ("fillet the edges it continues in as well"), by the test `tangent_chain` picks chains with, so `chain` builds it (it did before: 0.5 to 2 mm, deep check).
- **Fillets that meet where the third edge turns the other way** (turbofan F11: all cut edges of a casing whose bore has a groove, cone, cylinder, cone; "fillets of freeform edges that meet in a vertex are not built yet: fillet them one by one"). Where the cylinder's cut edge meets a cone's, the edge between the two in the bore is concave and the fillets convex. Near such a vertex the balls inside the body include balls that touch the cut plane and the concave edge itself: the ball rolls over that edge from one fillet into the other, a patch that is neither fillet (its border on the bore runs along the edge, or is a point of it). Not built; refused saying so (`Reason::FreeRollOver`), since filleting them one by one does not build either (the second fillet ends against the first one's face). Without the cylinder's cut edges the rest builds.
- **Tests:** `crates/kernel/tests/blend_welds.rs`: the weld of a tube into a tube at 90°, 54° and 35° (radii 2, 0.5, 4) and the mouth of a blind cross hole at 90° and 60°, every point of a grid round the weld against the definition (a point is in the welded union exactly when it is in the union or no ball of the radius outside both cylinders reaches it; the distance to the set of such centres in closed form: radial projections and the curve where the two offset cylinders cross), three scales, tilted; a weld that runs off the end of the post refused at three scales, a small one there built by the definition. `geom::refine`: the linear periodic solve is the dense one (to 1e-12, and on 3000 points of a circle). In the engine (`blend_findings.rs`): every reproducer above, built and checked deep, or refused with its reason.
- **Outcomes**, the bike's 63 and the turbofan's 22 reproducers against the commit before: 23 of the bike's go from refused to built (all nine tube-into-tube welds, the bottom bracket, the stays into the seat tube, the dropout welds at three bows, the four lofted-oval welds), 7 are refused with a truer reason (an invalid body or "could not be approximated" before); the turbofan's F8 and F11 and its fan blade root fillet are refused with the reason that holds. Nothing that built refuses. The benchmark's counts are unchanged.
- **Still refused** (work owed, with the general method): the roll-over patch (F11; also the concave fillets round a blade's root loop under *Chains and meets*), a vertex patch whose boundary is the two fillets' end arcs and the ball's track on the plane, with the edge's point as a corner; a weld whose ball runs past the end of a face onto the next (B2, the plain cylinder stay at the dropout), the border carried on over the edge as for a smooth edge in the middle of a face.

### Fitted curves proven on their surfaces (consolidation, 2026-10-16)

The fitted curves of a rolling blend were accepted when they were within the tolerance at sample points (the middles between the points they were fitted through, 9 points across a section, 64 chords): a curve can pass all of them and leave its surface between. Each acceptance is now a proof (`geom::certify`, method in [`geometry.md`](geometry.md)), one call site at a time:

- **The cut at an end** (`rolling::cut_curve`): proven within a quarter of the tolerance of the blend face and of the third face; a step where the proof stops is halved. Test: a third face waving between the middles, a hundred quarter-tolerances off the straight cut through them (refined, proven).
- **A meet's trim** (`meet::trim`): proven on the fillet face it trims. Its other side (where the trim leaves what the far face or the other fillet keeps, a maximum of two signed distances) is still held at the middles: no one surface to prove it on.
- **The edge going on to a corner** (`rolling::going_on`): proven on both faces, else fitted through twice the points.
- **The borders** (`rolling::refine_samples`): once the middles hold, each border (an iso-curve of the blend face) is proven on its face between every two samples, at most 1024 pieces a step, and a step where it is not is halved.
- **A border staying on its face** (`rolling::stays_on`): two curves proven ten tolerances apart (`curves_apart`), a pair seen closer or not told apart refused; the Gauss-Newton nearest points are gone.
- **Tests:** in `blend/rolling.rs` and `rolling/chain/meet.rs`, one per site, each a face that waves (or bumps) between the samples and middles the old check looked at, at three scales where the scale matters; each fails with the proof switched off (the curve 1.2 to 10 times the limit off its face) and passes with it.
- **Left sampled:** the blend surface's interior against the exact rolling-ball sections (the exact surface has no derivative bounds to prove against; held at the middles, 9 points across); a fillet's tangency along its borders (`tangent_or_refused`, seven points; an angular bound needs bounds on both normals); the events along a curved edge in `blend::fit` (64 samples); the sweep along a helix (`helix.rs`, against the true sweep at stations).
