# 3DCraft design: Blends: chamfers and fillets

Chamfers and fillets: the features (`doc::Chamfer`, `doc::Fillet`, `regen::blend`) and the kernel (`kernel::blend`, its front ends `kernel::chamfer` and `kernel::fillet`, and `blend::rolling`). Method 3 of the map ([`../architecture.md`](../architecture.md) §5). The Method says how blends are made today; *Findings* keeps, per finding, only what still describes live code; *History* has one dated line for everything else.

## Method

**What a feature holds.** A list of edges, each as the names of the two faces it lies between (every edge between them; the order says which face a chamfer's first distance or angle is measured on) or as one edge's own name, with the face of it a chamfer's first size is measured on where the two sides differ ([`names.md`](names.md) › *An edge by its own name*), a size (a chamfer's one or two distances, or a distance and an angle; a fillet's radius) and `chain`. At each regeneration every pair is found again (`regen::edges_between`) and every edge by its name (`Names::edge_named`), followed to the newest body of its chain; with `chain` an edge stands for the edges that continue it smoothly (`blend::tangent_chain`). New faces are `F9:chamfer(k)` / `F9:fillet(k)` (numbered by place in space when a reference gives several), corners `chamferCorner` / `filletCorner`; kept faces are copies, instance 0.

**Two constructions, chosen per edge**, being merged into one (*One blend construction* below). `blend::blend_inner` plans every edge (`plan`); an edge no closed form covers goes in place with every edge that shares a vertex with it (`mixed`); of the others, the routed class (`in_place`: a straight edge between two planes, or next to a cylinder along it, whose ends are vertices of three edges where no other requested edge ends, every face there with one loop) goes in place too and the rest by the tools (`construct`). Sources are carried back to the body the feature started from (`carry_back`).

- *Closed form: a tool and the boolean.* Where the section across the edge is the same all along (a straight edge between planes or along a cylinder, a circular edge between a plane, a cylinder or a cone about its axis), the section (two lines or arcs and the fillet's arc or the chord) is extruded or revolved into a tool solid (`tool`), subtracted at a convex edge and added at a concave one (`combine`). The fillet face is a cylinder or a torus, the chamfer face a plane or a cone. Where an edge ends the tool runs past the vertex when the other edges there turn like it, ends square at the vertex otherwise, or is cut in the plane of a slanted face it runs into (`lengthen`, `end_plane`, `trim`). Two blends that meet run out into each other; three of one kind and one radius meet in a corner (a flat triangle for chamfers, a piece of the ball for fillets), made by one more boolean (`corners`, `ball_corners`). The tools of a chain are combined one by one; the boolean takes the contact where one goes on in the other.
- *In place: the rolling ball* (`blend::rolling`). Balls of the radius touching both faces are found in planes across the edge (for a chain and round a ring, square to a cubic with continuous curvature through the centres found); the arcs between their feet (a chamfer: the lines between points at the distances) are interpolated into one B-spline surface, refined until it is within a quarter of the tolerance of the exact section between samples and tangent to both faces within the bound `bend` (the angular tolerance, or the angle the linear tolerance makes over the size); each pass halves every step over those bounds and every step the halving can push over them (`to_split`). The borders are the surface's own iso-curves, proven on their faces between the samples (`geom::certify`). Every open edge on its own is one construction (`blend_open`): sections from the middle out past both ends, against the edge's faces, or piece by piece where a border crosses a smooth edge of its face (*Borders carried across a smooth edge*); the surface exact where a translation carries the edge and both faces into themselves (`exact`); cut where it crosses the third face at each end; spliced in as a chain of one piece (`chain::splice_one`). A ring is the closed surface between the edge and a new border (`ring`, `splice_ring`). Chains share a section at each joint; two fillets that meet at an angle are trimmed against each other (`chain::meet`). At an end the corner is followed along the boundary with the third face (`walk_on`), the neighbours are shortened, and a face beside it may be grown (`NurbsSurface::extended`). A border may not come within ten tolerances of another edge of its face, proven (`stays_on`); no loop of a face may be left in what the blend takes (`no_stranded_loop`, `no_loop_behind`). A face the blends use up exactly (its borders come to lie on its other edges from end to end) goes, and the two edges on its sides become one, on a surface that does not close up on itself (`used_up`, *Faces a blend uses up exactly*).

**Never a wrong solid.** Whether a size fits is not estimated: the blend is built and the result held against what a blend is (every blend face present, in one piece, bordering exactly its neighbours and the faces at the ends of its edge: `finish`; the boolean's own checks), a fillet's tangency is measured along its borders, a chamfer whose line between the cut lines is found to run through a face beside the edge by more than the tolerance is refused (exactly in the plane across the edge for the closed forms, `section::enters`, and where the construction in place carries such a chamfer's cut line across a smooth edge onto the face beyond, `carried`; in place in 65 planes along the edge, `rolling::shown`: a search, not a proof, *A chamfer through a face beside it*, *A cut line carried onto the face beyond*), a fillet in place whose ball is found to take in a face beside the edge between the edge and its foot on that face by more than the tolerance is refused (in the same 65 planes, `rolling::shown::ball`; the closed forms need no such test, a line or a circle tangent to a circle meeting it nowhere else: *A fillet through a face beside it*), a fillet in place whose section runs to nothing at a place strictly inside its edge (the two faces tangent there with an angle on both sides, or the edge turning from convex to concave) is refused with the place before anything is built (`blend::along`: the turn across the edge is read along it, at stations evenly apart in the edge's parameter, 8 times the spans of its curve and 64 at least, not at its middle only; *A fillet pinched inside its edge*), and a chamfer face a tool builds meets each face it was measured along turning as the edge did, within that (`turns_back`), `topo::check` runs, and regeneration checks near the new faces for self-intersection. A blend that does not build is searched for the largest size that does (`blend::fit`, *What fits*), every edge of that size together; a largest size is said only where it is shown: where an event of the faces gives it with a build just before it and the seven sizes evenly between it and the size asked fail too, or, where no event explains it, where the result itself shows no room at the size asked, where a bisection on the builds ends (or 4 or 64 tolerances past it) and at the seven sizes between; never from the boolean's failures alone nor as nothing that fits; shown only up to the size asked, the search building nothing above it, and otherwise the refusal at the size asked as it is, the boolean's in the blend's words (*Too large only where it is shown*); every search is bounded (48 builds). The two constructions and the checks still sampled (*Fitted curves proven*) are structural debt (the map's §9).

**A fillet's offset is the fillet rebuilt** (`blend::rebuild`, 2026-10-18): a face operation that moves a rolling-ball fillet with the two faces it rolls along rolls a ball of the radius moved by the distance between their offsets, in planes across the old fillet's border started from its old centres, refined as above, in the old face's parameters ([`face-operations.md`](face-operations.md) › *Offset of a fillet: rebuilt*).

**A radius that varies along the edge** (`blend::law`, `kernel::fillet::fillet_by_law`: 2026-11-17, *A fillet's radius as a law along its edge*; the command takes a fade to nothing only, `feature.fillet` with `fade`: *A fillet that fades, as a command*). A fillet's radius may be a law: one radius along the edge, running to another at an end over a length along the edge, by `r = to + (radius − to)(1 − (1 − τ)³)` with `τ` the distance from that end over the length, measured along the edge by its arc length. The ball in the plane across the edge at a point has the law's radius there, and the face is the arcs between the feet of those balls. It is built by the construction in place whatever the edge is (a law has no closed form: `plan` hands it over as `Reason::KindLaw`), with three things of its own: each ball is started from its neighbour resized to its radius (`resized`); the fitted face's normal across it is held to the normal of the arcs between the feet, from the exact derivative of the section along the edge (`rolling::slope`), not to the ball's radius, which it is not where the radius changes; and the face is followed to an end itself and past it only as far as it has to be. A fade has to be at least as long as the radius changes over it (the radius then changes at most three times as fast as the length along the edge), and an end radius and a fade's length more than 16 tolerances: otherwise refused with what would be built (`KernelError::FilletFade`), since a face whose radius changes faster cannot be measured or meshed. A fade to nothing (`Fade::tip`) ends the fillet in a point: the face closes in a vertex on the edge, at the edge's end or `back` short of it, with nothing cut there, so that end asks nothing of its vertex (the edge may run on into another between the same two faces, or end where four faces meet), and the edge beyond the point stays as it is (*A fillet that ends in a point*). A law of one radius is the fillet of that radius, by the same path as before. Built along an open edge on its own whose ends are corners of three faces or such points; refused in a chain, round a ring, where another requested edge ends in one of its vertices and where its border would cross a smooth edge (`Reason::LawAlone`).

**Refused, with the reason**: vertex blends of three or more fillets in other arrangements, outside and inside blends that meet, two blends of one kind that meet where the third edge is of the other (the ball would roll over that edge), edges that end at four or more faces, a freeform edge that ends where it goes on straight into another edge (fillet the chain), blends whose border runs over a sharp edge of its face, or over a smooth one in a chain, a ring or a meet, or over more than one smooth edge from one end, or whose end runs over more than one face, creased joints, and the rows of *Remaining special cases*.

## Remaining special cases, with the general method owed

Conditions that exist for one configuration and are not (yet) a general method. Each is behaviour today: removing it changes results, so it waits for the general method named (most are steps of *One blend construction*). Iteration caps and the tolerances every fitted curve is held to are not listed: they are the general method's own.

## Owed, found by the coordinator's demo (2026-11-04)

- *The vertex patch where a concave fillet meets convex ones*: a blade root rounded onto a hub (concave) whose leading and trailing edges are rounded too (convex) meet at a vertex no corner patch here closes; a vertex blend of mixed sides (a setback patch, or a Gregory or subdivided N-sided patch as the fills build, [`surfacing.md`](surfacing.md)) is the method owed.

## A concave chamfer next to a cylinder whose chord enters it (2026-11-13; refused since 2026-11-14)

**A wrong solid, in 0.1.0 to 0.1.3** (0.1.3, by the pair of faces at 8 along the pipe and 1 along the top: 40123.44 mm³, status ok; measured 2026-11-19). A plate 60 × 40 × 10 and a pipe of radius 10 along x whose axis is at (y, z) = (20, 16), united: the concave edge where the pipe leaves the plate's top, chamfered 1 along the top and `d` of arc along the pipe. While the chord from the cut line on the top to the one on the pipe stays outside the pipe (`d` up to 4.4657, worked out by hand), the chamfer is the closed form (the triangle of the corner and the two cut lines less the pipe's segment the chord cuts off). Past it the chord runs through the pipe and the same construction is built, the deep check passing: the chamfer, which fills a concave corner, takes material away (`d` = 8 gives 40144.61 mm³, 30 gives 31981.21, against the union's 40165.78; by the pair of faces, both edges, 10 gave 39784.01 in 0.1.0). It is refused only from half a turn of the pipe on ("the largest distance that fits there is below 31.4158"). Since *A chamfer through a face beside it* (below, 2026-11-14) such a chord is refused as too large, with the largest distance that fits. Held by `crates/engine/tests/blend_edge_names.rs` › `a_concave_chamfer_whose_chord_would_enter_the_pipe_is_too_large` (ignored as owed until 2026-11-19, when it was run and passed: Check 44501216406); the sizes that are right: `a_chamfer_by_name_along_the_pipe_is_the_closed_form_while_its_chord_stays_outside_it`.

## A fillet of a cylinder's rim refused at some document tolerances, fine ones too: owed (2026-11-19)

*Found* by a prompt test of a published example, the RC yacht hull (its hatch cover, 60 mm across with its top edge rounded 1 mm, in a document at a tolerance of 0.02 mm; F-182 in the examples' feedback), and reduced to three steps: a new document, a cylinder of radius 30 mm and height 8 mm, and `feature.fillet` of 1 mm on its top rim.

    craft3d-cli run --new '{"units":"mm","tolerance":0.02}' --keep-going \
      --cmd feature.cylinder --params '{"radius":"30 mm","height":"8 mm","name":"Disc"}' \
      --cmd feature.fillet --params '{"edges":[["F1:face(0)","F1:face(2)"]],"radius":"1 mm","name":"Round"}'

*What it does* (measured on 0.1.3 and 0.2.0, which answer the same at every tolerance tried; 0.1.0 to 0.1.2 not tried): at document tolerances from 0.03 mm down to 0.0012 mm the fillet is refused: *"the boolean that cuts this fillet refused it: boolean does not support this: a piece of a face lies within tolerance of a face it is not coincident with (at the edge of tolerance) (face 0 of the first body)"*, from 0.01 mm down with *"; smaller radii tried fail the same way"*; at 0.0011 mm and finer it builds, to the closed form (22 579.3168 mm³), and at 0.05 mm too. The bottom rim is refused at 0.02, 0.01 and 0.002 mm and builds at 0.001 mm (at 0.02 mm naming face 1, the bottom). Other sizes are refused at other tolerances, fine ones too: on the same cylinder a 0.5 mm fillet builds at 0.03 and 0.02 mm and is refused from 0.01 mm down to 0.0002 mm, a 2 mm fillet only at 0.02 mm, a 3 mm fillet at none of 0.02 to 1e-5 mm; a 1 mm fillet on a cylinder of radius 10 mm and height 20 mm only at 0.02 mm, on one of radius 100 mm and height 8 mm at 0.03 mm and from 0.01 mm down to 0.0002 mm, on one of radius 5 mm and height 5 mm at none. Of the 65 published documents, 8 are at 0.01 mm and one (the yacht) at 0.02 mm; 46 keep the default of 1e-6 mm.

*Not traced* (inferred from `boolean::classify`): the refusal is the classification's: a point taken inside a piece of one face (`Outline::interior_points`, stepped in from the middle of a boundary piece by fractions of its chord, for a circle its diameter) lies within twice the tolerance of a face of the other body. The fillet's torus leaves the wall and the top tangentially, so beside each join it lies within twice the tolerance of the face it leaves over a band about 2√(r·t) wide (r the fillet's radius, t the tolerance), at any tolerance; whether the point lands there would depend on the rim's radius, the fillet's and the tolerance, as the pattern above does. Which piece of which face it is, is not known.

**Owed:** the fillet of a cylinder's rim at every document tolerance (other closed rims not tried); if the cause is the one inferred above, the boolean that cuts a fillet must not refuse at the fillet's own tangent joins. The same test's subtract where a deck crosses a torus-to-wall tangent circle (refused as *"tangent contact of higher order"*, at 0.02 to 0.002 mm) may be the same nearness; it was not reduced.

## A chamfer that lost track of an entity (found 2026-11-20, fuzz seeds 109228 and 117254; refused as too large since 2026-11-21)

*Found* by the random designs' 30-minute run of the plane cut's machines (Heavy 44825476736, [`plane-cut.md`](plane-cut.md) › *As built: the machines*): a chamfer failed with a bug the code caught itself, *"the chamfer lost track of an entity"* (`kernel::blend`, `Reason::Lost`), where it must be built or refused with a reason. Seed 109228 (scale 1e-3, tolerance 1e-9): a cone's rim chamfered, cut by four planes to a slab (the first and the last change nothing), the slab taken out of a slanted cylinder, and the edge between the cylinder's end and the slab's cut face, 0.0012 long, chamfered by 0.005. Seed 117254 (scale 1): a block's chamfered edges patterned five times round an axis, so that five of the chamfer faces make a pit shaped like an upside-down pentagonal pyramid in the top, cut by an extrusion, filleted, and three edges chamfered by 5, one of them a rim of the pit, 0.908 long; it has no plane cut. 0.2.1 fails on 117254 alike (measured 2026-11-21; it has no plane cut to run 109228).

*Reduced* (by commands, on `main` before the repair): in 117254 the pit's rim alone fails, from a distance of 1.3976 on, and is "too large, below 0.883883" (the width of the pit's wall) from 0.89 to 1.3975; in 109228 the edge fails from 0.0020693 on and is refused as not ending cleanly below it, at every distance (its other end runs into the cone). The smallest family is a block 20 × 30 × 10 with a pocket 3 deep, 10 wide, whose far end is a ramp, the plane x − y + z = 0 through the rim's corner (5, 15, 10) (`crates/engine/tests/blend_common` › `ramp_pocket`): the rim along the wall x = 5, 10 long, ends square against the pocket's near wall and at a slant in the ramp. Chamfered, it builds below 3 (the wall's height), is too large with 3 as what fits from 3 to 9.999, and failed with the bug's words from 10 on, the rim's length, at scales 1e-3, 1 and 1e6 alike; filleted, the same from 10 / (2 − √2) = 17.071 on (17.07 too large, 17.08 the bug).

*Cause.* Where a straight edge ends at a vertex whose one other face is a plane at a slant to it, the blend's tool is made longer and cut in that plane (`end_plane`, `trim`; *Where an edge ends* in the module's method), so its end lies in the face and the boolean merges it there. `trim` then looked for the tool's blend face among the faces the cut left and took it for a bug when there was none (or more than one). But the plane passes through the vertex, and the whole blend face lies past it as soon as every point of the section is further past the plane at the vertex than the plane slants back along the edge to where the tool begins: on the ramp, far · (u, w) = (u + w)/√3 past it for a point u along the top and w down the wall, against 10/√3 back along the rim to its square end, so from a chamfer of 10 (u + w = d on the cut line) or a fillet of 10 / (2 − √2) (u + w = (2 − √2) r at the middle of the arc). In 117254 both ends of the rim are cut, by the pit's two neighbouring walls, and between them they take the whole face from 1.3976 on (0.908 · 0.6725 / (2 · 0.2185) with the pentagon's angles); in 109228 the one end in the slab's other cut face takes it from 0.0020693 on (1.1947e-3 · 0.70 / 0.404). The search for what fits never ran, because `Reason::Lost` is no size a smaller one can mend.

*Which it is: a geometry no blend makes.* A blend face wholly past the plane it has to end in is no face at all; and before it goes, from the size at which the cut line leaves its face, the build is refused as too large already. So the right answer is a refusal, the one the check at the end gives a blend face that is not there or not in one piece (`Reason::InPieces`, *"the chamfer face came out in pieces or not at all: the distance is too large for what is near the edge"*), which the search for what fits takes as a size a smaller one can mend. **Repair:** `trim` refuses so, naming the edge, where it said the bug (both ways: no face left, or a face in two pieces, which no design found reaches, and which by reading (the review, 2026-11-21) a chamfer never reaches, since its face's section is a segment and every plane keeps one piece of a segment, nor a fillet between planes, since its arc is the part of the ball's circle seen from the edge, so that the point of the arc furthest past a plane through an end of the edge, when it is not an end of the arc, lies short of that plane and the face keeps one piece, also cut at both ends; only a fillet next to a cylinder that curves towards the ball, the cut edge of a halved rod, whose foot on the cylinder lies past the arc seen from the edge, could in principle lose a band inside its face, and is refused alike); nothing that built is touched, since only what was an error before answers otherwise now. The ramp's rim is too large with 3 as what fits at every distance from 3 on; 117254 at 5 is *"too large for edges.2 … the largest distance that fits there is below 0.625"* (another of its three edges; 0.624 builds and checks deep); 109228 at 0.005 is the refusal in pieces itself, since no smaller distance builds (the cone end is refused at every one).

## Too large only where it is shown (2026-11-25; the developer's decision of 2026-11-24)

*Found* by the review of the invalid tool (2026-11-23, *A tool boolean's invalid body* › *Owed*): where the boolean that cuts a blend refuses at sizes that fit, the search for what fits said *"too large … the largest radius that fits there is below X"* below radii that build, from the boolean's refusals other than an invalid body (43 of 45 such answers in 124545 and seven variants of it, before the boolean's repair of 2026-11-24). *Measured on `main` 22f0bb13* (a probe, Checks 45588331859 and 45590996086, `plan/too-large-shown`, the workshop's: the search switched per thread between `main`'s and the new one, traced; 121 configurations, the review's families of 124545, 103578, 110541, the rims on a ball and on a plane cut, 121649 and 128676 and a hole wholly in the strip a fillet takes, at scales 1e-3 to 1e6 and turned X to Y to Z and 30 degrees about Z; 899 refused sizes searched): 798 said too large, 577 from an event, 8 from an event after an invalid body (proven by the sizes between), 212 from the bisection on builds alone. *Rule* (`fit::explain`). A largest size is said only where it is shown:

- an event (the border reaches something of its face) with a build just before it, and the seven sizes evenly between it and the size asked failing (`proven`): the rule for an invalid body since 2026-11-23, now from every refusal. An event at the size asked to a tolerance (the rounding of its measure) has the size asked, which failed, as all there is between (a chamfer of a pocket's rim exactly as large as its wall is high, at scale 1e-3, `blend::tests`), and no build before it is made at or above the size asked (until the review's correction of 2026-11-25, 64 tolerances, and builds above the size asked);
- where no event explains it, the bisection on the builds, only where the result shows no room (`no_room`: the blend reaches a face it should not, takes a neighbour away, comes out in pieces, runs past an edge or off its face, folds, passes through a face beside it; not a carry across a smooth edge that the construction in place cannot make, `Reason::FreeCarry`, said as such since the review's correction) at the size asked, at the size the bisection ends on or 4 or 64 tolerances past it (the build at 4 failing too, the sizes between 4 and 64 not built: where a face is about to be taken whole the boolean refuses the sliver left of it, so the size said may be where those refusals begin, up to 64 tolerances below the first size tried that shows no room), and at the seven sizes between, each;
- never as nothing that fits (*"below 0"*): where nothing tried builds, no build shows where the room ends.

*The words say what is shown* (since the review's correction): nothing at or above the size asked is built, so a size found by builds is said as *"radius: too large for edges.0 (between …): no radius from X mm up to the R mm asked fits beside the face …; one fits just below X mm"* (a chamfer's *"no distance along the face … from X mm up to the D mm asked fits; …"*; where X is the size asked, *"a radius of R mm does not fit beside the face …; one fits just below X mm"*), not as the largest that fits: a larger size, which takes away whatever was in the way, may fit (`KernelError::FilletTooLarge`'s `shown`, `Shown::Builds`). The words *"with a radius of R the line along which the fillet touches the face … leaves that face; the largest radius that fits there is below X"* are kept where the faces show it, worked out (`Shown::Faces`: a fillet's ball that does not fit a rim's cylinder or cone, `section`; a ball past its rim's axis, `on_axis`).

Otherwise the refusal at the size asked, as it is: the boolean's in the blend's words (*"edges.0 (between …): the boolean that cuts this fillet refused it: …"*), or the blend's own (*"… takes away the whole of a face next to the edge, or does not reach it: the radius is too large"*), with *"; smaller radii tried fail the same way"* where every one tried does. Bodies are as they were: `explain` runs only after the first build failed and returns a refusal; a body comes from that first build alone.

*The reading.* The decision names the event rule. A bisection whose failures at the size asked and at the seven sizes between it and where the bisection ends all show no room from the result, and the failure where it ends too or one no further than 64 tolerances past it, is kept as shown: such a failure is the faces' (the result is held against what a blend is), where the boolean's refusals are no evidence of room. ### Faces a blend uses up exactly (2026-11-13, `rolling/used_up.rs`, `rolling/cross.rs` › `inside_along`)

**Finding.** A round or chamfer whose borders meet exactly on a face, so that nothing of it is left, was refused by regeneration's self-check: "the filleted body cannot be meshed to see whether it passes through itself (face 4 cannot be tessellated: its loops do not bound a region: they enclose no area …)". Four designs: the four uprights of a 14 × 6 × 10 box rounded 3 (each 6 wide end used up), both long edges of a 5 tall face chamfered 2.5, both uprights at one end of an 80 × 20 × 20 box rounded 10, the inside bend of an L whose inside faces run 35 from the bend rounded 35 (both inside faces used up). **Which side was wrong: the blend.** The faces' loops, dumped from the kernel's result, were each two edges between the same two vertices, run one way and back, on one line to the last digit: the second border lay exactly on the first (or on the face's far edge), `cut_back` had taken the short edges between whole (E7), and what was left of the face bounded no area. `topo::check` passed it (its region test leaves loops it cannot chart without doubt alone) and the volume was right; the tessellator was right to refuse it. Through the tools (circles: both rims of a pipe's end rounded half its width) the same design is refused as too large (`finish` reads the face the boolean took away as a lost neighbour); in place along a freeform edge (the box with spline ends) it was refused as a border running over a smooth edge (the first round's border, tangent to the end, is a smooth edge of it; below).

**Method.** (1) *A face used up goes.* After the blends are spliced in, every face the body had whose loops the blend changed is held to this: every coedge of its loops pairs with another of the face that runs between the same two vertices the other way, and the two curves are proven one within the tolerance (`geom::certify::curves_within`, [`geometry.md`](geometry.md) › *Two curves proven one*). A region whose boundary cancels is made of whole pieces of its surface that close up on themselves. A surface with a border of its own has none (a plane, a cylinder and a cone run off to infinity; a spline patch has the sides of its parameter rectangle wherever they are neither joined to the opposite side nor pinched to a point), so there the face bounds no area. On a surface that closes up everywhere (a sphere, a torus, a spline closed both ways or closed one way and pinched to a pole at both ends of the other) the same loops bound nothing or the whole surface (a band of no width round a torus and the torus less that circle have one and the same loops), so such a face is left as it is and refused further on, as before (`closes_up`); this holds however the face came to be so, and no claim about what a blend does to a face is needed. A face used up is taken out, and each pair becomes one edge, the lower-numbered one kept where it is proven on the surface of the face across the other (the other where not), which takes its place there, run as that face ran the edge it replaces. Where a pair is missing or neither edge is proven, the face stays and the body is refused further on, as before. The kept edge lies between other faces now, so it is reported as made by the blend, not as the body's. (2) *Inside by more than the tolerance* (`cross::inside_along`). Whether the boundary with the third face leads into what the blend takes, to a smooth edge the border would cross, was told by the sign of how far inside a point is. A face used up exactly has its far vertex on the border of what the blend takes, so the sign there was rounding: on the same design the box got as far as the face of no area at scale 1 and was refused as running over a smooth edge at 1e-3 (which the search for what fits then reported as too large). The boundary now has to be inside by more than the tolerance in that measure's own terms (`Blend::margin`): for a ball, how much nearer than the radius the centre of the ball on the point comes to the other face; for a chamfer, how much nearer than the distance the point comes to the edge. A vertex within that band of the border is where the border ends, not a way in, and nothing is crossed. For a ball the band is not the vertex's distance from the border along the face: a step along the face moves the centre towards the other face by the step times the sine of the angle at which the faces' surfaces meet, so along the face the band is the tolerance over that sine, wider at a shallow edge (5.8 tolerances where the faces turn by 10°). It only ever turns an answer from crossed to not crossed, and a vertex that is then further from the blend's corner than the tolerance is not taken by `cut_back`: the blend is refused as too large.

**Conditions and why they are general.** Nothing asks what the curves are, or how the face came to be used up: the pairing is topological, the coincidence and the kept edge are proven (not sampled) for any curve and surface kind, and the one condition on the surface is its topology (whether it closes up everywhere), told for every kind the kernel has. Only faces the blend changed are looked at (their loops compared in place, the faces in a shell found once: a fillet on a body of 16,006 faces took 72.9 ms with the first version of this step and 36.4 ms without it, and takes 37 ms now; 32,006 faces: 67 to 69 ms, 71 to 75 ms without it, an optimised test build on the laptop), so a face it did not touch stays as it was. The margin is the tolerance itself in the measure's units, scale-free; it moves answers only within its band of the border, where the sign was rounding, and only towards not crossed.

**Tests** (all but the guard fail without the change): `crates/kernel/tests/blend_used_up.rs`: the four designs at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6, on the world frame, a tilted one away from the origin and a tilted one far from it (moved by (1000, −700, 300)), in both request orders: valid, volume and area against the closed form from the section (its area times the length; twice its area plus its perimeter times the length), the used-up faces gone and the faces on their two sides sharing an edge (two rounds of one cylinder, two chamfers along the ridge, the round and the legs' ends), watertight meshes with their volume, the self-check clean; ### A fillet along an earlier round of its own radius (2026-10-21, an R1 blocker)

**Finding.** "Round all edges of a box in two steps", the upright edges first and then the rim of the top as a chain, was refused for every radius from half the rounds' on: along each arc of the rim (the top of an upright round, radius `R`) the ball of radius `ρ` has its centre `R − ρ` from the round's axis, so for `ρ ≥ R/2` its torus reaches its own axis (`Reason::Spindle`), and at `ρ = R` the search said "too large" (the border on the top reaches the axis).

**Method.** At `ρ = R` (the centre within the tolerance of the axis) the ball no longer turns about the axis: the fillet along the arc is a piece of that one ball, which `revolve` already makes a sphere for a section centred on the axis. Two changes make it a blend: the spindle refusal takes only a centre off the axis (`from_axis > tol`), and `finish` accepts a border drawn into a point on the axis (`Plan::border_on_axis`: the foot on that face as far toward the axis as the rim is from it) as meeting its face there, at a vertex, when that face is still in the result (a rod's top filleted with the rod's radius loses its top, which stays "too large"). The result is the box rounded all round in one step, ball for ball. **Past the axis** (`ρ > R`) no ball of the size touches the face across the rim; the plan records the radius that puts the centre on the axis (`Plan::past_axis`: `own = ρ R / (R − from_axis)`, `from_axis` being linear in `ρ`), `blend_inner` builds at it, and when that builds says "too large" with it as what fits (between it and half of it nothing is built, so it is the largest); when it does not, the search runs as before.

**Conditions and why they are general.** The centre on the axis within the linear tolerance is the test `revolve` makes a sphere by, so plan and tool agree; the border on the axis is measured from the plan's own feet and the rim's radius, for either face and any rim (convex or concave, a rod's or a round's); the face that loses its border must still have a piece, as `LostNeighbour` asks of every blend.

**Tests** (fail before): `crates/kernel/tests/fillet_along_round.rs` (the two-step box against the closed form `wdh − (4 − π) r² h − 2 (w + d − 4r)(1 − π/4) r² − 4 (π r³/4 − π r³/6)` and against the one-step body, volume and area, four balls about points on the rounds' axes, at three scales and turned; shelled as thick as its rounds; 1.5 refused as spindle, 2.5 and 3 too large with 2 as what fits, 0.5 four tori as before), `crates/engine/tests/fillet.rs` › `a_box_rounded_in_two_steps_of_one_radius` (checked deep, an edit of the box's width followed, shelled 2 open at the bottom, 3 too large saying 2). **Still refused:** `R/2 ≤ ρ < R` (a spindle torus: its spline twin from `revolve` builds the tool, but the boolean refuses its tangent contact with the next fillet's cylinder over too large an area).

**Within the tolerance of the round's radius (2026-10-22, the m9-r1 review).** A radius `ρ = R ± δ` with `δ` up to the tolerance puts the ball's centre within the tolerance of the axis, so the plan takes it as on the axis and builds it; the build fails for most such `δ` (the boolean: the ends of the straight fillets of radius `ρ` and the round of radius `R` are `δ` apart, "at the edge of tolerance"), and the search for what fits scaled every size down by one multiple and landed below `R/2` (0.9977 for `R = 2`), past the spindle band, though `R` builds. Now `Plan::past_axis` records the radius on the axis for a centre within the tolerance of it as well (`PastAxis::within`), and when the build fails, `on_axis` builds at that radius (once, no retry of the retry): if it builds and is smaller than `ρ`, "too large" with it as what fits; if it is larger (the centre short of the axis), the spindle refusal, which names the rim's own radius as one that builds, as for the sizes below. A centre past the axis by more than the tolerance is tried before the build, as before. What the band does now, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6 with `δ` from a few ulps to 1.1 tolerances: `R + δ` builds (a few ulps, where the booleans cannot tell the sizes apart) or is too large with `R`, which builds; `R − δ` builds or is a spindle, and `R` builds. *Test:* `fillet_along_round.rs` › `radii_within_the_tolerance_of_the_rounds_build_or_say_what_fits` (before: the false largest at all three scales). Building the band (snapping `ρ` to `R`) would be a snap of the kind D-open-16 asks about, so it is not done.

### Chamfers as built (M9, 2026-10-14)

`doc::Chamfer` has `edges` (references), `size` (`ChamferSize`: `Equal { distance }`, `TwoDistances { distance, second }`, `DistanceAngle { distance, angle }`, all expressions) and `chain`; it makes its body from the body of an earlier feature and takes it, like a transform. `kernel::chamfer::chamfer(body, edges, tolerance)` takes per edge the edge, its first face and a size, and returns the body with a source for every face (`Kept`, `Chamfer` of which requested edges, `Corner` at which vertex), edge and vertex.

- *An edge is the names of its two faces, in order* (`[F1:face(1), F1:face(2)]`): the order says which face the first distance is measured along and which the angle is taken from; after a boolean the edge's own name is that pair; when a later edit cuts the edge in two both pieces stay chamfered; no numbered name is stored. The price: two faces that meet in several edges are chamfered along all of them, and one of several cannot be picked by the pair (since 2026-11-13 by the edge's own name, [`names.md`](names.md) › *An edge by its own name*). The chamfered body is the body of the feature the first face is of, after the names are followed to the newest body of their chain.
- *Resolution.* Each pair is found again with `regen::edges_between`; a face that is gone, or faces that no longer meet, fail the feature with the parameter and the names; what is made from a failed chamfer fails naming it (`causedBy`). With `chain` each edge stands for the edges that continue it smoothly (`tangent_chain`: at each end the one other edge that leaves the vertex straight on, `straight_on`); along the chain the first face is the one the edges share, or what continues it.
- *Which face gets which distance.* `distance` along the first face, `second` along the second, each along the face at right angles to the edge; `angle` between the first face and the chamfer face inside the corner, so the second face is cut `distance · sin(angle) / sin(angle + θ)` back (θ the angle between the faces across the edge; the sine rule in `distances`). Turning the pair round turns the distances round.
- *Names.* A face that is still there is a copy, instance 0, with its aliases. The face of reference `k` is `F9:chamfer(k)`, or `F9:chamfer(k,0)`, `F9:chamfer(k,1)`, … in the order of where the edges' middles lie (x, then y, then z) when it gave several; the flat corner is `F9:chamferCorner(i,j,k)`; chamfer faces in one plane that became one face are named after the first, the others aliases. Edges and vertices are named by their faces (`Names::name_entities`).
- *The frame of a flat chamfer face* (`reframe`): z out of the body; x along the edge, in the direction of the cross product of the directions into the first and the second face; the origin where the cut line on the first face starts in that direction. The in-place construction gives its plane the same frame; the frame never moves the plane (where the cut line was carried onto the face beyond, the origin is on that face: *A cut line carried onto the face beyond*).
- **The tool** (`tool`). A straight edge between two planes: `kernel::extrude` of the triangle (edge, edge + d₁ along the first face, edge + d₂ along the second). A circular edge between a plane, a cylinder or a cone about its axis: the triangle in the half-plane through the axis, turned by `kernel::revolve`; the face is a cone. Convex or concave is read from the solid (`across`), never from the direction of the edge. Every tool is made from the body as given, so the order of the edges does not change the solid.
- **Where an edge ends.** Every other edge at the vertex convex like it (or every one concave): the tool goes on past the vertex (past where the chamfer plane cuts the other edges, with a margin; by an angle for an arc). Otherwise it ends across the edge at the vertex, which is right against a wall square to the edge and where the next tool of a chain begins; against one plane at a slant it is cut in that plane (*Blend findings of the engine block*). Any other end leaves a face nobody asked for, which `finish` refuses.
- **Corners.** Two chamfers that meet: a mitre (the line both planes share). Three of one kind that meet in a vertex with nothing else: the flat face through the three points where the cut lines cross, made by one more boolean with a block against that plane; when one chamfer is larger than the other two reach together it takes the corner whole and there is no corner face. It is what OpenCascade gives (a 4 × 3 × 2 block with all twelve edges by 0.25: volume, area and 26/48/24 counts to the last digits, through FreeCAD) and Fusion's corner type "chamfer"; the pointed mitre corner is not offered. More than three edges, or convex and concave chamfers, meet as their half-spaces.
- **Never a wrong solid.** After the last boolean the result is held against what a chamfer is (`finish`): every chamfer face there, in one piece, bordering both neighbours and nothing but those, the faces at the ends of its edge and the blends and corners there; meeting each neighbour turning as its edge did, within the tolerance (`turns_back`, the tools only); no other face of a tool left; no chamfer face part of a face of the body. Anything else is `KernelError::Chamfer` with the edge; the boolean's refusals are `ChamferBoolean`; a size that does not build is searched (*What fits*) and refused as `ChamferTooLarge` with the largest that fits.

|---|---|

### Fillets as built (M9, 2026-10-14)

`doc::Fillet` has `edges` (references, as a chamfer's), `radius` and `chain`; everything a chamfer does with its references a fillet does with the same code (`regen::blend`). `kernel::fillet::fillet(body, edges, tolerance)` takes per edge the edge and a radius and returns the body with its sources (`Kept`, `Fillet`, `Corner`).

- *An edge* is its two faces; their order says nothing: turning a pair round gives the same body and names. Names as a chamfer's: `F9:fillet(k)`, `F9:fillet(k,0)`, …, the corner `F9:filletCorner(i,j,k)`.
- *What can be put on a fillet face:* a datum axis (`datum.axis` with `face`: the cylinder's or the torus's own axis) and its edges with its neighbours (a later blend takes them by the pair of faces). Not a sketch: the face is not flat. A face next to a fillet keeps its frame.
- *After a fillet the neighbouring edge is part of a chain:* the top left edge of a block whose top front edge is rounded runs on smoothly in the arc where the fillet meets the left face; a blend of that edge alone stops in the middle of a chain and is refused ("does not end cleanly … fillet the edges it continues in as well"); with `chain` it runs round the corner.
- **The section and the tool.** The ball touches each face `r / tan(θ/2)` from the edge (`half_tan`), its centre on the bisector; the tool's section is the kite between the edge and the two tangent points less the sector of the ball, extruded along a straight edge (a **cylinder**) or revolved about a circular one (a **torus**). Every edge's tool is combined on its own, also in a chain: the boolean takes the contact of a cylinder that goes on in a torus since 2026-10-14 (`a_rod_that_goes_on_in_a_ring` in `boolean_surfaces.rs`) and of a torus that goes on in another torus (the closed form of geometry §5.1; `fillet_contact.rs` › `a_chain_of_arcs_that_go_on_in_each_other`, Pappus to 1e-13 at three scales). **`join` removed (2026-10-18, consolidation round):** until then the tools of a chain were joined into one body before the boolean (the two ends that lay against each other taken out and their edges and vertices made one), the one topological step of the tool path, written for a contact the boolean refused in 2026-10-14. Measured first with a switch (the filletcontact experiment, not in the tree): the kernel's blend, chamfer, fillet, offset-fillet and fillet-contact tests pass both ways and all 59 chained blends among their 1083 calls are the same bit for bit; the engine's chained blends differed in their last bits. Landed (`rm-join`, a974734) and measured bit for bit on every `build` result: faces, edges, vertices and names the same everywhere; of the engine blend tests' 12 distinct chained results 4 moved by one ulp of volume or area (all in `fillet.rs` › `a_boss_on_the_face_next_to_a_fillet_and_blends_on_neighbouring_edges`: the chamfer chain and the fillet chain round a rounded corner, at two of three scales), and of the engine block's 15 chained builds 2 (one ulp of volume; one area by three ulp): an expectation change logged in the dev log.
- **Where an edge ends** is the chamfer's rule.
- **Corners.** Two fillets that meet with the other edges sharp run out into each other in the curve their surfaces share (two cylinders at a square corner: an arc of an ellipse; OpenCascade's volume to 1e-11 and counts). Three straight fillets of one kind and one radius that meet in a vertex with nothing else: the piece of the ball that touches the three faces between the three planes through its centre across the edges (`ball_corners`, `ball_tool`: the cell those planes cut out, less the ball; why that piece is argued at `ball_corners`). Any other arrangement of three or more filleted edges at a vertex is refused (`CornerKind`, `CornerRadii`).
- **Never a wrong solid.** The chamfer's checks, and the tangency of every edge a fillet face shares with a neighbour or a ball corner at five points (`smooth_seams`, to the angular tolerance and never closer than 1e-9); anything else is `KernelError::Fillet` and no body.

### Blends next to a cylinder (M9, 2026-10-14)

A straight edge on a cylinder is one of its rulings, and a plane through it holds the axis' direction: the two faces look the same in every plane across the edge, and so does the blend (`blend::section`). In that plane each face is a line or a circle (`Trace`). A fillet is the circle of the radius tangent to both: its centre where the two curves moved by the radius cross (line and line, line and circle, circle and circle, in closed form; of two crossings the one nearest the ball between the tangent planes). A chamfer is the chord between the points the distances away along each curve (along the arc on a cylinder); with an angle the chord leaves the first point at the angle to the first curve. The tool is the extrusion of the section; the fillet face is a cylinder, the chamfer face a plane. Since step 3 (*Borders carried across*) an isolated such edge goes in place, with the routed chamfer's distances the section's chords. Corners of three such blends are not made. Tests: `crates/kernel/tests/blend_cylinders.rs` (a halved rod with one and both cut edges rounded, a lens's tip, the foot of a half-round boss, a halved rod chamfered along the diameter and the arc; closed forms by Green's theorem, three scales, a tilted frame).

**Two tip fillets of a lens (F1).** Both rims of the end of an extruded lens make two tori that run out into each other at the sharp tips. Two changes to the boolean (`kernel/src/boolean/section.rs`) make it: (1) a stretch of an intersection curve that is out of either face at its middle is dropped before it is held against the other face's boundary; (2) an approximated (spline) edge that leaves a surface tangentially at one of its ends meets the surface at its end and not where it crosses it again within the tolerance (an exact edge that lies on the surface up to a point is not touched). Test: `both_tip_edges_of_a_cambered_blade` (radii 0.05 to 0.3, both orders, three scales, tilted).

### Blends along freeform edges as built (M9, 2026-10-14)

`kernel::blend::rolling`: **a local operation**, built in place, the borders are edges of both faces from the start. (It was made local because the fillet face is tangent to both neighbours along curves with no closed form, a contact the boolean refused; since 2026-10-18 the boolean takes it, and the body less the tool a rolling fillet takes away is that fillet: [`booleans.md`](booleans.md) › *Tangent contact along an edge*.)

- **The face.** Each ball is found in a plane across the edge (past the ends on the edge's curve continued, a spline's end spans as the polynomials they are): its centre moves to where the two tangent planes at the nearest points, moved by the radius, cross the plane (`Solver::ball`: Newton steps on the two distances, never more than half the radius at once) until it settles; then it has to be the radius from both surfaces along their normals, on the right side. The face meets the ball in the short arc between its feet, a rational quadratic (`Section::net`). Which planes pick the balls does not change the surface. A chamfer is, in each plane, the line between the points the distances away from the edge's point (`Solver::cut`).
- **The approximation.** The arcs of a row of planes are joined by interpolating their homogeneous control points with a cubic (`surface_through`, `homogeneous`), so the surface passes through every sample; between two samples it is held against the exact section to a quarter of the linear tolerance, and a fillet's normal along its borders and across it to `bend`; samples are added until both hold (`refine`, at most 4096, laid through by a banded solve). The borders are the surface's iso-curves.
- **Where it ends.** Each end has to be a vertex of exactly three faces (`end_at`): the blend is continued past it until a whole section lies beyond the third face (`march_out`) and cut where it crosses that face's surface, along a cubic fitted to points found exactly (`cut_curve`, `sign_change`), proven within a quarter of the tolerance of both surfaces. The two other edges are shortened to the corners (`cut_back`), the third face gets the cut in place of its corner (`insert_cut`), the edge becomes the first border; every other entity keeps its number.
- **A closed edge** (one vertex that no other edge passes through: the rim of a slanted cut of a rod, the mouth of a slanted hole) has no ends: the sections go round it, a fillet's in the planes square to the closed path of the centres (`closed_path`), joined by the closed cubic with continuous curvature (`ring_surface`), refined the same way; the edge becomes the first border, the second border is new (`splice_ring`).
- **Never a wrong solid.** A ball that does not settle or does not fit (a face curves more tightly than the radius under it, as the fan blade's suction side at its leading edge for a radius of 0.049 mm: `FreeSurface`), an arc of half a turn, a cut not found, or an approximation that does not converge is refused with the edge and the reason; the body passes `topo::check` and the tangency is measured again along the borders at seven points (`tangent_along`, ten times `bend`). A large blend running into a face across the body is found by regeneration on a mesh (`tess::body_self_intersections`).
- **Tests** (`crates/kernel/tests/blend_freeform.rs`): a straight edge between a plane and a cone held point by point against the definition of a rolling-ball fillet (a point is kept exactly when a ball of the radius behind both faces holds it; Dykstra's projections) at three scales; the rim of a slanted cut of a rod; the same edge chamfered; a straight edge between two spline walls against the 2D fillet of the two Béziers times the height. Engine (`crates/engine/tests/blend_freeform.rs`, `chamfer.rs`): the turbofan's lofted fan blade's leading and trailing edges, a conical casing's cut edge, the rings of a slanted cut and a slanted hole, a spline wall chamfered against an independent integral.

### Chains and meets of freeform blends (M9, 2026-10-15)

`kernel::blend::rolling::chain` and `chain::meet`. **Grouping** (`chain::group`): where two requested edges end in one vertex and go on into each other (`continues`) they are a *joint* of a chain (walked from either end, or round to a closed chain); where they meet at an angle a *meet* (`chain::meet`); three or more at a vertex are refused. A closed-form edge that touches a freeform one joins it and is built in place too (`mixed`). A single open edge with no joint is a chain of one piece (*Method*).

- **One family of balls, a face per edge** (`along_spine`). A fillet chain is followed along the path of the balls' centres (`walk`: from a ball, a step ahead along n₁ × n₂, the next ball in the plane square to it; the step halves where the ball cannot be found or the path turns by more than a quarter radian, grows to a 24th of the edge where it runs straight). Each edge's face uses the planes square to a clamped cubic with continuous curvature through its balls (`path_through`, `clamped_tangents`), turned at the joints into the joint ball's plane by a correction that fades out over half the edge, flat to the third derivative at both ends (`Spine::at`; a family only C1 stalls the refinement), so the faces on both sides of a joint end in the same arc, their shared edge. A chamfer chain keeps the planes across the edges, which its lines depend on (`compute`). Both start from the same prelude and joint planes (`lay`, `joint_planes`).
- **Joints.** Both faces go on, or one goes on and the other changes into a face tangent to it across a smooth edge that leaves the vertex (cone into torus into plane along the casing's cut edge), or a seam of one face passes through the vertex (crossed as a smooth edge between the face and itself). Where a face changes, the shared ball is the one whose foot on the changing side lies on the smooth edge (`switch_ball`: bisection along that edge); the smooth edge is shortened to it, the vertex moves to the other foot, a new vertex and the arc are added (`splice_chain`). Tangency across every joint is measured (`smooth_joints`, ten times `bend`).
- **Meets** (two fillets of one radius ending in one vertex at an angle; three edges and three faces there, the third edge sharp, all convex or all concave). What is kept is what both fillets keep: each fillet's face where it lies inside the other's rounded corner and inside its far face. Both faces run on past the vertex and are trimmed per point across them by bisection on the larger of the two distances (`work_out`, `trim`); the two run out into each other in the curve they share, from where their borders on the shared face cross (`cross_borders`); the one whose border on its own face reaches the far face first is also cut by the far face; the third edge is shortened. Both reaching it in one point gives a vertex of four edges. Where the two run into each other there is a crease, as between two closed-form fillets that meet.
- **Tests** (`crates/kernel/tests/blend_chains.rs`): the rims of the cap of an extruded smooth outline against the swept volume (Pappus-Guldinus along a plane curve), three scales, tilted; a casing's cut edge through its root fillet against the rolling-ball definition; a chain with a closed-form edge apart; E6's boss foot; a half cone cut by a tilted plane, both top edges meeting at the cut edges, convex and concave, every point of a grid against the definition (Dykstra's projections onto the eroded convex sets); a lofted blade's tip rounded all round. Engine (`crates/engine/tests/blend_chains.rs`): the turbofan's core casing cut by its block, refilleted after an edit; the lofted blade's tip; E6 refused at 3 mm and built at 1 mm.
- **Not built** (refused with the reason): a border that runs over a smooth edge in the middle of an edge of a chain (E6 in the full engine block); concave fillets that meet round a sharp convex edge (a blade's root loop: the roll-over patch); a chain whose two ends meet at one vertex; chamfers whose face changes at a joint, or that meet.

### Blend findings of the impeller and the lamp (M9, 2026-10-15)

A compressor wheel's lofted blades and a lamp shade's cells; each change is a method for any geometry.

- **Meets where the two fillets touch the face they share** (F3, F4). The crossing of the two borders, two curves on one face that cross at the angle θ the edges meet at, is found directly (`cross_borders`: Gauss-Newton on the two curves; refused where tol / sin θ is not less than their length), and the trim curve is held to lying on both surfaces where its positions along them cannot be told closer (`trim`'s `on_both`). ### What fits, decided by building (M9, 2026-10-15)

`blend::fit::explain`, when a build fails in a way a smaller size can mend (`sized`): the sizes of every edge of the feature's size change together (`group`). First a quarter of the size at a time until one builds; then the sizes at which a border can first reach something of its face (`events`: a vertex, the nearest point of an edge, what another blend takes of the face, the axis of a circular edge, where a fillet's torus reaches its own axis), searched with builds just past (64 tolerances) and just before (4, 64, 1024) each; the event found is said as the largest size that fits when the seven sizes evenly between it and the size asked fail too (`proven`). Where none explains the failure the size is bisected on the builds, to a few tolerances, and said only where the result shows no room (`no_room`: the blend reaches a face, takes a neighbour away, comes out in pieces, runs past an edge or off its face, folds, passes through a face) at the size asked, at the size the bisection ends on (or 4 or 64 tolerances past it, the build at 4 failing too) and at the seven sizes between. Otherwise, and where nothing tried builds, the refusal at the size asked is reported as it is (the boolean's saying that smaller sizes fail the same way where they do, `smaller_too`). Until 2026-11-25 the bisection's size was said whatever the failures, too large with nothing that fits where every failure showed no room, and an event without the sizes between but from an invalid body; the boolean's faults at sizes that fit put such answers below radii that build (*Too large only where it is shown*). 48 builds at most; a spent budget ends the search with the original refusal. `fit` builds through the same router as the feature (`construct`), so the search builds whichever construction the edges take.

The rules of the estimate it replaced, and how building covers them: a boundary that runs on smoothly past its first piece (E2, E5) and an edge beyond an arc's sector (E3): the build sees the face as it is; a cut line exactly into a corner (E7): the boolean builds it, as three chamfers in one feature; a face that pinches to nothing beside the edge (E8): no size builds, refused with the boolean's own reason; the axis: one more event. *Cost of a refusal:* a few builds (in the kernel tests about 2 ms for a simple one; E8's chain, where nothing builds down to the smallest size, about 230 ms); a refused `feature.edit` on a model costs about what its regeneration does. *Still open:* events along a curved edge are found among 64 samples; a size between events is reported to a few tolerances; a limit that is no event, where the builds past it fail at the boolean (a fillet that would take a lens-shaped face whole), is not said since 2026-11-25 (*Too large only where it is shown*).

### Fitted curves proven on their surfaces (consolidation, 2026-10-16)

Each fitted curve of a rolling blend is accepted by a proof (`geom::certify`, method in [`geometry.md`](geometry.md)), not by samples: the cut at an end on the blend face and the third face (`cut_curve`); a meet's trim on the fillet face it trims (`meet::trim`; its other side, a maximum of two signed distances, is held at the middles); the edge going on to a corner on both faces (`going_on`); each border on its face between every two samples, at most 1024 pieces a step (`refine`); a border staying on its face (`stays_on`: `curves_apart` to ten tolerances, a pair not told apart refused; its test, a border bowing towards another edge, pins the behaviour but does not tell this from the old chords and Gauss-Newton, which caught it too: what changed is that nothing rests on Gauss-Newton converging). A proof says a curve is within the tolerance of its face's *surface*, continued past its domain; staying within the face is `stays_on`'s. A spline face that cannot be split into Bézier patches refuses the blend as not approximated. Tests in `blend/rolling.rs` and `rolling/chain/meet.rs`: one per site, a face that waves between the points the old checks looked at; the cut, the trim, the edge to a corner and the borders fail with the proof switched off, the `stays_on` test does not (above). **Left sampled:** the blend surface's interior against the exact sections (no derivative bounds to prove against; 9 points across, at the middles); a fillet's tangency along its borders (`tangent_along`, seven points); the events of `fit` (64 samples); the sweep along a helix.

### One blend construction: design (2026-10-16)

The map's debt row "two blend systems": the target is **one construction, the in-place one, with closed forms as exact surfaces, not a separate pipeline.** A tool needs the boolean to take the tangent contact between the blend face and its neighbours, which it can only where that contact has a closed form; the in-place construction makes the borders edges of both faces from the start, and its cost does not grow with the body.

- **The section is the same for every edge** (a ball's arc, a chamfer's chord); ends, chains, joints, meets and corners are the same steps for every edge.
- **The surface is chosen by a symmetry**: where one motion carries the edge and both faces into themselves, the blend face is the first section swept by it, in closed form (a translation: cylinder, plane; a rotation: torus, cone). Otherwise the fitted B-spline.
- **Edges in closed form where their two surfaces meet in closed form** (`exact::make_exact`): a line, a circle or an ellipse through the edge's vertices and its fitted curve's middle; otherwise the fitted curve, proven on both.
- **Corners are vertex patches of the same construction**: a sphere where three fillets' balls come to rest in one, a plane for chamfers; the general patch where not.

| The tool path does | The in-place path | Covered by |
|---|---|---|
| Exact cylinders, tori, cones, planes, spheres; exact edges | exact by translation (`exact`), fitted otherwise | the rotation (step 4) |
| Chamfers by a distance and an angle | the routed class by the plan's distances; refused elsewhere (`FreeAngle`) | the chord at the angle in every plane |
| Ends: past the vertex, square at it, cut in a slanted plane | the end cut against the third face of a vertex of three faces | the end cut carried round the faces in turn (F9, step 7) |
| Two blends that meet with the third edge sharp | fillets: `chain::meet`; chamfers refused | the meet's trim in closed form, chamfer meets (step 5) |
| Three blends in a vertex: ball corner, flat chamfer corner | refused | the corner patch (step 7) |
| Chains of lines and arcs: a tool per edge and the boolean | joints ending in a shared section | exact surfaces per piece (step 6) |
| Blend faces in one surface merged into one face | never made | faces of one feature on one exact surface merged (step 8) |
| The whole body seen: a blend that reaches a face elsewhere is found (`Reaches`) | the faces at the edge (`stays_on`); regeneration on a mesh | a loop wholly in the strip refused (done: *Holes*); the blend face held against the other faces (step 8) |
| A border across a smooth edge or a seam of its face | done for single edges (2026-10-16, *Borders carried across*) | chains, rings, meets |
| What fits by building | the same router (`fit` through `construct`); `FreeTooLarge`, `FreeRunsOver` count as no room | done (step 1) |

**What changes for users.** The same solids within the tolerance; a blend face is a cylinder, torus, cone, plane or sphere wherever it is one now (a datum axis on a fillet face, a chamfer face's frame and the STEP export keep what they have); names from the sources, which both constructions report alike, so **persistent names stay as they are**; an edge the blend only shortens keeps its number. A step that would change a name (a merged face, a corner) is a format or expectation matter, logged before it lands.

### Borders carried across a smooth edge, and step 3 (2026-10-16)

- **Ends walked along the boundary** (`walk_on`, used by `cut_back` for single edges, chains and meets): where a border's corner is not on the edge that leaves the vertex along the boundary with the third face, that boundary is followed across every vertex at which it goes on between the same two faces and nothing else meets (a split of one curve of contact, such as a cylinder's seam, which bounds nothing), and across the vertex where a crossed smooth edge leaves it; the edges passed are taken whole.
- **A border carried across a smooth edge** (`rolling::cross`, single open edges). A smooth edge of a face beside the edge that enters what the blend takes does so at a vertex on the boundary with a third face (`branch`; that boundary inside what the blend takes all along, `inside_along`, 17 points per edge, and ahead of the edge, `Blend::ahead`), or through the border in the middle (refused as running over). Along a candidate a point is inside when the ball touching the face there is nearer the other face than the radius (a chamfer: nearer the edge than the distance); one change from inside to outside, found by bisection (64 samples, 80 halvings), is the *switch*, and the plane across the edge through the ball's centre gives its place along the blend; inside all along, the face before it goes. The edge has to lead to another face tangent at the vertex and at the crossing (ten times `bend`), else no crossing. Anything else is refused as running over (`FreeRunsOver`).
- **The surface across a switch** (`blend_open`): on each side the sections are found against the faces there; the section at the switch, found against the faces before it, is moved onto the faces after it (`moved_onto`, a quarter of the tolerance) and has to be the ball those faces give there (else `NotTangent`); the pieces are refined each on their own faces (at least four samples each) and joined (`cross::join`). A border that cannot be carried is refused as running off its face (which `fit` reads as no room).
- **The faces after** (`cross::carry`): the border is cut at each switch into pieces, each an edge of the face it lies on; the crossed edge is shortened to the switch, or taken whole when it lay inside all along; the loops of the faces touched are linked again from their coedges (`relink`: refused, as running over, where a vertex has two ways on, a loop does not close, or the loops come out different in number); a face left with nothing goes (`compact_faces`). Each piece and the crossed edge are proven ten tolerances apart beyond the stretch near the switch where crossing alone brings them that close (`apart_beyond`, rho = 20 tol / sin θ); a tangent crossing, or that stretch over half an edge, is not shown apart and refused.
- **Step 3**: a straight edge between two planes or next to a cylinder along it goes in place when each end is a vertex of three edges where no other requested edge ends, with one loop on every face there; a chamfer next to a cylinder by the straight distances in the plane across the edge (the section's chords).
- **Tests**: `crates/kernel/tests/blend_across.rs` (a prism's front top edge rounded across an earlier round's tangent line, every point of a grid against the definition; chamfered; the top's strip narrower than the radius; a concave corner whose floor goes; a hole past the switch refused; three scales, tilted), `crates/kernel/src/blend/tests.rs` (the boss root at five seam angles, filleted and chamfered, in place against the tools), `blend_cylinders.rs` › `a_boss_root_fillet_does_not_depend_on_the_seam`, `crates/engine/tests/fillet.rs` › `a_fillet_whose_border_crosses_onto_an_earlier_round`. The turbofan's stator blade edge rounds and disc blade-root fillets go in place: volumes and areas within 9e-10 relative of the tools, every face, edge and vertex name the same, nine blade bodies listing their edges and vertices in another order (in place keeps the numbers of what it does not replace, the boolean renumbers).
- **Still refused:** a border crossing a smooth edge in a chain, a ring or a meet (E6, B2); more than one smooth edge crossed from one end; an edge that enters through the border and leaves through it again, or ends inside; an edge between a face and itself.

### Requests after a blend in place, renumbered (review, 2026-10-17)

**Found** (review; there since in-place blends could take an edge or a face whole): `blend::construct` planned the edges left for the tools on the body the in-place blends left, and `blend::mixed` blended the closed-form edges on the body the freeform ones left, both with the edge and face numbers of the body the feature started from. ### A blend face that folds through itself (fuzzer seed 14211, 2026-10-17)

**Found** (the random-design fuzzer, `wrong-unmeasurable/14211`): a ball of radius 7.5 united with a block it pokes out of by 0.016, then a chamfer of 0.625 on the rim of that cap (a concave circle between the sphere and the block's underside, built by `rolling`): accepted, valid by `check`, but `mass_properties` could not measure the chamfer face ("a boundary integral that does not settle"). **Which side was wrong: the chamfer.** The cap is sqrt(rim² + h²) ≈ 0.49 from rim to top along each section; a cut line 0.625 from the rim lies past the top on the far side, every chamfer line goes through one point of the cap's axis, and the face is a double cone through itself, its normal zero at the apex. The integrator was right to give up.

**Fixed** (`folds` in `blend/rolling.rs`, a new refusal `Reason::FreeFolds`, which `fit` counts as no room). The rule: *along every sample section of a blend face, and halfway between two, the surface normal keeps the side it has in the middle of that section* (a chamfer's against its normal there, a fillet's against the radius from the ball's centre); *a normal that vanishes is a fold.* General because the sections are a family of curves across the edge, one per plane across it; where those planes are not parallel they meet in a line (the axis of a circular edge), and a section that reaches past that line crosses its neighbours, so the surface folds with its normal passing through zero. Only the sections over the edge itself are held (those past the ends are cut away). Both rolling paths, the closed ring and the open edge, and fillets as well as chamfers.

**Tests:** `crates/kernel/tests/chamfer.rs` › `a_chamfer_wider_than_the_cap_it_cuts_into_is_refused` (the finding's ball and block at three scales: 0.5 and 0.625 refused naming the edge and the sphere; since 2026-11-13 the sizes from 0.326, where the line from the plate to the cap starts to run into the cap, refused as passing through it, and 0.2 and 0.306 built and the ring Pappus gives; until then 0.45 was built and held only to lie within the groove, *A chamfer through a face beside it*); `crates/engine/tests/fuzz_findings.rs` replays the reproducer.

### A fillet pinched inside its edge (2026-11-14, a face with no width in 0.1.2)

**Finding.** A loft through four "house" sections 15 apart (a rectangle 20 × 8 with a ridge 6, 2, 0 and 2 above the middle of its top): the two roof faces meet in the ridge edge, 45.7 long. The smooth interpolant of the ridge comes down to the eaves at the third section (x = 30), dips 0.014 below them (at x = 31.24) and is back at x = 32.46: the roof faces are tangent at those two places, the ridge a convex edge outside them and a valley between. `feature.fillet` of the ridge with a radius of 0.3 and of 3 answered `ok` (release/0.1 at 2134872, the code of 0.1.2, and the workshop's `main`): one four-sided fillet face whose two borders pass through both places, with no width there and crossed between them (what was built between is the ball in the wedge under the two faces' continuations, which lies in the material). `document.inspect` called the body valid, `document.check` could not measure it ("a boundary integral that does not settle"), and at 3 the STL export failed ("cannot be tessellated … jumps across the surface"); a radius of 1 was refused as not approximable. Kin of that ridge were refused with reasons that were not true: 6, 0, 6 (tangent at its middle, convex on both sides) and 6, 0, −6 (from convex to concave at its middle) as "the two faces are tangent along this edge", fillet and chamfer alike; 6, 3, −3 (convex at its middle, concave from x = 39.05) as an internal error ("came out not tangent to a face it should run into smoothly").

**Cause.** Whether an edge is convex, concave or smooth was read at its middle only (`across`), and the construction in place takes its sections at samples along the edge. Where the turn across the edge passes through zero, the stretch in which no ball is found is short and the samples step over it; past it the ball that touches both faces from the material's side is the one under their continuations, and its feet have changed sides. (Where the faces only touch and part again, without the edge changing side, that stretch is long, a sample lands in it, and the construction refused by itself, with its general reason: "the rolling ball could not be followed".)

**Method.** *A fillet in place is refused, before anything is built, where its section runs to nothing at a place strictly inside its edge* (`blend::along`, called from `rolling::blend`; the refusal is `KernelError::FilletPinched`: the edge, the place, what the faces do there and how many such places the edge has). Between the tangent planes of the two faces at a point of the edge, which turn against each other by φ there, the ball's feet lie `r·tan(φ/2)` from the edge: the section is `2r·tan(φ/2)` wide, and nothing where φ is nothing. The sine of the turn (`across_at`, negative where the edge is convex) is read at stations evenly apart in the edge's parameter: 8 times the spans of its curve, 64 at least and 2048 at most. A station is *tangent* where the sine is no more than 1e-6 (`SMOOTH_SINE`, below which two faces are tangent across an edge wherever the kernel asks, as it was asked at the middle); it is *opposite* where the edge turns the other way than at its middle by more than that. The radius has no part in it (since 2026-11-15: a test on the width refused fillets that were right, *The width test taken out*). Then:

- a stretch of stations not turned like the middle, one of them opposite: the place is where the turn passes through zero between the stretch and the station turned like the middle beside it (by bisection; on each side of the stretch that has such a station; said as "the edge is convex on one side of (x, y, z) and concave on the other");
- a stretch of tangent stations with stations turned like the middle on both sides: the place is where the turn is least ("the two faces become tangent at (x, y, z)");
- a station turned like the middle that is lower than both its neighbours, and near enough to the bound for the turn between the stations to reach it (eight times what a parabola through the three allows), is searched between them (golden section): a dip the stations step over is found;
- a stretch of tangent stations that reaches an end of the edge is the edge's end, not a place inside it, and so is a place within the linear tolerance of an end vertex: a fillet that narrows towards the end of its edge is cut off there by the face it runs into, and is built as before.

An edge tangent at its middle is read along itself in the same way (`along::tangent_at_middle`, from `oriented`, for both kinds of blend and both constructions): tangent at every station it has no corner, as before; otherwise it is refused with its middle as the place where the faces are tangent, a fillet as above and a chamfer as a `KernelError::Chamfer` that says so ("tangent at the middle of this edge, though not all along it": which side a chamfer is on is read at the middle). The refusal says that a fillet that ends before such a place and goes on after it is not built, so the edge cannot be rounded as it is, and, where the edge changes side, that no radius helps: nothing rounds such an edge today.

**Why it holds for every such edge.** The fillet face's two borders are where the ball's feet go. Where the turn changes sign it is zero between, at every radius: the feet meet in a point that is not a vertex of the face, and past it the ball lies on the other side of the body's surface; that is not a face. Where the faces are tangent by the kernel's own measure (the one by which an edge tangent at its middle has no corner to blend), with an angle on both sides, the ball has no corner to round there and the borders come together. The rule reads the faces' normals along the edge, nothing else, so it does not know the ridges above. What it does not see is left to the construction, as before (where the review reached such a place, the construction refused it with its own reason): a dip between two stations that leaves no station lower than both its neighbours (shorter than an eighth of a span of the edge's curve, and on a slope); a tangent place within a station's step of an end of the edge (the lowest station is then the end station, which is the edge's end: the ridge 6, 0, 6 cut off 1e-6 to 0.1 past the place, "could not be followed"); a change of side so near an end that the sine at the end station is still within 1e-6 (one within 10 tolerances of the end, by the review), or within the tolerance of the end vertex; the joint of a chain where the faces are tangent (it is each edge's end). A fillet that is narrower than the tolerance where the faces still make an angle is not refused here: the construction builds it as before, or refuses it as before.

### A fillet's radius as a law along its edge (2026-11-17, the first slice of the fade)

**Why.** A fillet that ends by fading where its edge runs on into a next edge between the same two faces (a hull's stem into its keel) is the rolling ball with a radius that falls to nothing over a length, so that the face closes in a point on the edge. Its first slice is the radius as a law, without the point: between two radii.

**The law** (`blend/law.rs`). `RadiusLaw` is a radius and, per end of the edge, an optional `Fade { to, over }`: within `over` of that end, measured along the edge, the radius is `to + (radius − to) f(τ)` with `f(τ) = 1 − (1 − τ)³` and `τ` the distance from the end over `over`. Where the fade begins `f = 1`, `f′ = f″ = 0`: the radius has a continuous second derivative along the edge. At the end `f ≈ 3τ`: linear, which is what lets a fade to nothing end with its borders at an angle. Two fades may meet in the middle of the edge and may not overlap. *Along the edge* is by arc length, so the law says the same whatever the curve's parametrisation: Gauss-Legendre quadrature of eight points on a quarter of each span between knots (a sixteenth of the range of a circle or an ellipse; a line's speed times the step), summed once to the pieces' ends. *Past an end* (`τ < 0`), where a blend is followed until it has left the face it ends against, the law goes on as `to + A (1 − exp(aτ + bτ²))` in place of the cubic, with `a = −3 (radius − to) / A` and `b = (6 (radius − to) / A − a²) / 2`: the same value, first and second derivative at the end, and one way only from there (`b` is below zero), towards `to + A` and never past it; `A = −to` where the radius falls towards the end (past it the radius goes on falling towards nothing and never reaches it), `A = to − radius` where it grows (it goes on growing by no more than it grew over the fade). The cubic itself, continued, runs to nothing or grows without bound within a few of its lengths: along a cone's cut edge a radius growing from 0.15 to 0.2 over 0.3 was 0.38 two steps past the end, and no ball was found. A first continuation, `3τ / (1 + τ + dτ²)`, turned back to `to`: it left a notch in the surface just past the end (the radius down to half of `to` and back within a fraction of the fade), which the validity check's nearest points fell into, and a fade from 3 to 1 over 1, 0.9, 0.8 and 0.7 came back as an invalid body ("edge 9 is 0.499 away from the surface of face 6", the border from its own surface); with the exponential every fade from 3 to 1, to 0.3 and to 4 over 6 down to 0.1 is built valid and clear in the deep check (27 of 27; `plan/fillet-fade/s2/probe-steep.txt`, before the bound below refused the steep ones). The continuation is a choice of this slice; the fade's design gives the law up to the end only.

**How fast the radius may change** (2026-11-17, the review's blocker). A law was returned `Ok` whose body the measure could not take: on a block's edge, 3 to 1 over 0.04, `mass_properties` says "a boundary integral that does not settle"; from over 0.03 the deep check's search cannot mesh it; the mesh grows long before (normal quality, 0.077 mm: 50 triangles at one radius, 1,898 for 3 to 1 over 10, 14,440 over 1, 72,306 over 0.3, 368,922 over 0.1, 1,043,636 over 0.05; the review's numbers). *The bound:* a fade is at least as long as the radius changes over it, `over ≥ |radius − to|`, so `|dr/da| ≤ 3` (the cubic is steepest at the end, three times its average); and an end radius and a fade's length are more than 16 tolerances (the least radius of a fillet in closed form). Refused with what is wrong and the least `over` that is built (`KernelError::FilletFade`, `FadeFault::TooShort { change, over, least }` and `EndTooSmall { to, least }`), before anything is built. *Why a bound on the rate:* a border of the fillet leaves each section at the angle whose tangent is `1 / (|dr/da| tan(φ/2))` (a foot lies `r tan(φ/2)` from the edge between faces whose normals turn by `φ`; the arc leaves its foot square to the edge), so as the radius changes faster the face's two parameter directions close up along its borders: 18° between planes at a right angle at the bound, 2° at ten times it, 0.4° where the measure gives up. Everything that reads a face through its parameters degrades by that angle; the bound keeps a factor of fifty from the first failure of the measure, and a factor of four and more from the tessellator's cap of 1024 segments (on the block, 40 long: met at a chord of 0.001 from 12 times as fast as the length, at 0.005 from 20, at 0.01 from 60). *The bound's price* (the review's count): of 264 laws past the bound that 3ceed7d2 built, 174 were sound (all of those on blunt corners, a spline foot and inside edges; 6 of 24 on sharp corners). A bound on the border's angle itself, `|dr/da| tan(φ/2) ≤ tan ψ`, would take those back where the faces meet bluntly (at 150° the same border angle allows a rate of 11, at 120° of 5.2) and would be tighter than today's at sharp ones (1.7 at 60°, 0.8 at 30°), where today's bound still lets the border lie flatter; it needs the faces' angle at every section (the construction has it: `Slope`), a least `over` found by a search along the edge and not by one subtraction, and a refusal that can no longer be said in one sentence. Not built; a decision. It is the law's rate alone, not the angle between the faces: at a sharper edge the border lies flatter at the same rate (11° at 60°), which the sweep below includes; a bound on the angle itself is owed. *What the mesh's growth is, below the bound:* the tessellator gives each Bézier patch of a face (one per span between two samples) a grid of one count in both directions, from the sum of its second-derivative bounds (`tess::segments_for`: `n = √((Muu + 2Muv + Mvv) / 8c)`). A law's face has more spans than one radius's (about 100 rows against 30), and in each the mixed derivative (the arc changing size along the edge) and the arc's own curvature set a count that is then used along the edge too, where one or two segments would do. A grid sized per direction (the same bound holds with `hu` and `hv` apart) would be a true mesh with far fewer triangles (by the counts each direction needs, about a tenth: an estimate, nothing built); that is owed in the tessellator, not here. At the bound a block with one fillet from 3 to 1 over 2 meshes to 9,258 triangles at a chord of 0.077 and 67,588 at 0.01, and takes 0.3 s to measure (2 ms with one radius); from 3 to 4 over 1, 12,684 and 93,960, 0.4 s. *The sweep* (`fillet_law.rs` › `a_law_at_the_bound_of_how_fast_it_may_change_is_built_sound_and_past_it_refused`): the block's edge, a prism's at 60° and at 120°, the spline rim and the cone's cut edge; the radius falling to a third, a tenth and a hundredth, growing to twice and three times, changing by a tenth; fades of 0.5, 0.99, 1, 1.01 and 3 times the change; at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6 on two frames: 441 built, every one valid, measured, meshed watertight at 0.05 and 0.01 with its volume and clear in the deep check; 294 refused as too short with the least length; none refused otherwise. The review's reproducer is `a_steep_law_is_refused_or_measured_and_searched` there; the reasons and their numbers, `what_is_wrong_with_a_fade_is_said_with_what_would_be_built` (end radii down to 5e-324, which were refused as an end that does not end cleanly; a fade of 0.001, which came back as an invalid body; a fade that changes nothing but is longer than the edge).

**Which ball, and which surface.** The ball in the plane across the edge at the length `a` has the radius `r(a)` and touches both faces; the face is the arcs between the feet of those balls. That is not the envelope of the balls: with one radius the face's normal at a point of a section is the ball's radius there, and where the radius changes it is that only along the borders. *Tangent to both faces for any law:* at a border the surface has the arc's tangent, square to the ball's radius, which is the face's normal, and the border's own tangent, which lies in the face; the two span the face's tangent plane. Between the borders the normal leans from the ball's radius by the rate of the radius: in the middle of the arc between planes at a right angle, by the angle whose tangent is 0.41 times `dr/da`.

**What the construction does for a law** (`rolling.rs`, `rolling/slope.rs`). (1) *The section*: `Solver::section` takes the radius from the law at its plane (`Solver::law`); with no law it is the code it was. (2) *The start of each ball* (`resized`): the neighbour's centre moved into the plane and then towards the edge's point in the ratio of the radii. The search moves a centre by half a radius a step at most, 100 steps: from a neighbour of radius 0.9 to a ball of 0.003 it would need 860, and the fade from 3 to 0.003 was refused as not followed until this. (3) *The normal the fitted face is held to* (`Solver::kink_across`, `Slope`): the derivative of the section along the edge, exactly. The construction's equations `(c − f_k) · n_k = σ r` and `N · (c − P) = 0` (centre `c`, feet `f_k`, the faces' normals `n_k`, the plane's point `P` and normal `N`, `σ` the side) differentiate to three linear equations for `c′`: `n_k · c′ = σ r′` and `N · c′ = |P′| − N′ · (c − P)`; each foot's rate then solves `(S_u + σ r n_u) u′ + (S_v + σ r n_v) v′ = c′ − σ r′ n_k` in its face's tangent plane, with the normal's derivatives from the surface's second derivatives; the arc's control points and weight follow, and the surface's derivative by the quotient rule. Held to the ball's radius instead, a law fillet between two planes is refused as not approximated (tried). (4) *To an end and past it*: in even steps to the end itself, then in steps that start at a twentieth of the end's radius and double, none longer than half that radius (`Steps::past`, `march_out`; doubling without that cap, a radius growing 1.2 to 2.4 over 1.2 towards the slanted end of a cone's curved edge was followed to where no ball was found, the review's second finding), so that the face goes past an end only as far as a whole section needs to leave the third face: a little where that face is square to the edge, as far as the slant asks where it is not. (5) *The passes*: 48 for a law (`LAW_PASSES`); with the first continuation a fade from 3 to 1 over 3 was 1.04 times over its bound after 16, with 192 samples, creeping one step a pass where the law joins its continuation. (6) `shown::ball` and the border's test of what lies inside the blend (`cross::Blend::inside`) take the radius of their own plane (`law::Along::radius_on`: the law read where the ball resting on a point has its centre along the edge). (7) No motion carries a law fillet into itself: never exact, always fitted.

**One radius is what it was.** `fillet_by_law` gives a law that does not change the radius to the blend as `Size::Radius`; `fillet` is not touched. Everything a law changes in the construction is behind `Solver::law`, which is `None` for every other blend. Measured: 366 bodies (fillets and chamfers of every edge of a block, a half frustum, a prism with a spline wall on two frames and a rod cut at a slant, pairs of edges that chain or meet, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6) built by `main` at 797ac38b and by this slice have the same faces, edges, vertices, curves and surfaces to the last bit (a hash of the body written out; the probe and both lists are with the dev log's entry).

**Tests** (`crates/kernel/tests/fillet_law.rs`, each oracle worked out in the test from the definition, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6, on the world frame and a tilted one away from the origin unless said): *between planes* at 60°, 90° and 120°, and an inside corner at 90°: the volume against `(cot(θ/2) − (π − θ)/2) ∫ r² da` with `∫ r²` in closed form over the fades (`L (to² + 3 to Δ/2 + 9Δ²/14)`), the area against the strips the faces lose and the fillet face integrated from its points, every point looked at of the face's own surface (a grid of 873, those along the edge, at least 500) on the arc of its own height within a quarter of the tolerance, the borders tangent; measured: volume within 0.013 of the face's area times the tolerance, area to 4e-11 of itself, points within 0.06 tolerances, the sine across the borders under 5e-13. A fade down to a hundredth of the radius and one three times as fast as the length are held to the same. *Between two spline walls* along a straight edge (a blade's section extruded): the volume against the 2D fillet of the two curves at each height, integrated (0.013 of the slack). *Along a spline rim* between a flat top and an upright spline wall, a curved edge: the section is a quarter circle in the plane across the rim, the volume `∫ ((1 − π/4) r² − κ (5/6 − π/4) r³) da` with `κ` the rim's curvature towards the material, the area and the points likewise (volume 0.013 of the slack, area 2.3e-10, points within 0.08 tolerances). *Between a plane and a cone*, slanted against both ends (world frame): every point of the face on the arc between the feet of the ball of some length along the edge, the ball found from its two equations and the length by the secant rule (within 0.07 tolerances; the foot on the cone lies off the plane across the edge, so this is the case that tells the arcs between the feet from the section in that plane). *One radius*: the same `FilletResult` as `fillet`, between planes and along the cone. *Refused*: numbers that are not sizes, fades longer than the edge or overlapping, a ring, two requested edges that meet; an edge elsewhere of one radius is built with it. *Held to the faces beside it by the radius of each plane* (`fillet_through.rs` › `a_radius_that_varies_is_held_to_the_wall_by_the_radius_of_each_plane`): beside the wall with a bead, a law of 1.03 times the radius at which the ball touches the bead and the wall at once, falling to 0.93 of it at one end, is refused naming the wall, and falling to 1.005 of it is built (held by the law's largest radius in every plane, the second is refused too: tried). Unit tests: the arc length against closed forms and against chords in the limit, the law's values, rates and continuation (`blend::law`); the section's derivative against central differences of the construction's own sections along a rim and between a sphere and a spline wall whose side is turned round, the borders' normals the faces' to 1e-9 for a law, the normal the ball's radius for one radius and not for a law (`rolling::slope`).

**What it does not do.** A radius that changes faster than three times the length, or an end radius of 16 tolerances or less, is refused (above). Across a place where the edge's curvature jumps (a spline's knot at which only its tangent is continuous) a law is refused with the place where the jump times the radius squared there is more than 2,000 tolerances (`FadeFault::CurvatureJump`, `law::JUMP`). *What a jump does, measured* (a spline rim with a double knot in its middle, the jump scaled; radius 1.5, tolerance 1e-6; triangles at a chord of 0.05, 1,382 without a jump): the centres' path changes speed there by the jump times the radius, the fitted face follows that only with samples bunched at the place, and the mesh grows with it: at 180 tolerances 1,382; 600, 1,614; 1,800, 2,762; 6,000, 8,114; 18,300, 26,354; 63,500, 95,318; 209,000, 239,366; 715,000, 1,043,840 and no mesh at 0.01. *What it is not:* the knot's multiplicity. A curve a boolean fits has every interior knot doubled, with jumps of the fit's own error (58 knots, 1.7e-5 at most, 70 tolerances where a plane meets one extruded cubic), and a law along it is built and sound (the review's finding on 3900d7ab, which refused it; `fillet_law.rs` › `a_law_along_a_boolean_fitted_edge_is_built`; `a_law_along_an_edge_whose_curvature_jumps_is_refused_with_the_place` holds the real jump refused and three thousandths of it built). Where the radius is changing at such a place the face also creases across it (about the radius's rate times the jump times the radius): a crease the refinement cannot hold is refused by it as not approximated, without the place (on the rim, at a rate of 0.005: built at a jump of 6,000 tolerances, not approximated at 18,300, both past the bound now); the crease's own refusal with its place is owed, since no case between the two was found to test it on. The fillet of one radius across a real jump is refused as before (not approximated, 16 passes). The arcs between the feet in planes across the edge are smooth only where those planes turn smoothly: where the edge's curvature jumps and the radius is changing, the curve of the centres has a corner (the plane's turning enters `N · c′` through `N′`), which no fitted face follows; a spine through the centres, as a chain has, is what would carry a law over such an edge. An offset of a law fillet's face is not rebuilt as a fillet (`rebuild` reads one radius off a face and holds 17 rows to it, so it takes the face for any freeform face): not tried.

**Behind it, tried once and not pinned.** A block 40 × 30 × 40 whose upright edge is rounded 3, falling to 1, 0.3, 0.03, 0.003 and 0.001 at the top over 15 (the face 3000 times narrower at one end than along the edge), cut by a box across the narrow end, across the fade, along the bisector of the corner (the fillet face split lengthwise) and through the face beside it: each subtraction and each intersection builds and is valid, and the two parts' volumes make the whole to 5e-6 mm³ of 47,900 at worst (the same at an end radius of 1). So a fitted face that narrows is taken by the booleans; a fillet of one radius whose face narrows because its two faces come tangent was refused by them (the fade's design, its 1.3), which is then a matter of those faces, not of the narrowing. A last row that is exactly a point is not shown by this.

**What the tip needs from here.** A section that is a point: `Section::net` and `resized` divide by the radius, `Solver::ball` settles and checks in units of it, and `bend` is the tolerance over the *largest* radius, so near a tip the tangency is asked far more closely than the tolerance over the local radius allows (the bound that must not blow up, the design's second risk); the end without a cut (`end_at`, `march_out` and the cut are skipped, the last row of the surface the vertex itself); the edge split at `back`. `Slope` gives the borders' directions at an end (`feet[k].1`), from which the angle between them at the tip follows.

### A fillet that ends in a point (2026-11-17, the second slice of the fade)

**What.** `Fade::tip(over, back)`: a fade whose `to` is nothing. The radius falls over `over` to nothing at the end of the edge, or `back` short of it, by the fade's cubic (`r = R (1 − (1 − τ)³)`, `τ` the distance from the point over `over`; at the point `r ≈ 3Rτ`). The fillet face closes in a vertex on the edge there. No command yet (`kernel::fillet::fillet_by_law`).

**The first risk of the design, settled by a run before anything else** (is a fitted rational face whose last row is exactly a point taken by what comes after a fillet?). Yes, by all six: on a block 40 × 30 × 40 whose upright edge is rounded 3 with a fade to nothing over 15, at the top vertex and 5 short of it, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6, on the world frame and a tilted one: the validity check; the measure (the closed form `(1 − π/4) R² (h − L + 9L/14)` within 0.010 of the face's area times the tolerance); the tessellator (watertight at chords 0.05 and 0.005, the mesh's volume the body's); the deep check's search on a mesh; six booleans, each as a subtraction and an intersection (a box through the tip, beside it, across the fade, along the face, one whose face lies at the tip's height, one with a corner in the face: all valid, the two parts making the whole within 4.4e-4 of the area times the tolerance); and STEP out and back (the same counts, the volume and area to 7e-16 of themselves, every edge proven). `crates/kernel/tests/fillet_tip.rs` › `a_face_that_closes_in_a_point_is_taken_by_what_comes_after_it`, `crates/io/tests/step_tip.rs`. The measure of a tipped body is slower: 70 to 190 ms for that block against 2 ms with one radius.

**Method** (`rolling.rs`, `law.rs`, `rolling/chain.rs`).
- *The law.* A tip lies at `s` 0 or 1, or where the edge's curve is `back` long from its end (`Along::tip`, by bisection on the length). At the point and beyond it the radius is nothing: no ball, no section. *What plays the part of the levelling-off past an end:* nothing. The blend is followed up to the point and not past it; the continuation is for a fade to a radius, which a third face cuts.
- *The sections.* From the middle of what the fillet runs along (`Along::span`: between the tips, where the middle of the edge may lie beyond one) in even steps short of the point (`march_short_of`), then the point itself: a section whose two feet and centre are the point (`Section::pole`), with the weight of the arc's middle control point in the limit, the cosine of half the angle between the faces' normals at the point (`Solver::pole`).
- *The surface.* The pole is the first or the last row; that row's control points are the point itself, to the last bit (`surface_through` sets them: the weighted point over its weight is not the point). Both borders, the surface's own iso-curves, end in it.
- *The refinement near the point* (the design's second risk). The fitted face's normals are asked as closely as the linear tolerance makes over the radius *of each place* (`refine`: `bend.max(4 · limit / r(s))`; for every law, also without a tip), not over the largest radius: near the point the arc is small against the tolerance, a face within the tolerance of it may lean by the tolerance over its radius, and no closer is asked. Where the radius is the tolerance itself the bound is 1: nothing is asked of the normal, and the face is still within a quarter of the tolerance of the arcs. No normal is taken at the pole (the first and last sample of an open face, `folds`). The post-check along the borders takes the radius of its place the same way (`tangent_along`, from the border's own parameter).
- *The end.* No cut and no third face (`end_at` is not asked; `chain::OneEnd::Tip`, `EndKind::Tip`): both borders end in the one vertex, nothing lies between them there, and the face's loop has three sides (two where both ends are tips). At the edge's own vertex nothing else changes: its other edges and faces are what they were, and the result reports the vertex as the body's own. Short of the end, the vertex is new, on the edge, and what is left of the edge beyond it is put back as an edge of both faces on the edge's own curve (`keep_beyond`).
- *Refused:* a tip short of the end by 16 tolerances or less (`FadeFault::BackTooSmall`: its borders could not be told from the edges at the end; with the least, or none); a fade shorter than the radius (the bound of every fade, `over ≥ radius` here); the fade and `back` together longer than the edge; `back` on a fade that is not to nothing (`FadeFault::BackNotATip`, saying so: it was "must be positive … got 1", the review's note; and a size under a billionth is written in powers of ten, so that a `back` of 5e-324 is not "0 short"); a face that would turn back on itself (below).

**Oracles** (all at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6, on two frames unless said). *Closed forms* (`fillet_law.rs`, the tests named `a_fillet_that_ends_in_a_point_…`): between planes at 60°, 90° and 120°, the tip at the top vertex and 6 short of it, an inside corner, both ends in a point: volume, area, every point of the face on the arc of its height within a quarter of the tolerance, borders tangent; a spline rim (the quarter circles, volume by the rim's curvature); the cone's cut edge (the arc between the feet of each ball), a tip at the narrow end, at the wide end short of it, and at both. *The tip* (`fillet_tip.rs`): the borders leave the point at the angle whose tangent is `3R/L · tan(φ/2)` (30.96° here; within 3e-14 rad), and the face is tangent to both planes at 7.5, 3, 1, 0.3 and 0.15 from the point within the tolerance over the radius there (measured: 1e-8 of that bound at most); beyond the point the edge, the two faces' surfaces, the other edges at the vertex and the vertex itself are what they were, to the last bit; the points of a grid round the edge (10 × 10 × 30, less those within 1e-4 of a boundary) are in the body or out of it as the law says. *The vertex:* two faces (the block's edge cut in two at 25, the lower piece faded into the cut: the upper piece is the same edge, three edges and three faces at the tip; the fillet of one radius has no end there); three (the block's corner); four (a hip of a square pyramid, a point at the apex and one at the base corner: a face of two sides, the closed form with the hip's own angle, 0.000 of the slack). *Refused* with the reason. *The cone's curved edge* (a cone of radius 10 and height 30 cut at half its height and by the plane y = 2.5; the review's third finding: all 72 tips it tried there were refused as not followed, where one radius and a fade to a radius build): tips at either end, at the vertex and 3 short of it, built and sound. The cause was not the tip: the face was followed past the edge's *other* end in steps that doubled without a cap, to where no ball lies; with the cap of the first slice (half the end's radius) they build. *A rim that turns* (a prism's spline rim, least radius of curvature 1.57): radius 4 through the turn refused with the place, 1.5 with the same tip and 4 ending before the turn built. The 366 bodies of one radius hash as on `main` again.

**The hull** (`plan/loft-tip/BOW.md`'s half-section hull read from its STEP export, tolerance 0.02; `plan/fillet-fade/s2/hull_probe.rs`, `hull-sweep.txt`, `hull-measured.txt`; pictures `pics/hull-bow.png`, `hull-forefoot.png`). The stem (87.2 long, one cubic spline with simple knots: curvature continuous) rounded 3 with a fade to nothing over 15 builds from 0.33 short of the foot (the least `back`, 16 tolerances) in 0.1 s, valid, clear in the deep check, through STEP and back with the same volume. Held to the definition by the probe's own means at 78 points of its borders: the ball resting there touches the other half with the law's radius within 3.1e-4 (the probe's own chords), the face leaning 0.056 of the tolerance over the radius at most. Built: radius 1 over 5 to 80; radius 3 over 10 to 80 (over 5 from `back` 3); radius 5 over 15 to 80 (over 10 from `back` 3). *Not built:* the tip in the foot itself (`back` 0), at any size: "the rolling ball could not be followed": within 0.1 mm of the foot the two halves' angle passes through nothing and folds (the design's 1.1), so no ball lies there; that is the hull's own corner, which only a redrawn forefoot mends. And the sizes too large for the forefoot's turn (the stem's least radius of curvature is 3.8 mm, 3 to 4 mm above the foot): radius 5 over 5, radius 5 over 10 with `back` 1 or less and radius 3 over 5 with `back` 1 or less are refused before the face is put in, with the place on the stem and the ball's radius there (next paragraph).

**A face that turns back on itself** (2026-11-17, the review's fourth finding; `rolling::turned_back`, `FadeFault::TurnsBack`). *What was true on the hull.* Where the stem turns into the keel, a ball still large there has its foot further from the edge than the edge's centre of turn: from section to section the foot moves backwards, the border reverses, and the face lies folded from there on. The construction did not see it: every section is a good ball, the fit converges on the folded face, and the fold check of every blend holds each section's normals against its own middle, so a section turned round as a whole passes. So it was a fault in the construction's checks, not a size the user got wrong in a way a bound on the law could have told: no face exists for those inputs, and the construction has to say so. *The measure.* On every section the face was refined through, at nine places across it, the part of the face's unit normal along the ball's radius: the cross product of the face's two directions (along: the exact derivative of the section, `Slope::surface`; across: the arc's tangent) over their lengths, signed by the side it lies on in the middle of the fillet. The rate of the radius leans the normal from the ball's radius but never square to it; it passes through nothing only where the face stops advancing along the edge. At nothing or less (`TURNED`) the law is refused with the edge's point there, the ball's radius there and what would build: a smaller radius, a longer fade, an end further from the place. Measured on the stem, 24 laws (radius 3 and 5; over 5, 10, 15; `back` 0.33, 0.5, 1, 3): the ten that fold −0.03 to −1.00, the fourteen that do not 0.07 to 0.75. The built face's own two directions, on the grid, say the same of five of the fourteen, the least among them (0.067 for 0.070, 0.120 for 0.138, 0.361 for 0.361, 0.280 for 0.297, 0.722 for 0.722), with no flip (`plan/fillet-fade/s2/hull-folds.txt`). *No margin:* the measure falls with the rate as well as with the turn (at the bound on the rate, along a straight edge: 0.32 at a border between faces at a right angle, 0.19 at 60°, 0.09 at 30°), and the least that a sound law of the tests measures is 0.017 (a radius growing at the bound into the slanted end of the cone's cut edge, at the end section itself; 0.75 within: a margin of 0.02, tried first, refused it), so only the sign says turned; a fold that falls between the places measured is left to the checks behind, as before. *Where the face cannot be made at all:* a law whose face is "not approximated" is asked the same of the sections refined that far, and refused with the place if they show the turn (the rim that turns, above: radius 2 and more; one radius there is told "could not be approximated" as before). *Not given:* the least `over` or `back` that clears the turn. It would take a search over constructions; the place and the radius there are what the refusal has.

### A fillet that fades, as a command (2026-11-17, the third slice of the fade)

**What the user writes.** `feature.fillet {"edges": [...], "radius": "3 mm", "fade": [{"at": vertex, "over": "15 mm", "back": "1 mm"}]}`: per faded end, `at` the vertex at that end of an edge the fillet rounds, by its persistent name (`document.edges` lists a body's vertices), `over` the length along the edge over which the radius falls to nothing, `back` (absent: 0) how far short of the vertex the face closes in its point. `over` and `back` are expressions like every size of a feature (variables, formulas). `feature.edit` takes `fade` too; `null` or `[]` is one radius all along again. There is no "start"/"end" of an edge: no command names an edge's ends that way, so `at` is a vertex's name.

**What it is not.** The kernel's `Fade` also runs a radius to another radius (`Fade::to`); the design's §3.1 gives that no form and the developer's decision (2026-11-17) keeps a general variable radius for later, so the command takes a fade to nothing only. A fade "by itself" where the faces come tangent is S4; chamfers that fade are S5.

**Data and format.** `doc::Fillet::fade: Vec<FilletFade { at, over, back }>`, at most two per edge (`MAX_FILLET_FADES`); empty is skipped in JSON and in the file, so a fillet without a fade is written as before. The format (`manifest::FeatureKind::Fillet::fade`, `manifest::FilletFade`) reads a file without it as no fade: format version 76, an identity step. The fade's expressions are among the feature's expressions (`FeatureKind::exprs`: `fade.i.over`, `fade.i.back`), so the regeneration cache keys on their values, variable renames reach them, and the validity check holds `at` to being a name.

**Regeneration** (`regen::fillet`). A fillet without a fade takes exactly the path it took before (`kernel::fillet_capped`, the same requests). With one, each `at` is followed to the newest body of its chain as an edge's name is (`follow::vertex`: through the copies of the name a body of copies or a kept vertex has; a vertex named by its faces has no such copy after a blend or a boolean, and following it fails naming the body whose vertex to give), refused unless every part of it is proven to stay with its vertex (`identity::unproven`, as an edge by its own name), and resolved to a vertex of the body (`Names::vertex_named`). It has to end exactly one of the picked edges, once per end, and the faded edge has to be on its own (no other picked edge ends in one of its vertices; a `chain` that adds one is said); `over` is more than zero, `back` zero or more, and together no longer than the edge by its arc length (`kernel::fillet::law_length`, the law's own quadrature). Every picked edge then goes to `kernel::fillet::fillet_by_law_capped` with its law, `RadiusLaw { radius, fades }` with `Fade::tip(over, back)` at each faded end (one radius elsewhere, which the kernel hands on as the fillet of one radius).

**Names.** The fillet face is `F9:fillet(k)` as ever. The tip's vertex is the edge's own vertex when `back` is 0 (the kernel reports it kept, so it answers to its old names, and its name by its faces lists every face there). Short of the vertex, the tip is a new vertex and what is left of the edge beyond it a new edge on the edge's own curve, both named by their faces **and by the end they belong to** (`regen::fillet::by_their_end`, the coordinator's decision on rule 4, 2026-11-17): the faces at the vertex the fade is at, other than those the tip lies on, are written into both names as faces near them (`entity::ByFaces::add_near`): `F9:corner[F9:copy(0)/a, F9:copy(0)/b, F9:fillet(k)|F9:copy(0)/c]` and `F9:between[F9:copy(0)/a, F9:copy(0)/b|F9:copy(0)/c]`. By their faces alone they would be the same at either end of the edge, and a fade moved to the other end would carry a datum point at the tip, or a note on the edge beyond, to the new place without a word (found by the review); so the old names go with it, and what named them fails with the name, or comes loose, and is where it was when the fade comes back (`fillet_fade.rs` › `a_fade_moved_to_the_other_end_takes_its_names_with_it`). Where the vertex has no face but the edge's two (a hull's forefoot) the names are those of the faces alone, which differ from those at an end with a third face; an edge whose two ends both have none would give its tips one name (not met). The edge beyond does not answer to the edge's own name (a first version said it was the edge shortened, which would have stayed on the piece at either end). The tip's name lists the fillet's face, so it is not proven to stay with its vertex (`identity`): a later fade or fillet that names it is refused, a note on it is held by place. And a fade at an end an earlier blend rounded is refused saying so (that end is such a vertex), with the ends a fade does take (the review's dead end: the refusal had offered the earlier blend's vertex by its name, which was then refused as not proven; `fillet_fade.rs` › `a_fade_at_an_end_an_earlier_fillet_rounded_is_refused_without_a_dead_end`). Proven in the naming suite (`naming_entities.rs` › `a_fillet_that_fades_names_its_tip_and_the_edge_beyond_through_edits`: a datum point at the tip and the edge beyond through four edits of the block's height and the fade's back; the fade taken away fails the point naming its vertex) and in `fillet_fade.rs` (the edge beyond found by its own name through an edit, a save and an undo). A chamfer of the edge beyond is refused: its end at the tip is a vertex with the fillet's pole face, which "does not end cleanly" for a freeform chamfer (owed with S5 and S6).

**What the result says** (`fades`, also in `document.inspect`): per fade `at`, `edge` (the place of its reference), `tip` (the point, `kernel::fillet::tip_point`: where the law puts it), `vertex` (that point's name in the filleted body), `over`, `back` and the edge's `length`.

**Messages.** The kernel's `FilletFade` reaches the user at the fade it is of, with the unit: `fade.0 (at F1:vertex(4)): the radius changes too fast along the edge: by 3 mm over a length of 1 mm. … give an over of at least 3 mm`; a `back` the tolerance cannot tell from the vertex, with the least; the place of a curvature jump. ### A fillet's offset: rebuilt (2026-10-18)

What the face operations ask of the rolling ball to rebuild a fillet between offset faces (`blend::rolling::rebuild`; the operation's side in [`face-operations.md`](face-operations.md) › *Offset of a fillet: rebuilt*):
- **Reading a fillet off its surface** (`fillet_of`): the shape `refine` gives (three rows across, rational quadratic in `v`), the radius and its side from the exact end rows, each of 17 rows an arc of the exact ball between the two faces in its own plane, to the tolerance. Not point for point against a ball in another plane: two fits of one blend take their sections a little apart along it (the saddle's: 1.8e-6 mm at an end), which moves no point off the surface.
- **The same `Solver`, `Spine`, `follow` and `refine`**, the spine the old border, the joints' planes the old end rows' at a chain's joints.
- **`Solver::settle`**: how little a centre may still move per step and be settled. Zero everywhere else (rounding, as before); a hundredth of the tolerance for a rebuild, whose feet on fitted offsets of high degree are found only to about 1e-12 of the part (the saddle's rear rim jittered by 1e-8 mm).
- **Feet on the faces** (`on_face`): past the part of the old fillet its face's edges reach, a ball is kept only while its feet lie within a thousandth of a freeform face's parameters past its border; past that the fillet would roll along a fitted offset extrapolated, or, past a smooth edge, along the wrong face.
