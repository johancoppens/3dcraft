# 3DCraft design: The document, sketches, datums and appearance

The document as data (`craft3d-doc`), expressions and variables (`craft3d-expr`), sketches and their solver (`craft3d-solve`, mapped in `regen/src/sketch.rs`), datums, and the appearance and material of bodies. The *Data* of the map ([`../architecture.md`](../architecture.md) §3).

## Method

**The document is the recipe.** `Document` holds units, tolerance, variables (in the order they were defined), the features in order of application, a rollback marker, the next feature id (ids are never reused), and beside the history `bodies`: the appearance and material given to the body of a feature. It is pure data with serde; nothing in it is derived, and `validate()` checks it. Every numeric parameter is an expression kept as source text (`"height - 2 * wall"`), evaluated at regeneration in the document's units; a bare number is in the document's unit.

**Expressions** (`expr`): dimensions (length and angle exponents) and units, a parser and evaluator with functions and `pi` (`sin cos tan asin acos atan atan2 sqrt abs min max`, and the whole-number functions `floor ceil round trunc mod`: below), and a `VariableTable` that evaluates in a deterministic order from the dependencies, detects cycles, fails only what depends on a bad variable, and rewrites references on rename. Sizes are capped against hostile input.

**Sketches** are features: a plane (a world plane, a face by name, or a datum plane, with an offset), point, line, circle, arc and spline entities (a polynomial B-spline on clamped uniform knots whose control points are point entities), and constraints in the order they were added. The stored coordinates *are* the last solution, so a reopened sketch does not flip. `solve` takes plain numbers: point coordinates and radii are the unknowns, gradients exact (dual numbers), steps minimum-norm with a line search from the stored solution, so free geometry moves as little as possible. It reports the degrees of freedom and, from the rank of the Jacobian in the order of the constraints, which ones are redundant or conflicting; a point on a spline is one equation with the curve's parameter eliminated at the foot of the point. Every loop is capped; "did not converge" is a normal answer.

**Datums** (`datum.plane`, `axis`, `point`, `helix`) are features without a body. Each is placed by small reference types (`PlaneRef`, `AxisRef`, `PointRef`: base geometry, a face by name, an edge by its two faces, a vertex by name, another datum) and found again at every regeneration; a sketch, a mirror, a revolution or a pattern can be placed by one.

**Appearance and material** are not features: regeneration does not read them. A body has the appearance and material of the feature that makes it, else of the body it was made from as instance 0; a face has the appearance of the nearest feature on the way its name records. Mass and inertia come from the material's density and are measured when asked, once per body.

**Whole-number functions.** `floor`, `ceil`, `round` and `trunc` take a plain number, `floor(x)`, or a quantity and a positive step of the same dimension, `floor(x, step)` = `step * floor(x / step)` (`round(width, 0.5 mm)`, `floor(a, 15 deg)`). A length or an angle alone is refused with a hint: its whole number would depend on the unit the value is held in (millimetres, radians), not on the unit the document shows, so `floor(width)` in an inch document would silently mean millimetres; a ratio (`floor(span / fingerW)`, `floor(width / 1 mm)`) or a step says which unit is meant. `round` takes ties to the even whole number (`round(2.5)` = 2, `round(3.5)` = 4, `round(-2.5)` = -2; the developer's decision, [`../decisions.md`](../decisions.md) 2026-10-16), so a row of ties does not drift one way. `mod(a, b)` = `a - b * floor(a / b)` for any dimension, the same for both: the rest has the sign of `b` (`mod(-7, 3)` = 2, `mod(7, -3)` = -2); `b = 0` is a division by zero; a step that is zero or negative is an error. An argument within four units in the last place of a point where the function jumps (the whole numbers; for `round` the halves) is taken to lie on it, so `floor(0.3 / 0.1)` is 3 and `round(0.35 / 0.1)` is 4, not 3: a ratio of decimal literals carries half a unit of rounding per literal, conversion and division. This holds for a typed literal too (`floor(2.9999999999999996)` is 3). The bound is relative to the jump point except at zero, where it is absolute (4ε: `floor(-1e-17)` is 0); and where it would reach half the gap between two jumps (from 2^49 for the whole numbers, 2^48 for the halves) there is no snap, so `floor(1e15 + 0.5)` is 1e15. From |a / b| = 2^52 on, `mod(a, b)` is 0: the quotient is a whole number in `f64`. No result is a negative zero. Farther away a value is taken as it is.

**Patterns inside a sketch.** `Sketch::patterns` repeats seed entities (any kind; the points of curves come along) as **rigid images of the solved seed**: a row or a grid (instance `(i, j)` moved by `i step + j step2`), a chained run (instance `k` moved by `k (to - from)`, the step taken from the solved seed) or a turn about a centre (`k angle`, a ring of runs with `chain`). Count, steps, angle and centre are expressions; a count is a whole number from 1 to 10 000 (within 1e-9, as a 3D pattern's), else the sketch fails with the reason. The instances have no unknowns and are not stored: they are made from the solved seed at every regeneration (`regen/src/sketch_pattern.rs`), so the degrees of freedom, redundancy and conflicts are the seed's, and a 400-instance row costs the seed's solve plus arithmetic. The joints of a chained run are computed once (`J_0 = from`, `J_1 = to`, `J_k = from + k (to - from)`, turned for a ring, `J_N = J_0` when a ring closes: a whole turn within the angular tolerance and the last joint within the linear tolerance of the first), and both instances that meet at a joint take the same numbers, so profile detection closes the loop at any count. **Image points** (`Geometry::Image { of, pattern, index }`: `e5[3]`, `e5[2,4]` in a grid, `e5[last]`) are points of an instance that constraints and lines can use: for the solver a derived point with no unknowns whose coordinates are affine in the unknowns of the seed point, the chain's ends and the centre (`solve::ImageMap`, exact gradients through the dual numbers, the same arithmetic as the instances are placed with). `coincident(C, B[last])` with C fixed lets the solver find the step of a run: `B = A + (C - A) / N`. `e5[k]` past the count is missing: its constraints are left out (`missing`) and the sketch fails naming it, a line on it fails the sketch, and it never stands for another instance; `last` follows the count by definition. A ring's closing equation (the end of the seed is its start turned by the angle) is a row of the solve of its own, reported as the pattern's `chainState`. Names: an instance curve is `e7[3]` (`e7[2,5]` in a grid), the seed keeps `e7`; a cap whose loop holds every instance of a seed curve names it once, `e7[*]` ([`names.md`](names.md)). **Nested patterns** (format 21): a pattern's seed may hold whole patterns (`SketchPattern::patterns`) besides entities: the run of tabs `p1` repeated cell by cell by `p2`, a ring of rows, a grid of bolt circles. The patterns of a sketch are trees: a pattern is in one seed, an entity the direct seed of one pattern, no cycles, at most `MAX_PATTERN_DEPTH` = 3 levels (checked when a pattern is made and by `Sketch::validate`, with a seen-set, for files). The outer seed repeats the points of its own entities and of the patterns in it, their image points included; a seed curve may be built on an image point of a pattern in its seed (a spacer from `e8[last]`), and a chain may end on one (when the cell is exactly the inner run, `to` is `e8[last]`). An image point may be the image of such an image point, one per level (`e8[last][last]`: the inner run's end in the outer run's last cell; `Sketch::image_path`); it comes after the one it is made from. Instance `(k; i)` is `T_outer,i ∘ T_inner,k`, so the maps stay affine in the seed's unknowns: no new unknowns, the degrees of freedom and blame are the seed's, and for the solver an image of an image is a derived point of a derived point (`solve`: a map's point may be an earlier image, at most eight deep), with exact gradients; an equation that would depend on more than 18 unknowns through its images is refused, naming it, as not supported yet. *Placing, bit for bit:* every point of an instance has a key, which is the point itself, a joint of a chained level, or the image of the key one level in under one instance of a level (`regen::sketch_pattern::Placer`); a level's joints are worked out once from the places of its chain ends (themselves keys of the levels inside it), and a point whose key one level in is a chain end of the level around it takes that level's joint, so every cell starts on exactly the numbers on which the cell before ended, at every level and every count, and places are worked out once per key. For patterns of one level this is the arithmetic of before, bit for bit. The instances are made the outermost level first, in index order, walking only the levels that hold curves somewhere; the product of the counts along a tree is capped by `MAX_PATTERN_CURVES` whether it makes curves or not (points-only trees could otherwise ask for 10^12 visits); their curves are counted with checked products of the counts before anything is made (`MAX_PATTERN_CURVES`, 100 000, construction curves counted on their own against it too; each level 1 to 10 000). *Carrying* goes through every level: which images moved is decided on their composed places (an outer image whose own map did not change moves when its inner one did), and an image made from moving images is carried after them, one stage of the path parameter per level, so that along each stage every image moves linearly in it (one Euler step across a product of two moving maps landed on the other root, `geometry_after_a_run_of_runs_follows_its_end_when_either_count_jumps`). **Skipping instances:** `SketchPattern::skip` lists instances of the pattern's own level that are left out (`skip: [3, "2,4"]`), in ascending order, never instance 0 (the seed, which is drawn and solved); `last` is the highest instance there is; a skip past the count is kept and has no effect until the count grows back (`sketch.inspect` lists it as `inactive`); an image point of a skipped instance is missing, as one past the count; names stay by origin (with `e7[3]` skipped `e7[4]` is still `e7[4]`). Refused with "not supported yet": an instance curve in a constraint (it is no entity), a constraint between images of one tree of patterns only, an entity in two seeds, an image point as a seed entity, a seed built on an image point of a pattern that is not in its seed, anything but a line built on an image point, a skip in a chained run or a ring (it would leave two open ends), a skip by formula or of one cell's instance only, a level inserted into the middle of a tree, a loft section made by nested patterns. A sweep hands the kernel the curves of two sketches: the path's numbers of nested curves are moved past the profile's and both are named from one table (`CurveIds::joined`), in the sides' names and in the kernel's messages.

**Carrying a sketch along when its images jump.** An image point has no unknowns, so when an edit changes its map (a count, step or angle of its pattern) it is somewhere else at once while what hangs on it starts where it was, on the far side of the image: from there Newton's method finds the other root of an unsigned length (a line one unit after a run's end lands one unit *before* it, and the profile folds back). The stored solution therefore includes the image points: `Sketch::image_places` (format 20) holds where each lay when the coordinates were solved and the numbers of the map that put it there (`PlaceMap`: the offset of a row or grid, the instance number `k` of a chained run, the angle and fixed centre of a turn), written with the coordinates by the solve after every edit (`settle_sketches`; a file from before is given them from its current solution before an edit that changes something, `image_places_to_record`, so a no-op edit stays one). The solve (`solve::Sketch::image_starts`, `crates/solve/src/carry.rs`) first follows a path from there by continuation: an unknown `t` goes from 0 to 1 and takes every image that moved with it **through its pattern's own family** when the stored map is of the same kind about the same points as the current one (the map's numbers go from their old values to their new ones: a ring's image swings round its centre, a run's slides along it), and in a straight line from its old place otherwise (the pattern's kind changed, or no map was stored); every row is `F(x) - (1 - t) r0` (`r0` the rows at the start, so a dimension changed in the same edit moves in the same measure and relations such as `Ly = ny cellP + t` stay consistent along the way), and steps in `t` are solved by Newton iterations with `t` as one more unknown and the row `t - next` first: the first iteration is the Euler predictor with the Jacobian where the sketch is (on the right side of every image), the next ones the corrector. A step is kept when every row holds (dependent rows too: the path goes on only while the sketch has a solution along it) after corrections that contract (each at most half the one before, or below the tolerance), otherwise halved (to 1/1024 at most, 64 tries); the carrying has half the work budget of its own and the solve after it the whole budget, as before carrying existed; should the solve from where the path ended fail, the solver solves from the stored coordinates as it did before carrying existed and says so (below: the sketch then fails all the same, with the reason, rather than with an error of the solver's). A stored map is followed only from where the image was: when its numbers on today's points (the ends of the run or the centre moved in the same edit) do not put the image within the tolerance of its stored place, the image goes in a straight line from that place. The point reached is only the starting guess of the ordinary solve, which keeps every verdict and guarantee of its own. Which images moved is decided on the sketch as given, before any unknowns are numbered: nothing is carried when none moved by more than the linear tolerance, so a fresh open of a saved file, whose places match its coordinates, solves exactly as the session that saved it did. `Solution::carry` says what happened: `Whole`, `Stopped { reached, moved, out_of_work }` or `Skipped { reason, moved }` (`moved`: the image points that had moved). **A sketch not carried all the way fails** (the developer's decision of 2026-10-17, [`../decisions.md`](../decisions.md)): a solution the sketch was not carried to may lie on another root than the one it was on (folded over), and which root it found would depend on the path of edits, so it is never stored nor built from. The sketch fails like any other feature ([`regeneration.md`](regeneration.md) › *A feature that fails*): what is made from it fails naming it, and nothing of the solve is kept, so its coordinates and image places stay those of the last state that was carried (or needed no carrying); every later solve carries from there again and fails the same way until the edit that made the jump is undone, the variables return (the start again, bit for bit, `a_sketch_carried_part_of_the_way_fails`) or the sketch is changed so that it can follow. The reason (`regen::sketch::carry_failure`) names the image points by their pattern instance (`e10 = e8[last]`, three at most and how many more), how far the carrying got and what to do. The sketch's own verdict comes first: a sketch that fails for a reason of its own (a conflict, a constraint left out, a ring that does not close) fails with that reason and its remedy, with "(nor could the sketch be carried along all the way to where its image points are now)" added, since no step size fixes a conflict (`a_conflict_is_reported_before_a_carry_that_stops`). The outcomes, judged one by one against the rule "never build from a solution that may have folded over, never depend on the edit path": *no image moved* (`None`): nothing to carry, the ordinary solve, never a failure. *Whole*: the solve started where the path ended; it is used. *Stopped, a step could not be taken* (the path meets a stretch where the sketch has no solution, a fold or a conflict; `reached` from 0 to 1): fails, "could be carried along only 41.2% of the way as image point e10 = e8[last] went from where it was to where its pattern puts it now: past there its constraints have no solution that moves with the images; …; make the change in smaller steps, or change the constraints on the geometry that hangs on the images" — also when the solve from where it stopped would be clean (`a_path_that_stops_part_way_fails_although_the_solve_is_clean`: a passing obstacle no longer moves the sketch to a point the next edit would not reach). *Stopped, out of work* (the carrying's half of the work budget ran out): fails, "carrying the sketch along as … ran out of work 12.0% of the way; …; make the change in smaller steps": a large sketch solved from the far side of its images is the same risk whatever stopped the path. *Skipped, an equation too full for the path parameter* (an equation on a moved image already holds the 18 unknowns one equation can, so `t` cannot be added): fails, "the sketch could not be carried along as …: an equation on a moved image point would depend on more unknowns than one equation holds once the path is added; …; make the change in smaller steps, or use other constraints on the images (each on fewer image points)" (`a_sketch_that_cannot_be_carried_fails`): the solve from the stored coordinates is the one before carrying existed, which folds. *Skipped, an equation without a value at the stored coordinates*, *the path took a radius below zero* and *the path left the sketch's unknowns*: fail the same way, for the same reason. *Skipped, carried all the way but the solve from there failed* (the solver then solved from the stored coordinates): fails, since that solution is again one from the far side of the images; a solve that fails from both starts fails with the solver's own error, as before. *A pattern whose kind changed in the same edit* (or a place stored without its map) is not a skip: the image is carried in a straight line from its old place, still a continuous path from the solution the sketch was on, and the outcome is judged as above (a `Whole` straight path is used, one that stops fails; a turn of nearly half a circle along a chord is where it stops). This replaces the interim rule of the night before (a stopped path whose final solve was clean was stored, with a warning on the edit's result), whose price was that a sketch did not return when its variables did. *Open:* "never depends on the edit path" holds for failures and for returns (a failed edit stores nothing; returning the variables returns the sketch), not between different successful sequences: changing a dimension and a count in one edit follows one homotopy, changing them one after the other follows two, and where the sketch has more than one solution near the path the two can end on different ones. Places in a file that are not in the form a document keeps them (of an entity that is no image point, repeated, not finite, out of order) are dropped on reading with a warning (`Sketch::tidy_image_places`, `n log n`), since a place is where the next solve starts, not part of the design; `Sketch::validate` holds a document to that form, and deleting an image point deletes its place.

**Offsets of profiles.** `FeatureKind::OffsetProfile { sketch, distance, corners }` (`feature.offsetProfile`) offsets every closed profile of a sketch by a distance in its plane: a positive one grows the material (outer loops out, holes in), a negative one shrinks it. It makes no body and no sketch of its own; at regeneration it takes the profiles of the solved sketch with its patterns expanded (`extrude::swept`), so it follows every edit and every count, and `kernel::offset_profile` makes the outline, kept in the feature's result (`FeatureResult::offset`). A feature that takes profiles (an extrusion, a revolution, a sweep's profile) takes the offset in the place of a sketch (`extrude::drawn`), and `file.export` of it writes the outline: a laser's beam path is the outline offset by kerf/2, one feature, with no kerf term in any dimension of the sketch. *The method* (exact for lines, arcs and circles): every loop runs with its material on its left, so each curve's offset lies `d` to its right (an arc's radius becomes `r ± d`; one whose radius is no longer above the tolerance is left out); at a corner where the offsets part, `round` adds the arc of radius `|d|` about the corner (the true offset, the Minkowski sum with a disc) and `sharp` carries both neighbours on along their tangents to a mitre (a line is lengthened, an arc gets a straight extension; next to a curve that was left out the corner is round; there is no mitre limit: a sharp corner of 1° puts the tip far out, which is the true mitre); where they overlap nothing is added. This raw offset is cut at every point where two of its curves meet (exact line and circle intersections; curves that follow each other at a smooth joint or a closed gap meet only there and are not intersected), and a piece is kept when its middle lies at least `|d| − tol/4` from the outline and, for `sharp`, outside every mitre wedge (the boundary of the offset region lies on the raw offset, and a piece nearer than `|d|` is inside the region). The kept pieces go through `detect_profiles`, which joins, orients and nests them and refuses loops that touch; every piece must then run the way it was made, or the outline is refused rather than handed on. A loop that leaves no piece (a hole that closes, a region shrunk to nothing) is refused with the largest distance at which every loop and region is still there (bisection to a thousandth of the tolerance); a result with other numbers of loops or regions (a region split in two, a channel closed into a hole) is kept and reported (`loopsBefore`, `loopsAfter`, `regionsBefore`, `regionsAfter`, `wholeUpTo`). A distance within the linear tolerance leaves the outline as it is. *Bounds:* inward past half the diagonal of the outline's box nothing can be left (no point of a region is further from its outline), so it is refused at once with the largest distance found by bisection below that; outward the size `reach + d` must stay below `tol / (1024 ε)` for the arithmetic to hold the tolerance, or it is refused with that largest distance. An outline with more pieces than the offset takes (262 144, `offset_profile::MAX_PIECES`, what profile detection takes; 4096 until 2026-10-17) is refused as such (`TooManyOffsetCurves`), and a raw offset that crosses a bound on the work (`CrowdedOffset`, naming the curves; [`sweeps.md`](sweeps.md) › *Offset of profiles at scale*) likewise, without a search for a distance; a search for the largest distance that meets a cap or a bound ends without one (the reason stays the outline's, with no largest distance). The pairs that may meet and the curves and wedges near a piece come from trees of boxes, judged as the old all-pairs sweeps judged them. *Not supported yet:* splines (refused by name), an offset as a loft section or as a sweep's path, an offset of an offset. *Names* ([`names.md`](names.md)): what a sweep makes of it is named after the offset's curves, `F10:side(F9:offset(e7[3]))`, `F10:side(F9:offsetCorner(e7,e8))` (the two curves in the direction the loop runs), `F10:side(F9:offsetExtension(e7,e8))` for an arc carried on to a mitre, and, where one curve left pieces in several loops, `F10:side(F9:loop(e1,e2,e7)/F9:offset(e7))`: each piece by the curves of the loop it closes, never by an order; a cap after the sketch curves whose offset its loop is, runs collapsed, `F10:endCap(F9:offset(e1,e2,e7[*]))`.

**Public parameters and document info (Jigs).** A variable may carry a `PublicParam` (`doc/src/public.rs`): label, description, group, order, kind (`number`, `integer`, `boolean` as 0/1, `choice` as 1..n with its labels in `choices`), `min`, `max` and `step` as expressions (a range may depend on other variables), the unit a form shows it in, and the `default` a reset goes back to. A document with one is a Jig (`Document::is_jig`); `Document::info` (`DocInfo`: title, description, author, licence, url, tags) is what a gallery shows. Both are beside the history: regeneration does not read them. The document checks only their shape (caps, unit symbols, choices only on a choice); what the expressions evaluate to is checked by the commands (`engine/src/param_cmds.rs`), against the variables in the expression language's base units, so the same check serves `variable.publish` (refused unless the value and the default are of the kind and within the range, and when it would make another parameter invalid), `param.set` (all values in one edit and one regeneration; a value outside its range, of another dimension or not of its kind is refused and nothing changes; so is a change that would make another parameter's value invalid through a range that depends on it; problems of a description, such as a range that no longer evaluates, are warnings, since a value cannot mend them; a whole-number kind is stored as the exact whole number, and a choice's label or `true`/`false` are accepted), `param.list` (what a form and an agent read: every parameter with its value, default, range and step evaluated in its unit, `valid` and its `problems`) and `document.check` (a parameter set out of range by `variable.set`, which stays the designer's tool and is not range-checked, is a problem there; `variable.set` and `variable.delete` list what they made invalid under `parameterWarnings`). A default is checked against the ranges as they are when every parameter is at its default, the state a reset gives. A public variable's expression must be a value (no variables; constant arithmetic such as `1 in / 16` is a value): `variable.publish` refuses a formula, and `variable.set` refuses to give a public variable one. `rename_variable` carries the description along and renames inside the ranges; `variable.delete` names a range that uses the variable. A run of `param.set` with `merge: true` on the same parameters is one undo step (`History::apply_merging`: a run per key, ended by any other edit, undo or redo, and no clock); a run that came back to its start and goes on makes a new step, never folding into an earlier run's. `file.save` writes `thumb.png` for a Jig ([`../file-formats.md`](../file-formats.md)).

## Findings and history

**The parameter form (2026-10-23, Jigs phase 2).** Built from `plan/jigs.md` §3 in `ui-egui/src/shell/params.rs`; the shell stays thin: it reads `param.list` (again whenever the active document's revision changes) and hands `param.set` to its host as a `ShellCommand` (`Shell::take_command`, `Shell::command_done`), which the desktop runs on its engine thread and the web page in its pump. Where the build differs from the plan, and why:

- **No tab for a document without parameters:** the plan's rule. A plain document keeps the feature list alone, so nothing moves for people who never make a Jig; the header's tooltip says how a variable is published.
- **A number field is the viewport's `NumberField`,** on a dark plate of its own (`plate_fill` at `plate_opacity`): it is made for the plates over the viewport and is unreadable on the light panel without one. Its id is shared (`param`); each parameter's field lives under its own egui id and the form remembers which parameter the drag, the focus or the typing belongs to.
- **A drag commits at most every 0.15 s and once more on release** (desktop), not only after a pause, so the model follows the hand; the first commit of a drag is its own undo step and the rest `merge: true`. A commit waits while one is under way, and a commit is handed to the host only after a frame was drawn saying *Regenerating…* (the web runs it on the page's only thread, so that frame is what the person sees meanwhile).
- **Good and broken are counted:** a commit broke the model when it reports a newly failed feature, or more features fail or assertions are broken than in the last state the form took as good; a document that failed before the form touched it is not the form's failure. While broken, the viewport draws the last good scene (an `Arc<Scene>` the form keeps) and *Back to last good* is a `param.set` of the last good expressions, not an undo, so it works after any number of commits.
- **Typed text:** a number, or a number with the parameter's own unit, is clamped and snapped; a length in another unit is converted with the document's units; anything else goes to `param.set` as typed, which reads units and refuses what is out of range (said at the field).
- **A dimension without a unit symbol** (an area) is shown but not dragged: `param.set` takes it only as a value with its units.

**Public parameters, document info and a thumbnail (2026-10-23, Jigs phase 1, format 32).** Built from the plan `plan/jigs.md` §2 with the developer's decisions of 2026-10-23 (the command refuses an out-of-range value, the form clamps; a Jig is a `.3dcraft` with public variables, info and a thumbnail). Where the build differs from the plan, and why:

- **A value is an expression without variables,** not only a literal: `1 in / 16` and `-3 mm` are values a form can show; what matters is that a parameter does not depend on other variables.
- **The kind and the choices are two fields** (`kind`, `choices`), as the file has them, so the conversion to and from the file cannot drop the labels of a kind that has none; a choice from a table column waits for tables (G2).
- **No minimum 3DCraft version in the info:** the format version already refuses a file that is too new with "a newer 3DCraft is needed", and every new capability a Jig can use is a format change.
- **Merging a run of `param.set` is asked for, not timed:** `merge: true` folds the change into the last undo step when that was a `param.set` of the same parameters. A one-second window would need a clock in the engine (none on wasm without a host call) and would make the history depend on timing; the form knows when a drag goes on and says so.
- **A step is a hint,** for the form and for sampling over the range (`document.sample`, phase 4), and is not enforced by `param.set`.
- **Another parameter made invalid by a change is refused** (`bore` above a maximum of `rootDiameter / 2` after `teeth` went down), unless it was invalid before (set out of range by `variable.set`), so a Jig moved by `param.set` alone stays valid; both are set in one `param.set`.
- **The thumbnail** is 400 × 300 pixels from the iso view, drawn by the CPU renderer as `view.render` draws it (13.6 kB for a small chamfered plate with a hole), so the same document saves to the same bytes on one platform and build (not promised across platforms: the renderer's trigonometry comes from the platform's maths library, and wasm differs again; so no cross-platform test compares `thumb.png`). It is drawn once per change of the document and kept for the next save. It is written by default for a Jig and on request for any document (`file.save {thumbnail}`), not by autosave; a Jig without bodies is saved without one, and the result says why.

**A screw pattern (2026-10-19, finding 3 of the Garabit pier, format 23).** `PatternLayout::Circular` carries `step: Option<Expr>`, one length: instance *i* is turned by *i* × `angle` about the axis and moved by *i* × `step` along it (negative: the other way along `axis`; about a datum axis, along the datum's direction). The turn and the shift commute, because the shift is along the line turned about, so instance *i* is `rotation_about(point, axis, i·angle).then(translation(axis · i·step))` whichever way it is read. A step within the document's linear tolerance is no step: the maps are the plain turn's, bit for bit, and the plain turn's refusals hold (an angle of zero, `count × angle` past a full turn); with a step the instances climb past each other, so a screw may go round as often as it likes (a conveyor's flights). `feature.pattern` takes `angle` and `step` together, `step` then one expression (a shift `[x, y, z]` with `angle` is refused and told what to give); `feature.edit` sets it with `step` and takes it away with `step: null`. ### From *Current state (2026-10-12)*

- **Expressions:** `expr` has dimensions (length and angle exponents) and units (`um mm cm m in ft`, `deg rad`), a parser and evaluator for formula text (`+ - * / ^`, functions, `pi`, variables, a unit after a number), and `VariableTable` (deterministic evaluation order, cycle detection, partial failure, rename that rewrites references). Source length, nesting depth and table size are capped. Not built: printing an expression back to text, storing the evaluation order (computed on demand; storing it is `doc`/`format`'s job).
- **Document:** `doc` has `Document` (units, tolerance, variables, ordered features, rollback marker, next feature id), features `Block`, `Cylinder`, `Sphere`, `Cone`, `Torus` whose parameters are expressions kept as source text, an `Orientation` (axis and angle) on all but the sphere, stable never-reused feature ids, and `validate()`. Sketches are features too (`doc::sketch`): a plane (a world plane with an offset), point, line, circle, arc and (2026-10-13) spline entities with ids that are never reused, sixteen kinds of constraint in the order they were added, and the last solution as the stored coordinates. Not built then: the other feature kinds, references between features (both built since: this document, [`names.md`](names.md), [`sweeps.md`](sweeps.md)).
- **Sketch solver:** `solve` takes a sketch as plain numbers (points, lines, circles, arcs; constraints with evaluated values) and returns solved coordinates, the degrees of freedom left, and per constraint whether it is satisfied, redundant or conflicting (the later constraint is blamed). Newton-type with minimum-norm steps from the given start, so a solved sketch does not move and a free part moves as little as possible; every loop is capped. It knows nothing about the document: `regen` maps one onto the other.

### 4. Document model (`craft3d-doc`)

A document is the recipe. Sketch of the types (final names are settled in M1/M2):

*The plan's sketch of the types. As built, `Document` has no `id` or `metadata` but `next_feature_id` and `bodies` (appearance and material); variables are kept in the order they were defined and evaluated in dependency order; `FeatureKind` has the variants in `crates/doc/src/lib.rs` (`Block`, `Cylinder`, … , `Extrude`, `Revolve`, `Loft`, `Sweep`, `Boolean`, datums, `Chamfer`, `Fillet`, the face operations, `Thread`); `Import` and `Draft` as planned here, a pattern and a mirror are built, `Primitive` is one variant per kind.*

```rust
pub struct Document {
    pub id: DocId,
    pub units: Units,                    // length + angle unit
    pub tolerance: Tolerance,            // linear + angular modelling tolerance
    pub variables: VariableTable,        // name -> expression, in stored evaluation order
    pub features: Vec<Feature>,          // THE history: explicit order of application
    pub rollback: Option<FeatureId>,     // history marker: regenerate up to here
    pub metadata: Metadata,
}

pub struct Feature {
    pub id: FeatureId,                   // stable, never reused within a document
    pub name: String,
    pub suppressed: bool,
    pub kind: FeatureKind,
}

pub enum FeatureKind {
    DatumPlane(DatumPlane), DatumAxis(DatumAxis), DatumPoint(DatumPoint),
    Sketch(Sketch),
    Primitive(Primitive),                // box, cylinder, cone, sphere, torus
    Extrude(Extrude), Revolve(Revolve), Sweep(Sweep), Loft(Loft),
    Boolean(Boolean),                    // union / subtract / intersect between bodies
    Fillet(Fillet), Chamfer(Chamfer), Shell(Shell), Draft(Draft),
    Pattern(Pattern), Mirror(Mirror), Transform(TransformBody),
    Import(ImportedBody),                // a history-free body from STEP, stored as a blob
}

pub struct Sketch {                      // as built in M5: crates/doc/src/sketch.rs
    pub plane: SketchPlane,              // World { plane: Xy|Yz|Zx, offset }, Face { face: PersistentName, offset }, Datum { plane: FeatureId, offset }
    pub entities: Vec<Entity>,           // id, construction flag, geometry: Point {x, y}, Line, Circle, Arc, Spline on point ids
    pub constraints: Vec<Constraint>,    // id + kind, in the order they were applied
    pub next_entity_id: u64,             // ids are never reused within a sketch
    pub next_constraint_id: u64,
}                                        // the point coordinates and circle radii ARE the last solution

pub enum Constraint {
    Coincident(..), Horizontal(..), Vertical(..), Parallel(..), Perpendicular(..), Tangent(..),
    Equal(..), Concentric(..), Symmetric(..), Midpoint(..), Fixed(..),
    Distance { a: EntRef, b: EntRef, value: Expr }, Angle { .. , value: Expr },
    Radius { .. , value: Expr }, Diameter { .. , value: Expr },
}

pub struct Expr { pub source: String /* "width / 2 + 3 mm" */, /* parsed form cached, not serialised */ }

pub enum Ref {                           // how a feature points at geometry
    Feature(FeatureId),                  // a datum or a whole body
    Name(PersistentName),                // a face / edge / vertex
}
```

- **Every numeric parameter is an `Expr`,** not a number. `Extrude { distance: Expr }` may be `"25 mm"` or `"height - 2 * wall"`. The expression's source text is what is saved.
- **Order is explicit.** `features` is the order of application. `variables` keeps a stored topological order; `constraints` keeps the order in which they were added. Saving and reopening must reproduce the same model, so nothing that affects the result may depend on map iteration or solver start-up luck.
- **Solved sketch state is saved.** Constraint systems have several solutions (mirror branches). The last solution is stored and used as the solver's starting point, so a reopened sketch does not flip.
- **No bodies in the document.** Bodies live in the regeneration result, keyed by feature id.

**Splines in sketches (2026-10-13, the first step of M9's sweeps).** `Geometry::Spline { degree, control }` is a polynomial B-spline of degree 1 to 7 whose control points are point entities, in order, on clamped uniform knots that are derived from the degree and the count (`doc::clamped_uniform_knots`) and not stored. It starts at its first control point, ends at its last and leaves each along the leg to the next; with the first point also as the last it closes on itself. Why this form: the unknowns of the solver are the points, so the constraints that exist (coincident, fixed, distance, horizontal, ...) shape a spline without the solver knowing the kind at all, it adds no degrees of freedom, and it closes a loop with lines and arcs by sharing its end points. A later rational or non-uniform spline adds `weights` and `knots` as fields that default to what this one means. A spline *through* given points is not a second kind: `sketch.spline` with `through` works the control points out once (the curve takes point i at the Greville abscissa of control point i, a system that is well conditioned whatever the points) and stores an ordinary spline; the first and last points are shared as given, the ones in between become new control points. `regen` does not hand a spline to the solver: its points are there already. A `tangent` constraint between a spline and a line, an arc or another spline holds at an end point the two share (one point entity, or two tied by a coincident constraint) and becomes a condition on the spline's end leg: parallel to the line or to the other spline's leg, tangent to the arc's circle at that point; without a shared end the sketch fails with that reason. A point is held on a spline by `pointOn` (the next paragraph, 2026-10-14). The other constraints (parallel, equal, midpoint, a radius) refuse a spline as not supported yet.

**A point on a spline (2026-10-14).** `pointOn` takes a point and a spline. For the solver a spline is still not an entity and has no unknowns: `solve::Sketch::splines` lists the splines that points are put on (degree, knots, control points as point entities; `regen` adds one the first time a constraint needs it), and `Constraint::PointOnSpline` is **one equation without an extra unknown**. The curve's parameter is eliminated: the residual is the signed distance `g = (P − C(u)) · n(u)` at the *foot* of the point, the nearest point of the curve (the nearest of eight places per knot span, then a bisection on `(C − P) · C′`, every loop bounded). At the foot `P − C` is at right angles to the curve, so `g` does not change to first order with `u` or with the direction of `n`, and its gradient is exact with both held still: `n` for the point and `−N_i(u) n` for control point `i` (`crates/solve/src/spline.rs`; a unit test holds it against central differences of the distance itself, which finds its foot anew, for degrees 1 to 7). So the row has the point and `degree + 1` control points in it, which is why a gradient now has room for 18 unknowns instead of 8 (`dual::SLOTS`; the solver's timings did not move: 87 against 92 ms for 1602 unknowns). Degrees of freedom, redundancy and conflict come from the rank of the Jacobian like every other constraint's: one less per point on a spline; the same constraint twice, or an end control point on its own spline, is redundant; a point held elsewhere against a held spline is conflicting, with the distance as its residual. Two choices the developer may want different: beyond its ends a spline counts as going on straight along its first and last leg (as a point on a line is held on the whole line), and where the nearest point is a corner (a polyline's) the distance is the plain distance to that corner. A spline all on one spot has no direction: the constraint is reported undefined, the rest solves. Tests: `crates/solve/tests/splines.rs` (lands on the curve by an independent measure, 1e-3, 1 and 1e6, degrees 1 to 6; a spline pulled through a fixed point; dragged; redundant, conflicting, beyond the ends, bad input), `crates/engine/tests/sketch_spline.rs` › `a_point_is_held_on_a_spline` (through the commands, a control point moved by a variable, save and reopen).

**Reference geometry (2026-10-13, M8.5).** Datum planes, axes and points are features of the history that make no body (`doc/src/datum.rs`: `FeatureKind::DatumPlane`, `DatumAxis`, `DatumPoint`). Their result is where they lie (`regen::Datum` in `FeatureResult::datum`: a frame, a point with a unit direction, a point), worked out in `regen/src/datum.rs` from the results of the features before them, cached and compared like every other result, and shown by `document.inspect` per feature under `datum`.

**The range of the linear tolerance (2026-10-15).** `file.new` took any positive tolerance (1e-300 passed) and a file kept whatever it was saved with. A document's linear tolerance is now between `Tolerance::LEAST_LINEAR` (1e-12) and `GREATEST_LINEAR` (1e3) of its length unit: 1e-9 of the smallest and 1e-3 of the largest size the kernel is built and tested for (1e-3 to 1e6 units, AGENTS.md rule 3). The size of the model is not known when the document is made, so the range is about the units the tolerance is written in, not about the model; within it, a tolerance too fine for the rounding at the model's size remains the user's choice. `file.new` refuses a value outside the range with the range and the unit in the message; `Document::validate` refuses it, so a document with one is not saved. *A file saved before* with a positive tolerance outside the range is read as the nearer end of it (`Tolerance::in_range`, in `format`), and `file.open` and `recovery.open` report that under `warnings`: refusing would lock the user out of a design over a value only the old bug could produce, and the result of regenerating at 1e-300 or 1e4 was not meaningful to keep. Zero, negative and non-finite tolerances are still refused on reading. The angular tolerance is unchanged (positive and finite; no command sets it). Tests: `crates/doc` › `validation_accepts_built_documents_and_rejects_broken_ones`, `crates/format/tests/format.rs` › `a_tolerance_outside_the_range_is_read_as_its_nearer_end`, `crates/engine/tests/commands.rs` (the refusals).

- *What a datum is placed by* is one of three small reference types, the same in the document, the file and the commands. A plane (`PlaneRef`): a base plane, a flat face by its persistent name (its frame as a sketch on that face has it, the normal out of the body), or a datum plane by feature id. A line (`AxisRef`): a world axis, a datum axis, or **the straight edge between two faces of one body, by the names of the two faces** (`regen::edges_between`; the pieces a boolean cut an edge into are one line, edges on different lines are refused). A point (`PointRef`): coordinates (three expressions), a datum point, a vertex by its persistent name, or the centre of the circular edge between two faces. No second naming scheme: faces by their names, edges by the faces they lie between, vertices by the names the features give them (primitives, sweeps and their copies by where they came from; after a boolean, and in a pattern whose instances are joined, by the faces that meet in them, `F7:corner[…]`, and by the name an operand gave them, as an alias: *Edges and vertices by their faces* below). `datum.references` lists, for one body, the vertices (with their aliases), the edges with their own names and their two faces, and the faces that have an axis, each with the name to use.
- *Planes* (`PlaneBy`): `Offset { from, distance }` keeps the origin and axes of `from`, moved along its normal; `Angle { from, about, angle }` is `from` turned about `about` counter-clockwise seen from the tip of the axis, **through the axis**, which has to be parallel to `from` (an edge of the face, or a line beside the plane; anything else is refused with the angle it makes); `ThreePoints` has its origin at the first point, x towards the second and the three counter-clockwise round the normal.
- *Axes* (`AxisBy`): `TwoPoints` (from the first to the second), `Edge { between }`, `Face` (the axis of a cylinder, cone or torus face, along the surface's own axis), `TwoPlanes` (along the cross product of the normals in the order given, through the point of the line nearest the first plane's origin; parallel planes are refused with how far apart they are). Which way the kernel runs an edge is not something a name says, so between two flat faces an edge axis points along the cross product of their outward normals in the order the faces are given; between other faces it has the direction of the edge's curve.
- *Where a datum is used.* A sketch lies on one (`SketchPlane::Datum { plane, offset }`: the datum's origin and axes); a mirror reflects in one (`Mirror::plane`); a revolution turns about one (`RevolveAxis::Datum`, which has to lie in the sketch's plane: off it or leaning out of it is refused with by how much); a circular pattern goes round one (`PatternLayout::Circular::about`); a datum plane is turned about one and a plane or axis goes through datum points. The commands take them by feature id or name in the parameter that was there (`plane` of `sketch.create`/`sketch.edit`/`feature.mirror`, `axis` of `feature.revolve`/`feature.pattern`), beside what they took before. A mirror and a pattern keep their own point, normal and axis unused, so that giving them again goes back.
- *Failure.* Every reference is found again at each regeneration. One that points at nothing fails the datum with the parameter, the feature and the name it lost (``from: `Base` no longer has a face F1:face(9); its faces are: …``); what is placed by a failed, suppressed, rolled back or later datum fails with the datum's name (``plane: `Shelf` failed; fix it first``); nothing else is touched. A datum is repaired by giving it another definition (`datum.plane` … with `replace`), which gives what building it that way from the start gives. Cycles cannot be built: a datum placed by a later feature fails as "comes after this feature".
- *Not built:* an axis or centre from an elliptic or freeform edge; a plane tangent to a face, normal to a curve, or midway between two planes; a transform about a datum axis; drawing datums in the viewport.
- *Tests:* `crates/engine/tests/datums.rs` (boxes, discs and rings on offset, angled and three-point planes at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6 against closed forms, with their boxes and centres; mirrors, revolves and patterns about datums; upstream edits; lost references and repair; save and reopen; everything that is refused), `crates/regen/tests/datums.rs` (documents no command builds: cycles, datums of the wrong kind, the cache), `crates/format/tests/format.rs` (the file).

#### 4.2 Appearance and material per body (2026-10-15), colour per face (2026-10-21)

A colour (`Appearance`: sRGB, and `metallic` and `roughness` from 0 to 1) and a material (`Material`: a name and a density in kg/m³) are given to the body of a feature (`body.appearance`, `body.material`) and kept in `Document::bodies`, a list by feature id beside the history (`crates/doc/src/appearance.rs`). They are not features: regeneration does not read them, an edit of a feature's parameters keeps them (the id stays), deleting the feature drops its entry, undo and redo treat them as any edit. Saved in `.3dcraft` from format 16 ([`file-formats.md`](../file-formats.md)).

**How they follow a body** (`crates/engine/src/appearance.rs`), a rule chosen so that a cutaway stays readable:

- *A body* has the appearance and the material of the feature that makes it, when that feature has one; otherwise those of the body it was made from as instance 0 (a boolean's target, the body a fillet rounds, a shell hollows, a pattern repeats or a transform moves), and so on back. So a cut keeps the colour of the plate it cut, a fillet does not turn a red part grey, and a material given to the first block of a part is the material of the finished part.
- *A face* has its body's colour, unless it came into the body as part of another body with a colour of its own (the developer's decision of 2026-10-15, as SolidWorks, Fusion and Onshape colour a cut). Its way back is the one its persistent name records (§4.1): `F7:copy(1)/F3:side(e2)` came through instance 1 of F7 (the tool) from F3; `F9:copy(0)/F2:face(1)` went through instance 0 of F9 and of every feature before it until F2. The first feature on that way with an appearance of its own gives the face its colour; a step through the tool of a subtraction (whose faces bound the target's material there, turned inside out) ends the way at the body there, the target's. So the wall a blue drill cut into a red plate is red, and a blue boss united with a red plate keeps its blue faces (an intersection likewise). Until 2026-10-15 the cut wall kept the tool's colour. A face a feature made itself (a fillet's round, a chamfer's flat) has the body's colour at that feature. A face whose way meets no appearance has the body's; a body without one is drawn in the theme's colour.
- *Setting a colour on a later feature* colours its whole body, tool faces included: the nearest feature wins.
- *A face given a colour of its own* (`face.appearance`, 2026-10-21) is kept in `Document::faces`, by the feature whose body has the face and the face's persistent name there (`crates/doc/src/appearance.rs`; one entry per name, so the pieces of a face cut in two, which share its name, are one entry). The walk above looks at each step for an entry of the name the face has in that step's body (the copy along the instances still to go, or the name itself) before that body's own appearance: the nearest feature on the way wins, a face over its body at the same feature. So a face coloured in the seed of a pattern is coloured in every instance and in the bodies made from those, one instance's face can be given a colour of its own, a face coloured before a cut keeps it on both pieces, and an upstream edit that keeps the face's name keeps its colour. A face of the tool of a subtraction ends the walk at the target as before (a coloured face of a drill does not colour the hole it cut). A colour given to a later feature's body goes before a face colour of an earlier one (`a_body_colour_on_a_later_feature_goes_before_an_earlier_face_colour`). A face answers to its own name and its aliases (faces of several instances or operands merged into one, names.md): an entry of any of them colours it, its own name's first, then the aliases' in their order (the target's or the first instance's first), at the body's feature and at every step of the walk; so instance 1's top coloured green stays green when an edit merges it into instance 0's top, and is not lost (2026-10-22, review).
- *A face that is gone* (an edit took it away: no face of the built body answers to the name, as its own or as an alias) keeps its entry: it colours nothing, never another face, and is said (`lostFaces` in `document.inspect` and in `face.appearance`'s reply, one problem line per entry in the scene, which the window shows); undoing the edit brings the face and its colour back, and `face.appearance` with `clear` takes the entry away by its name. An entry of a feature that did not build (failed, suppressed, rolled back) waits. Deleting the feature drops its entries. Not dropping them at the edit is deliberate: the regeneration that finds the face gone is not an edit, and a removal made then would be one behind the history's back (rule 2).
- The material is per body only (a mass needs one density): the instance-0 rule, so a union of a steel and a brass part weighs as steel. Per-face materials, and a mass per piece of a body made of several materials, are not modelled.

The colours reach every picture through `craft3d_render::FaceColors` (a colour per face id; §7.2): the scene's (`Scene::face_colors`, built with the meshes), `view.render`'s, `view.replay`'s per step; and the exports: 3MF base materials per object and per triangle (a body without a colour whose faces have some has the pictures' grey, `RenderParams::default().color`, as its object's material, since a triangle without a material of its own has its object's), STEP styled items per solid and per face ([`file-formats.md`](../file-formats.md)). `document.inspect` and `document.measure` report each body's appearance and material, and with a material its mass and its inertia tensor about the centroid (`craft3d_topo::inertia`: the second moments by the divergence theorem on the exact B-rep, summed about the middle of the body and moved to the centroid; checked against closed forms for a block, a cylinder, a sphere and a torus, tilted and off the origin, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e3 in `crates/topo/tests/inertia.rs`, and through the commands in `crates/engine/tests/appearance.rs`).

*Setting them costs nothing (2026-10-15).* `body.appearance` and `body.material` change `Document::bodies` and nothing regeneration reads, so the regeneration after them, and after undoing or redoing them, rebuilds no feature and checks none (`colours_and_materials_rebuild_nothing` counts it). `body.material` says which bodies have the material and from where; it measures nothing. It used to report each body's mass and inertia as well, and the exact measure of a body of spline faces takes seconds: on the impeller (99 faces, 62 of them freeform) 35 to 44 s of CPU (4 to 11 s on fourteen threads), for one command that only names a material. The mass and the inertia are measured when `document.inspect`, `document.measure` or `document.check` asks, once per body (`engine/src/mass.rs`), and both at once for a body with a material (`topo::mass_and_inertia`): the faces are integrated once with the second moments, and the area, volume and moments are taken at the doubling where they agree, settled with a copy of the work spent so far, which is what `mass_properties` alone does, bit for bit (`mass_and_inertia_at_once_are_each_bit_for_bit_what_they_are_alone`, on analytic and freeform reference solids at two scales). One measure of both costs about what the inertia alone did: on the impeller about 20 s of CPU (in `document.check`, the first to ask) where the mass and then the inertia took 35 to 44 s. `colours_and_materials_measure_nothing` counts the measures (`mass::fresh_measures`).

#### 5.4 Sketch solver (`solve`)

A 2D geometric constraint solver: entities become unknowns, constraints become equations, solved numerically (Newton-type with a rank-revealing step) from the stored previous solution. It reports degrees of freedom, which constraints are redundant or conflicting, and never loops forever (iteration cap → `DidNotConverge`). Dragging (later, with the UI) is the same solve with a soft target. Decomposition into small sub-problems is an optimisation for later; correctness and diagnostics come first.

As built (M5): the unknowns are point coordinates and circle radii; gradients are exact (forward-mode dual numbers); the Jacobian is factorised row by row in the order the constraints were added, so a row that depends on earlier ones marks its constraint as redundant (it holds) or conflicting (it does not). When the whole system does not converge, constraints are added one at a time from the solution of the earlier ones, and one that cannot be added is blamed. That diagnosis is this solver's verdict, not a proof. About 8% of dense random sketches (up to 40 arbitrary constraints) end as "did not converge" instead of a diagnosis (measured at M5). A constraint that has no value where the others put the sketch (an angle on a line that shrank to a point) is conflicting too, with the reason. `solve` repeats its pass from its own result until nothing changes, so `Ok` means that solving again gives the same coordinates, bit for bit, and the same verdict. `regen::settle_sketches` still does not store a solution with a conflict in it: a collapsed line no longer says which way it pointed, and the stored shape is what brings the sketch back on the same side. Steps are minimum-norm with a line search, so free geometry moves as little as possible and mirror choices (which side of a line, inside or outside a circle) follow the starting point. An angle is signed: counter-clockwise from the first line's direction to the second's.

#### Sketch coordinates as expressions (2026-10-22, format 29)

The R1 acceptance found that pinning a sketch corner to variables cost a fixed origin point plus two distance constraints per corner, the distances unsigned (the side taken from where the point was drawn): 32 steps for the phone stand's ten corners. Two constraint kinds were added, `x` and `y`: one point's signed coordinate along the sketch axis, an expression (`ConstraintKind::X`/`Y`; in the solver `CoordinateX`/`CoordinateY`, one row `p.x - value`). They are constraints like the others: stored in the history, evaluated at every regeneration (so they follow the variables), edited by `sketch.edit`, reported by `sketch.inspect`, blamed as redundant or conflicting in their turn. The commands write them: a new point given as `[x, y]` with an expression in it (a string; `sketch.point`, `line`, `polyline`, `arc`, `path`, `spline` control points and ends, and `sketch.rect`) is drawn at the values the expressions have now and held there by an `x` and a `y` constraint. A number beside an expression is held at that number, because `["-width / 2", 0]` reads as a place, not a guess along y; two numbers are only where the point is drawn, as before, so no older script changes meaning. A circle's radius given as an expression is a radius constraint. `sketch.rect` adds four lines kept horizontal and vertical, so two held corners define it. A held arc end of `sketch.path` is not snapped onto the circle through the start, as an end given as numbers is: its constraints would pull it back and the solver would move the centre or the start instead; it is drawn as given and the path warns. A number held beside an expression is written in its shortest exact form (`1e300`, not 301 digits). The expressions of one command are evaluated against one evaluation of the variables. Not held: a point a spline passes through between its ends (it is fitted to a control point, which an expression could not hold; refused), an arc's `through` point and a path arc's `radius` (construction data, not points). Tests: `crates/engine/tests/sketch_held.rs`, `crates/solve/tests/constraints.rs` (`alone_coordinates_are_signed`).
