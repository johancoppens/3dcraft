# 3DCraft design: Geometry and intersections

Curves, surfaces and their intersections (`craft3d-geom`, with `craft3d-math` beneath it). Method 1 of the map ([`../architecture.md`](../architecture.md) §5).

## Method

**The kinds.** `Curve3` is a line, circle, ellipse or NURBS curve; `Surface` is a plane, cylinder, cone, sphere, torus, Bézier patch or NURBS surface. Analytic kinds stay analytic, so results stay exact where they can; NURBS, rational ones included, are first-class everywhere. Every kind evaluates with first derivatives and normals, knows its parameter domain and periodicity, and projects a point onto itself: in closed form for the analytic kinds, for freeform surfaces by a search from a coarse grid followed by damped Gauss–Newton steps (`Surface::project`, or `project_from` when a nearby foot is known). The kinds of the first plan that are not built (trimmed, offset and intersection curves; extrusion, revolution and offset surfaces; parameter-space curves) are needed by nothing yet: a curve on a surface is recovered by projection.

**Splines.** NURBS are evaluated by de Boor's algorithm on homogeneous points. `refine` changes a description without changing the curve (knot insertion, degree elevation) and interpolates (a banded, totally positive collocation solve, linear in the samples); `extend` grows a surface past its domain without moving any point of it; `pole` finds a border of a patch that is one point; `closure` finds a surface whose control net closes on itself on clamped knots (`geom` gives it no period). Bounds on the first and second derivatives and on the normal come from Bézier nets (for rational ones by the quotient rule over the lightest weight) and are proofs, not samples.

**Intersections.** `meet_surfaces(a, b, within, tolerance)` intersects any two surfaces inside a box and returns nothing, `Coincident`, or curves and isolated touch points. Each curve has the range meant and flags for closed, tangent and exact; an approximate curve carries its deviation, at most the tolerance. The pair is put in a fixed order first, so the answer does not depend on the order asked. Curves never cross except at their ends: where two branches cross, each arm is a curve of its own.

- *Closed forms* wherever one exists: planes, quadrics and tori in the configurations of the table below (coaxial surfaces, sections of a cone, equal cylinders that cross, cones along a ruling, a cylinder or a torus that goes on in a torus).
- *Everything else is marched* (`intersect/march.rs`). One surface is walked in its parameters, the other is a signed distance. A quadtree discards the cells the distance proves empty and stops where a bound on the second derivatives proves the distance monotonic; a cell that reaches the floor (twice the tolerance) without either is searched for a point of tangency, which the Hessian of the distance classifies: a touch (a minimum or maximum), a crossing (a saddle), a tacnode or a cusp (rank one, by the splitting lemma, the arms then made as graphs near the point), else refused. Only the cells whose image can be inside the box are looked at, and a march that cannot go on outside the box ends there. Branches are marched with the step held by the turn of the tangent and by how far it goes (between half and twice what was asked) and fitted by cubic Hermite splines, refined until they are within half the tolerance of both surfaces at three places per span; the pieces the box cuts are refined again; two traces of one branch are one branch.
- *A curve against a surface* (`intersect_curve_surface`): closed forms for the analytic pairs, otherwise root isolation on the signed distance along the curve, with certificates of the same kind in one dimension.

**Proven bounds** (`certify`, since 2026-10-16). Two questions the kernel used to answer at samples are answered by proof. *How far a curve strays from a surface* over an interval of its parameter (`SurfaceBound::curve_within`): over each piece the curve is compared with the surface along the cubic Hermite path in the surface's parameters from the foot of one end to the foot of the other, leaving each along the curve's tangent projected into the tangent plane; any path bounds the distance from above, so how well the feet converged changes how tight the bound is, never whether it holds. The error is the cubic Hermite remainder, `max |e(end)| + ¼ max |e'(end)| + max |e⁗| / 384` (the Peano kernel keeps its sign, so it holds for vectors), with `e⁗` bounded by majorants: the surface's partial derivatives over the box the path sweeps (closed form for the analytic kinds; the differences of the Bézier net cut to the box for splines, rational ones through `A / w`) composed with the path's derivative bounds (Faà di Bruno, each term replaced by its bound). It falls with the fourth power of the piece, as a fitted curve's error does. Where the box crosses a knot line the Lipschitz bound is used. Distances are to the surface *continued* past its domain, as it evaluates (a spline's end patches go on as their polynomials, the analytic kinds are unbounded): a proof says the curve is near that surface, not that it stays inside a face or the spline's domain (a line five units outside a Bézier square, in the plane the square continues into, is proven on it). The rounding allowance scales with what enters the evaluations (the control net times its weight spread; an analytic kind's origin, radii and parameters). Analytic curves start in eight pieces, every starting foot is followed from the one before through points in between, and a middle's foot is the nearer of the two found from both ends' feet (one poor foot spoiled every halving below it: a circle at a latitude of a sphere over most of a turn was never proven). Pieces start at the curve's knots and the caller's breaks and are halved until each is within the target or the pieces allowed are spent: "not proven, here", never a guess. *Whether two curves keep apart* (`curves_apart`): pieces held in capsules round their chords (radius `max |C''| / 8`), a tree of boxes round them, pairs split from the roots down until their boxes or capsules are further apart than the threshold or two points of the curves are seen within it (every piece against every piece spent any budget on two borders of 90 knot spans each); for nearly parallel chords, whose closest points come through a difference that cancels, `√ε` of their lengths is allowed. Tests: `certify.rs` (curves fitted on every surface kind at three scales proven, the bound above every one of 4001 samples; a curve on the surface at its fit points and middles but 1e-5 off between them not proven at 1e-6; a bow towards a line seen close or proven apart, never the wrong way; lines, circles, an ellipse and rational spline circles a known distance from every analytic kind, at 1e-2 and 1e-5: every bound at least the exact distance, 0.95 of it never proven).

**What is refused**, as an error and never partially: a degenerate contact whose sign changes on every ring around it, surfaces tangent or coincident over an area (more than a million cells), curves that need more points than the caps allow, a fit that does not reach the tolerance, a tolerance finer than rounding allows at the size of the box. Some steps are checked by samples rather than bounds (the rings around a tangency, the tacnode model, the curvature of a freeform field); the map lists them as structural debt.

**Portable maths** (since 2026-10-31). Every sine, cosine, arc tangent, exponential, logarithm, power, cube root and hypotenuse in the workspace goes through `craft3d_math::Portable` (`x.psin()`, `y.patan2(x)`, `b.ppowf(e)`, ...), which calls the pure-Rust `libm` crate (MIT, a port of musl): plain Rust arithmetic, so the same bits on every platform, wasm included. The standard library's methods call the platform's C library (glibc, Apple's libm, the UCRT), which agree only to within an ulp, and one ulp is enough to flip a branch in the kernel, so the same document regenerated to different bodies on macOS, Windows and Linux (rule 5). `powi` is written out in a fixed order (the standard's leaves the order of its multiplications to the compiler); `mul_add` goes through `libm::fma`. `sqrt`, `abs`, `floor`, `min` and the like are exact under IEEE 754 and stay the standard's. Clippy's `disallowed-methods` (`clippy.toml`) refuses the standard's transcendental methods for `f64` and `f32` in every crate, tests included; the standalone format crates (`drawing`, `font`) call `libm` directly. Using `libm` was decided by the developer ([`../decisions.md`](../decisions.md), 2026-10-31). The contract is `craft3d_math::portable_fingerprint`, a hash of every portable function's bits over a fixed sweep (a NaN hashed as one NaN, since its sign and payload are the hardware's). Its value is pinned in a test, so a platform whose build computes different bits fails; it was measured the same on x86_64 Linux and on wasm32 in Node, debug and release (2026-10-31: a cdylib exporting it, run with `WebAssembly.instantiate`). A second test, on glibc only, holds the portable functions within two ulps of the platform's over the sweep (four for `tanh`), so the switch moved results by rounding only; Apple's library and the UCRT are not a contract. The GPU's shaders are outside this: GPU parity is held by tolerances.

**Memory.** Projectors (the grid a freeform search starts from) and numeric intersections are remembered by the exact bits of what was asked, so the union, difference and intersection of the same bodies, and a boolean done again, compute once; the answers are the same.

### From *Current state (2026-10-12)*

- **Maths:** `math` has `Vec3`, `Bbox3`, `Frame` (placement of analytic geometry), `Transform` (affine, with inverse), `Tolerance` (linear and angular, as data) and Gauss–Legendre quadrature. Not built: robust orientation predicates (first needed by booleans, M7), intervals, root finding.
- **Geometry:** `geom` has the `Curve3` enum (line, circle, ellipse, NURBS) and the `Surface` enum (plane, cylinder, cone, sphere, torus, Bézier patch, NURBS), each with evaluation, first derivatives, parameter domain and periodicity, normals, and closest-point projection (closed form for the analytic kinds, sampled Newton / Gauss–Newton for ellipses and freeform). NURBS are de Boor on homogeneous points with hodograph derivatives. Not built: 2D parameter-space curves, trimmed and offset curves, extrusion/revolution/offset surfaces, second derivatives. `geom::intersect` intersects every pair of surface kinds (`meet_surfaces`, inside a box) and every curve kind with every surface kind (`intersect_curve_surface`): in closed form where one exists, numerically otherwise, within the tolerance or an error (§5.1 › Intersections as built).

#### 5.1 Geometry (`geom`)

*The plan's enums. As built, `Curve3` is `Line`, `Circle`, `Ellipse`, `Nurbs` and `Surface` is `Plane`, `Cylinder`, `Cone`, `Sphere`, `Torus`, `Bezier`, `Nurbs`; `Trimmed`, `Offset`, `SurfaceIso` and `Intersection` curves, `Curve2` parameter-space curves and `Extrusion`, `Revolution` and `Offset` surfaces are (planned).*

```rust
pub enum Curve3 { Line(..), Circle(..), Ellipse(..), Nurbs(..), Trimmed(..), Offset(..), SurfaceIso(..), Intersection(..) }
pub enum Curve2 { Line(..), Circle(..), Ellipse(..), Nurbs(..) }           // parameter-space curves on a surface
pub enum Surface { Plane(..), Cylinder(..), Cone(..), Sphere(..), Torus(..), Extrusion(..), Revolution(..), Nurbs(..), Offset(..) }
```

Every kind implements evaluation with derivatives, parameter range and periodicity, bounding box, closest-point projection and inversion, and reversal. Projection as built (`Surface::project`): closed form for the analytic kinds; for Bézier and NURBS surfaces a search from the samples of a 16 × 16 grid that are nearer than their neighbours (and from a four times closer look around the nearest), each followed downhill by a damped Gauss–Newton iteration that puts the curving of the surface in when its steps fall short (Newton). It walks across the seam of a surface that closes on itself, half-steps towards a border that is a single point (a pole, a collapsed patch edge) and slides along a border of an open patch. The answer is a local nearest point, and the nearest one for a point closer to the surface than the surface curves, on a surface whose features are larger than a sixteenth of its domain (`crates/geom/tests/project.rs`). Intersections (curve/curve, curve/surface, surface/surface) are dispatched on the pair of kinds: closed-form where a formula exists (plane/plane, plane/cylinder, …), numeric marching with a tolerance otherwise. A pair that is not implemented returns `Unsupported`, and the test suite lists which pairs are covered.

##### Intersections as built (2026-10-13)

`geom::intersect::meet_surfaces(a, b, within, tolerance)` returns `None`, `Coincident { same_normal }` or `Meets(Meeting { curves, points })` for any two surfaces. Each `MeetingCurve` has the curve, the `range` of it that is meant, and the flags `closed`, `tangent` (the surfaces touch along it without crossing) and `exact`; an approximate curve carries a `deviation` that bounds its distance to both surfaces and is at most the linear tolerance. `points` are isolated touches. Curves are clipped to the box `within`, which is what makes a hyperbola or the curve of two crossing cylinders finite. The two surfaces are put in a fixed order first, so `meet(a, b)` and `meet(b, a)` are the same computation and the same result, bit for bit. **Curves never cross each other except at their ends:** where two branches cross (the surfaces are tangent there: two equal cylinders at right angles, a bitangent plane on a torus, Viviani's curve) every arm is a curve of its own that ends on the crossing point. `intersect_surfaces` (planes and cylinders, no box) is unchanged and still what `kernel::boolean` calls.

**The numeric method** (`intersect/march.rs`; the module comment has the argument in full). One surface is walked in its parameters, the other is a signed distance (closed form for the analytic kinds, closest point for freeform surfaces), and the intersection is the zero set of that distance over the first surface's parameter rectangle. A quadtree discards a cell when the distance at its centre exceeds what the surface can cover within the cell (a distance is 1-Lipschitz), and stops dividing when a bound on the second derivatives shows the distance to be monotonic along u or along v over the whole cell: such a cell holds no tangent point, and a branch through it is detected by the signs at its corners. Cells that reach the size of the tolerance without either holding are searched by Newton iteration for the point where the surfaces are tangent: a minimum or maximum of the distance is a touch point, a saddle is a crossing of two branches, anything flatter is refused. The same search runs earlier, as soon as cells keep failing both tests (around a point of tangency the gradient is small over an area that grows with the looseness of the bound, so thousands of cells per level fail there): where it finds the point and the gradient follows the Hessian's second-order model within a quarter on two rings around it, the disc inside the rings is settled without cells: it holds the touch, the two branches through the crossing (seeded along the directions in which the Hessian vanishes), or, around a critical point off the other surface with the distance of the sign its Hessian keeps, nothing. A touch is only recorded where the surfaces are within the tolerance and within a floor cell's reach of each other, which is where the cells alone would have found it. So no loop larger than a floor cell (twice the linear tolerance across) is missed, provided the derivative bounds and the rings hold: the bounds are proofs for analytic surfaces, Bézier patches and NURBS surfaces, rational ones included (since 2026-10-13: `RationalBezierPatch::derivative_bounds`, over the pieces of each span quartered twice), and estimates from samples for the curvature of freeform surfaces used as the distance (taken from how fast the normal turns on a grid near the point, doubled twice as margin); the rings are samples. Branches are marched with the step limited by the turn of the tangent, end where they close, leave the rectangle, run off the edge of the other surface or reach a crossing point or apex (a crossing point is looked for two steps ahead and from the radius at which its arms are seeded, since 2026-10-14: where the surfaces are a tenth of a tolerance apart at it, `f = 0` turns away just before the point, and a step that landed there went past it and on along the next arm, which was then drawn twice), and are fitted by a cubic Hermite spline that is refined until it is within half the tolerance of both surfaces and of the intersection itself at three places per span. Where the box cuts a branch, the pieces it keeps are refined again (since 2026-10-15): the span to the new end is a new cubic.

A contact where the Hessian of the distance is degenerate (the surfaces touch along a curve, or to more than second order) is a touch when the distance keeps one sign on three rings of samples around it: the surfaces do not cross there, so nothing is divided. It is judged on the valley across the place where the search for it stopped (since 2026-10-18, *A contact along a curve judged on its valley* below). Where such a contact has a closed form it never reaches the marcher: cones along a ruling, and a cylinder that goes on in a torus or a torus in a torus, where the distance does change sign along the circle of contact (at the two points where the second curve crosses it), are in the table above. **Refused, as errors:** such a contact where the sign changes on every ring (`Unsupported`); surfaces nearly tangent or coincident over an area (more than a million cells of the quadtree; 400,000 until 2026-10-14, when two pairs of a flat face on the tangent plane of a gently curved roof needed 401,264 and 440,000), a curve that needs more points or evaluations than the caps allow, a fit that does not reach the tolerance, a tolerance finer than rounding allows at the size of the box (`NoConvergence` or `InvalidParameter`). Nothing partial is returned.

**Tacnodes and cusps (2026-10-15, `intersect/march/tacnode.rs`).** Where the Hessian has rank one, `f` is `λ/2 s² + g(t)` after a change of coordinates (the splitting lemma): `s` across, along the eigenvector of the eigenvalue `λ`, `t` along the direction where it vanishes, and `g` the value of `f` along its *valley* (where the derivative across is zero). On a side of the point where `g` has the sign opposite to `λ` the zero set is two curves, one either side of the valley; where it has the sign of `λ`, none. So the point has four arms that leave it two by two in the same direction (a tacnode: a cylinder inside a torus' tube touching its top, `g ∝ t⁴`), two (a cusp, `g ∝ t³`), or none (a touch). The Newton search for the point took no step where the Hessian is flat; now it goes on along the valley (Newton across with the Hessian's `λ`, bisection along on the sign of the derivative along), and the point it finds there is used for the arms only: where none are made, the search's own point goes on to the touch test as before (a contact along a curve, such as a swept body's pieces joined, is left as it was). Where the point lies along the arms is ill-conditioned (the surfaces are within `t⁴` of each other along the valley): it is on both surfaces within the tolerance, and held within 64 tolerances of the true point (the root of the derivative along the valley, which grows as `t³`, is found only to the cube root of the rounding of the gradient: measured 9 tolerances off at scale 1e-3). A stationary search that ends anywhere on the valley within the reach where its gradient stays below what counts as tangent finds this point again, and a touch found there before the point is dropped. The arms are tangent to each other and closer than the tolerance for a while, where neither cells nor a march tell them apart; inside a disc of radius `Z` around the point they are *made*, as graphs over `t`: on the line across at `t` the valley by Newton, and each arm by bisection from the valley outwards (to the bracket's width, not to `|f| ≤ on`, which near the point can be far from the arm). `Z` is the smallest radius (doubling from eight floor cells) at which the arms at the rim are 64 tolerances apart and `g` there is clear of zero by four times `|f|` at the point; the model is checked on 32 lines across per side out to `2Z`, by samples and not by a bound (the derivative across has the sign that takes `f` away from the valley at four places a line, `g` is of one sign, the arms are within 0.15 of the direction along out to `1.25 Z`; a bound would need one on the third derivatives of `f`, which the chart's bounds do not give), else no tacnode is made and the contact goes on to the touch test and the refusal. The disc keeps cells out; seeds on the four arms at the rim are marched outwards with steps of at most a tenth of the distance to the point (so a step does not land on the other arm), a branch that comes within `1.25 Z` of the point ends on it with the tangent along when it is where the arms were verified (`|t| ≤ 2Z`, `|s| ≤ Z`) or on an arm (`tac_admits`; one that only passes near goes on, `a_branch_that_passes_near_a_tacnode_is_not_one_of_its_arms`) (the fit would otherwise make the arms leave a little apart, in an order of their own, and the regions would sort them wrongly), seeds the cells found inside the disc are not marched, and the refinement of a span inside `2Z` takes its middle from the graphs. Where the surfaces are a little apart at the point (`f = δ` there, within the tolerance), the arms are those of `f − δ (1 − (t/Z)⁴)²` inside the disc: that has the contact itself at the point, equals `f` from the rim on, and its arms are within `|δ|` of both surfaces, as a crossing a little apart is drawn to its point. Measured: `meet_numeric.rs` › `a_cylinder_touching_the_top_of_a_torus_tube_from_inside_meets_it_in_a_tacnode` (cylinders of a half to a tenth of the tube, lifted out or sunk in by three tenths of the tolerance, three scales: two loops with four ends on one point, within four tolerances of the exact curve) and the boolean in `boolean_contact.rs` (below); the cusp `x² − y³` against its tangent plane (`a_degenerate_contact_that_crosses_in_a_cusp_is_not_a_touch`) is such a contact with arms on one side.

**Two traces of one branch (2026-10-15, `intersect/march.rs` › `united`).** A seed that lies on a branch already traced starts nothing; one just past its end (half a tolerance) neither. Where a branch runs within the tolerance of a border of the chart (a curve that leaves a pole along the border, within `y² / 10R` of it), the march stops on the border wherever rounding puts it along that stretch, and a seed further along it traced the whole branch again: two curves along each other, which the boolean took for two (`boolean_poles.rs`, the octant cut through its pole by a block's edge, once each pair of faces was intersected in its own box; [`booleans.md`](booleans.md) › *The box of the two faces*). After each trace the new branch is compared with those found: when the quarter points (by chord length) of either lie on the other's curve within the tolerance (its cubic spans, searched: not its chords, which stray from the curve by more than two branches near each other are apart), they are one branch, the first with what the second reaches beyond either of its ends (an end of the first that lies on the second is one the second runs past, and is left out: it was where the march met the border, not where the curve ends), refined at the joints as any span. A second trace that reaches past neither end of the first is kept as a branch of its own: its seed was not on the first (the seed test is the looser one), so it is another branch that stays within the tolerance of the first all its short length (an arm leaving a point of contact along the first, cut short by the box). Closed branches are left to the seed test. *Held to:* `boolean_poles.rs` › `a_plane_along_the_border_at_the_pole_meets_the_patch_once` (two curves in some of its 40 boxes before), `meet_fixes.rs` › `the_cones_of_two_neighbouring_blades_meet_in_curves_on_both` (the turbofan's benchmark: two curves of the cones of neighbouring blades, taken for one against chords, were joined across 25 mm while branches were stopped at the box, an attempt dropped the same day; with branches followed past the box the chords do not meet that case, and the test is a guard).

**Only the box is walked (2026-10-15, `intersect/march.rs` › `seeds`; the bike's finding B9).** A top tube lofted through an oval and two circles less its core (the same loft 1.2 to 3 mm inside it) was refused, "the surfaces are tangent or nearly coincident over too large an area". The tube's tapered face (a ruled rational patch 325 mm long) and the core's cylinder were asked in the 12 mm box where both faces are, at the oval where the taper begins, where they are 1.2 mm apart. But the chart of a freeform surface is the whole patch wherever the box is (those of a torus and a sphere are the whole surface, and one cut to the box, a plane's, a cylinder's or a cone's, still reaches out of it at its corners), and the cylinder runs on inside the taper and comes out through it 300 mm further along at a shallow angle, where the cells needed to tell the two apart ran past the cap. The distance the image of a cell moves from that of its centre is at most `Lu hu + Lv hv`, the bound that already proves cells empty; a cell whose centre's image is further than that (and a tolerance) outside the box holds nothing of the surface inside the box and is left out. *Unproven:* a branch that came into a kept cell from one left out and went back out across the same side between two nodes would show no change of sign there and be missed. No case of it has been found: a search of such sides along their length, as the chart's borders are, gave the same results on 612 cases tried (a spline patch dipping into the box against cylinders and spheres, many radii, depths and scales) and was taken out rather than kept unproven. ### Proofs from a pole and on a closed spline's seam (2026-10-21, `certify.rs`)

`curve_on_surface` did not prove a curve that starts at a singular point of the parametrisation (a meridian from a sphere's pole, a ruling from a cone's apex): the foot there has no longitude, Gauss-Newton steps from it have no direction, and the feet either stayed at the pole or flew off along the singular border (`u = −5752`), so every bound carried the jump in `u`. Without a start given, a start on a spline's border is taken the same way (on a spline closed round, the projection of a seam point may name the end the curve does not go on from).
