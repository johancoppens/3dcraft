# 3DCraft design: Topology, checks and measures

The boundary representation and what is decided and measured on it (`craft3d-topo`), with the mesh-based checks of `craft3d-tess` that stand beside it. Methods 6 and 7 of the map ([`../architecture.md`](../architecture.md) §5).

## Method

**The B-rep.** A `Body` holds vectors of vertices, edges, loops (of coedges), faces and shells, referred to by `u32` ids; it is a solid (closed, oriented shells), a sheet or a wire. A face lies on a `Surface` and is to the left of its loops seen from outside (`reversed` turns its normal); an edge lies on a `Curve3` between two vertices; a coedge is the use of an edge by a loop, with a direction. Parameter-space curves are not stored; the document's tolerance is passed to every check, and a vertex or an edge may carry a looser one of its own (*Tolerant entities*, below). A body is shared whole (`Arc`), not part by part.

**Tolerant entities** (since 2026-10-21; the decision of 2026-10-18 in [`../decisions.md`](../decisions.md), option (4), a tolerance per entity). `Body::tolerances` is a sparse, ordered table (`topo::Tolerances`) of the vertices and edges that are looser than the document's tolerance; an entity not in it has the document's, and every body 3DCraft builds itself has an empty table (written out by `Bits` as nothing, so such a body is bit for bit what it was before). A vertex's tolerance is a ball the ends of its edges' curves lie in; an edge's is a tube the surfaces of its faces pass through along its whole curve. The tolerance that holds is the larger of the entity's and the document's, and a vertex is at least as loose as each edge that ends at it. A cap on any entity's tolerance is document data (`doc::Tolerance::loosest`, `None` = `topo::DEFAULT_LOOSEST` (1000) times the linear tolerance); above it the check reports the entity and an operation refuses. The API, for import and sewing:

- `Body::vertex_tolerance(v)`, `Body::edge_tolerance(e)`: the entity's own, `None` for the document's; `vertex_tolerance_at(v, linear)`, `edge_tolerance_at(e, linear)`: the one that holds.
- `Body::set_vertex_tolerance(v, t)`, `Body::set_edge_tolerance(e, t)` (`Result`, refuses a tolerance that is not positive and finite or an entity that does not exist; setting an edge raises its two vertices to at least `t`); `clear_vertex_tolerance`, `clear_edge_tolerance`.
- `Body::is_tolerant()`, `Body::working_tolerance(linear)`: the largest of the table and the document's, the tolerance the kernel works at on the body.
- `topo::measure_edge(body, e, tol, cap)`: how far the edge's curve is from the surface of each face that uses it, *proven* over the whole edge (`certify::curve_on_surface`, targets doubling from twice the farthest of five places, up to the cap); `topo::measure_vertex(body, v)`: how far the ends of its edges are, and at least their tolerances.
- `topo::record_edge(body, e, tol, cap)`, `topo::record_vertex(body, v, tol, cap)`: measure and put it in the table (`RECORD_MARGIN` = 1.125 larger, so that the same body moved still passes), nothing when it is within the document's; refused above the cap. Import records what it measures this way; it never moves geometry.
- `topo::check_capped(body, tol, cap)`, `topo::check_remembered_capped`: `check` with the document's cap (`check` uses the default, `craft3d_math::DEFAULT_LOOSEST` times the linear tolerance).
- `topo::record_vertices(body, vertices, tol, cap)`: `record_vertex` for many, in one pass over the edges. A gap within the cap is recorded at most at the cap: the margin never pushes it over (since 2026-10-22).

**Sewing** (`topo::sew(body, tol, distance, cap)`, since 2026-10-22; "merges topology and measures gaps; it never moves geometry"). Only *free* edges (used once by a loop) are sewn. Two free edges are a candidate pair when their end vertices are within `distance` of each other, either way round, each of `SAMPLES` (7) places inside each lies within `distance` of the other's curve (so two arcs between the same points, or an arc and its chord, are told apart), and the two uses run against each other, as the two uses of an edge of an oriented boundary do, by two different faces. The samples only choose candidates; acceptance is proven (below). Candidates are taken closest first (the larger of the gaps measured, then edge ids), each only while both edges are still free, joining its ends would not make one vertex of the two ends of any edge (sewing never collapses an edge), and neither edge has another candidate within the ambiguity margin, twice the largest gap any free edge has to its nearest candidate plus the tolerance (at most `distance`): an edge that could go either way (a slot narrower than the gaps) stays open and a solid with it is refused. The lower edge of a pair is kept with its curve; vertices joined through pairs are one, the lowest with its point; faces keep their ids; shells joined by a pair become one. Then, proven over each whole curve (`measure_curve`): the kept edge on both its faces' surfaces and the dropped edge on the same surfaces (its face's boundary is now the kept edge), the larger recorded on the kept edge; every joined vertex measured against the kept edges' ends, the dropped edges' ends and the points of the vertices merged into it. A gap beyond the document's tolerance is recorded, one beyond `cap` refuses (`SewError::Gap`, naming the entity and the distance). Free edges left are reported with the distance to the nearest free edge (`Sewn::open`, `worst_open`); a solid with one is the caller's to refuse. Free edges are found through a grid of their end vertices (cells `2 distance`, ordered maps); the ends looked at (`MAX_PROBES`, 2^27) and the pairs tested (`MAX_CANDIDATES`, 2^22) are capped, so thousands of edges at one point stay bounded. A body without free edges comes back as it is. `shells_alone` (and so `body.split`) keeps the tolerances of the entities it takes. **T-junctions** (since slice 3, `topo/src/sew_split.rs`): before pairs are sought, a free edge `A` along which two or more shorter free edges of other faces run end to end is split. Its *stations* are the places inside it (beyond the distance from both its ends) where an end of another face's free edge lies within the distance of its curve, one per place; the split is taken only when they are proven a chain: between each two neighbouring stations (and `A`'s ends) runs one free edge of another face with its ends there, its seven places inside within the distance of that stretch of `A` and the stretch's of it, its use against `A`'s, the ends of the two partners meeting at a station within the distance of each other, and no free edge runs along the whole of `A`. A piece keeps `A`'s curve over part of its range; the new vertex at a station is the curve's point there and goes into the shorter edges' ends (the lowest vertex's point is kept), so nothing moves and every gap is measured as any pair's is. The split stays only when every piece is then sewn; otherwise the edge is left whole and the body sewn again without it (at most twice). A split attempt that sewing refuses (a gap above the cap) or that leaves more edges open than sewing the body without any split is dropped, and the body is sewn as it is: a split never turns a sewing into a refusal, nor leaves more open (compared by count; the body is then sewn twice). `Sewn::split` lists each edge split with the edges its pieces became; STEP import counts them (`splitEdges`). Ends are kept in a grid of cells at least twice the distance and a sixty-fourth of their box wide (ordered maps, looked up by box); every cell and end looked at counts against `MAX_STATION_LOOKS` (2^24), and when that runs out nothing is split at all and sewing goes on as without T-junctions, never a refusal; an edge with more than `MAX_STATIONS` (1024) stations is left whole. *Conservative, left whole*: a seam of a closed curve that does not lie at a station (the stretch over the seam has no partner); an end of a third face touching `A`'s interior where no chain runs (a corner on the edge) adds a station whose stretches have no partner, so `A` stays whole and open, the solid refused.

**Validity** (`topo::check`): references in range, finite numbers, vertices on their edges, closed loops, edges on their faces' surfaces within the tolerance, every edge of a solid used once each way, Euler–Poincaré with the genus (`topo::genus`, which knows torus faces that keep a handle), per shell (since 2026-10-17: a shell's faces hang together through edges, no edge is in two shells, and in a solid of several shells each one fits Euler–Poincaré alone, `topo::shell_genera`; which shells are the outsides of lumps and which their cavities is geometry, `kernel::lumps`, [`booleans.md`](booleans.md) › *Lumps and cavities*), and on planes that a face's loops lie inside each other as a face's do (`topo::stranded`, since 2026-10-16: in the plane's frame turned to the outward normal, the other loops wind zero times around a point of an anticlockwise loop and once around a point of a clockwise one; the loops followed as polylines within 1e-4 of each conic's largest radius (bounded) and splines in 256 steps (their distance measured, not bounded), one point of each loop used, where it is clear of every other loop by that plus ten tolerances, so two loops that cross are found only where that point lies on the wrong side; each polyline keeps its box, so a face with many holes costs about a box test per pair of loops). For a sheet (since 2026-10-18): every edge is used once (an edge of its boundary) or once each way (else `EdgeUses`, `EdgeUsedTwiceSameWay`), no more than two boundary edges of a shell end at a vertex (`BoundaryPinch`: the boundary touches itself there), and each shell fits Euler–Poincaré with its boundary, `V − E + F − R − 2H = 2 (1 − G) − B` with `B` its loops of boundary edges (`topo::boundary_loops`; `ShellEuler`); two shells that share a vertex touch, which is geometry's to refuse, as for lumps. Since 2026-10-30 every face's loops must also bound a region in its parameters, on every surface (*Loops that bound a region*, below), and since 2026-11-14 each of them must be a simple closed curve, so that the face is its region counted once (*Loops that are simple*, below). It does not test self-intersection or that a face is bounded by its loops: those are the mesh checks below and the measure.

**Loops that bound a region** (`topo::region_fault`, `Defect::LoopsBoundNoRegion`, since 2026-10-30). Read in the surface's `(u, v)`, sampled per edge (a line in one step on a plane, two elsewhere, a conic in steps of at most π/8, a spline in eight; each step's middle projected too, its distance from the chord the polyline's error; a spline edge in eight times the least power of two such that every half step is shown to keep to what the walk can follow, by two things. (a) On a surface that closes about an axis, a cylinder, a cone, a sphere or a torus, its angle about the axis moves by less than a quarter turn over the half step, so the walk below goes round with it however many turns one edge makes: shown by the first of (1) the convex hull of the control points of the knot spans the half step meets in a wedge about the axis narrower than a quarter turn (then clear of the axis); (2) for a polynomial spline, the longest of its derivative's control points (a bound on its speed) times the half step's width over the least distance from the axis the stretch can have (its middle's less half that length), less than a quarter turn (one evaluation of the curve, counted against an operation's work, `geom::allowance`, only where (1) does not show it); (3) the Bézier pieces of the knot spans the half step meets, each made from its own span alone (`NurbsCurve::bezier_piece`: the degree's square, where the pieces of the whole curve, `bezier_pieces`, cost the square of its knots) and cut to the stretch by de Casteljau's algorithm, in such a wedge, for a stretch that meets no more than 16 spans (over more, the pieces say little that the control points did not, and the stretch is halved); past the ends of the curve's domain, where (1) and (2) say nothing, the first and the last piece are cut as far as the edge goes, the curve being that piece continued there, within two limits: not past the END of a domain that ends on a span of no width (knots not clamped, the end knot repeated), where `NurbsCurve::eval` continues that empty span's polynomial and not the last piece's, and only while the rounding of the cut grows by less than 2²⁰ (each of the degree's rounds of the cut multiplies it by `1 + 2s` for a cut `s` span widths before the span: a billionth past is shown at any degree, a whole width past up to degree 12); the points of a hull must keep clear of the axis by eight times what rounding can have moved them (16 units in the last place of the curve's largest coordinate for each control point of a span, 64 for a cubic, times the ratio of its largest weight to its least), so that a curve a rounding outside its hull as computed still turns about the axis as the hull says, to 7°, well within the half turn at which the chart would unwrap a step the wrong way; each looks at the points of a stretch only until they are too wide, bounds the movement over the whole stretch and so over any part of it, and each half step is halved only until it is shown. (b) In the chart of a plane, a cylinder, a cone or a sphere (not a torus: below), the edge's image runs one way over the half step (since 2026-11-14, *A strip that spirals about a point of the chart*): its direction keeps within a fan narrower than a half turn (three eighths of a turn as computed; a leg's direction is read to an eighth of a radian, and a leg shorter than sixteen roundings of its points has none, so the stretch is not shown). The direction of a spline with positive weights is, at each of its points, a sum with weights that are not negative of the legs between the control points of its span (a polynomial spline's derivative is a spline on those legs; a rational one's is a sum over pairs `i < j` of `Pj − Pi` times `Ni Nj' − Ni' Nj`, which is not negative, the basis being totally positive), so of the legs of the spans a half step meets, as (1) reads them, or of its Bézier pieces cut to it, (3); and the chart takes a direction `w` at a point to one of its own by a linear map there: on a plane `(w·x, w·y)`, the same everywhere; about an axis `u' = w·e / ρ` (`e` the unit vector round the axis at the point's angle, `ρ` its distance from the axis) and `v'` as the surface has it (a cylinder `w·z`; a cone the ruling's share of `w·z` and of `w·r`, `r` the unit vector away from the axis, the stretch kept to the cone's own side of its apex; a sphere `(ρ w·z − h w·r) / (ρ² + h²)`, `h` the height). Over the stretch's reach (its range of angle about the axis, of `ρ` and of `h`: the ends of the hull's wedge, the nearest point's distance times the cosine of half the wedge, the furthest point's, the lowest and the highest; or what (2) shows about its middle) each leg's image lies in a box of such directions, whose four corners stand for it, and the half step is shown where all of them lie in the fan. Cheaper ways are tried first about an axis: every leg running round the axis one way at both ends of the reach, two products a leg (`u` then moves one way, which is all a helix needs, so a helix is followed in the steps its angle needs); and, where (2) shows the angle, the curve's own direction at the stretch's middle, which (2) evaluates anyway, running round the axis by more than the angle's range and the bound of its second derivative over the stretch's spans (the longest control point of its derivative's derivative) can take from it. The image over the half step and the half step's chord then both cross every line square to the fan's middle once, so together they wind about no point more than once, and only about points that lie between them: the chord of a half step hides no turn about any point of the chart. That is all (b) shows. The walk takes the chord of the whole step, from its start to its end, and reads the edge only at the step's middle, where the chords of its two halves meet: two halves that each run one way can together, with the whole step's chord, wind about a point twice while the middle lies on that chord, and what lies between a half step and its own chord is not measured at all (*A strip that spirals about a point of the chart*, Owed (3) and (4)). On a torus (b) is not asked: a loop with a spline edge there is one off which nothing is read, and its edges are shown about the axis alone (*A band of a torus between curves that wind about its tube*: the read of the tube angle, built three times, refused right faces each time). On a spline surface, whose chart is not known, none of this can be shown: there an edge is held to running forward along its stretch's chord in space over each half of its eight steps, and only as a reason not to follow it (one that does is followed in eight steps, as every spline edge was before; one that does not, a spiral of many turns as one edge, is not followed). The whole edge is looked at first, by its control points alone: an edge shown at one look, which is nearly every edge, keeps its eight steps, and that look, one pass over the edge's control points (as the key a face is remembered by makes one) that stops at the first point or leg that does not fit, is not counted. No edge is followed in steps that are not shown, and showing is bounded: an edge that needs more than 2¹² steps (a curve through the axis among them), and a face whose spline edges take more than `MOST_PROOF_WORK` (2¹⁷) units to show and to follow (a point looked at is one unit, a leg read one on a plane, a half round an axis and two where its box of directions is made, a piece made the square of its points, a step of an edge followed in more than eight 32), is not followed, so it passes, and is counted (`topo::step_proofs`, which tests read: a count, never a clock). A unit is 10 to 35 ns as measured, so showing and following a face's spline edges adds 2 ms as measured at its worst, and under 5 ms at the slowest unit seen, to its check at the most; since 2026-11-14, *A helix of many turns as one edge*), each point projected (on a freeform surface from the one before, checked, else from nothing) and moved by whole periods to the one before, a face's loops must be one loop counter-clockwise about the outward normal enclosing the most area and the others clockwise; on every surface but a plane (where `stranded_loops` holds it) every point of a hole clear of the other loops by their errors and ten tolerances must be wound once by the outer loop and by no other hole (holes placed by the whole periods that bring them over the outer loop); and no two steps may cross properly by more than the errors and ten tolerances read in parameters at the surface's smallest scale over the face. A fault of these found on a plane, a cylinder, a cone or a sphere is verified before it is claimed (since 2026-11-15, *What lies between an edge and its chord*): the face is walked again in stretches each shown to run one way and to lie within a bound of its chord, and the fault is dropped only where those bounds show the loops bound a region; where they show neither that nor a fault within the work of a face, the walk's fault stands. A face the walk passes is not walked again. Passed without a word: loops that wind around a period (bands), surfaces closed both ways, points near a pole or an apex, freeform surfaces that close or collapse (one that closes is not charted: a miss, never a false alarm; *A spline surface that closes, charted* has the charting that is parked), a loop whose area its error cannot tell from zero, more than `MOST_PER_FACE` (2¹⁸) points or `MOST_PAIRS` (2²⁴) units of work, a spline edge whose steps are not shown (above: a spline through or very near the axis, a single edge of more than about a hundred turns, an edge that turns back at a point by three eighths of a turn or more, on a spline surface an edge that does not run forward over each half of its eight steps, a face past `MOST_PROOF_WORK`); on a sphere, loops all clockwise (the sphere less some caps). Work: the loops near a hole's point come from a grid over their boxes, and the crossing search sweeps each band of v along u, so a perforated face costs about its points, not their square. Faces that passed are remembered (`regionless`, since 2026-10-31) by everything the rule reads of them, every bit: the reach (ten of the body's working tolerances), the surface and its sense, and each loop's coedges as their sense, curve and range; a face a boolean or a regeneration carries over unchanged is then not followed again (a hit compares the key word for word and charges the work the check took, so an operation counts the same either way; a fault is never remembered; 16 MB of keys at most, the oldest let go first; `forget_passed` lets go of them too). *Measured* (Strix, debug profile as the tests run, the kernel's boolean corpus run back to back on one quiet runner, Check 41175274421, a measurement-only switch since dropped): the corpus took 60.0 / 60.0 s without the rule, 62.8 / 61.4 s with it unremembered (+3.5 %) and 62.7 / 59.8 s remembered (+2 %); the time spent following faces over the whole run, on every thread, 15.5 s unremembered and 2.4 s remembered (of which about 2 s the corpus's own call of the rule on each result). The 87 s and 115 s read earlier were the runner's load, not the rule. A perforated tube (`a_perforated_tube_is_checked_in_time_about_its_holes`): 100, 400 and 1600 holes 0.4, 1.8 and 6.5 ms, remembered 0.02, 0.07 and 0.26 ms. The tessellator refuses such a face ("its loops do not bound a region"); the check says so for every producer before the body leaves it. `topo::face_holds` reads the same chart to say whether points lie on a face (the parity of a ray's crossings, bands included): the wrap refuses a profile that leaves its face.

**Loops that are simple** (`topo::loops_not_simple`, `Defect::LoopNotSimple`, since 2026-11-14; `crates/topo/src/simple.rs`). Asked of every face whose loops bound a region (the rule above, whose walk it reads, so a face is followed once for both): each loop is a simple closed curve on the surface, so the face is its region counted once. Held in this order, the first found reported with the face, the loop and what is wrong: (1) no edge goes round its circle or ellipse more than once (a range longer than a turn by more than ten tolerances along it); (2) no loop goes round a surface that closes more often than a simple closed curve can: from the walk (each point moved by whole periods to the one before), a loop that ends k periods from where it starts goes round k times, and on a cylinder, a cone or a sphere |k| ≤ 1 (a band of a cylinder has two loops, each once round), on a torus (p, q) of no common factor or (0, 0) (faces on a torus are walked for this rule and the next two, the region rule passing them; held only of a loop of circles and ellipses: for a spline edge nothing shows how far it moves about the tube from one point to the next, *A band of a torus between curves that wind about its tube*); (3) no loop winds about a point it encloses other than once its own way round: each loop that goes round no period is cut along eight lines of constant v across its box (`SCANLINES`), between two crossings a line's points are wound by the sum of the senses of the crossings to their right, and a point clear of the loop by its margin (twice its polyline's error and ten tolerances in parameters) wound neither 0 times nor once with the sign of the loop's area is a fault (verified as the region rule's faults are, since 2026-11-15: dropped only where the bounds show no two of the loop's stretches that do not meet within their margins); (4) no two stretches of two different edges of the face's boundary are one curve along more than ten of the body's working tolerances (the boundary passing there twice). One edge used twice by one loop is not judged (since the gate of 2026-11-14): it is a cut of the face along an edge the face holds on both sides, a seam where the surface closes, a bridge joining two of its boundary curves (an annulus written as one loop, as sheet metal's collars and pans' rims are), or a spur (a dangling edge); each leaves the region and its measure as they are without the cut, and a bridge and a spur lie alike at one place of a plane, so nothing in the two uses tells a right body from a wrong one, and a rule that claims a fault only where it has shown one leaves them. Two stretches are one curve where they lie within the tighter of their two edges' own tolerances (the document's for an edge without one: each edge then lies within its own of the other) by a gap that is the same all along them to the rounding of their coordinates (64 units in the last place of the largest coordinate of their boxes): copies of one curve, or one offset by less than that tolerance. A face between one and ten tolerances wide is thin, not degenerate, and passes; a sharp tip of a face, whose two edges keep within the tolerance of each other near it by a gap that grows from nothing at its vertex, passes whatever its angle; the rule reads no angle, so the document's angular tolerance cannot make it claim more. (4) is exact for lines (both ends of one within the tolerance of the other's line, as far off at both; their ranges read on it overlapping) and conics (nine points of one within the tolerance of the other, which five points fix, at one gap, their centres within twice it; their ranges read on the first and taken modulo a turn overlapping, the length a lower bound along the conic); a spline against any curve is held at nine places along each (`PLACES`), a run of three or more in a row within the tolerance of the other, the first and the last more than ten tolerances apart, at one gap, being a stretch on it (the nearest point by Newton steps from the nearest of 16 to 64 samples, looked for only where a place lies in the box of the other's control points and within an eighth of a step of its samples' polyline; a run whose gaps differ is scanned on past, on a sheet too, where the two sides of a cut are passed only once a level run of them is found). Stretches of two loops are a fault wherever they lie. Two of one loop are one only where they lie at one place in the loop's parameters as followed, on every surface but a plane (whose chart is the plane): the place of each is found from the loop's own point at the start of the step that holds it, by a walk on the surface from there, and two places are one where they lie apart along each parameter by less than ten working tolerances on the surface at that parameter's speed there (a period of a large cylinder's angle is a short way in parameters and its circumference on the surface), so a seam's two uses, or two edges either side of a slit, lie a period or a spline surface's domain apart and pass whether or not the surface is known to close; a face whose loop cannot be followed, or a place where a parameter does not move the surface, is not held to it. On a sheet two stretches that run opposite ways are the two sides of a cut, a slit or a slot, which origami builds ([`origami.md`](origami.md) › *Slits and slots*), and pass; on a solid, whose boundary has no cut, either way is a fault. *Not held* (each a pass, never a fault): what the region rule passes without a word, which is not followed here either: a face on a surface it cannot chart without doubt (a loop through a sphere's pole or a cone's apex, every spline surface that `geom::closure` finds closed or with a pole: *A spline surface that closes, charted* lists the misses there), a loop whose area its polyline cannot tell from nothing, a face past its caps of points (`MOST_PER_FACE`) and work: such a face is held to (1) and to (4) between different loops only, so a loop gone round twice on a face of a revolved spline is caught only where a neighbouring face can be charted; a spline surface that closes within the tolerance but not to `closure`'s rounding of its net, or on knots that are not clamped, is charted as one that does not close: its seam passes (above), but a loop across the seam is not followed right; a face with a spline edge whose half steps are not shown (the region rule's walk, above) within the steps of an edge and the work of a face is not followed at all; a loop with a spline edge on a torus is not held to (2) or (3), nor to (4) between two stretches of its own: a spline edge on a surface that closes about an axis is followed in steps shown to move less than a quarter turn about the axis (the region rule's walk, above), but nothing shows how far it moves about a torus's tube, and how often a loop goes round the axis decides nothing without the other (three times round the axis is a simple loop with 13 turns about the tube and not with 3); a loop with a conic whose half step moves more than a quarter period (a conic near a pole) is not held to (2) or (3) either (read off the walk itself: a conic's half step is a sixteenth of a half turn of its own, which moves the three quarters of a period that would wrap unseen only where the axis passes between the arc and its chord); a loop narrower than twice its margin has no clear point for (3); a spline overlap shorter than about a quarter of both edges, or that its samples do not show (a spline that bends between them by more than an eighth of a step), and two fits of one curve whose gap differs along them by more than rounding, are not seen; on a body with loose entities, two stretches of exact edges within the body's largest tolerance but not their own are two curves (as they are). Work: the boxes of a face's edges (each boxed by its vertices, an arc within its sagitta of its chord, grown by twice the working tolerance) are paired pair by pair up to 32 edges, else in a grid of about as many cells over the two axes they spread furthest along (a pair taken in the first cell both reach), so a face costs about its edges; (3) stops after `MOST_WINDING_WORK` (2²⁴) points looked at on a loop and (4)'s spline test after `MOST_EVALUATIONS` (2²⁰) evaluations on a face, each then a pass ("cannot tell"; a hostile face otherwise costs the square of its points or 2²² pairs of a thousand evaluations). *The cache.* A face passes both rules or neither is remembered; its key (`face_key`) holds everything the verdict is a function of: the reach (ten working tolerances, which also sets how far the boxes grow), the angular tolerance (read by neither rule; kept so that one that reads it cannot be served a verdict made at another), whether the body is a sheet, the surface and its sense, and per coedge its sense, the edge's curve and range, the edge's own tolerance, its start and end vertices' points, and which earlier coedge of the face uses the same edge; the edges' and vertices' numbers are not read, the vertices' tolerances only through the working tolerance (they bound how far a vertex lies off its edge's end, which the boxes' growth covers), and the rules' caps are constants.

**Self-intersection and self-touch** (`tess::body_self_intersections`, `body_self_intersections_near`, `body_touches_itself`). The body is meshed, and triangles that meet without being neighbours are found exactly (orientation predicates with an exact fallback); each finding has a depth and the margin the approximation alone could explain, so it is *certain* or not. Shells that do not cross are placed by winding numbers. The *near* form takes a role per face (a known group, or new) and looks only where new faces, or faces of different groups, can meet. Touching is decided on the B-rep: a vertex within twice the tolerance of another vertex, or of an edge, of the same shell. Regeneration runs these per operation ([`regeneration.md`](regeneration.md)); the kernel does not, since it shares a layer with `tess`.

**Measure** (`topo::mass_properties`, `inertia`, `mass_and_inertia`). Area, volume, centroid and the inertia tensor are boundary integrals over the exact surfaces, in each surface's parameters, taken about the middle of the body. Each face's boundary is integrated with Gauss–Legendre panels, doubled until two sums in a row agree to 1e-12 (piece by piece of the boundary, and within a piece panel by panel where its sums disagree, within a work budget per face); a face that does not settle is an error, never a guess. On a rational surface each inner integral is summed in the parameter of the Möbius map that evens out the weights at its ends (2026-10-17). A panel ends wherever the integrand has a kink: at an edge's knots, where the edge crosses a knot line of its spline surface, and where an edge within the tolerance of a spline surface runs off its domain and its points are projected onto the border (2026-10-15). The boundary summed is the curve of the feet of the edges' points on the face's surface, with the feet's own rates of change, so that each piece reaches its last foot also where its edge lies off its face within the tolerance; the pieces are summed edge by edge, with no step between one edge's last foot and the next one's first, so a loop is closed only as well as its edges meet at its vertices (2026-11-14, *The rates of the feet*). Poles of spheres, cones and spline surfaces, seams of surfaces that close on themselves and faces that are their whole surface each have an explicit rule. Faces are measured on several threads and summed in face order.

**Transforms** (`Body::transformed`): rigid motions and mirrors map every curve and surface exactly and keep every entity id; `Body::bbox` is a conservative box from the exact geometry, and `Body::held_bbox` one that boxes a face on a sphere or torus by what it holds (below); `merge` puts the shells of several bodies into one.

### From *Current state (2026-10-12)*

- **Topology:** `topo` has the B-rep (`Body` with vertices, edges, loops of coedges, faces, shells), the validity checker `check` (references, finite numbers, vertices on edges, closed loops, edges on their faces' surfaces, each edge of a solid used once each way, Euler–Poincaré with genus) and `mass_properties` (area, volume, centroid by boundary integrals over the exact geometry). Fourteen reference solids (six primitives and hemisphere, and eight with trimmed faces: holes, half walls, a spherical cap, a frustum, an ellipse edge, Bézier and NURBS roofs) match their closed forms at three scales and in tilted frames. `mass_properties` also measures the faces a revolution makes: torus faces bounded around the tube, around the axis or both (the surface is closed both ways, so the boundary integral is right only up to a whole number of times the entire torus, which the area settles), and faces whose boundary passes through a pole of a sphere or the apex of a cone (the point stands for a stretch of the edge of the parameter range, which is added). It still refuses, rather than gets wrong, a boundary through a collapsed edge of a Bézier or NURBS surface, a boundary that winds around an axis more than once, torus loops that wind around the axis and the tube at once, and a boundary that turns back on itself at a pole. Deviations from the plan below: parameter-space curves are not stored (recovered by projection), no per-entity tolerances, persistent names are made by `regen` (M6) and not stored in `topo`, the inertia tensor is `topo::inertia` (2026-10-15), and the checker does not test self-intersection or that each face is bounded by its loops.

#### 5.2 Topology (`topo`)

```text
Body ── Shell ── Face ── Loop ── Coedge ── Edge ── Vertex
                  │                 │        │        │
               Surface           Curve2    Curve3   Point     (+ orientation flags, tolerances, persistent names)
```

- A **body** is a set of shells: a *solid* (closed, oriented shells), a *sheet* (open faces) or a *wire* (edges only).
- A **face** is a surface trimmed by loops; a **coedge** is the use of an edge by a face, with a direction and a parameter-space curve.
- Storage is a vector per kind with `u32` ids; a body is shared whole (`Arc`). *(Planned: arena storage shared part by part between a body and the one made from it, parameter-space curves on coedges, and tolerances on entities; the diagram above shows the plan. Names are kept by `regen`, not in the body.)*
- **Genus** (`topo::genus`): from Euler–Poincaré, `V − E + F − R − 2H = 2 (S − G)`. `R` is, per face, its loops beyond the first (−1 for a face without loops), which counts every face as a disc with holes. A face on a torus that still has the torus's handle (the whole torus, or the torus less the discs inside its loops) is not one and takes 2 off (`H`). A torus face with a loop around the axis or around the tube is a band, and one inside its loops is a patch: neither counts. The hole a drill leaves in a torus and the plug it takes out have the same loops walked the other way, so which of the two a face is, is read off the area its loops enclose in parameter space. A spline surface that closes on itself is not known to.
- **Validity checker** (`topo::check`): Euler–Poincaré, every edge of a solid used by exactly two coedges with opposite directions, loops closed, coedge curves on their surface within tolerance, vertices on their edges, consistent orientation (self-intersection is not a level of `check`: it is the mesh check of the next bullet). Every kernel operation is tested to return bodies that pass it.
- **Self-intersection, as built (2026-10-13):** `topo::check` does not test it. The test is `tess::body_self_intersections(&Body, &SelfCheckParams) -> SelfCheck`, in `tess` because it works on the mesh and so stays independent of the kernel it checks: it tessellates the body (chordal tolerance 1/1000 of its diagonal), finds the triangles that meet without being neighbours (§6), and reports crossings and overlaps between faces, each with a depth and with the margin `deviation(f) + deviation(g) + 2 × tolerance` that the approximation alone could explain (`deviation` is the chordal tolerance, or 0 for a plane bounded by straight edges, whose mesh is the face). A finding deeper than its margin is *certain*; one within it is reported as such and proves nothing (tangent faces, a ball resting on a plane). Findings within the margin between curved faces are looked at again with a mesh four times finer, twice by default. Flat faces within twice the modelling tolerance of one plane that share area are an overlap. Shells that do not cross are checked for where they lie, by the winding number of the other shells at a point of each: an outward shell must be in no material, a cavity in material. It is a test oracle (`crates/testkit/tests/self_intersection.rs`, [`testing.md`](../testing.md) oracle 9, which also lists what it cannot see) and, since 2026-10-15, a step of regenerating every operation that can make such a body, near what the operation changed (§5.5, *Never through itself*); it is not a level of `check`, and the kernel operations do not run it themselves (`kernel` and `tess` share a layer). It also reports where a shell touches itself, on the B-rep (§5.5). Run over the boolean results of the corpus and the random runs (2026-10-13) it found nothing in 39,834 of them.
- **Mass properties:** volume, surface area, centroid and inertia tensor, computed from the exact B-rep (surface integrals), independent of the tessellator. Volume and centroid are taken about the middle of the body, not the origin of the model (2026-10-13): a small body far from the origin is then measured as well as the same body at the origin. These are the backbone of the test oracles. As built (`topo::mass_properties`: area, volume, centroid): each face is a boundary integral in its surface's parameters, and the result does not depend on how the surface is parametrised. A sphere face is measured in a frame chosen for its boundary, not in the surface's own (one pole in the middle of the boundary, the other as far from it as it gets); on a cone the integral runs from the apex whenever the boundary comes near it. Every boundary integral is computed with half the panels its edges need, with all of them, and with twice as many until two sums in a row agree to 1e-12; a face for which they never do is an error (`Unsupported`), as is a face that lies outside its own loops (`UnboundedFace`). Only plane faces bounded by lines, circles and ellipses skip that check: their sums are exact. On a spline surface every panel, across the face and along its boundary, ends at the surface's knot lines (and an edge's panels at its own knots too), and the panels across the face are doubled along with the boundary's; a face that holds a pole of a spline surface of revolution is integrated from that pole. **A boundary through a pole of a freeform surface** (a border of a Bézier or NURBS patch that is one point: every control point of it the same, to 1e-9 of the net's size; `geom::pole`) is measured since 2026-10-14. Such a border is a whole side of the parameter range that no edge of the body stands for, as at a cone's apex: with the inner integral running from the pole the integral is zero all along it (the integrand is smooth up to the pole; the normal's length goes to zero there, which is what makes the area element integrable), and an edge that passes through the pole on its way is cut there. A boundary through the poles at *both* ends (the face a spline sweeps that starts and ends on the axis runs from pole to pole between two meridians and has no other edge) has no inner integral that vanishes at both: the face is divided along a line of constant parameter between the poles, each side is integrated from its own pole, and the two are joined by the integral over the rectangle between the places where the boundary crosses that line (found by bisection along the edges). Poles on borders of constant u exchange the roles of u and v. Held to closed forms (`crates/topo/tests/mass_poles.rs`: wedges, octants, caps and halves of a spline ball, cones with a ruled spline side, with the poles either way, to 2e-15 at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6 in two frames), to Pappus for the dome and the drop (`crates/kernel/tests/spline_sweeps.rs`, 1e-9), and all 32 patches of the teapot to their double integral (24 before). A face that passes through no pole is measured exactly as before (the printed worst errors of every suite of `topo` are the same to the last digit). Declined by name: a boundary through two poles that also crosses the seam of a surface that closes on itself. **A spline surface that closes on itself** (its control net ends on the row it starts with, on clamped knots; `geom` gives it no period, so the measure finds it from the net) is followed since 2026-10-14, closed in u, in v, both ways or to a point, with or without seam edges. A seam line of constant u adds nothing to `−∮ G du`; one of constant v does, so where the boundary jumps over the seam in v the inner integral runs along u instead (`∮ H dv`), and where it jumps both ways the loops are unrolled as on a torus and the area settles the whole number of surfaces. Loops that only walk seam edges there and back make the face its whole surface (also on a sphere and a torus, which were refused), and loops that run the wrong way round are the rest of a surface that closes all around. Until then a face that closes in v was measured wrong without an error. Faces that do not go over a seam are measured to the same bits as before. Held to the closed forms of a NURBS cylinder, a vase (Pappus), an egg and a NURBS torus with windows across its seams, to 1e-12 at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6, and to the mesh (`crates/tess/tests/closed_freeform.rs`; `crates/topo/tests/mass_seams.rs`). On a freeform surface the nodes along one piece of an edge are projected each from where the last one landed (`Surface::project_from`, a few evaluations instead of a search over the whole surface), and the search is run only where the short way does not land on a foot of the node: within 1e-5 of the body's size and square to the surface, which is the same point the search finds. A spline edge of eight knot spans or more (the curve in which two surfaces meet, as the kernel fits it) is summed first with the eight-node rule on one panel per span and then with the sixteen-node one, before its panels double.
- **Moving, mirroring, merging** (`topo::transform`): `Body::transformed` maps a body by an isometry exactly (no curve or surface changes kind; scaling and shear are errors) and keeps every entity id. Curves keep their parametrisation, so edges are untouched. A mirror turns right-handed things left-handed: frames become `(Tx, Ty, −Tz)`, every loop is walked the other way round, and faces on planes and freeform surfaces toggle `reversed`, while cylinders, cones (half-angle negated), spheres and tori keep their outward normal and get `v` negated. `Body::bbox` is a conservative box from the exact geometry (tight for planes, cylinders and cones with analytic edges; the whole surface for spheres and tori, which `Body::held_bbox` boxes by what the face holds; the control net for freeform). `merge` puts the shells of several bodies into one and reports the id offsets; it does not check that they are disjoint. These are what the transform, mirror and pattern features build on.

### Rational weights far apart (2026-10-17)

*The boolean checklist* ([`booleans.md`](booleans.md) › *Rational faces*): a face on a rational patch whose weights are far apart did not settle (the plate with its top as a bilinear patch with the weights 1, 100, 100, 1: refused), and, found here, settled *wrong*: the quarter rod's rational twin with its weights skewed a thousandfold along the axis (a Möbius reparametrisation of the span, the same cylinder) measured 7.4e-6 short in volume, and at ten thousand 0.17. Two causes.

*The inner integrals.* A rational surface along a line of constant parameter is `A(t) / w(t)` within a knot span, and where the weights at a stretch's ends are far apart `w` has a zero just outside the stretch, by the light end: the integrand changes on that scale and uniform panels converge slowly. Worse, on a spline surface the inner panels doubled with the boundary's only up to 64 a stretch, while the boundary's doubled seven times: the last two sums had the same inner panels and agreed in what those missed. Fixed in two ways. The inner panels now double to the last (`MAX_SPLINE_INNER_PANELS`, 2^7). And each stretch is summed in the parameter `s` of the Möbius map `t = start + (end − start) β s / ((1 − s) + β s)` with `β = (w(start) / w(end))^{1/q}`, `q` the degree in that direction (`mass::moebius`; the surface's denominator comes from `NurbsSurface::weight_at`, new): in `s` the Bernstein coefficients of the denominator are `w_i β^i`, so its ends are equal. It is a change of variable, so the integral is the same; only where the nodes fall changes. Where the weights have a product form (`w_ij = a_i b_j`: every surface of revolution) it takes the skew out exactly, and the skewed twins sum as the even ones do (the rod: 1.5 s and wrong before, 6 to 10 ms and right to the last digit now). Equal end weights (every polynomial surface) keep their stretches' nodes as they were; their sums still change in the last digits, by the inner panels that now double to the last and by the panels refined one by one below (the benchmark's measures by at most 5.6e-15 of their size, the rotor's blade by 1.7e-12, within its rounding allowance). Weights that stay further apart than 1e4 within a span after the corner weights are evened by Möbius maps (what no reparametrisation removes: `mass::uneven_weights`) are refused before anything is summed (`MassError::UnevenWeights`, naming the face and the ratio; `MAX_UNEVEN`): the measure is held to a thousand and refuses ten thousand by sums that do not settle, but from 1e7 the plate's sums settled wrong (22 for 24), on bodies whose edges no longer lie on their face (from 1e5).

*The boundary integral.* Where no reparametrisation helps (the plate: `w₀₀ w₁₁ / (w₀₁ w₁₀) = 1 / h²` in every one), the lines of constant u fan out from a heavy corner, and seen from the boundary integral almost all of the face lies on a stretch of the opposite edge `1 / h²` of it long next to that corner. Doubling every panel of the edge seven times reaches `1 / 128` (the sums at h = 100: 6.3, 6.6, 7.1, 8.0, 9.3, 10.6, 11.6, 11.93 of 12, past the budget). The panels of a piece are now refined adaptively (`cell_by_cell`): each panel of a piece at its coarsest is a cell; a round sums every open cell's halves and holds them first all together (the piece's sums, as before) and, where those disagree, each cell to its own halves; cells that agree are taken (the finer sum) and not summed again, the halves of the others are open in the next round. Every comparison is of sums that differ in everything refined: up to the seventh halving the inner panels double with each, past it each finer cell is also summed with half its inner panels and must agree with that (and its children take half as many inner panels where that agreed, twice as many where not, so a polynomial line costs two panels a stretch). The allowance of a cell is the share of its size its piece's is ([`CONVERGED`] and the rounding share of the piece's sums), so the cells' allowances add up to the piece's. Rules, each general: a panel is halved at most 40 times (`MAX_LEVEL`: sixteen nodes in 2⁻⁴⁰ of a piece are a few dozen units in the last place apart); one of no width sums to nothing, one too narrow to halve stays as it is with its inner panels refined; a piece with more open cells at once than it had panels at the seventh doubling (its first panels times 2^7) is given up, since its sums then scatter wherever they are looked at (a boundary through a sphere's other pole: refused at 1.15 M evaluations, about a fifty-eighth of the budget; `a_face_refined_part_by_part_stops_at_its_cap`, whose bound was a hundredth when the doublings ran out first: expectation changed). With the second moments the cells are first refined until the area, the volume and the moments agree, as without them, which gives those sums bit for bit with the same work (`mass_and_inertia`), then every taken sum whose second moments do not agree is opened again. ### The box of a face on a sphere or a torus (2026-10-15)

*Bike B4*: the default framing of `view.render` (the scene's box, `Body::bbox`) of a chain stay, a tube of radius 7 swept along an arc of a circle of radius 1332, was 2671 mm across for a stay of 455 mm: a face on a torus contributed the box of the whole torus, and one on a sphere that of the whole sphere.

`Body::held_bbox` (2026-10-15) boxes such a face by what it holds, and the scene's box (`engine::scene::bodies_box`, the default framing of `view.render`, `view.pick` and `replay`) is made of it. `Body::bbox` and `Body::face_bbox` keep the whole surface: with the tight box in `Body::bbox`, `crates/kernel/tests/blend_cylinders.rs › both_tip_edges_of_a_cambered_blade` failed at scale 1e-3 with r 0.05 ("a point too close to a face boundary to tell inside from outside", inside the fillet's boolean), because `boolean::section` clips the curves where two faces meet to the bodies' boxes grown by `16 tol + 0.013 × diagonal`, and a smaller box put the clip where something else was. That margin is the boolean agents' to make independent of how loose the operands' boxes are; until then the booleans keep the box they were measured with, and nothing pays the walk's cost but a picture. A picture asks for the scene's box three or four times (its size, its tolerance, its scene); `scene::held_box` keeps each body's box for as long as the body lives (regeneration hands on the same `Arc` for every body an edit did not touch), so it is worked out once per body. On the turbofan (37 bodies, 6405 faces, 64 of them on spheres and tori) `held_bbox` of all bodies took 9.2 ms against 8.5 ms for `bbox` (release, best of 5, load 7), and the frame's diagonal went from 508 to 406 mm. Along an axis `e`, the greatest `e · p` over a face is reached on its boundary (which the edges add, exactly for lines, circles and ellipses) or at an inner point where `e` is normal to the surface: `centre ± r e` on a sphere; on a torus the four points on the section of the tube in the plane of the axis and `e`, or, where `e` is along the axis, the circles along the top and the bottom of the tube. Each such point that the extent does not already reach adds its coordinate along that axis only, unless it is certainly not in the face (`certainly_outside`). A circle's points stand for the whole circle: if one of them is certainly outside, either the face holds none of the circle or its boundary meets the circle and so reaches the same height.

*Whether a point is in the face* is decided by walking from it along circles of the surface (`walk`): along the first one, the first place where the face's boundary crosses it tells, because the face lies to the left of its boundary seen from outside: beyond the crossing, the point is outside; back towards it, inside. Paths: on a sphere, great circles through the point and the middle of an edge of the boundary (which they cross there) and two turned away from the coordinate planes and their diagonals (a walk along a boundary that lies in its plane has no answer); on a torus, the circle about the axis and the section of the tube through the point, and to the middle of each of three edges a path along a section and then a circle about the axis, or the other way round. Crossings are found on the edges' samples, with the edges' ends taken as their vertices (so that two edges, or a closed edge with itself, see the same side where they meet and a crossing there is counted once), and then sharpened by regula falsi. A plane cuts a torus in two circles; a crossing counts for the walk's circle when it is clearly nearer to it than to the other (within a quarter of their distance apart). No answer, and the point is kept, where no path meets the boundary, the first crossing grazes the path (the boundary within about 6° of running along it), lies at the path's start, or is not clearly on either circle. The first path that answers decides. Faces without loops (whole spheres and tori) keep the box of the whole surface; faces on freeform surfaces keep their control net.

Measured: `crates/topo/tests/transform.rs › faces_on_spheres_and_tori_are_boxed_by_what_they_hold` (pipe elbows through 0.3, 1.9, 4 and 6 rad, a sphere octant through a pole, caps below and above the equator; three scales, two frames, nine isometries including mirrors: the box holds 513 × 513 samples of the face and the edges, and is within 2e-5 of their box, the sag of the samples); `crates/engine/tests/view_render.rs › the_default_frame_is_the_box_of_what_the_faces_hold` (the stay of the finding, a 70° turn of a profile with an arc, a 50° lune of a sphere: the frame holds the mesh's box and is larger by at most the mesh's tolerance). Cost: about 0.1 ms for a face whose box the boundary does not already give (best of 50 on the elbow, octant and cap), nothing for one it does.

### One slow face on every thread (2026-10-21)

*Finding* (the Garabit valley showcase, 2026-10-21): `document.measure` and `document.inspect` of the Garabit valley (112 bodies, 12,799 faces) took 33 s on top of regenerating, 31.7 s of it one face: the cut terrain's top, a bicubic NURBS surface of 55 × 68 knots trimmed by the curves the cut fitted (cubic splines of 1,566, 5,376 and 8,242 knots). *Fix*: the panels of one face are summed on as many threads as are idle (`boundary_integrals`, for every face refined panel by panel on a spline surface: the first sums of each panel and each round of halved panels in `cells_of`). Each run of a panel is summed on a copy of the face's work and comes back with the work it took and the work it had spent at each look at the budget; the sums are taken in panel order, and where the face would have been given up, had the panels been summed one after the other, is replayed from those looks (`replay`), so the sums, the work counted and the place a face is refused are the same bits on any machine and with any number of threads. Within work already shared out (the faces of a body) a face takes the threads that are idle (a count of busy threads, `BUSY`), so the last slow face of a body gets the machine as the others finish. *Measured* (release, 14 threads, a loaded machine): the terrain's body 31.7 → 8.0 s, the pier with one spline face 4.3 → 1.0 s, the valley's `document.measure` after regeneration 49.8 → 9.9 s (load 10), the Garabit pier's 2.1 → 1.7 s; `document.measure` and `document.inspect` of both give the same bytes as before. Held to: `runs_summed_apart_are_given_up_where_one_after_the_other_would_be` (the replay of the budget and the order of the items), every measure test of `topo` and the benchmark's work counts. On an analytic surface, and for a single run, the runs are summed one after the other on the face's own work as before (no copies, no recorded looks). Once the runs done so far took more than the face had left, no more are started; a run the replay reaches all the same is summed then, so a face that will be refused stops near its budget even on one thread. An internal error of a face (a panel the samples do not cover) is now found before any panel is summed, so it may come before a refusal for the budget that came first before; both are errors, and deterministic. *Still open*: the inner integrals themselves are the cost (the same per node as before); a face whose region spans many knot stretches would gain from summing the knot cells inside it as a whole rather than from its boundary.

*Fix: verify before refusing* (`crates/topo/src/region/chord.rs`). A face the walk is about to refuse, by the region rule or by the rule of simple loops' third rule (a point wound neither 0 times nor once its own way), on a plane, a cylinder, a cone or a sphere, is walked again in *stretches*, each shown to keep to a bound; a face the walk passes is never walked again, so its verdict, its bits and its cost are what they were. A stretch of an edge, from one parameter to another, its image in the chart running from `A` to `B`, is *shown* where (1) its image runs one way: the legs of points whose convex hull holds the stretch and whose legs' sums with weights that are not negative hold its direction (the control points of the knot spans it meets, over more than sixteen spans; else its Bézier pieces cut to it; for an arc of a circle or an ellipse of at most a quarter turn the three points of its rational quadratic; a line's two ends) lie in a fan narrower than a half turn, read along the plane, or about an axis each leg taken to its box of directions over every place the stretch can be (`AboutMap`, the one-way proof's own, the box widened by what rounding can have moved the leg); so the image and its chord together wind about no point more than once, and only about points between them; and (2) it lies within a *bound* of the segment `AB`: on a plane, whose chart is linear, the farthest of those points from the segment (a hull lies no farther from a segment than its farthest corner); about an axis, from the fan alone, the farther of the two other corners of the parallelogram `(A + cone) ∩ (B − cone)` (`B − A = α d1 + β d2` over the fan's two ends, turned outward by 1e-9 rad), measured from the segment, not from its line (in a cone nearly a half turn wide the corners lie far past the chord's ends: the first build measured them from the line, and the unit test caught a stretch 0.069 off its chord against a bound of 0.0125).
