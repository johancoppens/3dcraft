# 3DCraft design: Topology, checks and measures

The boundary representation and what is decided and measured on it (`craft3d-topo`), with the mesh-based checks of `craft3d-tess` that stand beside it. Methods 6 and 7 of the map ([`../architecture.md`](../architecture.md) §5).

## Method

**The B-rep.** A `Body` holds vectors of vertices, edges, loops (of coedges), faces and shells, referred to by `u32` ids; it is a solid (closed, oriented shells), a sheet or a wire. A face lies on a `Surface` and is to the left of its loops seen from outside (`reversed` turns its normal); an edge lies on a `Curve3` between two vertices; a coedge is the use of an edge by a loop, with a direction. Parameter-space curves are not stored; the document's tolerance is passed to every check, and a vertex or an edge may carry a looser one of its own (*Tolerant entities*, below). A body is shared whole (`Arc`), not part by part.

**Tolerant entities** (since 2026-10-21; the decision of 2026-10-18 in [`../decisions.md`](../decisions.md), option (4), a tolerance per entity). `Body::tolerances` is a sparse, ordered table (`topo::Tolerances`) of the vertices and edges that are looser than the document's tolerance; an entity not in it has the document's, and every body 3DCraft builds itself has an empty table (written out by `Bits` as nothing, so such a body is bit for bit what it was before). A vertex's tolerance is a ball the ends of its edges' curves lie in; an edge's is a tube the surfaces of its faces pass through along its whole curve. The tolerance that holds is the larger of the entity's and the document's, and a vertex is at least as loose as each edge that ends at it. A cap on any entity's tolerance is document data (`doc::Tolerance::loosest`, `None` = `topo::DEFAULT_LOOSEST` (1000) times the linear tolerance); above it the check reports the entity and an operation refuses. The API, for import and sewing:

- `Body::vertex_tolerance(v)`, `Body::edge_tolerance(e)`: the entity's own, `None` for the document's; `vertex_tolerance_at(v, linear)`, `edge_tolerance_at(e, linear)`: the one that holds.
- `Body::set_vertex_tolerance(v, t)`, `Body::set_edge_tolerance(e, t)` (`Result`, refuses a tolerance that is not positive and finite or an entity that does not exist; setting an edge raises its two vertices to at least `t`); `clear_vertex_tolerance`, `clear_edge_tolerance`.
- `Body::is_tolerant()`, `Body::working_tolerance(linear)`: the largest of the table and the document's, the tolerance the kernel works at on the body.
- `topo::measure_edge(body, e, tol, cap)`: how far the edge's curve is from the surface of each face that uses it, *proven* over the whole edge (`certify::curve_on_surface`, targets doubling from twice the farthest of five places, up to the cap); `topo::measure_vertex(body, v)`: how far the ends of its edges are, and at least their tolerances.
- `topo::record_edge(body, e, tol, cap)`, `topo::record_vertex(body, v, tol, cap)`: measure and put it in the table (`RECORD_MARGIN` = 1.125 larger, so that the same body moved still passes), nothing when it is within the document's; refused above the cap. Import records what it measures this way; it never moves geometry.
- `topo::check_capped(body, tol, cap)`, `topo::check_remembered_capped`: `check` with the document's cap (`check` uses the default, `craft3d_math::DEFAULT_LOOSEST` times the linear tolerance).
- `topo::record_vertices(body, vertices, tol, cap)`: `record_vertex` for many, in one pass over the edges. A gap within the cap is recorded at most at the cap: the margin never pushes it over (since 2026-10-22).

**Sewing** (`topo::sew(body, tol, distance, cap)`, since 2026-10-22; "merges topology and measures gaps; it never moves geometry"). Only *free* edges (used once by a loop) are sewn. Two free edges are a candidate pair when their end vertices are within `distance` of each other, either way round, each of `SAMPLES` (7) places inside each lies within `distance` of the other's curve (so two arcs between the same points, or an arc and its chord, are told apart), and the two uses run against each other, as the two uses of an edge of an oriented boundary do, by two different faces. The samples only choose candidates; acceptance is proven (below). Candidates are taken closest first (the larger of the gaps measured, then edge ids), each only while both edges are still free, joining its ends would not make one vertex of the two ends of any edge (sewing never collapses an edge), and neither edge has another candidate within the ambiguity margin, twice the largest gap any free edge has to its nearest candidate plus the tolerance (at most `distance`): an edge that could go either way (a slot narrower than the gaps) stays open and a solid with it is refused. The lower edge of a pair is kept with its curve; vertices joined through pairs are one, the lowest with its point; faces keep their ids; shells joined by a pair become one. Then, proven over each whole curve (`measure_curve`): the kept edge on both its faces' surfaces and the dropped edge on the same surfaces (its face's boundary is now the kept edge), the larger recorded on the kept edge; every joined vertex measured against the kept edges' ends, the dropped edges' ends and the points of the vertices merged into it. A gap beyond the document's tolerance is recorded, one beyond `cap` refuses (`SewError::Gap`, naming the entity and the distance). Free edges left are reported with the distance to the nearest free edge (`Sewn::open`, `worst_open`); a solid with one is the caller's to refuse. Free edges are found through a grid of their end vertices (cells `2 distance`, ordered maps); the ends looked at (`MAX_PROBES`, 2^27) and the pairs tested (`MAX_CANDIDATES`, 2^22) are capped, so thousands of edges at one point stay bounded. A body without free edges comes back as it is. `shells_alone` (and so `body.split`) keeps the tolerances of the entities it takes. **T-junctions** (since slice 3, `topo/src/sew_split.rs`): before pairs are sought, a free edge `A` along which two or more shorter free edges of other faces run end to end is split. Its *stations* are the places inside it (beyond the distance from both its ends) where an end of another face's free edge lies within the distance of its curve, one per place; the split is taken only when they are proven a chain: between each two neighbouring stations (and `A`'s ends) runs one free edge of another face with its ends there, its seven places inside within the distance of that stretch of `A` and the stretch's of it, its use against `A`'s, the ends of the two partners meeting at a station within the distance of each other, and no free edge runs along the whole of `A`. A piece keeps `A`'s curve over part of its range; the new vertex at a station is the curve's point there and goes into the shorter edges' ends (the lowest vertex's point is kept), so nothing moves and every gap is measured as any pair's is. The split stays only when every piece is then sewn; otherwise the edge is left whole and the body sewn again without it (at most twice). A split attempt that sewing refuses (a gap above the cap) or that leaves more edges open than sewing the body without any split is dropped, and the body is sewn as it is: a split never turns a sewing into a refusal, nor leaves more open (compared by count; the body is then sewn twice). `Sewn::split` lists each edge split with the edges its pieces became; STEP import counts them (`splitEdges`). Ends are kept in a grid of cells at least twice the distance and a sixty-fourth of their box wide (ordered maps, looked up by box); every cell and end looked at counts against `MAX_STATION_LOOKS` (2^24), and when that runs out nothing is split at all and sewing goes on as without T-junctions, never a refusal; an edge with more than `MAX_STATIONS` (1024) stations is left whole. *Conservative, left whole*: a seam of a closed curve that does not lie at a station (the stretch over the seam has no partner); an end of a third face touching `A`'s interior where no chain runs (a corner on the edge) adds a station whose stretches have no partner, so `A` stays whole and open, the solid refused.

**Validity** (`topo::check`): references in range, finite numbers, vertices on their edges, closed loops, edges on their faces' surfaces within the tolerance, every edge of a solid used once each way, Euler–Poincaré with the genus (`topo::genus`, which knows torus faces that keep a handle), per shell (since 2026-10-17: a shell's faces hang together through edges, no edge is in two shells, and in a solid of several shells each one fits Euler–Poincaré alone, `topo::shell_genera`; which shells are the outsides of lumps and which their cavities is geometry, `kernel::lumps`, [`booleans.md`](booleans.md) › *Lumps and cavities*), and on planes that a face's loops lie inside each other as a face's do (`topo::stranded`, since 2026-10-16: in the plane's frame turned to the outward normal, the other loops wind zero times around a point of an anticlockwise loop and once around a point of a clockwise one; the loops followed as polylines within 1e-4 of each conic's largest radius (bounded) and splines in 256 steps (their distance measured, not bounded), one point of each loop used, where it is clear of every other loop by that plus ten tolerances, so two loops that cross are found only where that point lies on the wrong side; each polyline keeps its box, so a face with many holes costs about a box test per pair of loops). For a sheet (since 2026-10-18): every edge is used once (an edge of its boundary) or once each way (else `EdgeUses`, `EdgeUsedTwiceSameWay`), no more than two boundary edges of a shell end at a vertex (`BoundaryPinch`: the boundary touches itself there), and each shell fits Euler–Poincaré with its boundary, `V − E + F − R − 2H = 2 (1 − G) − B` with `B` its loops of boundary edges (`topo::boundary_loops`; `ShellEuler`); two shells that share a vertex touch, which is geometry's to refuse, as for lumps. Since 2026-10-30 every face's loops must also bound a region in its parameters, on every surface (*Loops that bound a region*, below). It does not test self-intersection or that a face is bounded by its loops: those are the mesh checks below and the measure.

**Loops that bound a region** (`topo::region_fault`, `Defect::LoopsBoundNoRegion`, since 2026-10-30). Read in the surface's `(u, v)`, sampled per edge (a line in one step on a plane, two elsewhere, a conic in steps of at most π/8, a spline in eight; each step's middle projected too, its distance from the chord the polyline's error), each point projected (on a freeform surface from the one before, checked, else from nothing) and moved by whole periods to the one before, a face's loops must be one loop counter-clockwise about the outward normal enclosing the most area and the others clockwise; on every surface but a plane (where `stranded_loops` holds it) every point of a hole clear of the other loops by their errors and ten tolerances must be wound once by the outer loop and by no other hole (holes placed by the whole periods that bring them over the outer loop); and no two steps may cross properly by more than the errors and ten tolerances read in parameters at the surface's smallest scale over the face. Passed without a word: loops that wind around a period (bands), surfaces closed both ways, points near a pole or an apex, freeform surfaces that close or collapse, a loop whose area its error cannot tell from zero, more than `MOST_PER_FACE` (2¹⁸) points or `MOST_PAIRS` (2²⁴) units of work; on a sphere, loops all clockwise (the sphere less some caps). Work: the loops near a hole's point come from a grid over their boxes, and the crossing search sweeps each band of v along u, so a perforated face costs about its points, not their square. Faces that passed are remembered (`regionless`, since 2026-10-31) by everything the rule reads of them, every bit: the reach (ten of the body's working tolerances), the surface and its sense, and each loop's coedges as their sense, curve and range; a face a boolean or a regeneration carries over unchanged is then not followed again (a hit compares the key word for word and charges the work the check took, so an operation counts the same either way; a fault is never remembered; 16 MB of keys at most, the oldest let go first; `forget_passed` lets go of them too). *Measured* (Strix, debug profile as the tests run, the kernel's boolean corpus run back to back on one quiet runner, Check 41175274421, a measurement-only switch since dropped): the corpus took 60.0 / 60.0 s without the rule, 62.8 / 61.4 s with it unremembered (+3.5 %) and 62.7 / 59.8 s remembered (+2 %); the time spent following faces over the whole run, on every thread, 15.5 s unremembered and 2.4 s remembered (of which about 2 s the corpus's own call of the rule on each result). The 87 s and 115 s read earlier were the runner's load, not the rule. A perforated tube (`a_perforated_tube_is_checked_in_time_about_its_holes`): 100, 400 and 1600 holes 0.4, 1.8 and 6.5 ms, remembered 0.02, 0.07 and 0.26 ms. The tessellator refuses such a face ("its loops do not bound a region"); the check says so for every producer before the body leaves it. `topo::face_holds` reads the same chart to say whether points lie on a face (the parity of a ray's crossings, bands included): the wrap refuses a profile that leaves its face.

**Self-intersection and self-touch** (`tess::body_self_intersections`, `body_self_intersections_near`, `body_touches_itself`). The body is meshed, and triangles that meet without being neighbours are found exactly (orientation predicates with an exact fallback); each finding has a depth and the margin the approximation alone could explain, so it is *certain* or not. Shells that do not cross are placed by winding numbers. The *near* form takes a role per face (a known group, or new) and looks only where new faces, or faces of different groups, can meet. Touching is decided on the B-rep: a vertex within twice the tolerance of another vertex, or of an edge, of the same shell. Regeneration runs these per operation ([`regeneration.md`](regeneration.md)); the kernel does not, since it shares a layer with `tess`.

**Measure** (`topo::mass_properties`, `inertia`, `mass_and_inertia`). Area, volume, centroid and the inertia tensor are boundary integrals over the exact surfaces, in each surface's parameters, taken about the middle of the body. Each face's boundary is integrated with Gauss–Legendre panels, doubled until two sums in a row agree to 1e-12 (piece by piece of the boundary, and within a piece panel by panel where its sums disagree, within a work budget per face); a face that does not settle is an error, never a guess. On a rational surface each inner integral is summed in the parameter of the Möbius map that evens out the weights at its ends (2026-10-17). A panel ends wherever the integrand has a kink: at an edge's knots, where the edge crosses a knot line of its spline surface, and where an edge within the tolerance of a spline surface runs off its domain and its points are projected onto the border (2026-10-15). Poles of spheres, cones and spline surfaces, seams of surfaces that close on themselves and faces that are their whole surface each have an explicit rule. Faces are measured on several threads and summed in face order.

**Transforms** (`Body::transformed`): rigid motions and mirrors map every curve and surface exactly and keep every entity id; `Body::bbox` is a conservative box from the exact geometry, and `Body::held_bbox` one that boxes a face on a sphere or torus by what it holds (below); `merge` puts the shells of several bodies into one.

## Findings and history

> Moved verbatim from `docs/architecture.md` on 2026-10-15. Section numbers (§4.1, §5.3, …) in the text are those of that file before the split; [README.md](README.md) says where each one is now.

### From *Current state (2026-10-12)*

- **Topology:** `topo` has the B-rep (`Body` with vertices, edges, loops of coedges, faces, shells), the validity checker `check` (references, finite numbers, vertices on edges, closed loops, edges on their faces' surfaces, each edge of a solid used once each way, Euler–Poincaré with genus) and `mass_properties` (area, volume, centroid by boundary integrals over the exact geometry). Fourteen reference solids (six primitives and hemisphere, and eight with trimmed faces: holes, half walls, a spherical cap, a frustum, an ellipse edge, Bézier and NURBS roofs) match their closed forms at three scales and in tilted frames. `mass_properties` also measures the faces a revolution makes: torus faces bounded around the tube, around the axis or both (the surface is closed both ways, so the boundary integral is right only up to a whole number of times the entire torus, which the area settles), and faces whose boundary passes through a pole of a sphere or the apex of a cone (the point stands for a stretch of the edge of the parameter range, which is added). It still refuses, rather than gets wrong, a boundary through a collapsed edge of a Bézier or NURBS surface, a boundary that winds around an axis more than once, torus loops that wind around the axis and the tube at once, and a boundary that turns back on itself at a pole. Deviations from the plan below: parameter-space curves are not stored (recovered by projection), no per-entity tolerances, persistent names are made by `regen` (M6) and not stored in `topo`, the inertia tensor is `topo::inertia` (2026-10-15), and the checker does not test self-intersection or that each face is bounded by its loops.

#### 5.2 Topology (`topo`)

```text
Body ── Shell ── Face ── Loop ── Coedge ── Edge ── Vertex
                  │                 │        │        │
               Surface           Curve2    Curve3   Point     (+ orientation flags, tolerances, persistent names)
```

- A **body** is a set of shells: a *solid* (closed, oriented shells), a *sheet* (open faces) or a *wire* (edges only).
- A **face** is a surface trimmed by loops; a **coedge** is the use of an edge by a face, with a direction and a parameter-space curve.
- Storage is a vector per kind with `u32` ids; a body is shared whole (`Arc`). *(Planned: arena storage shared part by part between a body and the one made from it, parameter-space curves on coedges, and tolerances on entities; the diagram above shows the plan. Names are kept by `regen`, not in the body.)*
- **Genus** (`topo::genus`): from Euler–Poincaré, `V − E + F − R − 2H = 2 (S − G)`. `R` is, per face, its loops beyond the first (−1 for a face without loops), which counts every face as a disc with holes. A face on a torus that still has the torus's handle (the whole torus, or the torus less the discs inside its loops) is not one and takes 2 off (`H`). A torus face with a loop around the axis or around the tube is a band, and one inside its loops is a patch: neither counts. The hole a drill leaves in a torus and the plug it takes out have the same loops walked the other way, so which of the two a face is, is read off the area its loops enclose in parameter space. A spline surface that closes on itself is not known to.
- **Validity checker** (`topo::check`): Euler–Poincaré, every edge of a solid used by exactly two coedges with opposite directions, loops closed, coedge curves on their surface within tolerance, vertices on their edges, consistent orientation (self-intersection is not a level of `check`: it is the mesh check of the next bullet). Every kernel operation is tested to return bodies that pass it.
- **Self-intersection, as built (2026-10-13):** `topo::check` does not test it. The test is `tess::body_self_intersections(&Body, &SelfCheckParams) -> SelfCheck`, in `tess` because it works on the mesh and so stays independent of the kernel it checks: it tessellates the body (chordal tolerance 1/1000 of its diagonal), finds the triangles that meet without being neighbours (§6), and reports crossings and overlaps between faces, each with a depth and with the margin `deviation(f) + deviation(g) + 2 × tolerance` that the approximation alone could explain (`deviation` is the chordal tolerance, or 0 for a plane bounded by straight edges, whose mesh is the face). A finding deeper than its margin is *certain*; one within it is reported as such and proves nothing (tangent faces, a ball resting on a plane). Findings within the margin between curved faces are looked at again with a mesh four times finer, twice by default. Flat faces within twice the modelling tolerance of one plane that share area are an overlap. Shells that do not cross are checked for where they lie, by the winding number of the other shells at a point of each: an outward shell must be in no material, a cavity in material. It is a test oracle (`crates/testkit/tests/self_intersection.rs`, [`testing.md`](../testing.md) oracle 9, which also lists what it cannot see) and, since 2026-10-15, a step of regenerating every operation that can make such a body, near what the operation changed (§5.5, *Never through itself*); it is not a level of `check`, and the kernel operations do not run it themselves (`kernel` and `tess` share a layer). It also reports where a shell touches itself, on the B-rep (§5.5). Run over the boolean results of the corpus and the random runs (2026-10-13) it found nothing in 39,834 of them.
- **Mass properties:** volume, surface area, centroid and inertia tensor, computed from the exact B-rep (surface integrals), independent of the tessellator. Volume and centroid are taken about the middle of the body, not the origin of the model (2026-10-13): a small body far from the origin is then measured as well as the same body at the origin. These are the backbone of the test oracles. As built (`topo::mass_properties`: area, volume, centroid): each face is a boundary integral in its surface's parameters, and the result does not depend on how the surface is parametrised. A sphere face is measured in a frame chosen for its boundary, not in the surface's own (one pole in the middle of the boundary, the other as far from it as it gets); on a cone the integral runs from the apex whenever the boundary comes near it. Every boundary integral is computed with half the panels its edges need, with all of them, and with twice as many until two sums in a row agree to 1e-12; a face for which they never do is an error (`Unsupported`), as is a face that lies outside its own loops (`UnboundedFace`). Only plane faces bounded by lines, circles and ellipses skip that check: their sums are exact. On a spline surface every panel, across the face and along its boundary, ends at the surface's knot lines (and an edge's panels at its own knots too), and the panels across the face are doubled along with the boundary's; a face that holds a pole of a spline surface of revolution is integrated from that pole. **A boundary through a pole of a freeform surface** (a border of a Bézier or NURBS patch that is one point: every control point of it the same, to 1e-9 of the net's size; `geom::pole`) is measured since 2026-10-14. Such a border is a whole side of the parameter range that no edge of the body stands for, as at a cone's apex: with the inner integral running from the pole the integral is zero all along it (the integrand is smooth up to the pole; the normal's length goes to zero there, which is what makes the area element integrable), and an edge that passes through the pole on its way is cut there. A boundary through the poles at *both* ends (the face a spline sweeps that starts and ends on the axis runs from pole to pole between two meridians and has no other edge) has no inner integral that vanishes at both: the face is divided along a line of constant parameter between the poles, each side is integrated from its own pole, and the two are joined by the integral over the rectangle between the places where the boundary crosses that line (found by bisection along the edges). Poles on borders of constant u exchange the roles of u and v. Held to closed forms (`crates/topo/tests/mass_poles.rs`: wedges, octants, caps and halves of a spline ball, cones with a ruled spline side, with the poles either way, to 2e-15 at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6 in two frames), to Pappus for the dome and the drop (`crates/kernel/tests/spline_sweeps.rs`, 1e-9), and all 32 patches of the teapot to their double integral (24 before). A face that passes through no pole is measured exactly as before (the printed worst errors of every suite of `topo` are the same to the last digit). Declined by name: a boundary through two poles that also crosses the seam of a surface that closes on itself. **A spline surface that closes on itself** (its control net ends on the row it starts with, on clamped knots; `geom` gives it no period, so the measure finds it from the net) is followed since 2026-10-14, closed in u, in v, both ways or to a point, with or without seam edges. A seam line of constant u adds nothing to `−∮ G du`; one of constant v does, so where the boundary jumps over the seam in v the inner integral runs along u instead (`∮ H dv`), and where it jumps both ways the loops are unrolled as on a torus and the area settles the whole number of surfaces. Loops that only walk seam edges there and back make the face its whole surface (also on a sphere and a torus, which were refused), and loops that run the wrong way round are the rest of a surface that closes all around. Until then a face that closes in v was measured wrong without an error. Faces that do not go over a seam are measured to the same bits as before. Held to the closed forms of a NURBS cylinder, a vase (Pappus), an egg and a NURBS torus with windows across its seams, to 1e-12 at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6, and to the mesh (`crates/tess/tests/closed_freeform.rs`; `crates/topo/tests/mass_seams.rs`). On a freeform surface the nodes along one piece of an edge are projected each from where the last one landed (`Surface::project_from`, a few evaluations instead of a search over the whole surface), and the search is run only where the short way does not land on a foot of the node: within 1e-5 of the body's size and square to the surface, which is the same point the search finds. A spline edge of eight knot spans or more (the curve in which two surfaces meet, as the kernel fits it) is summed first with the eight-node rule on one panel per span and then with the sixteen-node one, before its panels double.
- **Moving, mirroring, merging** (`topo::transform`): `Body::transformed` maps a body by an isometry exactly (no curve or surface changes kind; scaling and shear are errors) and keeps every entity id. Curves keep their parametrisation, so edges are untouched. A mirror turns right-handed things left-handed: frames become `(Tx, Ty, −Tz)`, every loop is walked the other way round, and faces on planes and freeform surfaces toggle `reversed`, while cylinders, cones (half-angle negated), spheres and tori keep their outward normal and get `v` negated. `Body::bbox` is a conservative box from the exact geometry (tight for planes, cylinders and cones with analytic edges; the whole surface for spheres and tori, which `Body::held_bbox` boxes by what the face holds; the control net for freeform). `merge` puts the shells of several bodies into one and reports the id offsets; it does not check that they are disjoint. These are what the transform, mirror and pattern features build on.

### A fitted edge that runs off its surface (2026-10-15)

*Gate 60*: the deep check of `crates/engine/tests/blend_findings.rs › the_leading_edges_and_tips_of_the_blades` refused face 1 of the impeller's Blade and Splitter ("a boundary integral that does not settle") once the boundary was refined part by part ([`booleans.md`](booleans.md) › *A face with many holes*); with the whole-face run before it the same faces were measured. Instrumented: the face is the lofted blade's side, bounded by six pieces; four settle at their second sum, and the two fillet contact edges (cubic fits, within 8e-7 of the side, ending on its border `v = 1` tangent to it) never do. From one doubling to the next their sums moved by 1e-12 to 3e-11 of their size, unevenly, up to the seventh doubling (1.33072415029063 at d1 … 029058 at d7 for one of them); more inner panels changed nothing (the same digits at 2, 8 and 32 per span), nor did projecting every node by the full search. The cause: over the last half percent of such an edge the foot of its points lies beyond the border, so the projection holds them on it, and the parameters along the edge have a kink there, inside a panel; a sum over a panel across a kink converges too slowly to settle. The whole-face run had the same kink and settled by margin only: 7.6e-13 against 1e-12 at its third sum in the body, and on the face alone as a sheet (another middle) two of the three faces were refused by it too. Part by part each piece is held to its own size, and the budget ran out first (42 M evaluations at the fifth sum of a piece).

The fix is general: the panels of an edge end where it runs off the domain of a Bézier or NURBS surface, as they end where it crosses a knot line (`knot_crossings` › `run_off`): between two places along the edge, one within 1e-9 of the domain's width of the border and one clear of it, the kink is found by bisection, and it is one only where the point beyond it is held there: its parameter is the border's to rounding and it is off the surface (an edge that only reaches the border, at its end or tangent to it, or comes to it next to a pole, where the parameters say little, gets no extra panel end, and its sums are the bits they were; a border that is a pole is not looked at). The bisection projects with a walk of its own, so the places looked at for knot lines are projected as before. The criterion and the work bounds are unchanged. The three faces now settle at their second sum, each piece to 1e-14 of its size, in 0.9 to 1.5 M evaluations of the integrand (refused before after 22 to 43 M), and measure alike turned and moved a thousand units away to 1e-12 (`crates/kernel/tests/mass_run_off.rs`, the three faces as sheets in `crates/kernel/tests/fixtures/mass_run_off/`, every number in its shortest round-trip form; both tests fail without the fix). Against the slow sums without it the new value is where they were going: the piece above at d7 without the cut, 1.33072415029058, against 1.33072415029063 with it; the whole Blade face 32.10061442710017 against 32.10061442705436 settled by margin before (1.4e-12 apart).

### Rational weights far apart (2026-10-17)

*The boolean checklist* ([`booleans.md`](booleans.md) › *Rational faces*): a face on a rational patch whose weights are far apart did not settle (the plate with its top as a bilinear patch with the weights 1, 100, 100, 1: refused), and, found here, settled *wrong*: the quarter rod's rational twin with its weights skewed a thousandfold along the axis (a Möbius reparametrisation of the span, the same cylinder) measured 7.4e-6 short in volume, and at ten thousand 0.17. Two causes.

*The inner integrals.* A rational surface along a line of constant parameter is `A(t) / w(t)` within a knot span, and where the weights at a stretch's ends are far apart `w` has a zero just outside the stretch, by the light end: the integrand changes on that scale and uniform panels converge slowly. Worse, on a spline surface the inner panels doubled with the boundary's only up to 64 a stretch, while the boundary's doubled seven times: the last two sums had the same inner panels and agreed in what those missed. Fixed in two ways. The inner panels now double to the last (`MAX_SPLINE_INNER_PANELS`, 2^7). And each stretch is summed in the parameter `s` of the Möbius map `t = start + (end − start) β s / ((1 − s) + β s)` with `β = (w(start) / w(end))^{1/q}`, `q` the degree in that direction (`mass::moebius`; the surface's denominator comes from `NurbsSurface::weight_at`, new): in `s` the Bernstein coefficients of the denominator are `w_i β^i`, so its ends are equal. It is a change of variable, so the integral is the same; only where the nodes fall changes. Where the weights have a product form (`w_ij = a_i b_j`: every surface of revolution) it takes the skew out exactly, and the skewed twins sum as the even ones do (the rod: 1.5 s and wrong before, 6 to 10 ms and right to the last digit now). Equal end weights (every polynomial surface) keep their stretches' nodes as they were; their sums still change in the last digits, by the inner panels that now double to the last and by the panels refined one by one below (the benchmark's measures by at most 5.6e-15 of their size, the rotor's blade by 1.7e-12, within its rounding allowance). Weights that stay further apart than 1e4 within a span after the corner weights are evened by Möbius maps (what no reparametrisation removes: `mass::uneven_weights`) are refused before anything is summed (`MassError::UnevenWeights`, naming the face and the ratio; `MAX_UNEVEN`): the measure is held to a thousand and refuses ten thousand by sums that do not settle, but from 1e7 the plate's sums settled wrong (22 for 24), on bodies whose edges no longer lie on their face (from 1e5).

*The boundary integral.* Where no reparametrisation helps (the plate: `w₀₀ w₁₁ / (w₀₁ w₁₀) = 1 / h²` in every one), the lines of constant u fan out from a heavy corner, and seen from the boundary integral almost all of the face lies on a stretch of the opposite edge `1 / h²` of it long next to that corner. Doubling every panel of the edge seven times reaches `1 / 128` (the sums at h = 100: 6.3, 6.6, 7.1, 8.0, 9.3, 10.6, 11.6, 11.93 of 12, past the budget). The panels of a piece are now refined adaptively (`cell_by_cell`): each panel of a piece at its coarsest is a cell; a round sums every open cell's halves and holds them first all together (the piece's sums, as before) and, where those disagree, each cell to its own halves; cells that agree are taken (the finer sum) and not summed again, the halves of the others are open in the next round. Every comparison is of sums that differ in everything refined: up to the seventh halving the inner panels double with each, past it each finer cell is also summed with half its inner panels and must agree with that (and its children take half as many inner panels where that agreed, twice as many where not, so a polynomial line costs two panels a stretch). The allowance of a cell is the share of its size its piece's is ([`CONVERGED`] and the rounding share of the piece's sums), so the cells' allowances add up to the piece's. Rules, each general: a panel is halved at most 40 times (`MAX_LEVEL`: sixteen nodes in 2⁻⁴⁰ of a piece are a few dozen units in the last place apart); one of no width sums to nothing, one too narrow to halve stays as it is with its inner panels refined; a piece with more open cells at once than it had panels at the seventh doubling (its first panels times 2^7) is given up, since its sums then scatter wherever they are looked at (a boundary through a sphere's other pole: refused at 1.15 M evaluations, about a fifty-eighth of the budget; `a_face_refined_part_by_part_stops_at_its_cap`, whose bound was a hundredth when the doublings ran out first: expectation changed). ### The box of a face on a sphere or a torus (2026-10-15)

*Bike B4*: the default framing of `view.render` (the scene's box, `Body::bbox`) of a chain stay, a tube of radius 7 swept along an arc of a circle of radius 1332, was 2671 mm across for a stay of 455 mm: a face on a torus contributed the box of the whole torus, and one on a sphere that of the whole sphere.

`Body::held_bbox` (2026-10-15) boxes such a face by what it holds, and the scene's box (`engine::scene::bodies_box`, the default framing of `view.render`, `view.pick` and `replay`) is made of it. `Body::bbox` and `Body::face_bbox` keep the whole surface: with the tight box in `Body::bbox`, `crates/kernel/tests/blend_cylinders.rs › both_tip_edges_of_a_cambered_blade` failed at scale 1e-3 with r 0.05 ("a point too close to a face boundary to tell inside from outside", inside the fillet's boolean), because `boolean::section` clips the curves where two faces meet to the bodies' boxes grown by `16 tol + 0.013 × diagonal`, and a smaller box put the clip where something else was. That margin is the boolean agents' to make independent of how loose the operands' boxes are; until then the booleans keep the box they were measured with, and nothing pays the walk's cost but a picture. A picture asks for the scene's box three or four times (its size, its tolerance, its scene); `scene::held_box` keeps each body's box for as long as the body lives (regeneration hands on the same `Arc` for every body an edit did not touch), so it is worked out once per body. On the turbofan (37 bodies, 6405 faces, 64 of them on spheres and tori) `held_bbox` of all bodies took 9.2 ms against 8.5 ms for `bbox` (release, best of 5, load 7), and the frame's diagonal went from 508 to 406 mm. Along an axis `e`, the greatest `e · p` over a face is reached on its boundary (which the edges add, exactly for lines, circles and ellipses) or at an inner point where `e` is normal to the surface: `centre ± r e` on a sphere; on a torus the four points on the section of the tube in the plane of the axis and `e`, or, where `e` is along the axis, the circles along the top and the bottom of the tube. Each such point that the extent does not already reach adds its coordinate along that axis only, unless it is certainly not in the face (`certainly_outside`). A circle's points stand for the whole circle: if one of them is certainly outside, either the face holds none of the circle or its boundary meets the circle and so reaches the same height.

*Whether a point is in the face* is decided by walking from it along circles of the surface (`walk`): along the first one, the first place where the face's boundary crosses it tells, because the face lies to the left of its boundary seen from outside: beyond the crossing, the point is outside; back towards it, inside. Paths: on a sphere, great circles through the point and the middle of an edge of the boundary (which they cross there) and two turned away from the coordinate planes and their diagonals (a walk along a boundary that lies in its plane has no answer); on a torus, the circle about the axis and the section of the tube through the point, and to the middle of each of three edges a path along a section and then a circle about the axis, or the other way round. Crossings are found on the edges' samples, with the edges' ends taken as their vertices (so that two edges, or a closed edge with itself, see the same side where they meet and a crossing there is counted once), and then sharpened by regula falsi. A plane cuts a torus in two circles; a crossing counts for the walk's circle when it is clearly nearer to it than to the other (within a quarter of their distance apart). No answer, and the point is kept, where no path meets the boundary, the first crossing grazes the path (the boundary within about 6° of running along it), lies at the path's start, or is not clearly on either circle. The first path that answers decides. Faces without loops (whole spheres and tori) keep the box of the whole surface; faces on freeform surfaces keep their control net.

Measured: `crates/topo/tests/transform.rs › faces_on_spheres_and_tori_are_boxed_by_what_they_hold` (pipe elbows through 0.3, 1.9, 4 and 6 rad, a sphere octant through a pole, caps below and above the equator; three scales, two frames, nine isometries including mirrors: the box holds 513 × 513 samples of the face and the edges, and is within 2e-5 of their box, the sag of the samples); `crates/engine/tests/view_render.rs › the_default_frame_is_the_box_of_what_the_faces_hold` (the stay of the finding, a 70° turn of a profile with an arc, a 50° lune of a sphere: the frame holds the mesh's box and is larger by at most the mesh's tolerance). Cost: about 0.1 ms for a face whose box the boundary does not already give (best of 50 on the elbow, octant and cap), nothing for one it does.

Length and reach are taken at 128 places along the edge (the reach widened by the longest step): an estimate, as the rest of the bound is. *Held to* (`sew.rs` › `the_mass_bound_covers_the_gaps_of_a_sewn_body`): the block, the block less a cylinder, the block with a ball and the domed block, every plane face moved within its own plane by up to 30 and 300 tolerances (the surfaces, and so the solid, stay the original's; only the boundaries are off) and sewn, three seeds, three scales: in all 72 the measured volume and area lie within the gap term alone plus the original's own bound (worst 1.7 % and 1.2 % of it). *Finding*: the integrator's own allowance on such a body is already about a sixth of the gap term (its sums settle more slowly where a face's boundary does not close in its parameters), and covered every case alone; the gap term is the one derived from the tolerances, not from how the sums converged.

**Fits that miss half the tolerance, not done.** The owed item was a boolean whose intersection curve cannot be fitted within half the tolerance kept as a loose edge. No such refusal was found to turn: freeform booleans (a domed block against a tilted cylinder, a ball, a torus) at tolerances from 1e-6 to 1e-11 of the size, a domed block cut by a plane down to 6e-6 below its top, upright or tilted, and two crossing cylinders whose radii differ by 0.1 down to 1e-8 all fit (below 1e-12 the box check refuses first: "finer than rounding allows"). A strip whose NURBS surface ripples 100 to 4000 times, cut along it, is refused, but by the marching cap ("needs more points than the cap allows"), and a coarser tolerance (4, 16 and 64 times) does not lift it: a working tolerance would not turn that refusal into a result. The retry at a working tolerance waits for a model that needs it.

### One slow face on every thread (2026-10-21)

*Finding* (the Garabit valley showcase, 2026-10-21): `document.measure` and `document.inspect` of the Garabit valley (112 bodies, 12,799 faces) took 33 s on top of regenerating, 31.7 s of it one face: the cut terrain's top, a bicubic NURBS surface of 55 × 68 knots trimmed by the curves the cut fitted (cubic splines of 1,566, 5,376 and 8,242 knots). Every node along those edges runs an inner integral across the thirty-odd knot stretches between it and the reference, so the face is about 7,900 panels of sixteen such nodes at its first refinement; all of it ran on one thread, because faces were the unit of sharing out (a body of five faces is one thread). The next slowest, a pier with one spline face among 73, took 4.3 s for the same reason.

*Fix*: the panels of one face are summed on as many threads as are idle (`boundary_integrals`, for every face refined panel by panel on a spline surface: the first sums of each panel and each round of halved panels in `cells_of`). Each run of a panel is summed on a copy of the face's work and comes back with the work it took and the work it had spent at each look at the budget; the sums are taken in panel order, and where the face would have been given up, had the panels been summed one after the other, is replayed from those looks (`replay`), so the sums, the work counted and the place a face is refused are the same bits on any machine and with any number of threads. Within work already shared out (the faces of a body) a face takes the threads that are idle (a count of busy threads, `BUSY`), so the last slow face of a body gets the machine as the others finish. *Measured* (release, 14 threads, a loaded machine): the terrain's body 31.7 → 8.0 s, the pier with one spline face 4.3 → 1.0 s, the valley's `document.measure` after regeneration 49.8 → 9.9 s (load 10), the Garabit pier's 2.1 → 1.7 s; `document.measure` and `document.inspect` of both give the same bytes as before. Held to: `runs_summed_apart_are_given_up_where_one_after_the_other_would_be` (the replay of the budget and the order of the items), every measure test of `topo` and the benchmark's work counts. On an analytic surface, and for a single run, the runs are summed one after the other on the face's own work as before (no copies, no recorded looks). Once the runs done so far took more than the face had left, no more are started; a run the replay reaches all the same is summed then, so a face that will be refused stops near its budget even on one thread. An internal error of a face (a panel the samples do not cover) is now found before any panel is summed, so it may come before a refusal for the budget that came first before; both are errors, and deterministic. *Still open*: the inner integrals themselves are the cost (the same per node as before); a face whose region spans many knot stretches would gain from summing the knot cells inside it as a whole rather than from its boundary.

### Threads that become idle are taken up (2026-11-04)

*Finding*: the area of the fuel tank's shell (`docs/examples/tank.json`, `variable.measure skin`) took 31 s; one face (the hollow behind the crown, 2,996 panels in a round of halved panels) took 29 s of it. Its panels were shared out over the threads idle when its round began (`in_order` counted them once, at its start): begun while the other faces still had every thread, the round ran on one thread long after the others were done. *Fix*: the thread that shares the items out starts another helper between its own items whenever a thread of the machine is idle (counted busy before it starts, so two sharers do not take the same one) and more than `each` items are left; the results go to their slots as before, so nothing changes but the time. The area now takes 21 s (laptop, 14 threads, load about 4): what is left is the work itself, about 150 CPU seconds of evaluations in that face's last round, its inner integrals refined to 128 panels per knot span of a fitted offset surface (not touched: a coarser rule changes the bits).
