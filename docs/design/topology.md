# 3DCraft design: Topology, checks and measures

The boundary representation and what is decided and measured on it (`craft3d-topo`), with the mesh-based checks of `craft3d-tess` that stand beside it. Methods 6 and 7 of the map ([`../architecture.md`](../architecture.md) §5).

## Method

**The B-rep.** A `Body` holds vectors of vertices, edges, loops (of coedges), faces and shells, referred to by `u32` ids; it is a solid (closed, oriented shells), a sheet or a wire. A face lies on a `Surface` and is to the left of its loops seen from outside (`reversed` turns its normal); an edge lies on a `Curve3` between two vertices; a coedge is the use of an edge by a loop, with a direction. Parameter-space curves are not stored; the document's tolerance is passed to every check, and a vertex or an edge may carry a looser one of its own (*Tolerant entities*, below). A body is shared whole (`Arc`), not part by part.

**Tolerant entities** (since 2026-10-21; the decision of 2026-10-18 in [`../decisions.md`](../decisions.md), option (4), a tolerance per entity). `Body::tolerances` is a sparse, ordered table (`topo::Tolerances`) of the vertices and edges that are looser than the document's tolerance; an entity not in it has the document's, and every body 3DCraft builds itself has an empty table (written out by `Bits` as nothing, so such a body is bit for bit what it was before). A vertex's tolerance is a ball the ends of its edges' curves lie in; an edge's is a tube the surfaces of its faces pass through along its whole curve. The tolerance that holds is the larger of the entity's and the document's, and a vertex is at least as loose as each edge that ends at it. A cap on any entity's tolerance is document data (`doc::Tolerance::loosest`, `None` = `topo::DEFAULT_LOOSEST` (1000) times the linear tolerance); above it the check reports the entity and an operation refuses. The API, for import and sewing:

- `Body::vertex_tolerance(v)`, `Body::edge_tolerance(e)`: the entity's own, `None` for the document's; `vertex_tolerance_at(v, linear)`, `edge_tolerance_at(e, linear)`: the one that holds.
- `Body::set_vertex_tolerance(v, t)`, `Body::set_edge_tolerance(e, t)` (`Result`, refuses a tolerance that is not positive and finite or an entity that does not exist; setting an edge raises its two vertices to at least `t`); `clear_vertex_tolerance`, `clear_edge_tolerance`.
- `Body::is_tolerant()`, `Body::working_tolerance(linear)`: the largest of the table and the document's, the tolerance the kernel works at on the body.
- `topo::measure_edge(body, e, tol, cap)`: how far the edge's curve is from the surface of each face that uses it, *proven* over the whole edge (`certify::curve_on_surface`, targets doubling from twice the farthest of five places, up to the cap); `topo::measure_vertex(body, v)`: how far the ends of its edges are, and at least their tolerances.
- `topo::record_edge(body, e, tol, cap)`, `topo::record_vertex(body, v, tol, cap)`: measure and put it in the table (`RECORD_MARGIN` = 1.125 larger, so that the same body moved still passes), nothing when it is within the document's; refused above the cap. Import records what it measures this way; it never moves geometry.
- `topo::check_capped(body, tol, cap)`, `topo::check_remembered_capped`: `check` with the document's cap (`check` uses the default, `craft3d_math::DEFAULT_LOOSEST` times the linear tolerance).
- `topo::record_vertices(body, vertices, tol, cap)`: `record_vertex` for many, in one pass over the edges. A gap within the cap is recorded at most at the cap: the margin never pushes it over (since 2026-10-22).

**Sewing** (`topo::sew(body, tol, distance, cap)`, since 2026-10-22; "merges topology and measures gaps; it never moves geometry"). Only *free* edges (used once by a loop) are sewn. Two free edges are a candidate pair when their end vertices are within `distance` of each other, either way round, each of `SAMPLES` (7) places inside each lies within `distance` of the other's curve (so two arcs between the same points, or an arc and its chord, are told apart), and the two uses run against each other, as the two uses of an edge of an oriented boundary do, by two different faces. The samples only choose candidates; acceptance is proven (below). Candidates are taken closest first (the larger of the gaps measured, then edge ids), each only while both edges are still free, joining its ends would not make one vertex of the two ends of any edge (sewing never collapses an edge), and neither edge has another candidate within the ambiguity margin, twice the largest gap any free edge has to its nearest candidate plus the tolerance (at most `distance`): an edge that could go either way (a slot narrower than the gaps) stays open and a solid with it is refused. The lower edge of a pair is kept with its curve; vertices joined through pairs are one, the lowest with its point; faces keep their ids; shells joined by a pair become one. Then, proven over each whole curve (`measure_curve`): the kept edge on both its faces' surfaces and the dropped edge on the same surfaces (its face's boundary is now the kept edge), the larger recorded on the kept edge; every joined vertex measured against the kept edges' ends, the dropped edges' ends and the points of the vertices merged into it. A gap beyond the document's tolerance is recorded, one beyond `cap` refuses (`SewError::Gap`, naming the entity and the distance). Free edges left are reported with the distance to the nearest free edge (`Sewn::open`, `worst_open`); a solid with one is the caller's to refuse. Free edges are found through a grid of their end vertices (cells `2 distance`, ordered maps); the ends looked at (`MAX_PROBES`, 2^27) and the pairs tested (`MAX_CANDIDATES`, 2^22) are capped, so thousands of edges at one point stay bounded. A body without free edges comes back as it is. `shells_alone` (and so `body.split`) keeps the tolerances of the entities it takes. **T-junctions** (since slice 3, `topo/src/sew_split.rs`): before pairs are sought, a free edge `A` along which two or more shorter free edges of other faces run end to end is split. Its *stations* are the places inside it (beyond the distance from both its ends) where an end of another face's free edge lies within the distance of its curve, one per place; the split is taken only when they are proven a chain: between each two neighbouring stations (and `A`'s ends) runs one free edge of another face with its ends there, its seven places inside within the distance of that stretch of `A` and the stretch's of it, its use against `A`'s, the ends of the two partners meeting at a station within the distance of each other, and no free edge runs along the whole of `A`. A piece keeps `A`'s curve over part of its range; the new vertex at a station is the curve's point there and goes into the shorter edges' ends (the lowest vertex's point is kept), so nothing moves and every gap is measured as any pair's is. The split stays only when every piece is then sewn; otherwise the edge is left whole and the body sewn again without it (at most twice). A split attempt that sewing refuses (a gap above the cap) or that leaves more edges open than sewing the body without any split is dropped, and the body is sewn as it is: a split never turns a sewing into a refusal, nor leaves more open (compared by count; the body is then sewn twice). `Sewn::split` lists each edge split with the edges its pieces became; STEP import counts them (`splitEdges`). Ends are kept in a grid of cells at least twice the distance and a sixty-fourth of their box wide (ordered maps, looked up by box); every cell and end looked at counts against `MAX_STATION_LOOKS` (2^24), and when that runs out nothing is split at all and sewing goes on as without T-junctions, never a refusal; an edge with more than `MAX_STATIONS` (1024) stations is left whole. *Conservative, left whole*: a seam of a closed curve that does not lie at a station (the stretch over the seam has no partner); an end of a third face touching `A`'s interior where no chain runs (a corner on the edge) adds a station whose stretches have no partner, so `A` stays whole and open, the solid refused.

**Validity** (`topo::check`): references in range, finite numbers, vertices on their edges, closed loops, edges on their faces' surfaces within the tolerance, every edge of a solid used once each way, Euler–Poincaré with the genus (`topo::genus`, which knows torus faces that keep a handle), per shell (since 2026-10-17: a shell's faces hang together through edges, no edge is in two shells, and in a solid of several shells each one fits Euler–Poincaré alone, `topo::shell_genera`; which shells are the outsides of lumps and which their cavities is geometry, `kernel::lumps`, [`booleans.md`](booleans.md) › *Lumps and cavities*), and on planes that a face's loops lie inside each other as a face's do (`topo::stranded`, since 2026-10-16: in the plane's frame turned to the outward normal, the other loops wind zero times around a point of an anticlockwise loop and once around a point of a clockwise one; the loops followed as polylines within 1e-4 of each conic's largest radius (bounded) and splines in 256 steps (their distance measured, not bounded), one point of each loop used, where it is clear of every other loop by that plus ten tolerances, so two loops that cross are found only where that point lies on the wrong side; each polyline keeps its box, so a face with many holes costs about a box test per pair of loops). For a sheet (since 2026-10-18): every edge is used once (an edge of its boundary) or once each way (else `EdgeUses`, `EdgeUsedTwiceSameWay`), no more than two boundary edges of a shell end at a vertex (`BoundaryPinch`: the boundary touches itself there), and each shell fits Euler–Poincaré with its boundary, `V − E + F − R − 2H = 2 (1 − G) − B` with `B` its loops of boundary edges (`topo::boundary_loops`; `ShellEuler`); two shells that share a vertex touch, which is geometry's to refuse, as for lumps. Since 2026-10-30 every face's loops must also bound a region in its parameters, on every surface (*Loops that bound a region*, below), and since 2026-11-14 each of them must be a simple closed curve, so that the face is its region counted once (*Loops that are simple*, below). It does not test self-intersection or that a face is bounded by its loops: those are the mesh checks below and the measure.

**Loops that bound a region** (`topo::region_fault`, `Defect::LoopsBoundNoRegion`, since 2026-10-30). Read in the surface's `(u, v)`, sampled per edge (a line in one step on a plane, two elsewhere, a conic in steps of at most π/8, a spline in eight; each step's middle projected too, its distance from the chord the polyline's error; a spline edge in eight times the least power of two such that every half step is shown to keep to what the walk can follow, by two things. (a) On a surface that closes about an axis, a cylinder, a cone, a sphere or a torus, its angle about the axis moves by less than a quarter turn over the half step, so the walk below goes round with it however many turns one edge makes: shown by the first of (1) the convex hull of the control points of the knot spans the half step meets in a wedge about the axis narrower than a quarter turn (then clear of the axis); (2) for a polynomial spline, the longest of its derivative's control points (a bound on its speed) times the half step's width over the least distance from the axis the stretch can have (its middle's less half that length), less than a quarter turn (one evaluation of the curve, counted against an operation's work, `geom::allowance`, only where (1) does not show it); (3) the Bézier pieces of the knot spans the half step meets, each made from its own span alone (`NurbsCurve::bezier_piece`: the degree's square, where the pieces of the whole curve, `bezier_pieces`, cost the square of its knots) and cut to the stretch by de Casteljau's algorithm, in such a wedge, for a stretch that meets no more than 16 spans (over more, the pieces say little that the control points did not, and the stretch is halved); past the ends of the curve's domain, where (1) and (2) say nothing, the first and the last piece are cut as far as the edge goes, the curve being that piece continued there, within two limits: not past the END of a domain that ends on a span of no width (knots not clamped, the end knot repeated), where `NurbsCurve::eval` continues that empty span's polynomial and not the last piece's, and only while the rounding of the cut grows by less than 2²⁰ (each of the degree's rounds of the cut multiplies it by `1 + 2s` for a cut `s` span widths before the span: a billionth past is shown at any degree, a whole width past up to degree 12); the points of a hull must keep clear of the axis by eight times what rounding can have moved them (16 units in the last place of the curve's largest coordinate for each control point of a span, 64 for a cubic, times the ratio of its largest weight to its least), so that a curve a rounding outside its hull as computed still turns about the axis as the hull says, to 7°, well within the half turn at which the chart would unwrap a step the wrong way; each looks at the points of a stretch only until they are too wide, bounds the movement over the whole stretch and so over any part of it, and each half step is halved only until it is shown. (b) In the chart of a plane, a cylinder, a cone or a sphere (not a torus: below), the edge's image runs one way over the half step (since 2026-11-14, *A strip that spirals about a point of the chart*): its direction keeps within a fan narrower than a half turn (three eighths of a turn as computed; a leg's direction is read to an eighth of a radian, and a leg shorter than sixteen roundings of its points has none, so the stretch is not shown). The direction of a spline with positive weights is, at each of its points, a sum with weights that are not negative of the legs between the control points of its span (a polynomial spline's derivative is a spline on those legs; a rational one's is a sum over pairs `i < j` of `Pj − Pi` times `Ni Nj' − Ni' Nj`, which is not negative, the basis being totally positive), so of the legs of the spans a half step meets, as (1) reads them, or of its Bézier pieces cut to it, (3); and the chart takes a direction `w` at a point to one of its own by a linear map there: on a plane `(w·x, w·y)`, the same everywhere; about an axis `u' = w·e / ρ` (`e` the unit vector round the axis at the point's angle, `ρ` its distance from the axis) and `v'` as the surface has it (a cylinder `w·z`; a cone the ruling's share of `w·z` and of `w·r`, `r` the unit vector away from the axis, the stretch kept to the cone's own side of its apex; a sphere `(ρ w·z − h w·r) / (ρ² + h²)`, `h` the height). Over the stretch's reach (its range of angle about the axis, of `ρ` and of `h`: the ends of the hull's wedge, the nearest point's distance times the cosine of half the wedge, the furthest point's, the lowest and the highest; or what (2) shows about its middle) each leg's image lies in a box of such directions, whose four corners stand for it, and the half step is shown where all of them lie in the fan. Cheaper ways are tried first about an axis: every leg running round the axis one way at both ends of the reach, two products a leg (`u` then moves one way, which is all a helix needs, so a helix is followed in the steps its angle needs); and, where (2) shows the angle, the curve's own direction at the stretch's middle, which (2) evaluates anyway, running round the axis by more than the angle's range and the bound of its second derivative over the stretch's spans (the longest control point of its derivative's derivative) can take from it. The image over the half step and the half step's chord then both cross every line square to the fan's middle once, so together they wind about no point more than once, and only about points that lie between them: the chord of a half step hides no turn about any point of the chart. That is all (b) shows. The walk takes the chord of the whole step, from its start to its end, and reads the edge only at the step's middle, where the chords of its two halves meet: two halves that each run one way can together, with the whole step's chord, wind about a point twice while the middle lies on that chord, and what lies between a half step and its own chord is not measured at all (*A strip that spirals about a point of the chart*, Owed (3) and (4)). On a torus (b) is not asked: a loop with a spline edge there is one off which nothing is read, and its edges are shown about the axis alone (*A band of a torus between curves that wind about its tube*: the read of the tube angle, built three times, refused right faces each time). On a spline surface, whose chart is not known, none of this can be shown: there an edge is held to running forward along its stretch's chord in space over each half of its eight steps, and only as a reason not to follow it (one that does is followed in eight steps, as every spline edge was before; one that does not, a spiral of many turns as one edge, is not followed). The whole edge is looked at first, by its control points alone: an edge shown at one look, which is nearly every edge, keeps its eight steps, and that look, one pass over the edge's control points (as the key a face is remembered by makes one) that stops at the first point or leg that does not fit, is not counted. No edge is followed in steps that are not shown, and showing is bounded: an edge that needs more than 2¹² steps (a curve through the axis among them), and a face whose spline edges take more than `MOST_PROOF_WORK` (2¹⁷) units to show and to follow (a point looked at is one unit, a leg read one on a plane, a half round an axis and two where its box of directions is made, a piece made the square of its points, a step of an edge followed in more than eight 32), is not followed, so it passes, and is counted (`topo::step_proofs`, which tests read: a count, never a clock). A unit is 10 to 35 ns as measured, so showing and following a face's spline edges adds 2 ms as measured at its worst, and under 5 ms at the slowest unit seen, to its check at the most; since 2026-11-14, *A helix of many turns as one edge*), each point projected (on a freeform surface from the one before, checked, else from nothing) and moved by whole periods to the one before, a face's loops must be one loop counter-clockwise about the outward normal enclosing the most area and the others clockwise; on every surface but a plane (where `stranded_loops` holds it) every point of a hole clear of the other loops by their errors and ten tolerances must be wound once by the outer loop and by no other hole (holes placed by the whole periods that bring them over the outer loop); and no two steps may cross properly by more than the errors and ten tolerances read in parameters at the surface's smallest scale over the face. A fault of these found on a plane, a cylinder, a cone or a sphere is verified before it is claimed (since 2026-11-15, *What lies between an edge and its chord*, which has the two theorems and their proofs): the face is walked again in stretches each shown to run one way and to lie within a bound of its chord, and the fault is dropped only where the whole face is cleared by those bounds, which shows its loops bound a region; where it is not cleared within the work of a face, the walk's fault stands (or the fault the bounds show). A face the walk passes is not walked again, nor is a face on a torus or a spline surface: there the walk's fault stands as it is. Passed without a word: loops that wind around a period (bands), surfaces closed both ways, points near a pole or an apex, freeform surfaces that close or collapse (one that closes is not charted: a miss, never a false alarm; *A spline surface that closes, charted* has the charting that is parked), a loop whose area its error cannot tell from zero, more than `MOST_PER_FACE` (2¹⁸) points or `MOST_PAIRS` (2²⁴) units of work, a spline edge whose steps are not shown (above: a spline through or very near the axis, a single edge of more than about a hundred turns, an edge that turns back at a point by three eighths of a turn or more, on a spline surface an edge that does not run forward over each half of its eight steps, a face past `MOST_PROOF_WORK`); on a sphere, loops all clockwise (the sphere less some caps). Work: the loops near a hole's point come from a grid over their boxes, and the crossing search sweeps each band of v along u, so a perforated face costs about its points, not their square. Faces that passed are remembered (`regionless`, since 2026-10-31) by everything the rule reads of them, every bit: the reach (ten of the body's working tolerances), the surface and its sense, and each loop's coedges as their sense, curve and range; a face a boolean or a regeneration carries over unchanged is then not followed again (a hit compares the key word for word and charges the work the check took, so an operation counts the same either way; a fault is never remembered; 16 MB of keys at most, the oldest let go first; `forget_passed` lets go of them too). *Measured* (Strix, debug profile as the tests run, the kernel's boolean corpus run back to back on one quiet runner, Check 41175274421, a measurement-only switch since dropped): the corpus took 60.0 / 60.0 s without the rule, 62.8 / 61.4 s with it unremembered (+3.5 %) and 62.7 / 59.8 s remembered (+2 %); the time spent following faces over the whole run, on every thread, 15.5 s unremembered and 2.4 s remembered (of which about 2 s the corpus's own call of the rule on each result). The 87 s and 115 s read earlier were the runner's load, not the rule. A perforated tube (`a_perforated_tube_is_checked_in_time_about_its_holes`): 100, 400 and 1600 holes 0.4, 1.8 and 6.5 ms, remembered 0.02, 0.07 and 0.26 ms. The tessellator refuses such a face ("its loops do not bound a region"); the check says so for every producer before the body leaves it. `topo::face_holds` reads the same chart to say whether points lie on a face (the parity of a ray's crossings, bands included): the wrap refuses a profile that leaves its face.

**Loops that are simple** (`topo::loops_not_simple`, `Defect::LoopNotSimple`, since 2026-11-14; `crates/topo/src/simple.rs`). Asked of every face whose loops bound a region (the rule above, whose walk it reads, so a face is followed once for both): each loop is a simple closed curve on the surface, so the face is its region counted once. Held in this order, the first found reported with the face, the loop and what is wrong: (1) no edge goes round its circle or ellipse more than once (a range longer than a turn by more than ten tolerances along it); (2) no loop goes round a surface that closes more often than a simple closed curve can: from the walk (each point moved by whole periods to the one before), a loop that ends k periods from where it starts goes round k times, and on a cylinder, a cone or a sphere |k| ≤ 1 (a band of a cylinder has two loops, each once round), on a torus (p, q) of no common factor or (0, 0) (faces on a torus are walked for this rule and the next two, the region rule passing them; held only of a loop of circles and ellipses: for a spline edge nothing shows how far it moves about the tube from one point to the next, *A band of a torus between curves that wind about its tube*); (3) no loop winds about a point it encloses other than once its own way round: each loop that goes round no period is cut along eight lines of constant v across its box (`SCANLINES`), between two crossings a line's points are wound by the sum of the senses of the crossings to their right, and a point clear of the loop by its margin (twice its polyline's error and ten tolerances in parameters) wound neither 0 times nor once with the sign of the loop's area is a fault (verified on a plane, a cylinder, a cone and a sphere as the region rule's faults are, since 2026-11-15: dropped only where the whole face is cleared, every loop of it a simple closed curve of the sense a face's must have, no two meeting; not where that loop alone is one); (4) no two stretches of two different edges of the face's boundary are one curve along more than ten of the body's working tolerances (the boundary passing there twice). One edge used twice by one loop is not judged (since the gate of 2026-11-14): it is a cut of the face along an edge the face holds on both sides, a seam where the surface closes, a bridge joining two of its boundary curves (an annulus written as one loop, as sheet metal's collars and pans' rims are), or a spur (a dangling edge); each leaves the region and its measure as they are without the cut, and a bridge and a spur lie alike at one place of a plane, so nothing in the two uses tells a right body from a wrong one, and a rule that claims a fault only where it has shown one leaves them. Two stretches are one curve where they lie within the tighter of their two edges' own tolerances (the document's for an edge without one: each edge then lies within its own of the other) by a gap that is the same all along them to the rounding of their coordinates (64 units in the last place of the largest coordinate of their boxes): copies of one curve, or one offset by less than that tolerance. A face between one and ten tolerances wide is thin, not degenerate, and passes; a sharp tip of a face, whose two edges keep within the tolerance of each other near it by a gap that grows from nothing at its vertex, passes whatever its angle; the rule reads no angle, so the document's angular tolerance cannot make it claim more. (4) is exact for lines (both ends of one within the tolerance of the other's line, as far off at both; their ranges read on it overlapping) and conics (nine points of one within the tolerance of the other, which five points fix, at one gap, their centres within twice it; their ranges read on the first and taken modulo a turn overlapping, the length a lower bound along the conic); a spline against any curve is held at nine places along each (`PLACES`), a run of three or more in a row within the tolerance of the other, the first and the last more than ten tolerances apart, at one gap, being a stretch on it (the nearest point by Newton steps from the nearest of 16 to 64 samples, looked for only where a place lies in the box of the other's control points and within an eighth of a step of its samples' polyline; a run whose gaps differ is scanned on past, on a sheet too, where the two sides of a cut are passed only once a level run of them is found). Stretches of two loops are a fault wherever they lie. Two of one loop are one only where they lie at one place in the loop's parameters as followed, on every surface but a plane (whose chart is the plane): the place of each is found from the loop's own point at the start of the step that holds it, by a walk on the surface from there, and two places are one where they lie apart along each parameter by less than ten working tolerances on the surface at that parameter's speed there (a period of a large cylinder's angle is a short way in parameters and its circumference on the surface), so a seam's two uses, or two edges either side of a slit, lie a period or a spline surface's domain apart and pass whether or not the surface is known to close; a face whose loop cannot be followed, or a place where a parameter does not move the surface, is not held to it. On a sheet two stretches that run opposite ways are the two sides of a cut, a slit or a slot, which origami builds ([`origami.md`](origami.md) › *Slits and slots*), and pass; on a solid, whose boundary has no cut, either way is a fault. *Not held* (each a pass, never a fault): what the region rule passes without a word, which is not followed here either: a face on a surface it cannot chart without doubt (a loop through a sphere's pole or a cone's apex, every spline surface that `geom::closure` finds closed or with a pole: *A spline surface that closes, charted* lists the misses there), a loop whose area its polyline cannot tell from nothing, a face past its caps of points (`MOST_PER_FACE`) and work: such a face is held to (1) and to (4) between different loops only, so a loop gone round twice on a face of a revolved spline is caught only where a neighbouring face can be charted; a spline surface that closes within the tolerance but not to `closure`'s rounding of its net, or on knots that are not clamped, is charted as one that does not close: its seam passes (above), but a loop across the seam is not followed right; a face with a spline edge whose half steps are not shown (the region rule's walk, above) within the steps of an edge and the work of a face is not followed at all; a loop with a spline edge on a torus is not held to (2) or (3), nor to (4) between two stretches of its own: a spline edge on a surface that closes about an axis is followed in steps shown to move less than a quarter turn about the axis (the region rule's walk, above), but nothing shows how far it moves about a torus's tube, and how often a loop goes round the axis decides nothing without the other (three times round the axis is a simple loop with 13 turns about the tube and not with 3); a loop with a conic whose half step moves more than a quarter period (a conic near a pole) is not held to (2) or (3) either (read off the walk itself: a conic's half step is a sixteenth of a half turn of its own, which moves the three quarters of a period that would wrap unseen only where the axis passes between the arc and its chord); a loop narrower than twice its margin has no clear point for (3); a spline overlap shorter than about a quarter of both edges, or that its samples do not show (a spline that bends between them by more than an eighth of a step), and two fits of one curve whose gap differs along them by more than rounding, are not seen; on a body with loose entities, two stretches of exact edges within the body's largest tolerance but not their own are two curves (as they are). Work: the boxes of a face's edges (each boxed by its vertices, an arc within its sagitta of its chord, grown by twice the working tolerance) are paired pair by pair up to 32 edges, else in a grid of about as many cells over the two axes they spread furthest along (a pair taken in the first cell both reach), so a face costs about its edges; (3) stops after `MOST_WINDING_WORK` (2²⁴) points looked at on a loop and (4)'s spline test after `MOST_EVALUATIONS` (2²⁰) evaluations on a face, each then a pass ("cannot tell"; a hostile face otherwise costs the square of its points or 2²² pairs of a thousand evaluations). *The cache.* A face passes both rules or neither is remembered; its key (`face_key`) holds everything the verdict is a function of: the reach (ten working tolerances, which also sets how far the boxes grow), the angular tolerance (read by neither rule; kept so that one that reads it cannot be served a verdict made at another), whether the body is a sheet, the surface and its sense, and per coedge its sense, the edge's curve and range, the edge's own tolerance, its start and end vertices' points, and which earlier coedge of the face uses the same edge; the edges' and vertices' numbers are not read, the vertices' tolerances only through the working tolerance (they bound how far a vertex lies off its edge's end, which the boxes' growth covers), and the rules' caps are constants.

**Self-intersection and self-touch** (`tess::body_self_intersections`, `body_self_intersections_near`, `body_touches_itself`). The body is meshed, and triangles that meet without being neighbours are found exactly (orientation predicates with an exact fallback); each finding has a depth and the margin the approximation alone could explain, so it is *certain* or not. Shells that do not cross are placed by winding numbers. The *near* form takes a role per face (a known group, or new) and looks only where new faces, or faces of different groups, can meet. Touching is decided on the B-rep: a vertex within twice the tolerance of another vertex, or of an edge, of the same shell. Regeneration runs these per operation ([`regeneration.md`](regeneration.md)); the kernel does not, since it shares a layer with `tess`.

**Measure** (`topo::mass_properties`, `inertia`, `mass_and_inertia`). Area, volume, centroid and the inertia tensor are boundary integrals over the exact surfaces, in each surface's parameters, taken about the middle of the body. Each face's boundary is integrated with Gauss–Legendre panels, doubled until two sums in a row agree to 1e-12 (piece by piece of the boundary, and within a piece panel by panel where its sums disagree, within a work budget per face); a face that does not settle is an error, never a guess. On a rational surface each inner integral is summed in the parameter of the Möbius map that evens out the weights at its ends (2026-10-17). A panel ends wherever the integrand has a kink: at an edge's knots, where the edge crosses a knot line of its spline surface, and where an edge within the tolerance of a spline surface runs off its domain and its points are projected onto the border (2026-10-15). The boundary summed is the curve of the feet of the edges' points on the face's surface, with the feet's own rates of change, so that each piece reaches its last foot also where its edge lies off its face within the tolerance; the pieces are summed edge by edge, with no step between one edge's last foot and the next one's first, so a loop is closed only as well as its edges meet at its vertices (2026-11-14, *The rates of the feet*). On a spline surface with a pole, a boundary point whose foot the search does not find on it is looked for along a line of constant parameter, a search in one parameter: first the line its piece runs along, when it runs along one (the point of that line within rounding of it is its foot), then the surface's open borders while the run lies within what an edge may be off its face of one (the border's point less than half as far is its foot where the edge runs along the border there, and the strip between the edge and the border is counted in the bound); a run beside no border, or one that left the border it was beside for none, is let go. Where the piece runs along such a line the parameter across it has no rate; the looks are the face's work; a face with such a foot among its samples has its inner integrals shown to converge (two sums that agree do not show it on a plateau); and a face whose sums do not settle with such feet is measured again with the search's (2026-11-14 and 2026-11-15, *A rail beside its border*). Only the thread that measures a face takes feet along borders: any other takes the search's. Poles of spheres, cones and spline surfaces, seams of surfaces that close on themselves and faces that are their whole surface each have an explicit rule. Faces are measured on several threads and summed in face order.

**Transforms** (`Body::transformed`): rigid motions and mirrors map every curve and surface exactly and keep every entity id; `Body::bbox` is a conservative box from the exact geometry, and `Body::held_bbox` one that boxes a face on a sphere or torus by what it holds (below); `merge` puts the shells of several bodies into one.

## Findings and history

> Moved verbatim from `docs/architecture.md` on 2026-10-15. Section numbers (§4.1, §5.3, …) in the text are those of that file before the split; [README.md](README.md) says where each one is now.

### From *Current state (2026-10-12)*

- **Topology:** `topo` has the B-rep (`Body` with vertices, edges, loops of coedges, faces, shells), the validity checker `check` (references, finite numbers, vertices on edges, closed loops, edges on their faces' surfaces, each edge of a solid used once each way, Euler–Poincaré with genus) and `mass_properties` (area, volume, centroid by boundary integrals over the exact geometry). Fourteen reference solids (six primitives and hemisphere, and eight with trimmed faces: holes, half walls, a spherical cap, a frustum, an ellipse edge, Bézier and NURBS roofs) match their closed forms at three scales and in tilted frames. `mass_properties` also measures the faces a revolution makes: torus faces bounded around the tube, around the axis or both (the surface is closed both ways, so the boundary integral is right only up to a whole number of times the entire torus, which the area settles), and faces whose boundary passes through a pole of a sphere or the apex of a cone (the point stands for a stretch of the edge of the parameter range, which is added). It still refuses, rather than gets wrong, a boundary through a collapsed edge of a Bézier or NURBS surface, a boundary that winds around an axis more than once, torus loops that wind around the axis and the tube at once, and a boundary that turns back on itself at a pole. Deviations from the plan below: parameter-space curves are not stored (recovered by projection), no per-entity tolerances, persistent names are made by `regen` (M6) and not stored in `topo`, the inertia tensor is `topo::inertia` (2026-10-15), and the checker does not test self-intersection or that each face is bounded by its loops.

#### 5.2 Topology (`topo`)

```text
Body ── Shell ── Face ── Loop ── Coedge ── Edge ── Vertex
                  │                 │        │        │
               Surface           Curve2    Curve3   Point     (+ orientation flags, tolerances, persistent names)
```

- A **body** is a set of shells: a *solid* (closed, oriented shells), a *sheet* (open faces) or a *wire* (edges only).
- A **face** is a surface trimmed by loops; a **coedge** is the use of an edge by a face, with a direction and a parameter-space curve.
- Storage is a vector per kind with `u32` ids; a body is shared whole (`Arc`). *(Planned: arena storage shared part by part between a body and the one made from it, parameter-space curves on coedges, and tolerances on entities; the diagram above shows the plan. Names are kept by `regen`, not in the body.)*
- **Genus** (`topo::genus`): from Euler–Poincaré, `V − E + F − R − 2H = 2 (S − G)`. `R` is, per face, its loops beyond the first (−1 for a face without loops), which counts every face as a disc with holes. A face on a torus that still has the torus's handle (the whole torus, or the torus less the discs inside its loops) is not one and takes 2 off (`H`). A torus face with a loop around the axis or around the tube is a band, and one inside its loops is a patch: neither counts. The hole a drill leaves in a torus and the plug it takes out have the same loops walked the other way, so which of the two a face is, is read off the area its loops enclose in parameter space. A spline surface that closes on itself is not known to.
- **Validity checker** (`topo::check`): Euler–Poincaré, every edge of a solid used by exactly two coedges with opposite directions, loops closed, coedge curves on their surface within tolerance, vertices on their edges, consistent orientation (self-intersection is not a level of `check`: it is the mesh check of the next bullet). Every kernel operation is tested to return bodies that pass it.
- **Self-intersection, as built (2026-10-13):** `topo::check` does not test it. The test is `tess::body_self_intersections(&Body, &SelfCheckParams) -> SelfCheck`, in `tess` because it works on the mesh and so stays independent of the kernel it checks: it tessellates the body (chordal tolerance 1/1000 of its diagonal), finds the triangles that meet without being neighbours (§6), and reports crossings and overlaps between faces, each with a depth and with the margin `deviation(f) + deviation(g) + 2 × tolerance` that the approximation alone could explain (`deviation` is the chordal tolerance, or 0 for a plane bounded by straight edges, whose mesh is the face). A finding deeper than its margin is *certain*; one within it is reported as such and proves nothing (tangent faces, a ball resting on a plane). Findings within the margin between curved faces are looked at again with a mesh four times finer, twice by default. Flat faces within twice the modelling tolerance of one plane that share area are an overlap. Shells that do not cross are checked for where they lie, by the winding number of the other shells at a point of each: an outward shell must be in no material, a cavity in material. It is a test oracle (`crates/testkit/tests/self_intersection.rs`, [`testing.md`](../testing.md) oracle 9, which also lists what it cannot see) and, since 2026-10-15, a step of regenerating every operation that can make such a body, near what the operation changed (§5.5, *Never through itself*); it is not a level of `check`, and the kernel operations do not run it themselves (`kernel` and `tess` share a layer). It also reports where a shell touches itself, on the B-rep (§5.5). Run over the boolean results of the corpus and the random runs (2026-10-13) it found nothing in 39,834 of them.
- **Mass properties:** volume, surface area, centroid and inertia tensor, computed from the exact B-rep (surface integrals), independent of the tessellator. Volume and centroid are taken about the middle of the body, not the origin of the model (2026-10-13): a small body far from the origin is then measured as well as the same body at the origin. These are the backbone of the test oracles. As built (`topo::mass_properties`: area, volume, centroid): each face is a boundary integral in its surface's parameters, and the result does not depend on how the surface is parametrised. A sphere face is measured in a frame chosen for its boundary, not in the surface's own (one pole in the middle of the boundary, the other as far from it as it gets); on a cone the integral runs from the apex whenever the boundary comes near it. Every boundary integral is computed with half the panels its edges need, with all of them, and with twice as many until two sums in a row agree to 1e-12; a face for which they never do is an error (`Unsupported`), as is a face that lies outside its own loops (`UnboundedFace`). Only plane faces bounded by lines, circles and ellipses skip that check: their sums are exact. On a spline surface every panel, across the face and along its boundary, ends at the surface's knot lines (and an edge's panels at its own knots too), and the panels across the face are doubled along with the boundary's; a face that holds a pole of a spline surface of revolution is integrated from that pole. **A boundary through a pole of a freeform surface** (a border of a Bézier or NURBS patch that is one point: every control point of it the same, to 1e-9 of the net's size; `geom::pole`) is measured since 2026-10-14. Such a border is a whole side of the parameter range that no edge of the body stands for, as at a cone's apex: with the inner integral running from the pole the integral is zero all along it (the integrand is smooth up to the pole; the normal's length goes to zero there, which is what makes the area element integrable), and an edge that passes through the pole on its way is cut there. A boundary through the poles at *both* ends (the face a spline sweeps that starts and ends on the axis runs from pole to pole between two meridians and has no other edge) has no inner integral that vanishes at both: the face is divided along a line of constant parameter between the poles, each side is integrated from its own pole, and the two are joined by the integral over the rectangle between the places where the boundary crosses that line (found by bisection along the edges). Poles on borders of constant u exchange the roles of u and v. Held to closed forms (`crates/topo/tests/mass_poles.rs`: wedges, octants, caps and halves of a spline ball, cones with a ruled spline side, with the poles either way, to 2e-15 at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6 in two frames), to Pappus for the dome and the drop (`crates/kernel/tests/spline_sweeps.rs`, 1e-9), and all 32 patches of the teapot to their double integral (24 before). A face that passes through no pole is measured exactly as before (the printed worst errors of every suite of `topo` are the same to the last digit). Declined by name: a boundary through two poles that also crosses the seam of a surface that closes on itself. **A spline surface that closes on itself** (its control net ends on the row it starts with, on clamped knots; `geom` gives it no period, so the measure finds it from the net) is followed since 2026-10-14, closed in u, in v, both ways or to a point, with or without seam edges. A seam line of constant u adds nothing to `−∮ G du`; one of constant v does, so where the boundary jumps over the seam in v the inner integral runs along u instead (`∮ H dv`), and where it jumps both ways the loops are unrolled as on a torus and the area settles the whole number of surfaces. Loops that only walk seam edges there and back make the face its whole surface (also on a sphere and a torus, which were refused), and loops that run the wrong way round are the rest of a surface that closes all around. Until then a face that closes in v was measured wrong without an error. Faces that do not go over a seam are measured to the same bits as before. Held to the closed forms of a NURBS cylinder, a vase (Pappus), an egg and a NURBS torus with windows across its seams, to 1e-12 at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6, and to the mesh (`crates/tess/tests/closed_freeform.rs`; `crates/topo/tests/mass_seams.rs`). On a freeform surface the nodes along one piece of an edge are projected each from where the last one landed (`Surface::project_from`, a few evaluations instead of a search over the whole surface), and the search is run only where the short way does not land on a foot of the node: within 1e-5 of the body's size and square to the surface, which is the same point the search finds. A spline edge of eight knot spans or more (the curve in which two surfaces meet, as the kernel fits it) is summed first with the eight-node rule on one panel per span and then with the sixteen-node one, before its panels double.
- **Moving, mirroring, merging** (`topo::transform`): `Body::transformed` maps a body by an isometry exactly (no curve or surface changes kind; scaling and shear are errors) and keeps every entity id. Curves keep their parametrisation, so edges are untouched. A mirror turns right-handed things left-handed: frames become `(Tx, Ty, −Tz)`, every loop is walked the other way round, and faces on planes and freeform surfaces toggle `reversed`, while cylinders, cones (half-angle negated), spheres and tori keep their outward normal and get `v` negated. `Body::bbox` is a conservative box from the exact geometry (tight for planes, cylinders and cones with analytic edges; the whole surface for spheres and tori, which `Body::held_bbox` boxes by what the face holds; the control net for freeform). `merge` puts the shells of several bodies into one and reports the id offsets; it does not check that they are disjoint. These are what the transform, mirror and pattern features build on.

### A rail beside its border (2026-11-14, 2026-11-15)

*Finding* (two reviews of the nets that close an end face in a point, `geom::tip`; nothing a command builds yet): valid bodies measured short without a word from `mass_properties`. *Cause*, shown on the triangle. The two faces beside the corner are 9.9e-7 of their area short, the third is right to 6e-14, and all of what is missing is the sum of one piece of each: the long rail, the border `u = 1` of its face, which is `−∮ G du` along a line of constant `u` and should be nothing. *The inner integrals.* A rational surface along a line of constant parameter is `A(t) / w(t)` within a knot span, and where the weights at a stretch's ends are far apart `w` has a zero just outside the stretch, by the light end: the integrand changes on that scale and uniform panels converge slowly. And each stretch is summed in the parameter `s` of the Möbius map `t = start + (end − start) β s / ((1 − s) + β s)` with `β = (w(start) / w(end))^{1/q}`, `q` the degree in that direction (`mass::moebius`; the surface's denominator comes from `NurbsSurface::weight_at`, new): in `s` the Bernstein coefficients of the denominator are `w_i β^i`, so its ends are equal. It is a change of variable, so the integral is the same; only where the nodes fall changes. ### The box of a face on a sphere or a torus (2026-10-15)

*Bike B4*: the default framing of `view.render` (the scene's box, `Body::bbox`) of a chain stay, a tube of radius 7 swept along an arc of a circle of radius 1332, was 2671 mm across for a stay of 455 mm: a face on a torus contributed the box of the whole torus, and one on a sphere that of the whole sphere.

`Body::held_bbox` (2026-10-15) boxes such a face by what it holds, and the scene's box (`engine::scene::bodies_box`, the default framing of `view.render`, `view.pick` and `replay`) is made of it. `Body::bbox` and `Body::face_bbox` keep the whole surface: with the tight box in `Body::bbox`, `crates/kernel/tests/blend_cylinders.rs › both_tip_edges_of_a_cambered_blade` failed at scale 1e-3 with r 0.05 ("a point too close to a face boundary to tell inside from outside", inside the fillet's boolean), because `boolean::section` clips the curves where two faces meet to the bodies' boxes grown by `16 tol + 0.013 × diagonal`, and a smaller box put the clip where something else was. That margin is the boolean agents' to make independent of how loose the operands' boxes are; until then the booleans keep the box they were measured with, and nothing pays the walk's cost but a picture. A picture asks for the scene's box three or four times (its size, its tolerance, its scene); `scene::held_box` keeps each body's box for as long as the body lives (regeneration hands on the same `Arc` for every body an edit did not touch), so it is worked out once per body. On the turbofan (37 bodies, 6405 faces, 64 of them on spheres and tori) `held_bbox` of all bodies took 9.2 ms against 8.5 ms for `bbox` (release, best of 5, load 7), and the frame's diagonal went from 508 to 406 mm. Along an axis `e`, the greatest `e · p` over a face is reached on its boundary (which the edges add, exactly for lines, circles and ellipses) or at an inner point where `e` is normal to the surface: `centre ± r e` on a sphere; on a torus the four points on the section of the tube in the plane of the axis and `e`, or, where `e` is along the axis, the circles along the top and the bottom of the tube. Each such point that the extent does not already reach adds its coordinate along that axis only, unless it is certainly not in the face (`certainly_outside`). A circle's points stand for the whole circle: if one of them is certainly outside, either the face holds none of the circle or its boundary meets the circle and so reaches the same height.

*Whether a point is in the face* is decided by walking from it along circles of the surface (`walk`): along the first one, the first place where the face's boundary crosses it tells, because the face lies to the left of its boundary seen from outside: beyond the crossing, the point is outside; back towards it, inside. Paths: on a sphere, great circles through the point and the middle of an edge of the boundary (which they cross there) and two turned away from the coordinate planes and their diagonals (a walk along a boundary that lies in its plane has no answer); on a torus, the circle about the axis and the section of the tube through the point, and to the middle of each of three edges a path along a section and then a circle about the axis, or the other way round. Crossings are found on the edges' samples, with the edges' ends taken as their vertices (so that two edges, or a closed edge with itself, see the same side where they meet and a crossing there is counted once), and then sharpened by regula falsi. A plane cuts a torus in two circles; a crossing counts for the walk's circle when it is clearly nearer to it than to the other (within a quarter of their distance apart). No answer, and the point is kept, where no path meets the boundary, the first crossing grazes the path (the boundary within about 6° of running along it), lies at the path's start, or is not clearly on either circle. The first path that answers decides. Faces without loops (whole spheres and tori) keep the box of the whole surface; faces on freeform surfaces keep their control net.

Measured: `crates/topo/tests/transform.rs › faces_on_spheres_and_tori_are_boxed_by_what_they_hold` (pipe elbows through 0.3, 1.9, 4 and 6 rad, a sphere octant through a pole, caps below and above the equator; three scales, two frames, nine isometries including mirrors: the box holds 513 × 513 samples of the face and the edges, and is within 2e-5 of their box, the sag of the samples); `crates/engine/tests/view_render.rs › the_default_frame_is_the_box_of_what_the_faces_hold` (the stay of the finding, a 70° turn of a profile with an arc, a 50° lune of a sphere: the frame holds the mesh's box and is larger by at most the mesh's tolerance). Cost: about 0.1 ms for a face whose box the boundary does not already give (best of 50 on the elbow, octant and cap), nothing for one it does.

### One slow face on every thread (2026-10-21)

*Finding* (the Garabit valley showcase, 2026-10-21): `document.measure` and `document.inspect` of the Garabit valley (112 bodies, 12,799 faces) took 33 s on top of regenerating, 31.7 s of it one face: the cut terrain's top, a bicubic NURBS surface of 55 × 68 knots trimmed by the curves the cut fitted (cubic splines of 1,566, 5,376 and 8,242 knots). *Fix*: the panels of one face are summed on as many threads as are idle (`boundary_integrals`, for every face refined panel by panel on a spline surface: the first sums of each panel and each round of halved panels in `cells_of`). Each run of a panel is summed on a copy of the face's work and comes back with the work it took and the work it had spent at each look at the budget; the sums are taken in panel order, and where the face would have been given up, had the panels been summed one after the other, is replayed from those looks (`replay`), so the sums, the work counted and the place a face is refused are the same bits on any machine and with any number of threads. Within work already shared out (the faces of a body) a face takes the threads that are idle (a count of busy threads, `BUSY`), so the last slow face of a body gets the machine as the others finish. *Measured* (release, 14 threads, a loaded machine): the terrain's body 31.7 → 8.0 s, the pier with one spline face 4.3 → 1.0 s, the valley's `document.measure` after regeneration 49.8 → 9.9 s (load 10), the Garabit pier's 2.1 → 1.7 s; `document.measure` and `document.inspect` of both give the same bytes as before. Held to: `runs_summed_apart_are_given_up_where_one_after_the_other_would_be` (the replay of the budget and the order of the items), every measure test of `topo` and the benchmark's work counts. On an analytic surface, and for a single run, the runs are summed one after the other on the face's own work as before (no copies, no recorded looks). Once the runs done so far took more than the face had left, no more are started; a run the replay reaches all the same is summed then, so a face that will be refused stops near its budget even on one thread. An internal error of a face (a panel the samples do not cover) is now found before any panel is summed, so it may come before a refusal for the budget that came first before; both are errors, and deterministic. *Still open*: the inner integrals themselves are the cost (the same per node as before); a face whose region spans many knot stretches would gain from summing the knot cells inside it as a whole rather than from its boundary.

### What lies between an edge and its chord (2026-11-15)

*Finding* (the review of the spiral's repair, 2026-11-15; *A strip that spirals about a point of the chart*, Owed (3)): the walk measures how far an edge lies from the chord of a step only at the step's middle, so a wavy spline side whose eight steps end, and are measured, where it crosses its middle line reads as straight (the error read 9e-16 while the side lies 2.2e-2 off its chords, the ribbon 1.2e-2 wide), its chords cross the other side, and a right ribbon is refused, "its loop 0 crosses itself" (also "every loop runs clockwise" or "its hole … lies outside"), on `main` and in 0.1.x: 80 of the review's 288 right ribbons off tori (plane 48, cylinder 8, sphere 24) and 146 of its additions to `zz_review_lo2.rs` (ribbons and patches with a hole under each crest, on a plane, a cylinder, a cone, a sphere and an open spline sheet); 202 right faces in all off tori were still refused after the spiral's repair. It is also what made each read of loops with spline edges on a torus refuse right faces.

*Fix: verify before refusing* (`crates/topo/src/region/chord.rs`; first on a plane alone, then, with a theorem of its own, about an axis: below). A face on a plane that the walk is about to refuse, by the region rule or by the rule of simple loops' third rule (a point wound neither 0 times nor once its own way), is walked again in *stretches*, and the walk's fault is dropped only where the whole face is *cleared*: where seven conditions, each computed, hold, which the theorem below shows to mean that its loops bound a region. A face the walk passes is never walked again, so its verdict, its bits and its cost are what they were. A face on a cylinder, a cone or a sphere is walked again as *About an axis*, below, says (the earlier builds that verified faces there each passed a wrong face that the walk refuses: *The rounds*); a face on a torus or a spline surface is not walked again at all: there every verdict is the walk's, as before. What is not cleared is refused: with the walk's message, or, where the second walk shows a fault of another kind than the walk read, with the fault shown.

**The setting.** The chart of a plane is `c(p) = ((p − o)·x, (p − o)·y)`, `o`, `x`, `y` its frame's origin and axes: an affine map with no period and no pole, one to one on the plane, taking a point off the plane to its foot's place; and an isometry of the plane where `x` and `y` are unit vectors square to each other, which is asked to 1e-9 (`flat`; a frame is made orthonormal by `Frame::new`, to rounding), so a length in the chart is the model's. The *reach* `R` is ten of the body's working tolerances (as the walk's; a tolerance that is no positive number: no second walk). An edge's *image* is `c` of its curve over its range, run the way its coedge runs. A *stretch* is the part of one coedge's edge between two parameters; a loop's stretches, in the order the loop runs, take every coedge's whole range (`Walked::new`: a line in one, an arc of a circle or an ellipse in quarter turns at the most, a spline in two; `Walked::halve`: one stretch into two at its middle parameter). Stretch `i` of a loop of `n` runs from `Aᵢ` to `Bᵢ`, both placed (`Walked::place_at`: the curve's point through `c`; a point off the plane by more than `R`, or no number: no second walk). The *gap* `gᵢ = |Bᵢ − Aᵢ₊₁|` (indices round the loop) is nothing between two stretches of one edge and what two edges' ends miss each other by at a vertex. The loop's *curve* `Γ` is its stretches' images in order, each joined to the next by the straight segment across its gap: a closed curve. Its *polyline* `Π` is `A₀ A₁ … Aₙ₋₁`, with the segments `Sᵢ = Aᵢ Aᵢ₊₁`; the *piece* `πᵢ` is the image of stretch `i` followed by the segment across its gap, so `Γ` is `π₀ π₁ … πₙ₋₁`.

**What is shown of a stretch** (`Walked::bound_of`, `Walked::hull_bound`). Points are made for it, in *runs*, each run with a *clear* (how far rounding can have put them: for points made in closed form 64 units in the last place of their largest coordinate, `rounding_of`; for a spline `hull_rounding` times `HULL_CLEAR`, eight times the bound measured on 5,883 splines, times what a cut past the span grows it by), such that (H) the curve over the run's part of the stretch lies within the clear of the convex hull of the run's points, and (D) the curve's direction at each of its points there is a sum, with weights that are not negative, of the legs from each point of the run to the next:

- a line: its two ends;
- an arc of a circle or an ellipse of at most a quarter turn: the three points `P₀, P₁, P₂` of its rational quadratic (its two ends and where their tangents meet, the weights `1, cos(half the arc), 1`): the arc lies in their triangle, and its direction is a sum of `P₁ − P₀` and `P₂ − P₁` with weights that are not negative;
- a spline (weights positive, which `NurbsCurve::new` asks and `hull_rounding` asks again): over at most `MANY_SPANS` (16) knot spans the control points of its Bézier pieces cut to the stretch by de Casteljau's algorithm, a run a piece (`Pieces::each_piece`: each piece made from its own span, the first and the last continued past the domain as far as the edge goes, as the curve is evaluated there, while the cut's rounding grows by less than 2²⁰); over more spans the control points of the spans the stretch meets, one run. The direction of a polynomial spline is a spline on the legs between its control points with weights that are not negative; a rational one's is `Σ_{i<j} (Pⱼ − Pᵢ) (Nᵢ Nⱼ′ − Nᵢ′ Nⱼ) wᵢ wⱼ / W²`, whose coefficients are not negative, the basis being totally positive (de Boor, *A Practical Guide to Splines*, ch. XIII), and `Pⱼ − Pᵢ` is a sum of legs. *One curve*: a knot inside the domain that stands more often than the degree makes the curve jump from one control point to the next, and no hull holds what lies between, since nothing does; so each run must start where the one before ends, to their clears (`hull_bound`: the Bézier pieces of a spline meet at a knot; where a stretch ends on a knot the piece of no width after it is among its runs, so a jump at a stretch's end is seen too), and where the spans' control points are read none of their knots, the stretch's two ends included, may stand more often than the degree inside the domain (`jumps`). A stretch that is not one curve is not shown, however it is halved.

`c` is affine, so the image over a run lies within its clear of the hull of the points' images, and its direction is such a sum of the legs' images. Then, for a stretch that is *shown*:

- (S1) *the bound* `bᵢ`: the farthest of the points' images from the chord `Aᵢ Bᵢ`, plus that point's clear, twice the largest clear and 64 units in the last place of the chord's ends: the image lies within `bᵢ` of the segment `Aᵢ Bᵢ`. (The distance from a segment is a convex function, so over a hull it is largest at a corner.)
- (S2) *the aim* `Kᵢ`: the legs' images, each longer than ten times its run's clear (so read within `asin(2 clear / |leg|) ≤ asin(1/5)` of where it points), lie in a fan of at most three eighths of a turn as read (`ONE_WAY`); `Kᵢ` is that fan widened at both ends by the largest such angle and 1e-9: a cone of at most 135° + 2 · 11.6° < 180° that holds every leg's true direction, hence the direction of the image at each of its points, hence (a cone narrower than a half turn is closed under sums) every *move* along the stretch, from one of its points to a later one. So the stretch is a simple arc (along the cone's middle `n` its points move strictly forward), and its image and its chord are each met at most once by every line square to `n`. A stretch whose legs do not fit, or with a leg of no length among others, or shorter than ten clears, is shown without an aim (or not shown at all where the walk's own, looser reading of its legs fails), and a face with such a stretch is not cleared (C3).

**The lemmas.** `mᵢ = bᵢ + gᵢ` is the *margin* of the segment `Sᵢ`; `N(S, m)` are the points within `m` of a segment `S`, a convex set.

- (L1) `πᵢ ⊂ N(Sᵢ, mᵢ)`. The image lies within `bᵢ` of `Aᵢ Bᵢ` (S1); the point `Aᵢ + t (Bᵢ − Aᵢ)` of that chord lies within `t gᵢ` of the point `Aᵢ + t (Aᵢ₊₁ − Aᵢ)` of `Sᵢ`; and the segment across the gap lies within `gᵢ` of `Aᵢ₊₁`.
- (L2) A point `p` farther than `mᵢ` from `Sᵢ` for every `i` of a loop is wound by `Γ` as by `Π`. As chains `Γ − Π = Σ (πᵢ − Sᵢ)`, each `πᵢ − Sᵢ` a closed curve inside the convex `N(Sᵢ, mᵢ)` (L1), which winds about no point outside it.
- (L3) The area `Γ` bounds, the integral of its winding number, is `Π`'s within `Σ 2 mᵢ (Lᵢ + mᵢ) + π mᵢ²`, `Lᵢ` the length of `Sᵢ` (`area_error`). `πᵢ − Sᵢ` is the sum of the image closed by its chord and the triangle `Aᵢ Bᵢ Aᵢ₊₁`. The first is met at most twice by every line square to `n` (S2), once going each way, so it winds at most once about any point, and about none outside `N(Aᵢ Bᵢ, bᵢ)`: at most `2 bᵢ |Aᵢ Bᵢ| + π bᵢ²` in area. The triangle is at most `gᵢ |Aᵢ Bᵢ| / 2`. With `|Aᵢ Bᵢ| ≤ Lᵢ + gᵢ` both together are within `2 mᵢ (Lᵢ + mᵢ) + π mᵢ²`.
- (L4) Two segments farther apart than `mᵢ + mⱼ + R` hold pieces farther apart than `R` (L1).
- (L5) Let stretch `b` start at `P` where stretch `a` ends at `E` but for the gap `g = |E − P|`, and let both cones, as the loop runs, fit in a fan narrower than a half turn by `θ = 2 asin(2 g / R)` (by 1e-9 at the least; `g` under `R / 4`). Then the two stretches meet only within `R / 2` of `E`, and where `g` is nothing only at `E` (`neighbours_apart`). The fan's middle `n` has `n·d ≥ sin(θ/2) |d|` for every direction `d` of either cone. A point of both is `E − u` with `u` a move of `a` (or nothing) and `P + v` with `v` a move of `b` (or nothing), so `u + v = E − P` and `sin(θ/2) (|u| + |v|) ≤ n·(E − P) ≤ g`, that is `|u| + |v| ≤ g / sin(θ/2) = R / 2`; with `g = 0`, `u = v = 0`. (The *slack* of an aim, counted with `g`, is twice the clear of a run whose points are all one: a piece of no width where a stretch ends on a knot.)

**Cleared** (`judge`) is all of:

- (C0) every stretch is shown (`Walked::rings` gives nothing otherwise), with an aim (`neighbours` takes a stretch without one for not apart);
- (C1) every loop has three stretches or more;
- (C2) every two segments that do not follow each other in one loop, of one loop or of two, are farther apart than `mᵢ + mⱼ + R` (`crossing`, `near`: two segments that do not cross properly are as far apart as the nearest of their four ends is from the other segment; every pair whose boxes, grown by `2 (2 m_max + R)`, meet is looked at, `pairs_of_steps`, and a pair not looked at lies farther apart than `2 m_max + R`; past `MOST_PAIRS` pairs nothing is cleared);
- (C3) every two stretches that follow each other in a loop, the last and the first among them, satisfy (L5) (`neighbours`);
- (C4) every loop's polyline bounds more area than its error (L3): `|area Π| > Σ 2 mᵢ (Lᵢ + mᵢ) + π mᵢ²`;
- (C5) exactly one loop's polyline runs counter-clockwise about the face's normal, and its area less its error is more than any other's with its error;
- (C6) the point `A₀` of every other loop is wound once by the outer loop's polyline and not at all by any other hole's (a polyline winds about no point outside its box).

**Theorem.** If a face on a plane is cleared, then:

1. every stretch is a simple arc;
2. two pieces of two different loops, and two pieces of one loop that do not follow each other, lie more than `R` apart;
3. two stretches that follow each other meet only within `g / sin(θ/2) ≤ R / 2` of their joint (`g < R / 4` its gap, `θ` what the fan of their two cones lacks of a half turn), and only at the joint where it has no gap;
4. the curve of each loop bounds an area of the sign of its polyline's, and larger than nothing; exactly one loop runs counter-clockwise about the face's normal, it bounds more than any other, and every other runs clockwise;
5. every point of every other loop's curve is wound once by the outer loop's curve and not at all by any other hole's.

So no two loops meet, and each loop's curve is a closed curve that meets itself nowhere but, at the most, within `R / 2` of a vertex where two edges' ends miss each other, between those two edges; where every edge ends on the next one's start, each is a simple closed curve, the outer one holds every hole, no hole holds another, and the face is the region inside the outer loop and outside the holes. None of the faults the walk can claim on a plane then holds: a sense, an outer loop that bounds less than a hole, two loops or two parts of one crossing by more than `R`, a point farther than `R` from a loop that it winds about other than once its own way.

*Proof.* (1) is (S2), which C0 and C3 give every stretch. (2): two pieces that do not follow each other in one loop have segments that do not follow each other, which C2 holds farther apart than `mᵢ + mⱼ + R`, so (L4). (3) is C3 with (L5); by C1 two stretches that follow each other share one joint only (in a loop of two they would share two, and (L5) at one says nothing of the other), and every pair of two different stretches of a loop either follows each other (C3) or does not (C2). The consequence: a point where `Γ` meets itself lies on two pieces, or twice on one; not twice on one stretch (1); not on two pieces that do not follow each other (2); on two stretches that follow each other only as (3) says; and the segment across a gap, shorter than `R / 4`, belongs to its piece, so against every piece but its own stretch and the next it is held apart by (2). (4): `|area Γ − area Π|` is within the error of C4 (L3, with (S2) for every stretch), so `area Γ` has the sign of `area Π` and is not nothing; C5 then orders the true areas. (5): `A₀` of a hole is an end of two of its segments, so by C2 it lies farther than `mⱼ + R` from every segment `Sⱼ` of every other loop, and each other loop's curve winds about it as its polyline does (L2), which C6 reads; the hole's curve is connected and meets no other loop's (2), so every other loop's curve winds about each of its points as about `A₀`. ∎

**What stays refused because it is not shown.** Every step above that cannot be taken leaves the walk's fault standing: a face on any surface but a plane; a plane whose frame is not orthonormal; a point that cannot be placed; a stretch not shown or without an aim once it cannot be halved further (a spline with a control point doubled or an end tangent of no length; a spline that jumps); two stretches that follow each other whose cones do not fit (a hairpin or a cusp, at a vertex or inside an edge; two lines folded back on each other; a spur), or whose gap is a quarter of `R` or more; two pieces that do not follow each other within their margins and `R` of each other once their margins are within `R` (a place narrower than `R`; a hole within `R` of another loop; an edge used twice by a loop, a bridge or a seam); a loop of fewer than three stretches; an area not larger than its error; and running out: `MOST_ROUNDS` (24) rounds of halving, `MOST_STRETCHES` (2¹³) stretches of an edge, `MOST_PAIRS`, and the work of a face (`MOST_PROOF_WORK`: a point placed 16 units, a leg 1, a piece made the square of its points, two pairs of segments 1, four pairs of neighbours 1, a winding half a unit a point of the polyline; counted as it goes in `topo::chord_proofs`, apart from the walk's): `spend` returns nothing once the bound is passed, every caller hands that up, and `Verifier::region_shown` and `Verifier::winding_shown` read no verdict as "the fault stands". The faults of the other rules (an edge round its circle twice, two stretches on each other) are never verified.

**Why the two faults found before cannot recur on a plane.** (a) *Two stretches that meet at a point were never held to each other* (round 1). Here every pair of two different stretches is of exactly one of two kinds, following each other in one loop or not, each kind is asked of every pair (C3; C2), and C1 keeps the kinds apart; pieces of two loops are all of the second kind. (b) *Pieces a period apart in the chart lie on one place of a closed surface* (round 2). A plane's chart has no period and is one to one on the plane, so two points of the plane are one where, and only where, their chart points are; nothing is unwrapped. Nor has it a pole or a scale that differs by place or direction: `R` is one length everywhere (the review's "ten tolerances at the face's smallest scale", which on a cylinder of radius 1e-3 is 10,000 tolerances along the axis).

**What the proof takes as given.** The rounding bounds, which are measured, not proven (`hull_rounding` with its factor of eight, as the walk's own step proofs take them; the 64 units in the last place of points made in closed form and of a placed point); that the frame of a plane is right-handed, as the walk reads it; and `geom`'s curves as evaluated (the unit test below holds each stretch to its bound and its cone at 64 places).

**About an axis: a cylinder, a cone and a sphere** (since 2026-11-15, the second hand-in of round 3; `crates/topo/src/region/chord.rs`). On these the chart `(u, v)` is the angle about the axis and the height, the place along the ruling or the latitude: defined off the axis, with the period `T` of a turn along `u`, and no isometry (`|∂u|` is the distance `ρ` from the axis, `|∂v|` a constant `σ`: 1, `√(1 + slope²)`, the radius). A face there that the walk is about to refuse is walked again as on a plane, with three things changed; the plane's lemmas and conditions are then read of the loops' *lifts* to the chart's *cover*, the plane of `(u, v)` with `u` unwrapped, where a curve that keeps off the axis has one lift once its start is placed.

- *The scaled chart.* Every chart point, chord and direction is `(u, v)` times `(|∂u|, |∂v|)` at the first point placed (`Walked::unit`), a fixed affine change of the chart, so that both axes are lengths: a cylinder is unrolled without stretch, and on a cone or a sphere the surface's lengths are the scaled chart's times a ratio between `s_min` and `s_max`, the range of `ρ` over that at the first point (read over each stretch's reach, the least and the most distance from the axis its hull can have, as well as at the points placed) and 1 (`Walked::reaches`). Two reaches take the place of `R`: pieces must be apart by `R_far = R / s_min` (C2: a straight line of the chart that long is at least ten tolerances long on the surface, whichever way it runs, at the heights or latitudes the face has), and a joint is allowed `R_near = R / s_max` ((L5), C3: the gap under `R_near / 4`, two stretches that follow each other meeting within `R_near / 2` of their joint, at most five tolerances on the surface). On a cylinder both are `R`. (The second review's reading of round 2, which took one reach, ten tolerances over the least of `|∂u|`, `|∂v|`: on a cylinder of radius 1e-3 that was 10,000 tolerances along the axis, and a gap of 2,500 was allowed at a vertex.)
- *A stretch shown* (`Walked::about_bound`, round 2's, read and held by both reviews): its points keep in a wedge about the axis narrower than a quarter turn and clear of it by eight times their rounding (`Wedge`), so its lift moves less than a quarter period, and the chord of its lift is its placed end moved by the whole periods that bring it nearest its start; each leg is taken to its box of chart directions over the wedge's reach (`AboutMap`: `u′ = w·e / ρ`, `v′` as the surface has it, over every place the stretch can be), widened by what rounding can have moved the leg; the aim (S2) is the fan of those boxes' corners, within three eighths of a turn, and the bound (S1) is from the aim alone: a curve from `A` to `B` whose direction keeps within a cone `K` lies in the parallelogram `(A + K) ∩ (B − K)`, so no farther from the segment `A B` than the farther of its two other corners (`parallelogram`). (L1) to (L5) use (S1), (S2) and the geometry of a plane alone, so they hold of the lifts in the scaled cover word for word.
- *The period.* A loop whose lift does not close, ending a period from where it starts, goes round the axis (`Walked::rings`): a band's side, or a cap about a pole or the apex. The walk passes a face it reads so without a word, and its rule of simple loops' third rule skips such a loop, so no fault to verify comes from one; where the second walk reads one, the face is not cleared. For the rest, two points of the cover a whole number of periods apart are one point of the surface, so:
  - (C2′) every two segments, a segment and itself among them, are farther apart than both margins and `R_far` also where one is moved by any whole number of periods, but for the segment itself and two that follow each other in one loop, unmoved (`crossing`). *How many translates.* The loops are first moved by whole periods to lie over the first (their boxes' middles nearest); they then span `U` along `u`. A segment moved by `d` periods can come within `M = 2 m_max + R_far` of another only where `|d| T ≤ U + M`. So `n + 1` copies of every loop, each a period on from the last, `n = ⌊(U + M) / T⌋`, hold every such pair: the copies `d` and 0 where `d ≥ 0`, else 0 and `−d`. A copy is a loop of its own to the sweep, so two segments are passed over as following each other only within one copy. A face that spans less than a period less `M` needs one copy, and costs what a plane's does; more than three copies (a strip of three turns and more) are not made, and nothing is cleared.
  - (C6′) a hole's point `A₀` is wound once by the outer loop's polyline and its translates together and not at all by another hole's and its translates (`wound`: the translates whose box holds the point).
  - On a sphere every loop may run clockwise, the face being the sphere less the discs of the cover they bound: then (C5″, C6″) no loop's point is wound by another's polyline or its translates.

*Theorem about an axis.* If a face on a cylinder, a cone or a sphere is cleared, then (1) the plane's (1) to (4) hold of the lifts in the scaled cover, with `R_far` between pieces and `R_near` at a joint; (2) no translate of a lift by a whole number of periods meets a translate of another lift, or another translate of itself: so the loops' curves on the surface are closed curves that meet themselves and each other nowhere but, at the most, within `R_near / 2` of a vertex where two edges' ends miss each other, and go round the axis no times; (3) the disc each lift bounds in the cover maps one to one onto the surface; (4) with an outer loop, every other loop's curve lies in the outer loop's disc on the surface and in no other hole's; on a sphere whose loops all run clockwise, no loop's disc holds another loop. So the loops bound a region of the surface: the outer loop's disc less the holes', or the sphere less the discs.

*Proof.* (1) is the plane's theorem in the scaled cover: its proof uses C0 to C4 and (L1) to (L5) alone. (2): a point of a lift moved by `k` periods and of another moved by `k′` lies on a piece of each, each within its margin of its segment's translate (L1); by C2′ those are farther apart than both margins and `R_far` unless `k = k′` and the two are one segment or follow each other, which is (1)'s case; and C2′ for every `k` follows from the pairs of copies looked at, by the count above. (3): a lift and its translate by `k ≠ 0` periods are disjoint closed curves (2) that bound discs of one area, so neither disc holds the other; two discs of a plane whose boundaries do not meet are nested or disjoint; so the translates of a lift's disc are pairwise disjoint, and the disc maps one to one. (Where a loop's curve has the little tangles (1) allows at a vertex, read "the disc" as the points it winds about; the statements are then of winding numbers, as on the plane.) (4): `A₀` lies farther than `mⱼ + R_far` from every translate of every segment of another loop (C2′), so each translate of that loop's curve winds about it as its polyline's translate does (L2); the sum over the translates is how often the loop's disc on the surface holds the point, once at the most by (3), which C6′ or C6″ reads; the hole's curve is connected and meets no translate of another loop's (2), so the sum is the same at each of its points. ∎

*Poles, the apex and the seam.* A lift lives in the cover, which has neither pole nor apex: a loop that goes round the axis no times bounds a disc of the cover, which holds none. A stretch near the axis is not shown (no wedge narrower than a quarter turn and clear of the axis holds it), and a point within ten tolerances of a pole or the apex is not placed, so a loop through or beside one is never cleared. The seam is no place of the cover: a loop across it is lifted like any other, and a loop that uses a seam edge twice (a whole band written as one loop) has its two uses a period apart, on one place of the surface: not cleared (C2′).

*What stays refused about an axis*, beyond the plane's list: a face with a loop the second walk reads as going round the axis; a loop through or within ten tolerances of a pole or the apex; pieces whose translates come within their margins and `R_far` (a ribbon whose ends lie within that of each other across the seam; an edge used twice across a period); a face that spans three periods or more; a gap of `R_near / 4` or more. The torus (two periods, and the tube angle of a spline edge not shown) and spline surfaces (the chart's map of directions not known) are not walked again.
