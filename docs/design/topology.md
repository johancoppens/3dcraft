# 3DCraft design: Topology, checks and measures

> Moved verbatim from `docs/architecture.md` on 2026-10-15. Section numbers (§4.1, §5.3, …) in the text are those of that file before the split; [README.md](README.md) says where each one is now.

## From *Current state (2026-10-12)*

- **Topology:** `topo` has the B-rep (`Body` with vertices, edges, loops of coedges, faces, shells), the validity checker `check` (references, finite numbers, vertices on edges, closed loops, edges on their faces' surfaces, each edge of a solid used once each way, Euler–Poincaré with genus) and `mass_properties` (area, volume, centroid by boundary integrals over the exact geometry). Fourteen reference solids (six primitives and hemisphere, and eight with trimmed faces: holes, half walls, a spherical cap, a frustum, an ellipse edge, Bézier and NURBS roofs) match their closed forms at three scales and in tilted frames. `mass_properties` also measures the faces a revolution makes: torus faces bounded around the tube, around the axis or both (the surface is closed both ways, so the boundary integral is right only up to a whole number of times the entire torus, which the area settles), and faces whose boundary passes through a pole of a sphere or the apex of a cone (the point stands for a stretch of the edge of the parameter range, which is added). It still refuses, rather than gets wrong, a boundary through a collapsed edge of a Bézier or NURBS surface, a boundary that winds around an axis more than once, torus loops that wind around the axis and the tube at once, and a boundary that turns back on itself at a pole. Deviations from the plan below: parameter-space curves are not stored (recovered by projection), no per-entity tolerances, no persistent names yet (M6), no inertia tensor, and the checker does not test self-intersection or that each face is bounded by its loops.

### 5.2 Topology (`topo`)

```text
Body ── Shell ── Face ── Loop ── Coedge ── Edge ── Vertex
                  │                 │        │        │
               Surface           Curve2    Curve3   Point     (+ orientation flags, tolerances, persistent names)
```

- A **body** is a set of shells: a *solid* (closed, oriented shells), a *sheet* (open faces) or a *wire* (edges only).
- A **face** is a surface trimmed by loops; a **coedge** is the use of an edge by a face, with a direction and a parameter-space curve.
- Storage is arena-based with typed ids, and persistent: an operation builds a new body that shares unchanged parts with its input.
- **Genus** (`topo::genus`): from Euler–Poincaré, `V − E + F − R − 2H = 2 (S − G)`. `R` is, per face, its loops beyond the first (−1 for a face without loops), which counts every face as a disc with holes. A face on a torus that still has the torus's handle (the whole torus, or the torus less the discs inside its loops) is not one and takes 2 off (`H`). A torus face with a loop around the axis or around the tube is a band, and one inside its loops is a patch: neither counts. The hole a drill leaves in a torus and the plug it takes out have the same loops walked the other way, so which of the two a face is, is read off the area its loops enclose in parameter space. A spline surface that closes on itself is not known to.
- **Validity checker** (`topo::check`): Euler–Poincaré, every edge of a solid used by exactly two coedges with opposite directions, loops closed, coedge curves on their surface within tolerance, vertices on their edges, consistent orientation, no self-intersection (the expensive checks behind a level flag). Every kernel operation is tested to return bodies that pass it.
- **Self-intersection, as built (2026-10-13):** `topo::check` does not test it. The test is `tess::body_self_intersections(&Body, &SelfCheckParams) -> SelfCheck`, in `tess` because it works on the mesh and so stays independent of the kernel it checks: it tessellates the body (chordal tolerance 1/1000 of its diagonal), finds the triangles that meet without being neighbours (§6), and reports crossings and overlaps between faces, each with a depth and with the margin `deviation(f) + deviation(g) + 2 × tolerance` that the approximation alone could explain (`deviation` is the chordal tolerance, or 0 for a plane bounded by straight edges, whose mesh is the face). A finding deeper than its margin is *certain*; one within it is reported as such and proves nothing (tangent faces, a ball resting on a plane). Findings within the margin between curved faces are looked at again with a mesh four times finer, twice by default. Flat faces within twice the modelling tolerance of one plane that share area are an overlap. Shells that do not cross are checked for where they lie, by the winding number of the other shells at a point of each: an outward shell must be in no material, a cavity in material. It is a test oracle (`crates/testkit/tests/self_intersection.rs`, [`testing.md`](../testing.md) oracle 9, which also lists what it cannot see) and, since 2026-10-15, a step of regenerating every operation that can make such a body, near what the operation changed (§5.5, *Never through itself*); it is not a level of `check`, and the kernel operations do not run it themselves (`kernel` and `tess` share a layer). It also reports where a shell touches itself, on the B-rep (§5.5). Run over the boolean results of the corpus and the random runs (2026-10-13) it found nothing in 39,834 of them.
- **Mass properties:** volume, surface area, centroid and inertia tensor, computed from the exact B-rep (surface integrals), independent of the tessellator. Volume and centroid are taken about the middle of the body, not the origin of the model (2026-10-13): a small body far from the origin is then measured as well as the same body at the origin. These are the backbone of the test oracles. As built (`topo::mass_properties`: area, volume, centroid): each face is a boundary integral in its surface's parameters, and the result does not depend on how the surface is parametrised. A sphere face is measured in a frame chosen for its boundary, not in the surface's own (one pole in the middle of the boundary, the other as far from it as it gets); on a cone the integral runs from the apex whenever the boundary comes near it. Every boundary integral is computed with half the panels its edges need, with all of them, and with twice as many until two sums in a row agree to 1e-12; a face for which they never do is an error (`Unsupported`), as is a face that lies outside its own loops (`UnboundedFace`). Only plane faces bounded by lines, circles and ellipses skip that check: their sums are exact. On a spline surface every panel, across the face and along its boundary, ends at the surface's knot lines (and an edge's panels at its own knots too), and the panels across the face are doubled along with the boundary's; a face that holds a pole of a spline surface of revolution is integrated from that pole. **A boundary through a pole of a freeform surface** (a border of a Bézier or NURBS patch that is one point: every control point of it the same, to 1e-9 of the net's size; `geom::pole`) is measured since 2026-10-14. Such a border is a whole side of the parameter range that no edge of the body stands for, as at a cone's apex: with the inner integral running from the pole the integral is zero all along it (the integrand is smooth up to the pole; the normal's length goes to zero there, which is what makes the area element integrable), and an edge that passes through the pole on its way is cut there. A boundary through the poles at *both* ends (the face a spline sweeps that starts and ends on the axis runs from pole to pole between two meridians and has no other edge) has no inner integral that vanishes at both: the face is divided along a line of constant parameter between the poles, each side is integrated from its own pole, and the two are joined by the integral over the rectangle between the places where the boundary crosses that line (found by bisection along the edges). Poles on borders of constant u exchange the roles of u and v. Held to closed forms (`crates/topo/tests/mass_poles.rs`: wedges, octants, caps and halves of a spline ball, cones with a ruled spline side, with the poles either way, to 2e-15 at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6 in two frames), to Pappus for the dome and the drop (`crates/kernel/tests/spline_sweeps.rs`, 1e-9), and all 32 patches of the teapot to their double integral (24 before). A face that passes through no pole is measured exactly as before (the printed worst errors of every suite of `topo` are the same to the last digit). Declined by name: a boundary through two poles that also crosses the seam of a surface that closes on itself. **A spline surface that closes on itself** (its control net ends on the row it starts with, on clamped knots; `geom` gives it no period, so the measure finds it from the net) is followed since 2026-10-14, closed in u, in v, both ways or to a point, with or without seam edges. A seam line of constant u adds nothing to `−∮ G du`; one of constant v does, so where the boundary jumps over the seam in v the inner integral runs along u instead (`∮ H dv`), and where it jumps both ways the loops are unrolled as on a torus and the area settles the whole number of surfaces. Loops that only walk seam edges there and back make the face its whole surface (also on a sphere and a torus, which were refused), and loops that run the wrong way round are the rest of a surface that closes all around. Until then a face that closes in v was measured wrong without an error. Faces that do not go over a seam are measured to the same bits as before. Held to the closed forms of a NURBS cylinder, a vase (Pappus), an egg and a NURBS torus with windows across its seams, to 1e-12 at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6, and to the mesh (`crates/tess/tests/closed_freeform.rs`; `crates/topo/tests/mass_seams.rs`). On a freeform surface the nodes along one piece of an edge are projected each from where the last one landed (`Surface::project_from`, a few evaluations instead of a search over the whole surface), and the search is run only where the short way does not land on a foot of the node: within 1e-5 of the body's size and square to the surface, which is the same point the search finds. A spline edge of eight knot spans or more (the curve in which two surfaces meet, as the kernel fits it) is summed first with the eight-node rule on one panel per span and then with the sixteen-node one, before its panels double.
- **Moving, mirroring, merging** (`topo::transform`): `Body::transformed` maps a body by an isometry exactly (no curve or surface changes kind; scaling and shear are errors) and keeps every entity id. Curves keep their parametrisation, so edges are untouched. A mirror turns right-handed things left-handed: frames become `(Tx, Ty, −Tz)`, every loop is walked the other way round, and faces on planes and freeform surfaces toggle `reversed`, while cylinders, cones (half-angle negated), spheres and tori keep their outward normal and get `v` negated. `Body::bbox` is a conservative box from the exact geometry (tight for planes, cylinders and cones with analytic edges; the whole surface for spheres and tori, the control net for freeform). `merge` puts the shells of several bodies into one and reports the id offsets; it does not check that they are disjoint. These are what the transform, mirror and pattern features build on.
