# 3DCraft design: Face operations

Offset face, shell, move face, draft, thicken and delete face (`kernel::tweak` and its users; `regen::face_ops` and the module per operation; `engine::face_cmds`). Method 5 of the map ([`../architecture.md`](../architecture.md) §5).

## Method

**Give faces new surfaces and close the body again, keeping every entity.** `kernel::tweak::resurface` takes a body and new surfaces for some faces. Every vertex on such a face is found again where the surfaces of its faces meet, nearest where it was (Newton on the signed distances); every edge that ends in such a vertex is made again on its two faces' surfaces (a line, or a circle about the axis of its round face; any other edge, and a line or circle on a freeform face that is one no longer, as a cubic through points found where the surfaces meet, held to a quarter of the tolerance; an edge along a border of a freeform face's parameters as that border of its new surface). Nothing about how the body hangs together changes: every face, edge and vertex of the result has the number it had. The result is then held against what a body is (edges that shrink to nothing or turn round, flat faces whose loops change their turning or cross, round faces whose rims change places, points just inside and outside each moved edge classified by ray casting, `topo::check`).

- **Offset face**: a plane moves to the parallel plane, a cylinder, sphere or torus changes its radius, a cone its radius at the frame; a freeform face becomes a spline surface within a quarter of the tolerance of its exact offset, in the same parameters (`geom::offset`), once it is proven not to fold: fitted a step past every border that is not a seam and cut back, so its borders lie inside the fit, and joined with its first derivative across the knots where the surface is twice differentiable, so its normal is continuous wherever the exact offset's is (what a ball rolling along it asks); positive is away from the material. A rolling-ball **fillet face** that moves with the two faces it rolls along, by the same distance, is not offset but **rebuilt**: the fillet of radius `r ± d` between those faces' offsets, exact and tangent to them by construction, in the old face's parameters (`blend::rebuild`, *Offset of a fillet: rebuilt* below).
- **Move face**: one rigid motion of several faces (`resurface_rigid`): what lies wholly on moved faces is moved exactly, what lies between moved and fixed faces is found again as for an offset.
- **Draft**: flat faces square to the pull turn about their line on the neutral plane, cylinders along the pull become cones; closed forms only.
- **Shell**: every face offset inward by the thickness, open faces outward, and the hollow taken from the solid by the boolean; rolling-ball blends whose radius the thickness reaches are first collapsed into the edges and corners they round, so the hollow there is sharp (*Shell as thick as its rounds* below).
- **Thicken**: the layer between the solid with the faces moved outward and with them moved inward (two offsets and a boolean), as a body of its own. Thicken is for faces of a *solid* whose layer ends where the solid's neighbouring faces, continued, cut it (a plate on a block's top as wide as the block, a sleeve in a hole). Faces of a *sheet*, or faces that should end on rims of their own along their edges with joints to other plates, are a plate (`feature.plate`, [`plates.md`](plates.md)); a plate of a solid's faces ends square along their edges, not on the neighbours.
- **Delete face**: a group of faces that fills holes of the faces around it goes, and those faces go on across (topological only); a strip (a chamfer or fillet along an edge, a round of a rim) collapses into the edge where its neighbours meet when continued (`resurface_at`); a group of blends that meet (strips and the corners between them) collapses as a whole (2026-10-21).

**Too far** is found, not guessed: a check that fails at the given size but passes at a smaller one gives the largest that fits (halving, 48 times at most, the whole search within a work bound: *Bounded work* below) and the edge or face that stops it; so is the offset of a freeform face that would fold over (the distance reaches a radius of curvature on the side it moves to). **Refused, with the faces**: a neighbour tangent to a moved face that does not move with it (for a fillet face, a face it rolls along that does not move with it by the same distance: refused before anything is built; a fillet whose radius would reach zero is too far, with the radius, in closed form), a vertex of four or more faces that would have to become an edge, an edge between analytic faces, one moved and one fixed, that would no longer be a line or a circle, a freeform face with a pole or a border drawn into a point.

**Names**: every entity of an offset, move or draft is the copy of what it was; a shell or thicken names its skin and the faces of the hollow or layer as copies in instance 0 and 1; a deletion names edges and vertices by their faces. A sketch on a moved face moves and turns with it. What local checks cannot see, two faces far apart that ran into each other, regeneration finds on a mesh: near the change for move face and draft, over the whole body for offset face, shell and thicken (structural debt, the map's §9).

### Face operations as built (M9, 2026-10-14)

`feature.offsetFace`, `feature.moveFace`, `feature.draft`, `feature.shell` and `feature.thicken`; `kernel::offset_face`, `kernel::move_face`, `kernel::draft`, `kernel::shell`, `kernel::thicken`, and what they share in `kernel::tweak`; `regen::face_ops`, `regen::offset_face`, `regen::move_face`, `regen::draft`, `regen::shell`, `regen::thicken`; `engine::face_cmds`.

**Route: a local operation, and the boolean only to take the hollow out.** The roadmap's recipe for the face operations is "give a face a new surface, extend the neighbours, intersect again, sew". For the blends a tool solid and the boolean was the better route (above); for moving a face it is not. Inward, the body cut by the half-space behind a moved plane would be exact; outward, the new material is bounded by the neighbours' surfaces continued past their edges, so a tool would have to be built from exactly the computation that closes the body directly, and the boolean would then add a face (a block whose top moves up would have seven faces, the old top flush with a new one, until merged). So `kernel::tweak::resurface` does it directly, and nothing about how the body hangs together changes: every face, edge and vertex of the result is the one with the same number in the body given. (1) Every vertex on a face with a new surface is found again where the surfaces of its faces meet, nearest where it was: Newton's iteration on the signed distances to the surfaces, with the smallest step that puts the point on every surface whose normal adds a direction (one step for planes; a face tangent to another says nothing the other does not, and is held to its distance at the end). (2) Every edge that ends in such a vertex is made again on its two faces' surfaces: a straight edge through its two vertices; a circular edge about the axis of the round one of its faces (a cylinder, a cone, a torus, or a sphere about the line along the old circle's axis), through its first vertex, with its frame's axes kept so its parameters keep their meaning; an elliptic or spline edge only when neither face moved and its ends still lie on it. Each is held at five points against both surfaces. (3) The result is held against what a body is: an edge that shrinks to nothing or turns round (which is how a vanishing neighbour shows), a flat face whose loops change their turning or whose outline crosses itself or loses a hole (`profile::detect_profiles` on the face's loops), a round face whose rims change places along its axis, `topo::check`, and next to every moved edge a point just outside and just inside each face, which the booleans' ray classification must find outside and inside (a face that ran into another part of the body, or into the plane of another face, fails this). What such local checks cannot see, two faces far apart that ran into each other, regeneration finds on the mesh (`tess::body_self_intersections`, as for lofts) before the body is handed on.

**What moves.** A flat face goes to the parallel plane at the distance; a cylinder and a sphere change their radius, a torus its tube's; a cone keeps its axis and angle and its radius changes by the distance over the cosine of the half-angle (its frame moves along the axis when the apex passes it). Positive is outward, away from the material: a hole narrows, a boss thickens. So the neighbours that follow are flat faces at any angle (the slanted sides of a frustum are continued until they meet the moved top: closed form at three scales), round faces about an axis the moved face is square to (a hole through a face that moves gets longer) and the flat faces across an axis when the round face moves (the mouth and the floor of a hole, the foot and the crown of a boss, a ball half sunk in a block).

**Too far.** A failure of the checks in (3), or of a vertex to be found, is a matter of how far when a smaller distance works: the distance is halved (48 times at most, each a whole resurface) for the largest that fits, and the error names the edge (by the two faces it lies between) or the face, the reason and that bound ("what fits is below 3.99999 mm"). When no distance at all works, the geometry is one that is not built and that is said instead, with the faces.

**Not built, and refused with the faces:** a neighbour tangent to the moved face (the top next to a fillet: its continuation does not meet the moved plane where the edge was); a vertex where four or more faces meet that would have to become an edge (a slanted side of a frustum on a plinth); a freeform face next to a moved one outward (it cannot be continued past its edge) and an elliptic or spline edge on a moved face; the offset of a freeform face; a cone whose radius at its frame would turn negative in a way the frame cannot follow. Replace face is not built; see the M9 row of the roadmap. Move face, thicken, draft and delete face are built (below).

**Shell.** `kernel::shell` offsets every face of the solid at once by the thickness, inward, except the open faces, which move outward by the same amount, so that the hollow reaches through them and the opening is as wide as the hollow is there. Since the offset keeps every entity, the hollow has the solid's faces, face for face. The shell is the solid less the hollow (`boolean_with`, the operands trusted since both were just checked); without open faces the hollow lies inside, the boolean leaves it as a cavity, and the result has two shells. After the boolean: every face that keeps a wall must be there as skin and as a face of the hollow, and the hollow must come out through every open face (it is gone, or an edge of the boolean crosses it), else an error with the face. A rounded box gets inner rounds of the radius less the thickness; at a thickness of the radius or more the hollow has a sharp edge there (since 2026-10-21, *Shell as thick as its rounds* below). Behind an open face between walls that stand round it (the floor of a pocket) the hollow is wider than the opening, and a piece of the open face moved out is the hollow's ceiling there; the result is a valid solid, measured in the tests. A boss that stands on an open face would be cut loose by the hollow coming out round it, leaving a body in two pieces; that is not tried in the tests and not refused yet (open). FreeCAD's `makeThickness` gives the same volume, area and counts as ours on a box, a cup, a tube, a rounded tray (three thicknesses) and an L (with its join set to intersection; its default puts an arc in the hollow's outside corner), and `makeOffsetShape` the same as an offset of every face of a block and of a cylinder (`target/scratch-face-ops/thickness.py`; only the cylinders' seam edges differ in the counts).

**Move face** (`feature.moveFace`, `kernel::move_face`, `regen::move_face`; 2026-10-15). Faces of a solid move together by one rigid motion, given as `feature.transform` gives one (a turn by `angle` about the line through `pivot` along `axis`, then a shift by `translate`). Every moved face gets the image of its surface (`Surface::transformed`, which keeps the parametrisation), and `tweak::resurface_rigid` closes the body: a vertex all of whose faces move and an edge both of whose faces move are the images of what they were, exactly and of any kind (so a boss moved with its top keeps its top rim as it was, and two moved faces that meet in an ellipse still do); everything between a moved face and one that stays is found again as for an offset, with the same checks. So the top of a block shifted up is an offset (a slanted shift counts by its part along the normal), tilted about a line across it is a wedge, the wall of a hole or the wall and top of a boss moved across their face put the hole or the boss elsewhere. **Too far** is the offset's halving on the part of the motion (the same part of the shift and of the turn): the kernel's `value` and `largest` are how far the farthest vertex of a moved face goes and may go, and regeneration says the part and the shift and angle it is (`translate, angle: the move is too far: the edge shrinks to nothing …; what fits is below 0.833333 of it, translate (0, 0, -4.99…) mm`). **Refused, with the faces,** where the topology round a moved face would have to change: an edge between a moved face and one that stays that would no longer be a line or a circle (a cylinder tilted against a flat face meets it in an ellipse: `OFF_SURFACE` at every part of the motion, so not "too far"), and everything the offset refuses (a tangent neighbour that stays, a vertex of four faces, a freeform neighbour). **Names** are an offset's: every face, edge and vertex is the copy of what it was, so a fillet on an edge of a moved face survives edits of the shift and the turn (`crates/engine/tests/move_face.rs`, with the fillet's closed form along an edge that runs across a tilted top). A sketch on a moved face lies on it and is turned with it the shortest way onto its new normal (`sketch::resolve_face`, `turned_onto`): for a turn that tilts the face, which is every turn that changes it, that is the face's own turn. *Tests:* `crates/kernel/tests/move_face.rs` (shifts, a wedge about the middle and about an edge, a hole and a boss moved, a tilted frame, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6; the part that fits; the tilted hole refused; bad input) and `crates/engine/tests/move_face.rs` (the same through the command, checked deep, the fillet that survives, the sketch that turns, too far and refused in words, bad parameters, `feature.edit`, save and reopen, undo and redo).

**Draft** (`feature.draft`, `kernel::draft`, `regen::draft`; 2026-10-15). Faces of a solid tilted by an angle about a neutral plane so that the part comes out of a mould along the pull direction, the plane's normal. The neutral plane is a world plane, a datum plane or a flat face (`PlaneRef`, as datum planes take it); for a face the pull goes *into* the body the face bounds, so a block drafted with its bottom face as neutral narrows toward its top and with its top face toward its bottom, and `reversed` turns the pull round. A drafted face keeps the line or circle in which it meets the neutral plane and leans in by the angle as it goes along the pull; a negative angle leans out. **Closed forms only:** a flat face whose normal is square to the pull turns about the line it shares with the neutral plane (the rotation about that line, applied to its frame: still a plane); a cylinder whose axis runs along the pull becomes the cone about that axis whose radius at the neutral plane is the cylinder's and whose half-angle is the draft angle, narrowing along the pull for a boss and widening for a hole. Then `tweak::resurface` closes the body as for an offset: the top of a drafted block shrinks, edges between drafted faces are found again where the new surfaces meet, and a flat face tangent to a drafted cylinder stays tangent to its cone, since both are drafted from the line or circle they share at the neutral plane: the vertex where they and the top meet is found where the plane and the top meet, nearest where it was, which is the point of tangency (the "one direction" rule of the iteration), so a rounded box drafted is a rounded frustum, to its closed form. **Too far** is the offset's halving on the angle: an angle at which a face is drawn to nothing (the top of a frustum to a point) fails with the edge and the largest angle that fits. **Refused, with the face and the reason, before anything is built:** a flat face whose normal is not square to the pull (the top of the block, a face drafted already), a cylinder whose axis is not along it, a cone (it has an angle of its own; changing it is not built), a sphere, a torus and a freeform face (no closed form of the kind we build), and round them what the offset refuses (a neighbour tangent to a drafted face that is not drafted itself, a vertex of four faces). **Names** are an offset's: every face, edge and vertex the copy of what it was; a sketch on a drafted face turns with it as on a moved one. *Tests:* `crates/kernel/tests/draft.rs` (a block from its bottom, its top and its middle, one side, a rod and a hole into frusta of cones, a rounded box into a rounded frustum, a tilted frame, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6; the angle that fits; what is refused) and `crates/engine/tests/draft.rs` (the same through the command with the neutral plane as a face, `xy` and reversed, a datum plane whose distance is a variable with a fillet on the drafted top and a peg on a drafted side through edits of the level and the angle, too steep and refused in words, bad parameters, `feature.edit` of the neutral plane, the angle, the faces and the pull, save and reopen, undo and redo).

**Thicken** (`feature.thicken`, `kernel::thicken`, `regen::thicken`; 2026-10-15). What is thickened is a set of faces of a solid (a sheet made by `feature.fill` since 2026-10-18 is not taken yet), into a solid of its own: the layer between those faces and the same faces moved along their normals by the thickness, `outside` (away from the material, the default), `inside` or `both` (half each way). The source keeps its body (`FeatureKind::source` says so, as `keep` does for a transform); the layer is joined to it, or to anything, with a boolean. **Method:** two offsets and the boolean, as the shell: the solid with the faces moved outward by the part outside (or the solid itself) less the solid with them moved inward by the part inside (or the solid itself). So at its rim the layer is bounded by the faces round the set, *continued as an offset continues them*, not by walls along the faces' normals: the top of a block thickened is a plate as wide as the block, the wall of a rod a tube as long as the rod, the wall of a hole a sleeve in it or round it, the top and a side of a block an angle, the slanted top of a frustum thickened inward a cap whose rim lies in the slanted sides. That is the layer a solid's faces have, and it is the one route that needs no new kind of face; a thickness along the normals at a rim that is not square to its neighbours (a frustum's top thickened outward with upright walls) is not built. Every face of the set must come out on both sides of the layer, else it is refused with the face; a thickness at which a face of either offset vanishes is the offset's "too far", in the whole thickness (also for `both`). **Names** are a shell's: a face lying on or along a face of the source is its copy in instance 0 (`F9:copy(0)/F1:face(1)` is the far side of a plate on a block's top, `F9:copy(0)/F1:face(2)` its rim along the front), one lying under a face of the set its copy in instance 1 (the near side); edges and vertices are named by their faces with the copies of their old names as aliases. A sketch on the near side looks into the source, as on the faces of a hollow (`sketch::resolve_face`). *Tests:* `crates/kernel/tests/thicken.rs` (a plate on, under and through a block's top, an angle on two faces, a tube on a rod, sleeves in and round a hole, a tilted frame, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6; what fits; bad input) and `crates/engine/tests/thicken.rs` (the same through the command, checked deep, the plate joined back into a taller block, the source kept, a fillet on the layer and a pin from its near side through edits of the thickness and the block, `feature.edit` to another face and side, save and reopen, undo and redo, bad parameters).

*Read back by FreeCAD:* `step_freecad/face_ops.rs` has a second set for these three (a wedge, a boss moved with its top, an angle thickened on two faces of a block, a sleeve on both sides of a hole's wall, a drafted frustum and a drafted rounded box, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6): FreeCAD finds valid solids with our volume and area within 1.2e-15 and our counts (bar the seams it adds to closed faces), 18 files.

**Delete face** (`feature.deleteFace`, `kernel::delete_face`, `regen::delete_face`; 2026-10-14). The faces given fall into groups that hang together by edges, and each group is closed on its own, one after the other; a group that neither way below fits is refused with the faces (and the edge) and the reason, and nothing is built.
- *(a) Holes filled.* A group whose every edge to the rest of the body lies in a hole of a face round it, each such hole bounded by the group alone: the wall of a hole through a plate, the wall and floor of a blind hole, the wall and top of a boss, the walls and floor of a pocket, the faces of a closed cavity (a whole shell with nothing round it). The group goes, those loops go out of their faces, and the faces round it go on across where the holes were on the surfaces they had: purely topological. What is checked: every such loop is a hole of its face (sampled and taken into the face's own parameters, where the normal is ∂u × ∂v, an outer loop turns counter-clockwise seen from outside and a hole clockwise; a loop that goes round a periodic direction, the rim of a cylinder, is no hole; one round a pole of a sphere cannot be told and is refused), a neighbour that shares only part of a loop with the group refuses it, the result passes `topo::check` and encloses a positive volume (deleting the outside of a hollow block leaves the cavity inside out: refused).
- *(b) A strip collapsed.* One face with one loop of four edges (two sides along the faces beside it, an end against a face at each end: a chamfer or a fillet along one edge) or with two loops of one closed edge each (the round of a rod's rim, of a hole's mouth, of a boss's foot). The face goes, its two sides become one edge between the faces beside it, the two vertices of each end edge become one, and `tweak::resurface_at` finds those vertices where the surfaces round them meet and makes every edge ending in them again on its faces' surfaces, with all of `resurface`'s checks: the faces beside the strip are continued until they meet, and the faces at its ends lose the end edge and gain the corner. Which edges are the sides: the two along which the face is tangent to its neighbours when there are such (a fillet), else both pairings are tried, the longer sides first, and the first that closes the body is taken (a chamfer on a block: its ends lie in parallel faces that never meet). Refused, with the faces: an end face tangent to the strip or to a face beside it (the next blend of a chain, the ball in a corner of three fillets), an end vertex where more than three faces meet (two blends in a mitre, a corner of the part), faces beside it that do not meet in a line or a circle when continued (the top of a block or of a thin wall, the wall of a boss alone), the edges they would meet in being elliptic or freeform.
- *Regeneration* names the corner where blends meet (`chamferCorner`, `filletCorner`) when the kernel refuses a deletion that holds one (it goes only with the blends round it, (c) below), and meshes the result to find two faces far apart that the continuation ran into (`tess::body_self_intersections`, as for offsets). Faces are copies (`F9:copy(0)/…`), edges and vertices are named by their faces with the copy of their old name as an alias; the edge a strip collapsed into and the vertex its ends became have only the name by their faces. After a block, a fillet on an edge and the fillet face deleted, the block's own names (`F1:face(1)`) do not answer again: the faces answer to `F3:copy(0)/F2:copy(0)/F1:face(1)`, as after every feature made from another, and the restored sharp edge is the edge between two such names (a chamfer on it is built in the tests).
- *Tests:* `crates/kernel/tests/delete_face.rs` (a block filleted or chamfered on an edge, upright or in a tilted frame, inside fillets and chamfers of a step, the round and the bevel of a rod's rim, of a hole's mouth and of a boss's foot, two fillets at once, a hole, a blind hole, a boss, a pocket, a cross hole in a rod and a cavity deleted, each against the body before the blend or the cut: volume, area and counts to 1e-9 at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6; what is refused), `crates/engine/tests/face_ops.rs` (the same through the command with `document.check {"deep": true}`, upstream edits that move the hole, a hole that misses so the face is gone, `causedBy`, `feature.edit`, inserting at the rollback marker, save and reopen, undo and redo, bad parameters), a row of `failed_features.rs`, and `step_freecad/face_ops.rs` (five cases at three scales: FreeCAD finds valid solids of our volume within 1.2e-15).

**References and names** (§4.1): faces by name, all of one body (offset) or of `source` (shell's `open`), resolved at every regeneration as the blends resolve theirs; a cap's name survives a change of its outline as everywhere. The result's faces are copies: `F9:copy(0)/…` for every face of an offset (moved or not; a face that moved is still that face) and for the shell's skin; a face of the hollow is the copy in instance 1 of the face it lies behind, `F9:copy(1)/F2:side(e7)`. After an offset every edge and vertex is the copy of what it was (as after a move); in a shell they are named by their faces with the copy of their old name as an alias. A sketch on a face that an offset moved lies where the face lies now (`sketch::resolve_face` drops the frame's origin onto the face's plane and keeps its axes); a sketch on a face of the hollow looks into the hollow (its normal is the face's own, its x axis that of the face it lies behind, so its y is mirrored, as a wall seen from behind). The same holds for a face a subtraction keeps of its tool, turned inside out (the floor of a pocket, the wall of a hole), since 2026-10-14: a sketch or a datum plane on it looks out of the material, its x axis that of the tool's face and its y mirrored (`sketch::resolve_face`; before, it looked into the material as the tool's face did, and a pin extruded from a pocket's floor went down).

- *Tests:* `crates/kernel/tests/offset_face.rs` and `shell.rs` (closed forms at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6; the largest distance that fits; what is refused), `crates/engine/tests/face_ops.rs` (the same through the commands with `document.check {"deep": true}`, upstream edits, a lost face, `feature.edit`, sketches on a moved face and on a face of the hollow, a chamfer on an edge of a shell, save and reopen, undo and redo, bad parameters), two rows of `failed_features.rs`, and `step_freecad/face_ops.rs` (33 files, FreeCAD finds valid solids of our volume within 3e-15).

### Shell as thick as its rounds (2026-10-21, an R1 blocker)

**Finding.** The classic enclosure, a box whose edges are rounded 2 mm and then shelled 2 mm, was refused: the inner offset of a round of radius `r` by the thickness `t` has radius `r − t ≤ 0`, so the offset (which keeps every entity, `tweak`) cannot make it, and the shell said "too far" with the largest thickness just under `r`.

**Method: the hollow of the body with the vanishing blends collapsed.** The hollow is the solid eroded by a ball of radius `t` (the points at least `t` from its outside). A rolling-ball blend of radius `r` is, there, the solid *opened* by a ball of radius `r` (eroded by it and grown back). For `t ≥ r` the erosion by `t` of the opening is the erosion of the solid itself: the opening lies in the solid; and a point whose ball of radius `t` fits in the solid has the ball of radius `t − r` round it fitting in the solid eroded by `r`, which grown by `r` lies in the opening, so its ball of radius `t` fits in the opening too. So the hollow is the offset of the body in which exactly those blends are collapsed into the sharp edges and corners they round (`shell::vanishing`, then `delete_face::delete_faces`), every other face offset as before (a round of a larger radius rebuilt at `r − t`, a concave one grown to `r + t`). The identity holds for any faces the ball rolls along, so analytic and freeform blends alike: cylinders, tori, the balls in corners, and the fillet faces `offset_blend` recognises (their supports read off them). Faces of the hollow are named after the faces of the solid they lie behind, as before; the collapsed blends have a skin and no face in the hollow, and the edge they collapsed into is named by its faces.

**Which faces vanish (each condition and why it is general).**
- *The radius reaches the thickness:* `r + along ≤` the linear tolerance, with `along` the inward distance along the surface's own normal (`−t` for a convex face, `+t` for a reversed one) for a cylinder, a sphere and a torus's tube; for a recognised fillet face its ball convex and `r − t ≤` the tolerance. The same test the offset uses to say "the radius would reach zero", so nothing in between is left to the offset.
- *It is a rolling-ball blend:* a strip whose two *sides*, the edges along which its ball rolls (lines along a cylinder's axis, circles about a torus's axis, the edges to the two faces a fillet face rolls along), are tangent to their neighbours (normals within a sine of 1e-6, delete face's measure), or a ball (sphere) every edge of which it shares tangentially with other vanishing blends. Read off the surface's own axis and the edges' directions, never off names or the feature that made them; a face that loses what makes it a blend (a corner whose neighbour does not vanish) drops out, until none does. A cylinder that is no blend (the wall of a rod, whose edges are circles) never qualifies, so a rod thinner than the wall stays "too far".
- *Its sides run along faces that keep a wall:* an open face with a blend along it keeps a wall in the hollow and is opened afterwards (*An open face with a blend along it* below), so a blend along it vanishes like any other.
- *The collapse succeeds:* otherwise the shell is refused as before (too far, with what fits).

**Delete face, (c): blends that meet, collapsed together** (`delete_face::collapse_network`, the general form of (b)). A group of faces that meet, each a strip (`pairings`: its sides along faces, at least one of them a face that stays) or a face every edge of which it shares with the group (a corner), goes as a whole: every strip's end edges and every corner's edges shrink to points (their vertices merged, union by union, the least vertex the representative), a strip's two sides become one edge between the faces beside it when both stay, and vanish when they shrink to a point too (a round along a round: its side on the round shrinks with the round's end, and its side on the top with it); a strip with one side shrunk and the other not, a loop left empty, an edge between two faces that stay shrunk to a point, or an end face tangent to the strip (a blend going on that stays) is refused. The faces round the group are then continued until they meet (`tweak::resurface_at` on the merged vertices, as for one strip) and the result held as every step is (`held`). So the corner where blends meet, the mitre of two blends and the whole network of a block rounded all round go back to sharp edges; a corner alone, or a ball with only some of its blends, is still refused (`feature.deleteFace` names the corner then).

### An open face with a blend along it (2026-10-22)

**Finding.** A box rounded all round, shelled open at its top, was refused at every thickness: the open face is moved outward so the hollow comes out through it, and a blend tangent to it cannot follow (the offset refuses a tangent neighbour that does not move with the face; the round's offset `r − t` ends tangent to where the top would be at `−t`, never at `+t`).

**Method** (`shell::blend_along`, `shell::lip_fits`, `shell::prism`). An open face that a neighbour *keeping a wall* is tangent to across an edge (a blend runs along it; also the flat side of a slot, which its half-cylinders are tangent to; a tangent neighbour left open too goes outward with it, as before) is moved *inward* like every face that keeps a wall, so the blends along it keep theirs (offset to `r − t`, or collapsed from `t = r` on, `vanishing` now counting such a face as one that keeps a wall), and the hollow is then opened through it: the hollow's face behind it (flat, its edges lines and circles) is extruded along its outward normal by `2t` (as far past the face as an open face is moved outward, `t`) and united with the hollow, which the solid then loses as before. What this gives is the shell's own rule, a wall of the thickness behind every face that keeps one: the lip is the round's wall, `t` thick all along, ending square to the open face where the face's rim was (its ends are faces of the prism, named after the hollow behind the open face, `F9:copy(1)/F1:face(1)`); from `t = r` on the rounds are collapsed in the hollow, the walls run straight up and a ring of the top `t − r` wide is left on the lip. Every other neighbour of such a face has to be square to it (at the ends and the middle of every edge, within the sine 1e-6): then each face of the prism lies in the plane of a wall of the hollow and the union merges them, so the result there is the result of moving the face outward. A neighbour slanted against it (in or out) is refused naming both faces, since the prism square to the open face would cut that wall thinner over the top `t` (0.63 for 1 at 4 over 10) instead of continuing its offset; so is a face that has a blend keeping a wall along it and another tangent face left open. Every face of the hollow behind the open face gets its prism (all made from the hollow as offset), should it come out in pieces. Open faces without a tangent neighbour that keeps a wall are moved outward as before (a slanted wall goes on slanting to the opening); an open face with a blend along it that is not flat, or whose face behind has another curve, is refused with that reason. *Names:* every lip end is a face of a prism and named after the hollow behind the open face, so the ends share one name (`F3:copy(1)/F1:face(1)`), as pieces of one face do; a name per end, after the blend it ends, is owed.

**Tests** (fail before, pass after): `crates/kernel/tests/shell.rs` › `a_block_rounded_all_round_and_open_at_its_top_keeps_a_lip_along_its_rounds` (0.5, 1, 2, 2.5 against `V(r) − V_closed hollow − (w − 2m)(d − 2m) t`, `m = max(r, t)`; four lip ends below the radius, none from it on, a ring of the top past it; upright to 1e-9 and turned to the tolerance over the area; three scales; replaces the assertion that it is refused, an expectation change logged in the dev log), `a_round_left_open_with_the_faces_it_joins_goes_outward_with_them` (open at the top, the round and the front: the old path, closed form 198.283 at scale 1; refused by the first form of this change), `an_open_face_with_a_blend_along_it_and_a_slanted_neighbour_is_refused` (trapezoid prism slanting in and out), `a_slot_open_at_a_side_its_ends_are_tangent_to` (a channel: the slot less the slot `t` smaller less the slab `L (H − 2t) t`); `crates/engine/tests/face_ops.rs` › `a_box_rounded_all_round_shells_open_at_its_top_with_a_lip` (fillet then shell, 1, 2, 3 by `feature.edit`, closed form, `document.check` deep, the lip ends' and the top ring's names, three scales) and `a_round_left_open_with_its_faces_shells_as_before` (deep check).


### Offset of freeform faces (2026-10-16)

`feature.offsetFace`, `feature.shell` and `feature.thicken` refused a Bézier or NURBS face ("the offset of a freeform face is not built"), so the bike's saddle (a smooth loft whose rims are rolling-ball fillets) could not be shelled. They build freeform faces now, against closed forms and an independent volume (Tests, below). The saddle itself is shelled 2 mm only when its fillets' tangency error, carried into the offset, is accepted: before the merge review's bound (below, *Tangent faces share their normal*) it built and passed the deep check with its volume Simpson's rule over its wall areas; with the bound it is refused as too far (at 1e-6 mm nothing fitted; since the fits are smooth across borders and knots, 2026-10-16, what fits is below 0.106 mm, found by building bodies in 4.6 min instead of 21 s: the bodies below that build, each a whole shell of the saddle, about twenty of them in the halving; at 1e-5 and 1e-4 mm what fits is below 0.374 and 0.380 mm, its fillets being tangent only to about 6.6e-5 at 1e-4 mm), and at 0.3 mm the boolean that takes the hollow out refuses skin and hollow as nearly coincident. The developer decided that no such deviation is accepted: the bounded turn stays ([`decisions.md`](../decisions.md) › *2026-10-16: An offset across faces that are tangent only within the tolerance*). The general method owed, the offset of a blend face as the blend rebuilt between the offset faces (radius r ± d), is built since 2026-10-18 (*Offset of a fillet: rebuilt*, below), and the saddle shells.

**The surface** (`geom::offset`). The offset `S + d N` of a spline is no spline (the unit normal has a root in it). It is approximated by a B-spline in the *same parameters*, point for point: the exact offset is sampled on a grid, a quintic is laid through the samples (banded interpolation along v, then along u, `refine::interpolate`) piece by piece between *breaks*, and the fit is held against the exact offset at the middle of every step (charged to the direction whose own line of samples explains the error, else both) and, once it holds there, at the quarters: measured there, not bounded between (in the merge review's fuzzing one fit in a hundred exceeded its limit between samples, by 0.3 %; the limit is a quarter of the tolerance, so the body stays within it); steps over a quarter of the tolerance are halved, with the neighbours the halving would push over (`steps_to_halve`, the creep the rolling-ball blends found, with the decay of the quintic). The breaks are the knots of the surface at which its offset is less than twice continuously differentiable (any knot of a cubic, the double knots of rational arcs): across one, a fit that goes on smoothly converges at second order and refines whole rows; between them the offset is analytic and the fit converges at sixth order (a quarter-sphere patch to 2.5e-7 relative in about 50 × 50 samples). Same parameters means the same domain and seams; the fit may also be made over a box past the domain, where the spans go on as the functions they are (`offset_over`), for a face that grows. Since 2026-10-16 (*Fillets along the rims of offset faces*, below) the box is fitted in one piece across the domain's ends (they are no breaks: the surface goes on past them as its end spans do, and the turn, held there at what it is on the border, only bends the offset by its slope times the distance, far below the tolerance over a step), in steps past the domain as long as those of the span next to it; the fit reaches a step further (a fifth of the end span) past every end in a direction in which the surface does not close, and is cut back to the box exactly (`NurbsSurface::restricted`, knot insertion), so the face's borders lie inside the fit; and at a break where the surface is twice continuously differentiable (a simple knot of a cubic: the offset is once there) the pieces join with a knot of the degree less one, one interpolation across them (`refine::interpolate_on`), so the fitted normal does not jump. Caps: the samples a surface may have (`MAX_CONTROL_POINTS`) and the banded solve's size; past them, "could not be approximated".

**Folds, proven, not sampled.** The offset is regular where `1 − d κ > 0` for both principal curvatures, which is the positive definiteness of `|n| G − d W` with `n = w⁴ (S_u × S_v)`, `G` its first fundamental form and `W` its second times `w⁴ |n|`, all Bernstein forms built exactly per Bézier span from the homogeneous control net (products and derivatives of `P = w S` and `w`). Per piece of a span, three tests, cheapest first: Gershgorin's discs on the ranges of the entries scaled by the diagonal of `G` (a parametrisation much faster one way than the other spoils unscaled discs); the scaled matrix diagonally dominant point by point with `|n|` replaced by the chord of the square root over the range of `n · n` (below it) and its tangent (above it), forms linear in `n · n`, so no product of high degree; then the leading entry and the determinant of the matrix at both bounds of `|n|` (it is affine in `|n|`), forms of twice the degree built only for a span that needs them and split along. A piece that none proves is halved both ways; a piece at whose middle the matrix is not positive definite is a fold, with the radius of curvature there; a piece neither when the pieces run out is "not proven" (within a few per cent of a radius of curvature). A cheap bound from the control net alone (`derivative_bounds`, the weighted normal net) proves most polynomial spans outright. Poles (`geom::pole`) are refused before: the normal is not defined there; a surface of degrees too high for the forms (about sixteen times its degrees each way, past the coefficients a form may have) is refused as such. The proof covers the surface's own normal; the turn below (`offset_along`) is bounded by its caller instead. The rational fillets of the saddle (300 spans each, rational in one direction) first took 20 s each; the weights' powers in the homogeneous forms vary several-fold over a span, which defeats tests on ranges, and the chord test and binomial rows built once per product brought it to 1.5 to 2.7 s.

**Closing the body round it** (`kernel::offset_face`, `kernel::tweak`, `kernel::offset_normal`):
- *An edge that is no longer a closed form* (an ellipse, a spline, a line or circle on a freeform face that is one no longer) is found again where its faces meet: the old curve's points, moved as its ends moved (in proportion along it, so the points run from the new start to the new end; a closed edge has no ends and its points start where they were), each to the point where the surfaces meet in the plane through it square to the old curve (Newton from where the one before landed; without the plane a point that lies on both surfaces can be anywhere along where they meet, and a coarse loft's cap edge came out with samples a tolerance apart along it), a cubic through them at the old parameters, refined to a quarter of the tolerance. Faces tangent along the edge (their outward normals within `TANGENT`, decided on the old faces) meet in it as one surface: the points are found on the first, the other held to it at the end; deciding it point by point made the points jump.
- *An edge along a border of a freeform face's parameters* (the borders of a rolling-ball blend, the seam between two halves of a loft) is that border of the new surface, between the new vertices (`Iso`): on that surface exactly, where a curve found between tangent surfaces can slide along them. A vertex at the end of such an edge where another face crosses the border is found on the border (`onto_border`).
- *Tangent faces share their normal.* A blend is tangent to its neighbours only as well as it was fitted (the saddle's rolling-ball fillets to 1.2e-6 in the normal); offsets by 2 mm, each along its own normal, part by 2.4e-6 mm, more than the document's tolerance of 1e-6 mm, and the faces no longer meet. The exact offset of a rolling-ball blend is tangent to its neighbours' offsets, so along each border of a moving freeform face that lies along an edge to a tangent face moving by the same distance, the face is offset along the normal the two share there (the neighbour's, or the mean when the neighbour is turned along that edge too), blended across the face as a Coons patch is; at a corner the border whose neighbour is not turned holds. The turn moves the face's offset by the distance times the sine it turns by, and that is held to a quarter of the tolerance (`TURN_SHARE`; at the border's samples and on a grid across the face, measured there), so that with the fit's quarter the face stays within half the tolerance of its exact offset; past it the operation is too far, with the largest distance that fits (`TURN_TOO_FAR`): faces that part by more at that distance do not meet as tangent faces within the tolerance, and calling them tangent would be a deviation the document's tolerance does not allow. A border's turn is measured where its edge covers it and falls to nothing at the border's ends past that part (a quintic smoothstep, twice differentiable, since 2026-10-16; linearly before), so a part of the border along other edges, or along none, is moved by less than the bound; past an end of the border that its edge reaches it holds at what it is there (a face that grows past the border has the edge go on along it; it fell to nothing there before), and past the domain the turn is what it is at the nearest point of the domain's border, so it is nowhere longer than on the domain, where it is held to its share (the merge review: continuing the spline of the turn past what was measured would leave it bounded only where it was sampled).
- *`meet`*: a surface nearly tangent to one before it (sine within `TANGENT`) that the point lies on within the tolerance asks no step: the step it would ask runs along the tangent plane by the residual over the sine, and at the saddle's front vertex sent the point 34 mm across the part. Within the tolerance, not a share of it: two fitted offsets that share a border are each within a quarter of the tolerance of it, so within half of it of each other, and with half as the share a lofted tube's quarter pieces were taken as two directions.
- *A face that grows past its surface* (inward on a ball, the band between two planes grows toward the poles): the new place of every vertex is foreseen where the tangent planes, moved, meet, and a freeform face whose nearest point to it lies on a border, not square below it by any amount, is continued past that border (`borders_passed`); when the faces still do not meet where a freeform one ends, the faces named are continued past every border, then further (`REACHES`, a few tries). A moving face is offset over the wider box; a freeform neighbour that does not move is continued exactly (`NurbsSurface::extended`) or, where its weights would not stay positive, within a quarter of the tolerance. Continuing everything by the distance, the first try, put the saddle's side over the samples a surface may have.

**Refused, with the face:** a pole or a border drawn into a point; a fold, as too far, with the largest distance at which the offset is proven not to fold: found by halving on the proof alone, not by building, since close to a fold the offset forms a cusp and a body built there costs more the closer it comes (a quarter rod's tries took 4, 5, 20, 45 and 86 s toward its radius; a body at the limit reported may still be refused on another check, which then says so); the band just short of a radius of curvature that the bounds cannot prove (a quarter of an elliptic rod whose smallest radius of curvature is 0.5 is proven regular to 0.4999998); an offset that does not come within a quarter of the tolerance in the samples a surface may have; what the closed forms refuse. The halving of every face operation now stops when what fits is known to within the tolerance (it went on 48 times).

**Cost.** The saddle shelled at 2 mm before the bound (release, loaded machine): the side's two halves 0.15 s each, the four fillets 1.4 to 2.7 s each, closing the body under 1 s; then `tweak`'s check that every face next to a moved edge is still skin (64 points classified by the booleans' ray casting) 23 s, against fitted surfaces of up to 117 × 168 and 3500 × 11 control points: the classification's cost, for the boolean kernel to look at; the boolean that takes the hollow out 15 to 18 s; the whole command 60 to 90 s.

**What each new condition is, and why it is general.**
- `OFFSET_SHARE` and `EDGE_SHARE` (a quarter of the tolerance): how far a fitted offset and a found-again edge may be from the exact ones, the share the rolling-ball blends are held to, so that two fitted things that meet are within half the tolerance of each other and the checks at the tolerance hold.
- The breaks of the fit (knots where the offset is less than twice continuously differentiable) follow from the order of the fit; `FIT_DEGREE` 5 (sixth-order convergence) is a cost choice, `START` (one piece's worth of samples per span) follows from it, and `QUINTIC_DECAY`/`CUBIC_DECAY` are the roots of the splines' Euler–Frobenius polynomials, not tuned.
- `MAX_PIECES` (4096) and `MAX_DEPTH` (14) for the regularity proof, `PLAIN_DEPTH` (3), `PASSES`, the sample caps, `EDGE_START` (8), `EDGE_SAMPLES` (4096), `BORDER_STEPS` (32), `ALONG` (32 samples of a turn), `REACHES` (1, 4, 16 times the distance) and `MOST_TRIES` (8) are budgets: what runs out of them is refused or not proven, with the reason, never built wrong.
- `SQUARE` (1e-9, a sine): a foot is a nearest point when the offset is square to the surface to rounding; whether a vertex passes a border is decided by it, not by the tolerance (a vertex past a border by less than the tolerance is past it: the 1e-3 tube showed it).
- `HELD` (the tolerance): see `meet` above; it is what the check at the end of `meet` asks.
- `TANGENT` (1e-4, a sine between outward normals) is the one classification constant: faces meeting at a smaller angle are taken as tangent by construction (a blend and what it rolls along, the pieces of one surface), which turns their normals to a shared one along iso borders, finds an edge between them on one of them, and lets `meet` treat them as one direction. It is not derived: a crease any design means is far steeper (1e-4 is 0.006°), and what fitting leaves of a tangency is far smaller (the saddle's fillets 1.2e-6, at the default tolerance of 1e-6 mm on 265 mm). It no longer decides how far a face may move: the turn it allows is held to `TURN_SHARE` of the tolerance at the distance asked (above), so a crease of 0.005° shelled 10 mm is refused as too far, not built half a micrometre off. The turn is general within that bound: any moving freeform face along any border of its parameters that lies along an edge to a face tangent to it and moving by the same distance, which is what the exact offset of a rolling-ball blend (tangent to its neighbours' offsets) asks. It is not general beyond the class: two faces tangent along an edge that is no border of either's parameters (two lofts meeting along a trimmed edge) are not turned, and their offsets part by the distance times their mismatch, refused when that is more than the tolerance; and faces that meet at a crease shallower than a tenth of a degree but steeper than `TANGENT` (the coarse loft's two halves at its closed sections' seam, 1.2e-4) leave `meet` an intersection that the fits' own error moves by their error over the sine, and may be refused as "no longer meet".
- `TURN_SHARE` (a quarter of the tolerance): how far a turn may move a face's offset; with the fit's quarter, half the tolerance per face, the tolerance for two faces that meet.
- The domain's ends were breaks of a fit made over a box past them (what the offset was made along, a turned normal held past the domain, did not go on smoothly there); since 2026-10-16 they are not: the turn held past them bends the offset only by its slope (of the size of what fitting left, about 1e-7 over the domain) times the distance, which a fit going on across holds, and a break there left the surface only continuous along the border of a face that grows past it, where a blend asks for its normal.
- (2026-10-16) The margin is a step of the surface's own end knot span, also where the fit's box reaches past the domain: the steps there are as long as those of that span, so it is one such step whatever the box. A wide fit that is not proven regular, or does not hold, is not an error: the fit ends at the box as before.
- (2026-10-16) The margin past each end, a fifth of the surface's end knot span (`START` steps per span, the fit's own first step), in every direction in which the surface does not close on itself within the fit's share of the tolerance (a seam's ends have to stay where they are): a step of the fit's own sampling, so the border is as far inside the fit as the nearest sample is; taken only where the surface continued that far is proven not to fold, else the fit ends at the box as before. Not tuned: any margin of at least a step puts the border inside the interpolation; more costs samples.
- (2026-10-16) Cutting the fit back takes a knot within 16 rounding units (of the parameter's size and the domain's width) of the cut as the cut itself: the knots are averages of the parameters, and one that is the domain's end but for rounding left a span 1e-17 wide past which the surface stalled (the rational ball zone's hollow). Rounding-level, not a tolerance.
- (2026-10-16) The join at a break: of the degree less one (once continuously differentiable) where the surface is twice (`degree ≥ multiplicity + 2`: the offset is then once), of full multiplicity otherwise; each piece keeps its own averaged knots, the piece after a smooth join one more (averages of one parameter fewer), so the interpolation stays square. Where that knot vector gives no solution (Schoenberg–Whitney) the pieces join at a point as before; that is the old construction, not a special case.
- (2026-10-16) Steps past the domain as long as the steps of the span next to it (at least one): an interpolating spline through steps that change in length by a large factor at once swings between them, and refining there only made the contrast worse (the box's five steps of 0.0014 next to steps of 0.067 never fitted).

- The halving of every face operation stops when the bracket is within the tolerance: what fits is reported to the tolerance.

### Fillets along the rims of offset faces (2026-10-16)

The bike's saddle shelled first (2 mm) and filleted after, the designer's order: the outer rims (4 and 3 mm, on the skin, which is the loft itself) built; the inner ones (2 and 1 mm, on the hollow, which is the loft's offset) were refused, `FreeJoint` at the rear and "could not be approximated" at the front; the same with a two-section loft whose side was moved ±2 mm, while the same rims on the loft, on a cavity and on a plane-trimmed loft built. **The cause was in the offset's surfaces, not in the blend**: the rolling ball asks of a face that its normal be continuous where the face is smooth, to rounding (a ball is settled to 1e-14 of the size of the part; a joint's two balls may part by three quarters of the tolerance, about 3e-7 in the normal at a radius of 3 mm), and the fitted offsets gave it three discontinuities, each within the quarter of the tolerance the surface was held to in position:

1. *A kink along the face's own border.* The fit over a box past the domain kept the domain's ends as breaks, pieces joined at a point; where a face grows past a border (the outward offset) that border lay on the break, and its normal there was the next piece's, 2e-8 off its own: the ball at a joint, found by bisection along the smooth edge, ended on the jump (5.7e-8 off the radius, `switch_ball`'s check), `FreeJoint`.
2. *A kink at every knot of the loft.* Between sections a lofted side is a cubic with simple knots; its offset is only once differentiable there, so the fit broke there too, and two fits that meet at a point leave the normal turning by what each fit's slope is off: the balls rolling over the knot line jumped by more than the fillet could follow (the refinement stalled at 1.2 to 4 times its bound at the knot, 300 samples in), "could not be approximated".
3. *The slope at the ends of the fit.* An interpolation is least accurate in its slope at its ends: where the hollow's two side halves meet along their smooth seam, one half ended its fit there, and the two were tangent only to 9e-7, decreasing along the seam; a ball of 3 mm turns that into 3.4e-6 between the two halves' balls at the joint, `FreeJoint` again.

A fourth, met on the way: the box's part past the domain was cut into five steps of its own whatever its width (0.0014 next to steps of 0.067), so a fit going on across the domain's end swung between them and never held.

**The fix** (`geom::offset`, `geom::extend`, `geom::refine`, `kernel::offset_normal`, `kernel::offset_face`): the fit goes on across the domain's ends as one piece, in steps past the domain as long as those of the span next to it; it reaches one step further past every end that is not a seam and is cut back exactly (knot insertion), so the face's borders lie inside it; it joins once differentiably at the knots where the offset is once differentiable (a knot of the degree less one, one interpolation across the pieces); and the turn of tangent faces' normals (*Tangent faces share their normal*) falls off smoothly (a smoothstep) and is held past an end the edge reaches (where it used to fall to nothing at once) and past the domain, so it is nowhere longer than on the domain, where it is bounded. The blend is unchanged. Measured on the small loft's offset: the fitted normal against the exact one 3e-8 at the borders (9e-7 before), the two halves tangent to 1.1e-8 to 2.9e-8 along their seam (to 9.2e-7 before), no jump across a knot (5e-13, the curvature over 1e-12 of parameter). The bench models are unchanged to the last bit: the engine block, the turbofan, the rotor and the impeller's blades regenerate with no failed feature, the same volumes and the same names (each run with the release CLI before and after).

**The saddle shelled first, all four rims** (`target/work/bike/offset/saddle-shell-first.json`, release, 2 min): every fillet builds, `document.check` deep passes, 125 135.220 mm³ (the halved one 62 276.97). What was written when it was not built (decided 2026-10-16): Rolling a ball between faces an offset made is what it needs and works now; what is missing is that a body does not say which of its faces is a rolling-ball blend, of which radius, between which faces: a face is a surface. Two ways, neither clean today: (a) the blend face carries its definition (a surface kind for a rolling-ball blend: its radius, convexity, its two supports and its spine, evaluated by the fitted spline it has now), which the offset then rebuilds with `r ± d` between the supports' offsets, as `blend::rolling` would; (b) the shell rebuilt in the history (the solid's blends taken off, the solid shelled, the blends put back on both skins), a reordering of features, which is the designer's order and not a method for any body. ### Bounded work: the hangs the design fuzzer found (2026-10-17)

The random-design fuzzer (`apps/craft3d-fuzz`) found offsets and shells of blend faces (fillets and chamfers of spheres, tori and revolved rings) that ran for ten minutes and more. Sampled (`gdb` on a release build with line tables): nothing looped; every step was bounded and the time was their product. Such a blend face is a NURBS of a few hundred spans (581 knots along a fillet of a ball); one offset of it costs a second (its regularity proof, `geom::offset`, up to 4096 pieces of polynomial forms of 16 times its degrees, then the fit), up to fifteen when it is continued past its borders; the tweak then classifies points beside every moved edge against those faces (`buried`, a ray each, seconds); `at` tries up to three reaches per size, and `fitting_with` halves to the tolerance, twenty and more sizes. The legitimate dearest face operations of the tests (a two-section loft moved ±2 mm, and shelled) cost about the same as one such try.

**The rule: a face operation's search has a work bound** (`face_op_work`: `FACE_OP_WORK`, 450 million units of `geom::allowance`, plus 20 000 per span of a freeform face that moves, a million per edge on a face that moves and 50 000 per freeform span of the body the points are classified against, so that a shell of many faces is not held to what one offset may do). The unit is one freeform evaluation (or 256 multiplications on nets and polynomial forms), charged where the work is done; it follows the time within a factor of two or three (one to three million units a second in release). `fitting_with` holds its whole search to the bound; the loops that can run long ask at each pass (the regularity proof per piece and per span, the fit per pass, `at` per reach, `surfaced` per face, the tweak per edge and before its classification, `contains_points` per point, `Chart::turn` per step, `sigma_near` per question, the tessellator per round) and stop with an error. The operation is then refused with the faces of its first failure: "it needs more work than one operation is allowed …". A loop that stops returns an error, never "nothing found", and a try during which the bound was reached is not believed whatever it gave (the classification of `buried` lets a body through when it cannot tell), so a body that comes out is one whose try ran to its end. The dearest face operations of the action matrix's rotor are its blades' start caps moved 0.17 mm: 82, 275 and 293 million units on the three blades, though they are one geometry turned about the hub. The classification casts its rays along fixed axes and splits the fine pieces of a surface's tree (`PatchTree::finer`) six times as often where the blades lie at a slant to them (1.3 against 8.1 million pieces, measured), so the work is not the same, and the bound is half as much again as the dearest so that every copy gets the same verdict (`crates/engine/tests/face_op_bound.rs`, ignored, release). The end cap moved 0.055 mm, a ratchet entry, takes 87 million.

**What an operation counts is a function of its inputs** (review, 2026-10-18). Caches would make it depend on what ran before: a hit skips the work a miss counts (the same offset counted 1.8 million units cold and 0.1 million warm). Two rules, by the kind of cache. *Answers remembered across operations* that are a pure speed-up of a counted search (the projections a surface remembers, the meshes of faces `tess::remembered` keeps) are kept with the units their making took, measured on the thread that made them, and a hit charges them again; a cache keeps nothing made while not counting or past the bound (`allowance::counting`). *Structures and answers made once and asked for again and again* (patch trees, their fine pieces, numeric intersections, `check_remembered`) keep the work their making took (`allowance::cost_of`, which measures also inside an uncounted region) and charge it once per operation under a bound (`allowance::once`, `first_time`), made then or found; projectors, pieces and the projection grid are made uncounted (`allowance::uncounted`, inside which the count stands still and nothing is marked charged). The memos of a patch tree (nearest points, sigma bounds, lower bounds) are kept per operation under a bound (`ScopedMemos`, by `allowance::scope`): the search is counted, and remembered for the rest of the operation only. Thread pools (the tessellator's, the mass integral's) add each helper's count to the waiting thread when it is joined. Held by `the_same_operation_counts_the_same_whatever_ran_before` (kernel: an offset and a shell of fresh bodies, cold, warm and after another boolean) and `a_self_check_counts_the_same_whatever_was_meshed_before` (tess: 1 and 4 threads, the meshes kept or not).

**What is not bounded.** Booleans (also the subtract of a shell and the union of a thicken, which run after the search), blends (fillet, chamfer), sweeps, lofts, delete face and the joins of patterns: none of them sets a bound, and the loops polled for the face operations do nothing outside one. They are bounded by their own caps (of pieces, steps, rounds), as before.

**And the work made smaller where it was waste.** `NurbsSurface::normal_net` (every freeform face pair of a boolean, and the classification of a point against freeform faces) computed four binomials per pair of coefficients: computed once per direction, bit for bit the same products, the loft's offset went from 7.1 to 4.4 s (release).

**Measured** (release, the 19 reproducers one after another on a machine at load 30 to 100): the offsets and shells of blend faces end in 0.5 to 2.5 minutes, refused at the bound or (5599, 6195, 1279, 14064) by what limits them, where they ran for ten minutes and more; the chamfers whose self-check is spent in 8 to 30 s; the pattern of 10 000 balls in 2.5 s. `cargo xtask actions --models rotor,impeller-blades,turbofan --per-kind 0`: every ratchet entry of a face operation holds (the one regression, a turbofan chamfer whose volume is off by 3 %, is the same on work without this change). **Open**: a try on blend faces is dear, so the bound is high and a refusal slow; the classification of points against freeform faces is most of it (the boolean's work); the refusal does not say which size was found to fit.

**Tests.** `crates/geom/tests/offset.rs` (an offset under a bound too small stops with `OffsetError::Spent`, within the bound and a pass); `crates/kernel/tests/offset_freeform.rs` (refused by name under no bound; the elliptic rod's fold search ended half way by half its work; a shell the same; the counts above); `crates/engine/tests/fuzz_findings.rs` (each reproducer replayed on a thread of its own that fails at a hard limit instead of hanging, each step within a bound on its work, what was built valid under `document.check` deep, the outcome of every feature pinned; six in the gate, the others `cargo test --release -p craft3d-engine --test fuzz_findings -- --include-ignored`).

**The halving has a bound of its own (2026-10-21).** Five of the eleven timeouts of the fuzz run of 2026-10-20 (seeds 20795706, 20814691, 20818576, 20832173, 20833261: shells and offsets of chamfers and fillets on cones, balls and tori, at the three scales) were refusals: the try at the whole distance failed in seconds, and the search for the largest part that fits then halved to the tolerance, 13 to 20 tries of 5 to 18 million units and 4 to 48 s each, together within `FACE_OP_WORK` (which the rotor's legitimate offsets need) but minutes long. The halving only finds what to say in the refusal, so once the try at the whole has failed it may spend `HALVING_WORK` (16 million units): a try is made only while what the halving has spent and the dearest try so far (the whole one included, taken as what the next will cost) fit within it; otherwise it stops, with the largest part found to fit so far (a part that does fit: `FaceOpTooFar`) or, when none was found yet, with the reason the whole does not (`FaceOp`). Where tries are cheap (analytic faces count nothing) it halves to the tolerance as before; where the whole try alone costs more, the refusal comes after it. The dearest try left, an offset of a chamfer on a torus that is built (seed 20795706, 48 s, 13 million units: the classification of points against the face spent its time where nothing is counted, searching the whole tree of the surface for each point), is 16 s since that search passes by what cannot change its answer ([`booleans.md`](booleans.md) › *The fuzzer's timeouts of 2026-10-20*). Held by `offset_face::tests` (cheap tries halve to the tolerance; dear ones stop at the bound, with the largest found or the reason) and the replays in `crates/engine/tests/fuzz_findings.rs`. Measured (the test profile, on a machine at load 4 to 7, which runs these as fast as release): the shrunk reproducers' refusals in 15 to 50 s where they ran for minutes; of the full seeds, 20832173 81 → 28 s and 20833261 97 → 31 s in all (every oracle of the fuzzer). *Open*: one try on blend faces of a big body costs up to 50 million units and 30 to 70 s (seed 20814691's shell 72 s, 20795706's offset 50 s, within `FACE_OP_WORK`, which the rotor's legitimate offsets need), most of it uncounted classification and fitting.

### Offset of a fillet: rebuilt (2026-10-18)

The method the developer decided on 2026-10-16 (*An offset across faces that are tangent only within the tolerance*): the offset of a fillet face is the fillet rebuilt between the offset faces it rolls along, with its radius moved by the distance, exact and tangent by construction; its fitted spline is not offset. `kernel::offset_blend` (which faces, the refusals, the order) and `blend::rebuild` (reading and rebuilding a fillet); `offset_face::offset_for` takes them, so offset face, shell and thicken all do.

**Why it is the offset.** A fillet is the surface balls of radius `r` sweep, their centres (the spine) `r` from both faces. Its exact offset by `d` is what balls of radius `r + d` (convex: the centres in the material) or `r − d` (concave) about the same centres sweep, and those are exactly the balls that far from both faces moved by `d`: the fillet between the offset faces. So it is tangent to them, where the old route offset each face along its own normal and left them meeting only as well as the fit was tangent.

**What a face is, read off it, checked.** The B-rep does not say that a face is a fillet; its surface and its neighbours do, and its name says which radius it was made with. A freeform face that moves is taken for a fillet when (1) its surface has the shape the rolling ball makes (`blend::rolling::refine`: three rows across, rational quadratic in `v` over `[0, 1]`); (2) its borders `v = 0` and `v = 1` each lie along edges to one face only (`offset_normal::v_border_neighbours`), the two faces it rolls along; (3) the radius measured at its two end rows, which are exact sections (`ρ = (q₁ − q₂) · (n₁ − n₂) / |n₁ − n₂|²` from the feet and the faces' outward normals there, its sign whether the centres lie in the material), is one radius: the radius its name gives (`regen::face_ops::blends`: a face whose name goes back to `F3:fillet(…)` gets that fillet's radius) is taken when both agree with it to the tolerance, else the measured one when the two ends agree to 1e-9; (4) at 17 places along it each row is an arc of the exact ball of that radius between the two faces, found in the row's own plane, to the tolerance: on the ball, in the plane of its centre and feet (another circle of the ball through the feet is tangent to the faces too), its ends on the faces; (5) proven, not sampled, between the rows: the fillet rebuilt between the same faces with the same radius (`rebuild::proven`), and the face's lines at `v = k/8` proven within three quarters of the tolerance of it over the part its edges reach (`certify::SurfaceBound::curve_within`, up to 65 536 pieces a line; the saddle's fillets take about 16 000), so within the tolerance of the exact fillet. A face that fails any of these is offset as every freeform face is. The review's probes (rows turned off the ball's great circle by 0.05 to 0.5, which passed (4) without the plane; a fillet of 33 rows whose odd rows bulge by their weight, which passed at the 17 sampled ones) are not recognised at three scales (`offset_blend.rs` › `a_face_that_only_looks_like_a_fillet_is_not_rebuilt`).

**Rebuilt.** The new ball is found in the plane through the old surface's first border at `u`, square to it, which holds the old centre (the radius from the first face along its normal, square to the border), started from that centre: the new section at `u` is the ball about the same centre, so the new surface keeps the old one's parameters (an edge along a border is that border again, `tweak::Iso`). At a joint with the next fillet of a chain the planes turn into the joint's own plane (the old end row, exact), so two fillets of a chain rebuilt each on its own meet in one section. Sections start evenly (as many as the old surface has spans, an even start: an interpolation through steps whose lengths jump swings between them), from the old surface's part the face's edges reach (`extent`: a fiftieth more each way, a thousandth past a joint), the face's own ends among them; past that the balls are followed only while their feet stay on the faces they roll along (`on_face`: within a thousandth of a freeform face's parameters past its border; further a fitted offset extrapolated is no face, and past a smooth edge the fillet rolls along the next face), and further where the closing of the body asks the face to grow. Then refined as every rolling-ball blend is (`refine`: a quarter of the tolerance from the exact sections between samples, tangent to `bend`, the borders proven on their faces). Where the rebuilt fillets make no body (a fillet that cannot be rebuilt, or one rebuilt whose body the closing refuses), the operation is tried once more with every fillet offset as any freeform face, which holds each to its exact offset within its share of the tolerance or refuses it; both tries within one work bound together, and the refusal said is the first one.

**Refused before anything is built.** A fillet that moves while a face it rolls along does not move with it by the same distance (its offset would not meet that face tangentially; a fillet of another radius between the faces as they are is not its offset): "a face this fillet rolls along does not move with it by the same distance (it stays, moves by another distance, or is left open) …", with both faces. A shell open at a face a fillet rolls along is one (the hollow would need the fillet cut off by the opening: a change of topology). A distance at which the radius reaches zero is too far, the largest that fits being the radius (less the tolerance) in closed form: no body is built in the search.

**Closing the body round rebuilt fillets** (`tweak`, for the faces it is told are rebuilt; every other operation as before, bit for bit): a vertex of a rebuilt fillet is looked for from where its old point is on the new fillet (the same parameters), not from where it was (as far away as the distance, its nearest points on curved new faces can lie past their ends where faces meet at a shallow angle); and next to a rebuilt fillet `meet` draws a point toward the border foot of a freeform surface whose nearest point is on its border (the unsigned distance's gradient: two fillets tangent to one face and crossing each other at 0.28° meet it off one fillet's border sideways by the fits' errors over the angle), and lets a surface that meets those before it at less than `SHALLOW` (a sine of 0.05) wait until the point lies on them. An edge's points still start between its ends moved: a point carried along the fillet's normal is off a face it crosses by the distance times what the angle between them leaves (the saddle's ring meeting itself: 1.5 mm).

**Constants, and why.** `HELD_AT` (17 rows held against the ball), `MOST_START` (512), `END_TURN` (a twentieth of the range over which the planes turn into a joint's), `JOINT_MARGIN` (a thousandth past a joint) and the fiftieth past the face's edges are budgets and margins; `PAST_FACE` (a thousandth of a face's parameters past its border) is how far a fitted offset's end spans are taken as the face; `settle` (a hundredth of the tolerance) is when a ball on fitted offsets of high degree has settled: its feet are found only to about 1e-12 of the part (the saddle's rear rim: the centre jittered by 1e-8 mm per step and never settled to rounding), a twenty-fifth of the share its surface is held to. `SHALLOW` (0.05) orders the iteration only; the answer is held to the tolerance as before.

**Measured.** Closed forms (`crates/kernel/tests/offset_blend.rs`, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6): the rim of a quarter rod (a rational cubic twin of a cylinder) filleted 0.25 and moved out 0.3 and in 0.2 with its top and wall, against Pappus's rule with the tube radius `0.25 ± d`, off by 4e-9 relative, the fillet face on the torus to a quarter of the tolerance; the concave foot of a quarter pocket the same; two balls joined or as their lens, their circle filleted 0.5 (rolling-ball rings between spheres), every face moved ±0.2, made hollow 0.2 (the hollow's ring `0.5 ∓ 0.2`) and thickened, against `π ∫ ρ²` in closed form piece by piece (off 1e-8 relative); a block whose top is a bilinear spline with its front edge filleted 0.4 (a cylinder), its top, front and fillet moved ±, the block shelled open at its bottom and the three faces thickened, against the box less the corners, the fillet on its cylinder of radius `0.4 + d` to a quarter of the tolerance; a fillet rolled along a face an offset made, moved again; the radius told and not told the same body; the same twice bit for bit; too far by the radius at once; refused at once without its faces. Through the commands (`crates/engine/tests/offset_blend.rs`): the balls offset, shelled and thickened, checked deep, the fillet named `F5:copy(0)/F4:fillet(0)`, an edit of the fillet's radius followed. **The bike's saddle** (fitted fillets of 4 and 3 mm tangent to their walls to 6.6e-5) shelled 2 mm: built and checked deep in 1 min 18 s (release; refused as too far after 5 min before), 125 135.2206 mm³, against the other order, the designer's (shelled first, the four rims filleted after: 4, 3, 2 and 1 mm), 125 135.2200 mm³: the same solid to 4.6e-9 relative (5.8e-4 mm³, a hundredth of half the tolerance times the area) (`crates/engine/tests/offset_freeform.rs`, `#[ignore]`d).

**The fuzzer's offsets and shells of blend faces** (`crates/engine/tests/fuzz_findings.rs`, release, three at a time, both at a load of 30 to 40): the fillets' end at once now, every pin as it was: 9601 offset of a fillet on a revolved ring 204 s → 0.5 s, 14064 and 7802 offsets of a fillet on a pattern of balls 174 s → 0.4 s and 92 s → 0.5 s, 12333 shell of a filleted revolved ring 286 s → 0.4 s, all refused because a face the fillet rolls along does not move with it (a single fillet moved, or a shell open at its face); 9534 shell of a filleted cylinder and ball 84 s → 31 s, after work's merge refused by the cylinder's radius reaching zero (thickness 10 on a radius 2.5), the search tried with the rebuilt fillet and again without; 267 (a shell whose limit lies elsewhere, its fillets rebuilt in each try) 169 s → 50 s; 5457 34 s → 9 s. The chamfers' are unchanged in kind (1279 168 → 112 s, 5599 64 → 61 s, 6195 44 → 33 s, 6578 70 → 42 s): a freeform chamfer is not rebuilt (below).

**Not rebuilt: a chamfer.** The offset of a chamfer's ruled face is no chamfer: its normal turns along each ruling unless the face is developable, so the rulings of the exact offset are curves, and a chamfer "with its distances changed accordingly" between the offset faces would lie off the exact offset by the distance times that turn. It meets its faces at an angle, so nothing is tangent and the fitted offset is exact to its share; it stays as dear as it was (its regularity proof and fit per try).

**Open.** A fillet whose border crosses a smooth edge of the face it rolls along within the face (the rolling ball's `cross` switches): refused by the shape test (two faces across a border), offset as a freeform face; a fillet next to an open face of a shell; a variable-radius blend (none is built).

### Thicken of the Garabit terrain (2026-10-21, the Garabit valley's finding 6)

### Hollow freeform bodies (2026-11-01)

The class-A fuel tank (`docs/examples/tank.json`) and the modern mouse were to be hollow. Two causes, both general:

- **The tank's sides were creased inside.** `feature.shell` at 1.2 mm was refused as "the offset of the freeform face could not be approximated" on a side. The side's knots along the crown (multiplicity equal to its degree, so they looked like breaks of the representation only) were creased in fact: the normal jumped by 2.6e-3 at u = 0.8 across the middle of the side, by 2.3e-4 at u = 0.2, where the side is held G2 to the cubic crown. The exact offset tears there by the angle times the distance (3 µm at 1.2 mm), so no fit within a quarter of the tolerance exists, and the fit's error stayed at three times its limit however finely it was refined. The cause is in the network, not the offset: the curvature hold spread the kink of the crown's second order over the whole sheet (`surfacing.md` › *Kinks of a curvature hold*). With the kinks on a band of their own the side is smooth inside to rounding, and its offset is fitted in 2 s (98 × 81 control points).
