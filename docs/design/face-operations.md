# 3DCraft design: Face operations

Offset face, shell, move face, draft, thicken and delete face (`kernel::tweak` and its users; `regen::face_ops` and the module per operation; `engine::face_cmds`). Method 5 of the map ([`../architecture.md`](../architecture.md) §5).

## Method

**Give faces new surfaces and close the body again, keeping every entity.** `kernel::tweak::resurface` takes a body and new surfaces for some faces. Every vertex on such a face is found again where the surfaces of its faces meet, nearest where it was (Newton on the signed distances); every edge that ends in such a vertex is made again on its two faces' surfaces (a line, or a circle about the axis of its round face; an elliptic or spline edge only when neither face moved). Nothing about how the body hangs together changes: every face, edge and vertex of the result has the number it had. The result is then held against what a body is (edges that shrink to nothing or turn round, flat faces whose loops change their turning or cross, round faces whose rims change places, points just inside and outside each moved edge classified by ray casting, `topo::check`).

- **Offset face**: a plane moves to the parallel plane, a cylinder, sphere or torus changes its radius, a cone its radius at the frame; positive is away from the material.
- **Move face**: one rigid motion of several faces (`resurface_rigid`): what lies wholly on moved faces is moved exactly, what lies between moved and fixed faces is found again as for an offset.
- **Draft**: flat faces square to the pull turn about their line on the neutral plane, cylinders along the pull become cones; closed forms only.
- **Shell**: every face offset inward by the thickness, open faces outward, and the hollow taken from the solid by the boolean.
- **Thicken**: the layer between the solid with the faces moved outward and with them moved inward (two offsets and a boolean), as a body of its own.
- **Delete face**: a group of faces that fills holes of the faces around it goes, and those faces go on across (topological only); a strip (a chamfer or fillet along an edge, a round of a rim) collapses into the edge where its neighbours meet when continued (`resurface_at`).

**Too far** is found, not guessed: a check that fails at the given size but passes at a smaller one gives the largest that fits (halving, 48 times at most) and the edge or face that stops it. **Refused, with the faces**: a neighbour tangent to a moved face, a vertex of four or more faces that would have to become an edge, a freeform neighbour of a face moved outward, an edge between moved and fixed faces that would no longer be a line or a circle, the offset of a freeform face.

**Names**: every entity of an offset, move or draft is the copy of what it was; a shell or thicken names its skin and the faces of the hollow or layer as copies in instance 0 and 1; a deletion names edges and vertices by their faces. A sketch on a moved face moves and turns with it. What local checks cannot see, two faces far apart that ran into each other, regeneration finds on a mesh: near the change for move face and draft, over the whole body for offset face, shell and thicken (structural debt, the map's §9).

### Face operations as built (M9, 2026-10-14)

`feature.offsetFace`, `feature.moveFace`, `feature.draft`, `feature.shell` and `feature.thicken`; `kernel::offset_face`, `kernel::move_face`, `kernel::draft`, `kernel::shell`, `kernel::thicken`, and what they share in `kernel::tweak`; `regen::face_ops`, `regen::offset_face`, `regen::move_face`, `regen::draft`, `regen::shell`, `regen::thicken`; `engine::face_cmds`.

**Route: a local operation, and the boolean only to take the hollow out.** The roadmap's recipe for the face operations is "give a face a new surface, extend the neighbours, intersect again, sew". For the blends a tool solid and the boolean was the better route (above); for moving a face it is not. Inward, the body cut by the half-space behind a moved plane would be exact; outward, the new material is bounded by the neighbours' surfaces continued past their edges, so a tool would have to be built from exactly the computation that closes the body directly, and the boolean would then add a face (a block whose top moves up would have seven faces, the old top flush with a new one, until merged). So `kernel::tweak::resurface` does it directly, and nothing about how the body hangs together changes: every face, edge and vertex of the result is the one with the same number in the body given. (1) Every vertex on a face with a new surface is found again where the surfaces of its faces meet, nearest where it was: Newton's iteration on the signed distances to the surfaces, with the smallest step that puts the point on every surface whose normal adds a direction (one step for planes; a face tangent to another says nothing the other does not, and is held to its distance at the end). (2) Every edge that ends in such a vertex is made again on its two faces' surfaces: a straight edge through its two vertices; a circular edge about the axis of the round one of its faces (a cylinder, a cone, a torus, or a sphere about the line along the old circle's axis), through its first vertex, with its frame's axes kept so its parameters keep their meaning; an elliptic or spline edge only when neither face moved and its ends still lie on it. Each is held at five points against both surfaces. (3) The result is held against what a body is: an edge that shrinks to nothing or turns round (which is how a vanishing neighbour shows), a flat face whose loops change their turning or whose outline crosses itself or loses a hole (`profile::detect_profiles` on the face's loops), a round face whose rims change places along its axis, `topo::check`, and next to every moved edge a point just outside and just inside each face, which the booleans' ray classification must find outside and inside (a face that ran into another part of the body, or into the plane of another face, fails this). What such local checks cannot see, two faces far apart that ran into each other, regeneration finds on the mesh (`tess::body_self_intersections`, as for lofts) before the body is handed on.

**What moves.** A flat face goes to the parallel plane at the distance; a cylinder and a sphere change their radius, a torus its tube's; a cone keeps its axis and angle and its radius changes by the distance over the cosine of the half-angle (its frame moves along the axis when the apex passes it). Positive is outward, away from the material: a hole narrows, a boss thickens. So the neighbours that follow are flat faces at any angle (the slanted sides of a frustum are continued until they meet the moved top: closed form at three scales), round faces about an axis the moved face is square to (a hole through a face that moves gets longer) and the flat faces across an axis when the round face moves (the mouth and the floor of a hole, the foot and the crown of a boss, a ball half sunk in a block).

**Too far.** A failure of the checks in (3), or of a vertex to be found, is a matter of how far when a smaller distance works: the distance is halved (48 times at most, each a whole resurface) for the largest that fits, and the error names the edge (by the two faces it lies between) or the face, the reason and that bound ("what fits is below 3.99999 mm"). When no distance at all works, the geometry is one that is not built and that is said instead, with the faces.

**Not built, and refused with the faces:** a neighbour tangent to the moved face (the top next to a fillet: its continuation does not meet the moved plane where the edge was); a vertex where four or more faces meet that would have to become an edge (a slanted side of a frustum on a plinth); a freeform face next to a moved one outward (it cannot be continued past its edge) and an elliptic or spline edge on a moved face; the offset of a freeform face; a cone whose radius at its frame would turn negative in a way the frame cannot follow. Replace face is not built; see the M9 row of the roadmap. Move face, thicken, draft and delete face are built (below).

**Shell.** `kernel::shell` offsets every face of the solid at once by the thickness, inward, except the open faces, which move outward by the same amount, so that the hollow reaches through them and the opening is as wide as the hollow is there. Since the offset keeps every entity, the hollow has the solid's faces, face for face. The shell is the solid less the hollow (`boolean_with`, the operands trusted since both were just checked); without open faces the hollow lies inside, the boolean leaves it as a cavity, and the result has two shells. After the boolean: every face that keeps a wall must be there as skin and as a face of the hollow, and the hollow must come out through every open face (it is gone, or an edge of the boolean crosses it), else an error with the face. A rounded box gets inner rounds of the radius less the thickness; at a thickness of the radius or more they would vanish into a sharp edge, which needs the hollow's topology to change, and is refused with the round and the largest thickness that fits. Behind an open face between walls that stand round it (the floor of a pocket) the hollow is wider than the opening, and a piece of the open face moved out is the hollow's ceiling there; the result is a valid solid, measured in the tests. A boss that stands on an open face would be cut loose by the hollow coming out round it, leaving a body in two pieces; that is not tried in the tests and not refused yet (open). FreeCAD's `makeThickness` gives the same volume, area and counts as ours on a box, a cup, a tube, a rounded tray (three thicknesses) and an L (with its join set to intersection; its default puts an arc in the hollow's outside corner), and `makeOffsetShape` the same as an offset of every face of a block and of a cylinder (`target/scratch-face-ops/thickness.py`; only the cylinders' seam edges differ in the counts).

**Move face** (`feature.moveFace`, `kernel::move_face`, `regen::move_face`; 2026-10-15). Faces of a solid move together by one rigid motion, given as `feature.transform` gives one (a turn by `angle` about the line through `pivot` along `axis`, then a shift by `translate`). Every moved face gets the image of its surface (`Surface::transformed`, which keeps the parametrisation), and `tweak::resurface_rigid` closes the body: a vertex all of whose faces move and an edge both of whose faces move are the images of what they were, exactly and of any kind (so a boss moved with its top keeps its top rim as it was, and two moved faces that meet in an ellipse still do); everything between a moved face and one that stays is found again as for an offset, with the same checks. So the top of a block shifted up is an offset (a slanted shift counts by its part along the normal), tilted about a line across it is a wedge, the wall of a hole or the wall and top of a boss moved across their face put the hole or the boss elsewhere. **Too far** is the offset's halving on the part of the motion (the same part of the shift and of the turn): the kernel's `value` and `largest` are how far the farthest vertex of a moved face goes and may go, and regeneration says the part and the shift and angle it is (`translate, angle: the move is too far: the edge shrinks to nothing …; what fits is below 0.833333 of it, translate (0, 0, -4.99…) mm`). **Refused, with the faces,** where the topology round a moved face would have to change: an edge between a moved face and one that stays that would no longer be a line or a circle (a cylinder tilted against a flat face meets it in an ellipse: `OFF_SURFACE` at every part of the motion, so not "too far"), and everything the offset refuses (a tangent neighbour that stays, a vertex of four faces, a freeform neighbour). **Names** are an offset's: every face, edge and vertex is the copy of what it was, so a fillet on an edge of a moved face survives edits of the shift and the turn (`crates/engine/tests/move_face.rs`, with the fillet's closed form along an edge that runs across a tilted top). A sketch on a moved face lies on it and is turned with it the shortest way onto its new normal (`sketch::resolve_face`, `turned_onto`): for a turn that tilts the face, which is every turn that changes it, that is the face's own turn. *Tests:* `crates/kernel/tests/move_face.rs` (shifts, a wedge about the middle and about an edge, a hole and a boss moved, a tilted frame, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6; the part that fits; the tilted hole refused; bad input) and `crates/engine/tests/move_face.rs` (the same through the command, checked deep, the fillet that survives, the sketch that turns, too far and refused in words, bad parameters, `feature.edit`, save and reopen, undo and redo).

**Draft** (`feature.draft`, `kernel::draft`, `regen::draft`; 2026-10-15). Faces of a solid tilted by an angle about a neutral plane so that the part comes out of a mould along the pull direction, the plane's normal. The neutral plane is a world plane, a datum plane or a flat face (`PlaneRef`, as datum planes take it); for a face the pull goes *into* the body the face bounds, so a block drafted with its bottom face as neutral narrows toward its top and with its top face toward its bottom, and `reversed` turns the pull round. A drafted face keeps the line or circle in which it meets the neutral plane and leans in by the angle as it goes along the pull; a negative angle leans out. **Closed forms only:** a flat face whose normal is square to the pull turns about the line it shares with the neutral plane (the rotation about that line, applied to its frame: still a plane); a cylinder whose axis runs along the pull becomes the cone about that axis whose radius at the neutral plane is the cylinder's and whose half-angle is the draft angle, narrowing along the pull for a boss and widening for a hole. Then `tweak::resurface` closes the body as for an offset: the top of a drafted block shrinks, edges between drafted faces are found again where the new surfaces meet, and a flat face tangent to a drafted cylinder stays tangent to its cone, since both are drafted from the line or circle they share at the neutral plane: the vertex where they and the top meet is found where the plane and the top meet, nearest where it was, which is the point of tangency (the "one direction" rule of the iteration), so a rounded box drafted is a rounded frustum, to its closed form. **Too far** is the offset's halving on the angle: an angle at which a face is drawn to nothing (the top of a frustum to a point) fails with the edge and the largest angle that fits. **Refused, with the face and the reason, before anything is built:** a flat face whose normal is not square to the pull (the top of the block, a face drafted already), a cylinder whose axis is not along it, a cone (it has an angle of its own; changing it is not built), a sphere, a torus and a freeform face (no closed form of the kind we build), and round them what the offset refuses (a neighbour tangent to a drafted face that is not drafted itself, a vertex of four faces). **Names** are an offset's: every face, edge and vertex the copy of what it was; a sketch on a drafted face turns with it as on a moved one. *Tests:* `crates/kernel/tests/draft.rs` (a block from its bottom, its top and its middle, one side, a rod and a hole into frusta of cones, a rounded box into a rounded frustum, a tilted frame, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6; the angle that fits; what is refused) and `crates/engine/tests/draft.rs` (the same through the command with the neutral plane as a face, `xy` and reversed, a datum plane whose distance is a variable with a fillet on the drafted top and a peg on a drafted side through edits of the level and the angle, too steep and refused in words, bad parameters, `feature.edit` of the neutral plane, the angle, the faces and the pull, save and reopen, undo and redo).

**Thicken** (`feature.thicken`, `kernel::thicken`, `regen::thicken`; 2026-10-15). Nothing in the history makes a sheet body yet (`BodyKind::Sheet` exists for imports and tests only), so what is thickened is a set of faces of a solid, into a solid of its own: the layer between those faces and the same faces moved along their normals by the thickness, `outside` (away from the material, the default), `inside` or `both` (half each way). The source keeps its body (`FeatureKind::source` says so, as `keep` does for a transform); the layer is joined to it, or to anything, with a boolean. **Method:** two offsets and the boolean, as the shell: the solid with the faces moved outward by the part outside (or the solid itself) less the solid with them moved inward by the part inside (or the solid itself). So at its rim the layer is bounded by the faces round the set, *continued as an offset continues them*, not by walls along the faces' normals: the top of a block thickened is a plate as wide as the block, the wall of a rod a tube as long as the rod, the wall of a hole a sleeve in it or round it, the top and a side of a block an angle, the slanted top of a frustum thickened inward a cap whose rim lies in the slanted sides. That is the layer a solid's faces have, and it is the one route that needs no new kind of face; a thickness along the normals at a rim that is not square to its neighbours (a frustum's top thickened outward with upright walls) is not built. Every face of the set must come out on both sides of the layer, else it is refused with the face; a thickness at which a face of either offset vanishes is the offset's "too far", in the whole thickness (also for `both`). **Names** are a shell's: a face lying on or along a face of the source is its copy in instance 0 (`F9:copy(0)/F1:face(1)` is the far side of a plate on a block's top, `F9:copy(0)/F1:face(2)` its rim along the front), one lying under a face of the set its copy in instance 1 (the near side); edges and vertices are named by their faces with the copies of their old names as aliases. A sketch on the near side looks into the source, as on the faces of a hollow (`sketch::resolve_face`). *Tests:* `crates/kernel/tests/thicken.rs` (a plate on, under and through a block's top, an angle on two faces, a tube on a rod, sleeves in and round a hole, a tilted frame, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6; what fits; bad input) and `crates/engine/tests/thicken.rs` (the same through the command, checked deep, the plate joined back into a taller block, the source kept, a fillet on the layer and a pin from its near side through edits of the thickness and the block, `feature.edit` to another face and side, save and reopen, undo and redo, bad parameters).

*Read back by FreeCAD:* `step_freecad/face_ops.rs` has a second set for these three (a wedge, a boss moved with its top, an angle thickened on two faces of a block, a sleeve on both sides of a hole's wall, a drafted frustum and a drafted rounded box, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6): FreeCAD finds valid solids with our volume and area within 1.2e-15 and our counts (bar the seams it adds to closed faces), 18 files.

**Delete face** (`feature.deleteFace`, `kernel::delete_face`, `regen::delete_face`; 2026-10-14). The faces given fall into groups that hang together by edges, and each group is closed on its own, one after the other; a group that neither way below fits is refused with the faces (and the edge) and the reason, and nothing is built.
- *(a) Holes filled.* A group whose every edge to the rest of the body lies in a hole of a face round it, each such hole bounded by the group alone: the wall of a hole through a plate, the wall and floor of a blind hole, the wall and top of a boss, the walls and floor of a pocket, the faces of a closed cavity (a whole shell with nothing round it). The group goes, those loops go out of their faces, and the faces round it go on across where the holes were on the surfaces they had: purely topological. What is checked: every such loop is a hole of its face (sampled and taken into the face's own parameters, where the normal is ∂u × ∂v, an outer loop turns counter-clockwise seen from outside and a hole clockwise; a loop that goes round a periodic direction, the rim of a cylinder, is no hole; one round a pole of a sphere cannot be told and is refused), a neighbour that shares only part of a loop with the group refuses it, the result passes `topo::check` and encloses a positive volume (deleting the outside of a hollow block leaves the cavity inside out: refused).
- *(b) A strip collapsed.* One face with one loop of four edges (two sides along the faces beside it, an end against a face at each end: a chamfer or a fillet along one edge) or with two loops of one closed edge each (the round of a rod's rim, of a hole's mouth, of a boss's foot). The face goes, its two sides become one edge between the faces beside it, the two vertices of each end edge become one, and `tweak::resurface_at` finds those vertices where the surfaces round them meet and makes every edge ending in them again on its faces' surfaces, with all of `resurface`'s checks: the faces beside the strip are continued until they meet, and the faces at its ends lose the end edge and gain the corner. Which edges are the sides: the two along which the face is tangent to its neighbours when there are such (a fillet), else both pairings are tried, the longer sides first, and the first that closes the body is taken (a chamfer on a block: its ends lie in parallel faces that never meet). Refused, with the faces: an end face tangent to the strip or to a face beside it (the next blend of a chain, the ball in a corner of three fillets), an end vertex where more than three faces meet (two blends in a mitre, a corner of the part), faces beside it that do not meet in a line or a circle when continued (the top of a block or of a thin wall, the wall of a boss alone), the edges they would meet in being elliptic or freeform.
- *Regeneration* refuses the corner where blends meet by its name (`chamferCorner`, `filletCorner`) before the kernel would refuse it in numbers, and meshes the result to find two faces far apart that the continuation ran into (`tess::body_self_intersections`, as for offsets). Faces are copies (`F9:copy(0)/…`), edges and vertices are named by their faces with the copy of their old name as an alias; the edge a strip collapsed into and the vertex its ends became have only the name by their faces. After a block, a fillet on an edge and the fillet face deleted, the block's own names (`F1:face(1)`) do not answer again: the faces answer to `F3:copy(0)/F2:copy(0)/F1:face(1)`, as after every feature made from another, and the restored sharp edge is the edge between two such names (a chamfer on it is built in the tests).
- *Tests:* `crates/kernel/tests/delete_face.rs` (a block filleted or chamfered on an edge, upright or in a tilted frame, inside fillets and chamfers of a step, the round and the bevel of a rod's rim, of a hole's mouth and of a boss's foot, two fillets at once, a hole, a blind hole, a boss, a pocket, a cross hole in a rod and a cavity deleted, each against the body before the blend or the cut: volume, area and counts to 1e-9 at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6; what is refused), `crates/engine/tests/face_ops.rs` (the same through the command with `document.check {"deep": true}`, upstream edits that move the hole, a hole that misses so the face is gone, `causedBy`, `feature.edit`, inserting at the rollback marker, save and reopen, undo and redo, bad parameters), a row of `failed_features.rs`, and `step_freecad/face_ops.rs` (five cases at three scales: FreeCAD finds valid solids of our volume within 1.2e-15).

**References and names** (§4.1): faces by name, all of one body (offset) or of `source` (shell's `open`), resolved at every regeneration as the blends resolve theirs; a cap's name survives a change of its outline as everywhere. The result's faces are copies: `F9:copy(0)/…` for every face of an offset (moved or not; a face that moved is still that face) and for the shell's skin; a face of the hollow is the copy in instance 1 of the face it lies behind, `F9:copy(1)/F2:side(e7)`. After an offset every edge and vertex is the copy of what it was (as after a move); in a shell they are named by their faces with the copy of their old name as an alias. A sketch on a face that an offset moved lies where the face lies now (`sketch::resolve_face` drops the frame's origin onto the face's plane and keeps its axes); a sketch on a face of the hollow looks into the hollow (its normal is the face's own, its x axis that of the face it lies behind, so its y is mirrored, as a wall seen from behind). The same holds for a face a subtraction keeps of its tool, turned inside out (the floor of a pocket, the wall of a hole), since 2026-10-14: a sketch or a datum plane on it looks out of the material, its x axis that of the tool's face and its y mirrored (`sketch::resolve_face`; before, it looked into the material as the tool's face did, and a pin extruded from a pocket's floor went down).

- *Tests:* `crates/kernel/tests/offset_face.rs` and `shell.rs` (closed forms at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6; the largest distance that fits; what is refused), `crates/engine/tests/face_ops.rs` (the same through the commands with `document.check {"deep": true}`, upstream edits, a lost face, `feature.edit`, sketches on a moved face and on a face of the hollow, a chamfer on an edge of a shell, save and reopen, undo and redo, bad parameters), two rows of `failed_features.rs`, and `step_freecad/face_ops.rs` (33 files, FreeCAD finds valid solids of our volume within 3e-15).
