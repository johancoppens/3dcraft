# Selection and selecting by description

The selection the developer and an agent share, and the query that selects by describing (`select.query`). Decided by the developer on 2026-10-21 ([`../decisions.md`](../decisions.md), B1 to B6); the protocol is in [`../control-protocol.md`](../control-protocol.md) › *Selection*. Code: `crates/engine/src/selection.rs` (the list and what names mean), `crates/engine/src/select_cmds.rs` (`select.get`, `select.set`, `select.query`, the context), `crates/engine/src/set_cmds.rs` and `crates/doc/src/sets.rs` (named sets), `crates/ui-egui/src/shell/selection.rs` (the window), `crates/automation/src/mcp.rs` (the resource and the tool).

## Method

### The selection

- **One ordered list per open document**, held by the engine's `OpenDocument` beside the history, not in it: not saved, not undone (B1). Every frontend sees the same list: the window draws it, the commands read and set it, MCP serves it as the resource `craft3d://selection` and the tool `selection_get`. Holding it in the engine rather than in the window's `UiState` is what lets a headless agent (`craft3d-cli`, MCP without a window) use it too, and keeps one list when the window and an agent both change it; the window keeps a copy as of its last sync for drawing.
- **An item is `{kind, name, feature}`.** A body, feature, sketch or datum is its feature, by id (`F3`), with the feature's name for reading; a face, edge or vertex is its persistent name ([`names.md`](names.md)) within the body of `feature`. Two items are the same when kind, feature and (for an entity) name agree, so a renamed feature stays selected.
- **What a name means** (`Resolver`): a feature by its name or id; a face by its persistent name or an alias (stored as its canonical name); an edge by its own name or an alias; a vertex by its own name. Without a kind it is read from the name. Lookups go through a per-body table built once per question, so selecting thousands of faces costs one pass over each body's names.
- **Through regeneration**: before it is read (and before it is changed) the list is checked against the document's current regeneration, once per revision. Each entry keeps its **seed**, the item as it was selected, and is found again from the seed every time (never from what it was followed to last), so following is the same function of the document at every revision: undo, suppress and edit in any order give the same answer. A stored item is found again by its feature's **id only** (`F3`), never by a name, and a face, edge or vertex by its name within that feature's body: a selection never moves to another feature that took a name (rule 4). New feature names may not be written like an id (`F` and digits; refused by `feature.*` and `feature.rename` since 2026-10-21), and text written as an id means that id wherever a body is looked up (`Scene::faces_named`, `edges_named`, `vertices_named`), so a feature an older file named `F1` cannot stand in for F1. What resolves stays, in its order; what does not drops out and is listed under `dropped` ("face F7:copy(0)/F2:face(1): …"): what dropped out at the last change of the document, so an undo that follows clears it. A face edited upstream keeps its name and stays selected (the hole of 6 mm made 6.5 mm is still selected); a deleted feature's faces drop out; undo does not bring them back, since the selection is not history.
- **Following into later features** (slice 2, 2026-10-22): a face whose body a later feature took (a boolean, a fillet, a pattern, a split) is followed to its images in the newest bodies by the rule features follow names by (`craft3d_regen::follow_face_pieces`, names.md: the copy names), through any number of takers: into **every** piece of a body a split cut in pieces; where a taker made several copies (the instances of a pattern) it goes on with the first, the lowest instance, which is the face where it was (a feature refuses then, since it must not choose for its author; a pointer may). Nothing is narrowed silently: `select.get` › `followed` lists each item that is not what was selected, with `from` (what was), `copies` (how many copies the takers made; the item is the first) and `pieces` (how many items it became). An edge is followed by the two faces it lies between: every edge between their images in each newest body (both halves of an edge a cut parted). A body is followed to the body it went into, or to every piece of a split. A vertex is not followed yet: it drops, saying so. What the taker left no image of (the bottom of a drill below the plate it cut) drops out and says why ("the face F2:face(1) is gone: the body of `Drill` went into `Cut`, a boolean, which left no face of it"). Suppress the taker and the seed is its feature's own again: the item is the plate's face again; undo that and it is followed again.
- **Hidden, not dropped** (decided here, 2026-10-22): an item whose feature has no body for now because it is suppressed, rolled back or failed stays in the list, is not drawn and not counted, and is listed under `hidden` with why ("`B` is suppressed"); when the feature has a body again it is back in its place. The alternative, falling back to the feature item while hidden, was not taken: it would change the item's kind under the person's hands and an action on "the selection" would then act on the whole feature. `clear` removes hidden items too; a deleted feature's items drop.
- **Modes**: replace, add (in order, no duplicates), remove, toggle (Shift+click), clear. At most 10000 items. An item given means an entry when it is what the entry is now or what it was selected as: a click on the followed face toggles the entry, and a replace that keeps an entry keeps its seed.

### In the window

- A click selects what *Pointing* says it points at (vertex, else edge, else face, each when it has a persistent name), replacing the selection; a click on nothing clears it; Shift toggles; a double click selects the body. The highlight of the last click (`state.highlight*`) is kept as before, and what is both highlighted and selected is drawn once (`Marks::with_selection`).
- **Picking through**: `craft3d_render::pick_all` is the renderer's own test of one sample, `pick_cut`'s, keeping every triangle under the position instead of the nearest, sorted by the depth key and one per face. A click within 3 pixels of the last one, with the camera and the model unchanged, or an Alt+click, selects the next face behind the last, round to the front. A right click lists the same faces (at most 24); hovering lights an entry's face.
- **The box** (Ctrl or Cmd and a drag; with Shift added): the scene's mesh points are projected with the renderer's `Viewpoint`; a body is selected when every point of it lands inside, otherwise its faces each of whose points does. Hidden faces count: a box selects what lies inside it, not what is seen (as a window selection in other modellers). Chosen over "touching the box" because a box around a hole should not take the plate.
- **Drawing**: the faces of selected bodies and faces join the highlight's face ids (`craft3d_render::Highlight`, the copper `face_highlight` of the Mission theme, eight runs of ids: a body's faces are one run, so whole bodies always show); the outlines of selected bodies and faces (their edges), selected edges and selected vertices are overlay marks, the highlight's line and dot, drawn dimmed where something is in front, so what is selected behind something else still shows. Outlines are capped at 40000 segments. The status bar says "3 bodies, 2 faces selected" and how many dropped out. `selectedFeature` (the feature list's row, the sketch drawn as selected) follows the first body, feature, sketch or datum of the selection; `ui.set {selectedFeature}` and a click on a row set the selection.

### `select.query`

A face that a boolean cut into pieces that keep its name is **one item**: the name means the pieces together (the window lights them all, its area is theirs together, its box holds them all), so a query's `count` is what its `then` selects. JSON filters (B4), parsed and checked before anything is looked at: an unknown key, a value of the wrong dimension ("3 deg" for a radius, "3 mm" for an area), an axis without direction, more than 1000 filters or nesting deeper than 32 is an error that names it. Candidates are enumerated in document order (bodies, features, sketches, datums in history order; faces, edges, vertices body by body in the order of `document.inspect` and `document.edges`), at most 4 million, so the answer is deterministic. The result is cut at `limit` (default 1000, at most 10000) with `count` and `truncated` saying so. A filter that does not apply to an item (a volume of a face) does not hold for it.

| Filter | Holds for |
|---|---|
| `all`, `any`, `not`; an object with several keys, or an array | the combinations |
| `set` | what the named set holds now: its kinds and its filter, read from the document (a set that uses itself, through others too, is an error) |
| `kind` | the kinds given |
| `name` | a glob (`*`, `?`) on the name; a feature-like item also on its id |
| `type` | a glob on the feature's type (`extrude`, `boolean`, …), of the body's feature for an entity |
| `in` | an entity of that body, or that body |
| `madeBy` | an entity whose persistent name has that feature anywhere along its way (`F6:copy(0)/F5:copy(1)/F3:copy(0)/F2:face(0)` is made by F6, F5, F3 and F2), and an edge or a vertex named by its faces is made by what made any of them (the edges of a fillet's face; since 2026-10-22, from the second field test); a body or feature made from it, all the way through the features' sources |
| `copyOf` (`instance`) | an entity that is a copy made by that pattern or mirror (of that instance): its name's way is walked feature by feature through the instances (names.md, the role copy), as the appearance of a face is; that feature's body |
| `color` (`within`) | the colour of the face (as the viewport shades it, `face_appearances`) or of the body, within that distance in RGB; `null`: no colour of its own |
| `material` | a glob on the body's material; `null`: none |
| `volume`, `area`, `radius`, `length` | the exact measure within the range: a body's volume and area and a face's area by the integrals `document.measure` sums (`craft3d_topo::face_areas`), a radius of a cylindrical or spherical face or a circular edge, an edge's length (exact for lines and circular arcs). A value is a number in the document's unit or an expression with units (`"6.5 mm / 2"`); `{eq, tol}` (tol defaults to a millionth of eq plus the document's tolerance) or `{min, max}` |
| `surface` | the kind of a face's surface; `freeform` is a Bézier or NURBS surface |
| `hole` | the wall of a hole: a cylindrical face whose outward normal points at its axis (its material is outside it) and whose pieces together go all the way round the axis (its boundary has points in every direction round the axis, no gap wider than 30°). A concave fillet (a reversed cylinder a quarter turn round) and the end of a slot (half a turn) are not holes. A countersink (a cone) and a hole whose wall is a NURBS surface are not holes either, for now |
| `normal` | `along`: a flat face whose outward normal is within `tol` (0.5 degrees) of the axis; `perpendicular`: a flat face whose normal is square to it, or a cylinder or cone whose axis lies along it |
| `axis` | a cylinder, cone or torus face, or a circular edge, whose axis lies along it, either way round |
| `inside`, `above`, `below` | the item's box wholly within the box, at or above, at or below a level of x, y or z, with the document's tolerance as slack. A vertex is its point; an edge exact for a line, a circle and an ellipse, and for a freeform curve the hull of the control points of the part of the curve the edge is, cut into its Bézier pieces, each halved three times (positive weights: the curve lies inside); a flat face the box of its edges (a flat face lies within the hull of its boundary); a curved face and a body the B-rep's held box, which certainly contains them |

Each face, edge and vertex in the answer also has `body` and `origin` (the feature at the end of its name's way, the one that first made it: the cylinder that drilled a hole), which is what an agent edits to change it: "select all 6 mm holes and make them 6.5 mm" is a query for `{hole: true, radius: "3 mm"}` and a `feature.edit` of each origin's radius (`crates/engine/tests/selection.rs`).

### Context (slice 2)

So an agent can act on an item without asking again, `select.get` (and `select.set`, and `select.query` with `context: true`) gives per item, besides `body` and `origin {feature, name, type}`: for a face its `surface` (`type`; a plane's outward `normal` and a `point`; a cylinder's `radius`, `axis`, `center` and `hole`; a cone's `radius`, `halfAngle` in degrees, `axis`, `center`; a sphere's `radius`, `center`; a torus's `major`, `minor`, `axis`, `center`), for an edge its `curve` (`line` with `direction` and `length`, `circle` with `radius`, `axis`, `center`, `length`, or `ellipse`/`freeform` with `length`), for a vertex its `point`; `drivers`, the parameters that set its measure, each with the `edit` (`{command, params}`) that changes it; and `copyOf`, the patterns and mirrors it is a copy made by, outermost first, with the `instance`. A feature-like item gets its `type`. Numbers are rounded to 12 significant digits for reading. Drivers come from the feature at the end of the name's way and the role there: a block's face its `size` (`index` the axis), a cylinder's wall its `radius` and a cap its `height`, a cone's, a sphere's and a torus's parameters, a fillet's `radius`, a chamfer's `distance`, an extrusion's end cap its `distance`; a side or an edge an extrusion or a revolution swept from a sketch curve that curve: its `radius` or `diameter` constraint, or a line's `distance`, else through `equal` constraints the nearest curve that has one (`via`), else the curve with `constraint: null`. An edge of a block takes the size along which it runs: the axis neither of its two faces is square to (right however the block is turned); a circular edge of a cylinder or cone its radius, a straight one its height; any other edge the drivers of its first curved face (the wall it is the rim of). A vertex has none: everything that meets there places it. **What an edit changes is said**: a feature's driver has `affects` (how many faces of the current bodies that feature made) and `alike` (how many of them are this same face, copies that patterns and mirrors made, which the edit changes alike), with `shared: true` when more than one: one bolt hole of the housing says its cylinder's radius changes six walls. A sketch constraint's driver lists the `curves` it sizes (all those equal to the one it is on) and `affects`, the faces swept from them. A value that is a formula has `formula: true`; one that is a single variable also `variable`, and its `edit` is `variable.set` of that variable, so a blind edit keeps the design's formulas. Cheap: no measure is integrated; the per-body names are looked up once. "Make this hole 6.5 mm" is the driver's `edit` with the new value (`crates/engine/tests/selection_sets.rs`).

### Named sets (slice 2)

A named set (B2) is a query kept in the document under a name: `set.define {name, kind?, where?}`, `set.edit {set, kind?, where?, name?}` (a part given replaces that part, `null` removes it), `set.remove {set}`, `set.list {}`. The document keeps the query as JSON text (`craft3d_doc::NamedSet`, beside the history like the appearances, not read by regeneration; each change is an undo step; format 27), with the features of `in`, `madeBy` and `copyOf` written as ids, so a rename changes nothing. It is computed again whenever it is used: as the filter `{set: name}` (combinable with others), as `select.set {set}`, and in `set.list`, which says how many items each holds now, or why it does not read (a feature it names was deleted). A set renamed is renamed in the sets that use it, as formulas follow a renamed variable, so a set defined later under the old name changes nothing; a set another uses is not removed (the error names the users). A query is checked before it is kept: everything `select.query` checks, and a set that comes back to itself through other sets is refused ("uses itself: a -> b -> a"). `document.inspect` lists the sets as kept (without counts, which would cost a query each). At most 1000 sets, a query at most 64 KiB.

### Plate thickness (2026-10-30)

`thickness` is a numeric filter of faces like `area` (a value matched within a millionth of it plus the document's tolerance, `{eq, tol}` or `{min, max}`, each an expression of the variables, evaluated whenever the query is, so a named set follows the variables). The thickness of a face (`verify::plate_thickness`) is defined for a flat face only: the length of a line from a point of the face along the inward normal until it first leaves the body, which must be the same, within the linear tolerance, from every point, and must end on a flat face parallel to it and facing the other way (within the angular tolerance; at an edge the line crosses, one of the faces meeting there). Anything else has no thickness and never fits: a curved face, a face of an open body, a face with a slanted back, a face over a step or a pocket. The lines are exact (`Probe::line_hits` against the faces' surfaces). They start from a grid over the face's triangles (up to 32 points) and, because a step behind a face need not show in its triangles, from either side of where each vertex of the body behind the face projects onto it: where the distance changes, it changes across the projection of an edge of the faces behind, and every such edge ends in a vertex. Only the vertices behind the face that project within its box are sampled around; a face with more than 4096 of them has no thickness (it never fits) rather than an answer that may have missed a step. A face cut into pieces that keep its name has a thickness when every piece has the same one. The body's probe and mesh are made once per query and body, and each face's thickness once.

*Speed (2026-10-30).* One query over the flat faces of a plate with 500 bosses (2506 faces, 4008 vertices) took 6.7 s on the Strix: 804 000 lines, each followed through the whole body (16.5 million hits: a line from a boss's side crossed every boss of its row). Now: (1) the grid's lines are cast first, so a face over a step the grid shows is told before any vertex is looked at; (2) once one distance `lo` is known, a line is followed only to `lo` + 4 tolerances (a line that leaves later widens the range past the tolerance whatever it meets there, and every face met within the tolerance of where it leaves lies before that), so the answer is the same; (3) only vertices no deeper than the deepest line met (plus twice the tolerance) are sampled around, and vertices within the tolerance of one taken are taken once: wherever the length of the lines changes away from a length `d` some line has, an edge no deeper than `d` projects there (the opposite face's own edge, at `d`, or the edge of a face in front of it), and an edge of the body that ends deeper leaves the face's box or meets the opposite plane at a vertex at `d` (`a_narrow_rib_behind_a_face_is_found_from_where_it_meets_the_plate` pins a rib the grid misses, at three scales); the 4096 cap counts the vertices sampled; (4) an edge that crosses the face's box (its box meets the face's box swept to the deepest line) with an end that is not sampled, outside the box or deeper, is sampled at 33 points along it, each that projects within the box standing for a vertex: a step bounded by a long edge is told without a grid point on it (`a_groove_whose_ends_lie_outside_the_face_is_found_from_its_edges`), which let the grid go from 256 points to 32; (5) the thicknesses are remembered per body (by its `Arc`, held by a `Weak`, as the mass properties are) and tolerance across queries, so a named set by thickness asked again costs nothing until the body is rebuilt. The same query: 0.36 s on the Strix (test profile; grid 0.17 s, the loops over vertices and edges 0.12 s, about 130 000 lines), 4 ms asked again; same answers (`crates/engine/tests/units_and_plates.rs`).

### Not yet

- **Relations and extremes**: neighbours of, the same size as, symmetric to, coaxial with, the highest or largest or nearest *n*; distance to an item; faces of an edge's body side. Spatial words relative to the camera are not built (B3); directions are world axes.
- A vertex is not followed into a later feature; a face that a pattern copies is followed to its first copy only (said under `followed`). A curved face is still boxed by the B-rep's held box, whose spline edges count with the hull of the whole spline (`inside` may miss a curved face with spline edges near the box); a spline edge whose range runs backwards (past the seam of a periodic curve) is boxed by its whole hull. A set from the current selection (a set by names) is not a command yet: write it as a query of `name` globs.
- A cone (countersink) as part of a hole; edges' direction for `normal`/`axis`; the section's cap in the list of hits; picking datums and sketches by clicking.
- Preview and confirmation of large AI actions (B6).

(c) `file.close {document: 0, discard: true}` was refused: `document` was not a parameter; it is now (the active document stays active).
