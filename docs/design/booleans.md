# 3DCraft design: Booleans

Union, difference and intersection of solids (`kernel::boolean`, one file per step in `crates/kernel/src/boolean/`). Method 2 of the map ([`../architecture.md`](../architecture.md) §5); what is still owed is the boolean checklist of [`../roadmap.md`](../roadmap.md).

## Method

`boolean(a, b, op, tolerance)` returns the body and a source for every face, edge and vertex of it (`FaceSource`, `EdgeSource`, `VertexSource`; `merged` lists every operand face a result face has a piece of). Operands are any solids on planes, quadrics, tori, Bézier and NURBS surfaces (rational ones, with poles, closing on themselves), with edges on lines, circles, ellipses and splines.

1. **Section** (`section.rs`, `hits.rs`). For each pair of faces that can meet (boxes, sides and covers: *Faces that lie near one another* below), the curves in which their surfaces meet come from `geom::meet_surfaces`, asked inside the box the two faces share (grown by 16 tolerances and 1.3 % of the larger face's box; when what lies in that box cannot be traced, asked again with the room of the smaller face, *A plane beside a rippled surface* below), so what else either body holds does not change them. A curve is cut where an edge of either face meets the other's surface (closed forms: lines, circles and ellipses against planes and cylinders, and since 2026-10-22 lines and circles against balls and lines against cones, whose distance is monotone between turning points in closed form; else roots of the signed distance isolated by a bound on how fast it can change, never by sampling) and where it ends; a stretch is kept when its middle is in or on both faces, and left out without that when its middle or an end lies clear of either face's box (between its cuts a stretch crosses the boundary of neither face); every edge of the result on a part of a spline curve gets that part as its curve (knot insertion, after merging), so an edge's curve reaches no further than the edge. A vertex is always where an edge's *own* curve meets a surface, except that the hit of a spline edge (one that stands for where two surfaces meet) is taken to where its two faces' surfaces and the other surface meet when that is more than a quarter of the tolerance away and within the tolerance of the edge (`corner.rs`, since 2026-10-22: *A spline edge that meets a surface at a shallow angle* below).
2. **Imprint.** Edges are split at the vertices found; edge pieces and stretches become arcs, two arcs between the same vertices along the same curve are one, and an edge that lies on a face or edge of the other body is shared from then on. Where two faces' surfaces only touch (points and no curve: a contact along a curve with no closed form reads as touches), an edge of either that lies on the other's surface is a stretch of its own, its own curve clipped like any meeting curve (`section::lying_edges`, since 2026-10-18: the strip of a fillet's tool on the face it rounds, *Tangent contact along an edge* below). Nothing is matched by position afterwards.
3. **Regions** (`regions.rs`). Around each vertex the arcs are sorted by direction in the tangent plane, told apart further along when they leave together (`untie`), except that an imprinted arc that leaves along a piece of the face's boundary is put on the face's side of it without measuring (`order_run`, *Tangent contact of higher order at the boundary* below); loops are followed keeping the region on the left and grouped in a chart of the surface.
4. **Classify** (`classify.rs`, `chart.rs`). One point well inside a region decides it: on a coincident face of the other body, or inside or outside it by ray casting. A region that holds no point four tolerances from its boundary (a sliver: the corner a curve cuts off a face just past its vertex) is read at its boundary instead, from what the other body is along an arc of it (`classify::at_boundary`, *Slivers read at their boundary* below); a sliver the other body does not pass through (no arc of it is on that body: a face that is itself a sliver, left by an earlier boolean) lies on one side of it as a whole and is told by a point as close as a quarter of the margin to its boundary (`classify::samples`, *A face that is itself a sliver* below). Inside or outside a face is a winding number in a chart of its surface computed from points in space, so seams and poles are ordinary points: conformal maps for the analytic kinds (a torus cut open along a circle around its tube or its axis that the loops provably do not cross, and held to that along every stretch of the winding sum: *The fuzzer's wrong results* below), the parameter rectangle (drawn as a wedge, a lens, a disc or an annulus where a border is a pole or the surface closes) for freeform faces, whose nearest points and bounds come from a `PatchTree` of Bézier pieces (`freeform.rs`).
5. **Select** by the table below (coincident faces with the same or opposite normals included).
6. **Sew** (`sew.rs`). Where more than two faces meet along an arc or a vertex, each fan gets its own copy, so every result is a manifold.
7. **Merge** (`merge.rs`). Pieces of one operand face, or coincident faces of both, that share an edge and a surface become one face again; the result passes `topo::check` or the operation fails.

**Tolerances.** Points within half the tolerance are one vertex, apart at twice it, refused in between; the same for faces over each other, and a lens thinner than twice the tolerance between faces of different kinds, or beside a curve geometry calls a touch (since 2026-10-22), is measured: up to half the tolerance they touch, above it the operation refuses. No decision is taken on the sign of a number that could be rounding. Bodies that only touch have nothing in common: the union is both bodies, the intersection empty, the difference the first body. These distance rules, and the snapping of hits onto the ends of edges, are structural debt (the map's §9): they read one tolerance for every entity.

**Tolerant operands** (since 2026-10-21, `boolean/loose.rs`; the decision of 2026-10-18, option (4)). An operand with loose entities (a vertex or an edge with a tolerance of its own, [`topology.md`](topology.md) › *Tolerant entities*) is worked on at the largest tolerance of both operands and the document's, the *working tolerance*: every rule above runs unchanged with that number in place of the document's (no rule reads an entity's own tolerance yet; that is for later, one rule at a time, where a measurement shows the coarseness costs something). A working tolerance above the cap (`BooleanOptions::loosest`, the document's `tolerance.loosest`, by default 1000 times the linear tolerance) is refused (`KernelError::ToleranceAboveCap`). Each operand is first checked at the document's tolerance with its own table and the cap: a gap no entity records is the operand's defect and refused (`InvalidBody`), never hidden by the working tolerance. The result's entities: a vertex or edge kept from an operand in which it was loose keeps that tolerance; every other one starts exact. The result is checked at the document's tolerance with its table, and each entity not within its own is *measured* (`topo::measure_edge`, `measure_vertex`) and given what it needs (`RECORD_MARGIN` above the measure, never more than the working tolerance it was built at), but only an entity the boolean could have moved: one it made, one loose in its operand, or an exact one next to those (an end of a new or loose edge, an edge ending at a new or loose vertex, an edge of a face merged from both operands or bounded by the other operand's edges). Every tolerance given is reported in `BooleanResult::raised` (entity, tolerance, origin: new, loose or exact), and `document.check` lists every body with loose entities (`loose`). What still fails is refused (`InvalidBody`), never returned. So new entities that lie within the document's tolerance stay exact, and a result whose loose entities were all cut away is exact again. Two exact operands never take this path: their boolean is bit for bit what it was. The operations on one body (fillet, chamfer, offset, move, draft and delete face, shell, thicken) do the same since 2026-10-22 (`kernel/src/loose_op.rs`, *Tolerant modelling, the second slice* below), the boolean between such a body and a tool made from it at four times the working tolerance (`loose_op::for_tool`, *the third slice*); a sweep, a thread or an extrusion meets a loose body only through this boolean.

**Speed.** The faces of B a face of A can meet, the faces a ray or a point near the boundary can reach, and the edges of a face near the other face of a pair come from trees of boxes (`boolean/facetree.rs`, *Many lumps* below), in the order testing every one would take them, so the work grows with the faces, not with their product. A face whose box is loose (a curved piece turned off the axes) is covered by boxes of patches of its surface, a tree made as it is asked (`boolean/cover.rs`, since 2026-10-22): two faces whose covers are further apart than 16 tolerances are not intersected, and a point further than four tolerances from a face's cover is outside it without its outline being asked. Outlines are made when a pair of faces first needs them, kept with a box per boundary piece; pure functions are remembered by the exact bits of what was asked (nearest points per tree, the trees themselves, checked bodies, projectors, numeric intersections), so nothing changes but the time. Each change meant only for speed is held bit for bit to the corpus digest and the random runs.

**Lumps and cavities** (`kernel::lumps`, `topo::check` per shell; since 2026-10-17). A solid is a set of closed shells, one outer shell per lump and any cavities in it. The boolean needs nothing special for them: a region is classified by the parity of a ray against the whole other operand, every shell of it, so a shell that does not meet the other body is kept or dropped by the same rule as the faces that do, and sewing makes the shells as the connected components of what is kept, so a result falls into lumps and cavities by itself. Which shell is which is geometry, told by `lumps(body, tolerance)` for every consumer (STEP export, `document.check` deep): a few points of a shell classified against each other shell alone (each other shell prepared once, for the points of every shell its box holds) say which shells it lies in: a point on the other shell is refused as shells that meet, points that disagree or none that can tell are unsupported, since that shells do not cross is the self-intersection check's to find; an outside lies in an even number of shells, a cavity in an odd number, the shell it lies in most deeply is of the other kind one level up, and a cavity belongs to that lump: that is what lumps and cavities are, and it decides (since 2026-10-20; before, the sign of the volume decided and the nesting had to agree). The volume a shell encloses (`topo::shell_volumes`: the faces integrated once, summed per shell, with the integrator's bound and the shell's area) is held to it where it has a sign to read, beyond the integrator's bound plus what the tolerance allows (twice the tolerance times the area, and the flux through the gaps of up to twice the tolerance along the shell's edges, bounded by two thirds of the tolerance times the diagonal of the box the point the integrals are about lies in times the edges' length: `lumps::uncertainty`; a shell whose sign the body's diagonal leaves unread is measured again alone, about a point in its own box, and held to its own diagonal); a shell whose readable volume says the other kind is turned inside out. Anything else is `Defect::ShellNesting`. `topo::check` holds each shell to hanging together through edges, sharing no edge with another shell and, in a solid of several, to Euler–Poincaré on its own (`V − E + F − R − 2H = 2(1 − g)`, `topo::shell_genera`). Volume is the signed sum over the shells. `document.check` deep reports a body that is not lumps and cavities as a problem and one whose shells cannot be sorted under `marginal`; the STEP export sorts nothing when exactly one shell faces outward. A feature whose result has several lumps gives one body (decided 2026-10-18; separate bodies through `body.split`, owed). Held to: `crates/kernel/tests/lumps.rs` (cuts into two lumps, cavities of a block, a ball, a ring and a spline cone, a ball in a ball, operands with a cavity or two lumps opened, kept, filled, drilled and joined, a hollow in a hollow, seeded mistakes of nesting; closed-form volumes, lumps and cavities, the genus of each shell, watertight meshes, the full check and the identities of sets, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6) and `crates/engine/tests/lumps.rs` (the same through the commands, with fillets, offsets, shells, delete face, STEP and the round trip); FreeCAD reads six such documents at three scales (`step_freecad.rs`). Sheet bodies are refused as operands ("booleans need solids"): owed.

**Sheets** (`boolean/cut.rs`, `classify::sheet`; since 2026-10-18). A sheet (an open shell, [`topology.md`](topology.md)) is an operand of a subtract or an intersect with a solid, read as point sets, and the result is of the target's kind. *A sheet trimmed by a solid* (the sheet the target): its regions are classified by the solid's rays as any region is; intersect keeps those inside, subtract those outside, nothing of the solid is kept, and the result is a sheet, so `area(S ∩ A) + area(S ∖ A) = area(S)`. *A solid cut by a sheet* (the sheet the tool): the sheet is a wall whose material lies behind it, as behind a face of a solid; the sheet's regions inside the solid are the cap, kept as they are for intersect and turned over for subtract (subtract keeps the side the sheet's normal points to: decided 2026-10-18); the solid's regions have no ray to tell them (a sheet has no inside), so each is given its side where it meets the sheet: along an arc where the sheet meets the solid's boundary (the cap's boundary, where the sheet passes through the boundary; and the boundary of a region of the solid that lies on the sheet, where the sheet's contact with a face ends: since 2026-10-20) the direction from the arc into the region (`n × t`, the face to the left of its loop) points along the sheet's normal (in front: subtract keeps it) or against it (behind: intersect keeps it), and regions that share an arc that is no such meeting are on one side (a flood over the solid's arcs). So `vol(front) + vol(behind) = vol(A)`. The sheet must reach across the solid: every arc of the cap's boundary lies on an edge or across a face of the solid (checked before the solid's faces are divided), divides the face it crosses (a ring cut once is one piece of material on both sides of the cut), every region is reached from a meeting, and none from both sides; otherwise, and for a cut that is tangent along a curve, it is refused with the reason. *A piece of the sheet on a face of the solid* counts as inside the solid, a closed set (decided 2026-10-18, built 2026-10-20), whichever way the face looks: a trim keeps it for intersect and takes it away for subtract; in a cut it is a face the solid and the material behind the sheet share, so the solid's region there stands for it, kept as between two solids (`classify::solids`: intersect keeps it when it looks the sheet's way, subtract when it looks the other way), told by its class and not by its arcs, and the sheet's piece is never a cap (a sheet on a face of a block, facing out, leaves nothing in front and the whole block behind). A boundary edge of the sheet lying inside such a face is imprinted on it from the edge itself (`section::lying_edges`, `Lying::Boundary`: an edge of a solid is imprinted there by its second face, a boundary edge has none), so the face is divided where the sheet on it ends, from where the rim enters the face to where it leaves it (`section::edge_crossings`: the rim and the face's edges lie on one surface, so where they cross is found between the curves, since 2026-10-21); the region beyond continues the sheet's surface and is told nothing by that arc (reached from another meeting, or refused: a disc on a plate around a boss does not reach across the plate). A sheet whose shell is closed is a solid's shell mislabelled and is refused by `topo::check` (`SheetClosed`): edges and none on the boundary, or a face without loops on a surface that closes all around (a sphere, a torus, a freeform surface closed both ways; since 2026-10-21). A sheet result whose boundary comes back to itself in a point (a hole tangent to what a trim takes away, the figure of eight a plane leaves of a ring it touches inside) is refused as zero thickness: a vertex within twice the tolerance of another vertex or an edge of its shell (`craft3d_topo::touches`, the rule regeneration holds every body to); the sewing gives each side its own vertex there, so `topo::check` alone does not see it. *Two sheets* (`two_sheets.rs`, decided 2026-10-22, built the same day): the target sheet is cut by the tool sheet as a solid is, by the same flood: its faces are divided where the tool passes through them, a region beside such an arc is in front of the tool when the direction into it within the target's surface points along the tool's normal, regions joined by an arc the tool does not meet are on one side; subtract keeps the part in front, intersect the part behind, and the result is a sheet of pieces of the target's faces, facing as they did. A union sews two sheets along boundary edges they share (their edge pieces are one arc where they coincide, and coincident faces that share one merge), and is refused where they meet otherwise. Refused as well (work owed): a union of a sheet with a solid, and a shell of the solid the sheet does not cross (which side of an open sheet a lump or cavity lies on is not told yet). The check of the result (`verify.rs`) holds a trim to every rule of a boolean (the volume of a sheet is zero; no point is compared, a sheet has no inside), and a cut to the volume and reach of a difference, to its seeded points one way (inside the result is inside the solid) and to the side kept: a cut face (on the surface of a face of the sheet and of no face of the solid) faces against the sheet's normal for a subtract and along it for an intersect; the far-face rule does not hold for a cut, which keeps of a face far from the sheet the side it is on. Ray classification of points against a sheet (`contains_points`, `classify_points`) and `interference` with one are refused.

**Interference** (`contact.rs`, `interference`; the command `document.interference`; `common_volume` is its first step alone, for the `overlap` measure, since 2026-10-26). Whether two solids are clear, touch or overlap, read-only. Boxes grown by twice the tolerance that do not meet are clear at no further cost. Otherwise the intersection (parts that touch allowed) is the overlap; when it is empty, the section says whether and how they meet: a stretch of curve of positive length is contact along an edge, vertices where both meet, points where faces only touch and a corner of one on a face of the other are points; where faces of both lie on one surface with their outward normals opposite (a face's orientation counts: a pin in its hole, a body in a cavity), their union decides between a shared area (the union's boundary is smaller than both boundaries by twice that area, which is reported) and only an edge of it. Contact points and the faces they are on come with it; a refusal of the boolean is an error, never "clear". Held to closed forms at three scales (boxes on a face, an edge and a corner, a pin in its hole, a block against an inner wall, a body filling a cavity, a cylinder through, lying on and a ball on a plate, a tilted block on an edge and a corner) and to the laser box of the showcase, whose overlaps at kerf 0.15 mm agree with an independent cell grid to 4 digits (`crates/engine/tests/interference.rs`). The command checks at most 2000 pairs whose boxes meet per question.

**Its own check** (`verify.rs`, since 2026-10-17). Before a result is returned it is held to what every exact boolean of its operands keeps, and refused as `KernelError::WrongResult` (a bug, named) when it does not: every vertex and the middle of every edge within the tolerance of where the result can be (the box of A or of B for a union, of A for a difference, of both for an intersection), and every face of an operand whose box is further than the tolerance from the other operand's box in the result as it was (union: of A and of B; difference: of A): the same surface, side, loops and edges. `boolean::verify` with `VerifyOptions::default()` adds the volume bounds and seeded points (below, *The boolean's own check*); the tests and `document.check` deep run that, and `CRAFT3D_BOOLEAN_CHECK=full` makes every boolean run it.

**Held to**: closed forms, the identities of sets, analytic twins of rational faces, 400 to 4000 seeded points against exact membership, the mesh self-intersection check, and a corpus of 3996 pairs against FreeCAD (11,988 outcomes: 11,573 agree, 249 refused, none wrong or invalid; [`../testing.md`](../testing.md)).

### Booleans as built (M7, generalised 2026-10-13)

`kernel::boolean::boolean(a, b, op, tolerance)` returns a `BooleanResult`: the body and, per face, edge and vertex of it, a source (`FaceSource`, `EdgeSource`, `VertexSource`). `boolean_with` takes `BooleanOptions` (`merge`, on by default). `boolean::contains_points` classifies points against a solid with the same ray casting the booleans use; it does not go through `mass_properties`, so a count of sampled points measures a body independently of it.

**Method** (imprint, classify, select, sew, merge; one file per step in `crates/kernel/src/boolean/`):

1. *Section* (`section.rs`). For each face of A and face of B that can meet (their boxes meet and neither lies wholly to one side of the other's surface), the curves in which their surfaces meet are asked of `meet_surfaces`, with the box where both faces are, grown by 16 tolerances and 1.3 % of the diagonal of the larger face's box (until 2026-10-15 the box where both *bodies* are, so that what else a body held decided where two surfaces were looked at: *The box of the two faces* below). A curve is cut where an edge of either face meets the other face's surface (`hits.rs`: the closed forms of `geom::intersect`, and where there is none the roots of the signed distance along the curve, isolated by the fact that the distance changes no faster than the curve moves), and where the curve itself ends. A stretch between two cuts is kept when its middle is in or on both faces. The boolean does not know which pair of surfaces a curve came from, nor whether it is exact: a vertex is always where an *edge's own curve* meets a surface, and is then found on the stretch within the tolerance.
2. *Imprint.* Edges are split at the vertices found on them. Edge pieces and stretches are registered as arcs; two arcs between the same vertices along the same curve are one arc. An edge of B that lies on a face of A, or along an edge of A, is shared from then on, and nothing is matched by position afterwards.
3. *Regions* (`regions.rs`). A face with arcs across it falls into regions: around each vertex the arcs are sorted by direction in the tangent plane (at the apex of a cone: seen along the axis), arcs that leave in one direction are told apart a little further along, and loops are followed keeping the region on the left. An arc with the same region on both sides divides nothing and is dropped. Loops are grouped in a chart of the surface: a loop that goes around a point just to its left is an outside, any other a hole of the innermost outside around it.
4. *Classify* (`classify.rs`, `chart.rs`). One point well inside a region decides for the region: on a face of the other solid with a coincident surface, or inside or outside the other solid by ray casting (a ray that meets an edge or grazes a face is abandoned for another, twelve at most; a point within twice the tolerance of the other body is no use, and the next of up to four is tried). **Inside or outside a face is a winding number in a chart of its surface**, computed from points in space, so no seam or pole is a special place: a plane is its own chart; a cylinder is mapped conformally (angle and `exp(height / radius)` as polar coordinates, evaluated without the exponential); a cone is dropped along its axis onto a plane, which makes the apex an ordinary point; a sphere is projected stereographically from a point chosen clear of the boundary; a torus is cut open along a circle that the boundary does not cross; a freeform face has its own parameters, drawn as a wedge or a lens where a border of its patch is one point. A face on a sphere or a torus may contain the place its chart leaves out; the winding of its loops about a point just inside it says which. A face without loops is its whole surface.
5. *Select.* See the table below.
6. *Sew* (`sew.rs`). Faces connected through edges form a shell. Where more than two faces meet along an arc, faces and material alternate around it: each face that runs against the arc is paired with the next face counter-clockwise (faces tangent there are put in order a little way off the arc) and every pair gets its own copy of the edge. Where several fans of faces meet at a vertex, every fan gets its own copy of the vertex. The copies are in one place and share nothing, so every result is a manifold. A bounded face on a sphere gets a frame whose poles are clear of its boundary (a sphere is the same surface whichever way its poles point; what reads a face through its parameters is at its best that way).
7. *Merge* (`merge.rs`). Faces that share an edge and lie on one surface facing one way are one face when they are pieces of one face of an operand, or pieces of a face of A and a face of B that are coincident; every edge inside such a group goes and the loops are spliced where it was. A vertex left with two edges that continue each other along one curve goes too, unless its operand had it like that. The face that remains has the source of the lowest-numbered face of A in the group, else of B; a joined edge the lesser of its sources (an edge of A before an edge of B before an intersection). The result passes `topo::check` or the operation fails.

**Contact without overlap.** Bodies that only touch (face against face along a line, a circle or at a point; an edge against a face or an edge; a vertex against anything) have nothing in common: the intersection is `EmptyResult`, the difference is the first body with the entities it had. A curve along which two faces touch is imprinted like any other; where the result does not need it (it ends inside a face, or has one face on both sides) it leaves no trace, and where it does (a cylinder that touches all four sides of its block cuts it into four parts) it is an edge.

**Zero thickness (the developer's decision of 2026-10-15).** A result whose parts (shells) touch each other, along an edge, a line or in a point, is refused, as other modellers refuse it ("Cannot create zero-thickness geometry"): `KernelError::ZeroThickness`, with the place and a face of each operand there; regeneration names both faces by their persistent names and suggests moving the parts apart by more than the tolerance or into each other. That is the union of bodies that only touch, and any result whose parts hang together only by an edge or a vertex (two opposite quarters cut out of a plate, the corners a cylinder leaves of the block it touches on all four sides, the two halves of a rod that goes on in a ring). Separate bodies may touch; a cavity apart from the outside is two shells and fine. Parts can only touch where the operands meet, or where an operand already touches itself (a shell meeting itself, or two of its shells touching: such an operand cut in two may leave parts touching where the operands never met), so only those places are looked at (`zero_thickness` in `boolean/mod.rs`): the vertices both operands have, the vertices the section made, the points where two faces only touch (`Sections::touches`), a point inside every stretch of intersection curve, the vertices of either operand that lie on a face of the other (`on_the_other`, only those in the other's box, against the faces whose boxes hold them, through a grid of face boxes), and the places where an operand's vertex lies on a vertex or an edge of that operand not its own (`craft3d_topo::touches`). Regeneration's `body_touches_itself` looks at every result for those vertex and edge forms as well. A place is a touch when it is within twice the tolerance of a face of one part and of a face of another, not outside either face's boundary, and the nearest points of the two faces are within twice the tolerance of each other: the parts are no further apart than the tolerance can tell, the rule `craft3d_topo::touches` applies to vertices and edges. With the refusal off (`BooleanOptions::refuse_zero_thickness`, used by blend's tool steps and the cut of an extrusion up to a face, whose results regeneration checks) the result is both parts in one body, shells that share no entity, every edge shared by exactly two faces; the result is the same, bit for bit, as before the refusal (the corpus digest `19af10963b6f12c2` and both random digests unchanged).

*What it changed (expectations):* the corpus counts 773 such refusals (tangent 295, contact 281, near 197), each held to FreeCAD's answer by the body made without the refusal and to a point on two of its parts (`contains_points` of each part alone); the score reads 10800 agreeing + 773 refused by design. The random runs refuse 45 (flat) and 30 (every shape) results, all on the grid; `self_intersection.rs` 18. **Documents that relied on it no longer regenerate:** a union of bodies that touch, an extrusion of profiles that touch at a corner, a loft, sweep, pattern or mirror whose shells touch (a pattern's instances are united, and regeneration's check, `body_touches_itself`, now looks between the shells of a solid too). Owed: tangent contact of two faces where the section reports no stretch and no touch point; faces of one operand that touch each other with no vertex there (an operand that is itself zero thick that way); a result whose faces cannot be outlined is not looked at for this.

**Where an edge meets the other body's surface** (2026-10-18, the fuzzer's seed 19604). A ball that touches the rim of a cone's base from outside, in one point in the plane of the base, was united into two shells that touch: no vertex of either lies there, the section's curves (the base's plane cuts the ball in its equator) leave no stretch inside both faces, and `Sections::touches` holds only points where two surfaces touch, which the plane and the ball do not. The only sign is that the rim meets the ball's surface there, which `edge_cuts` finds and `clip` drops with the stretch it bounds. Every such point (`Sections::edge_meets`: where an edge of one face meets the other face's surface, for every pair whose surfaces meet in a curve) is now a place `zero_thickness` looks at. *What it changed (expectations):* 158 unions of the corpus that were scored `match` (157 of two shells, FreeCAD's answer too, and `contact/cone-spline/005` of one) are refused as zero thickness now, all in `contact` (100) and `near` (58), where an edge of one body touches a face of the other; each is held by the corpus's own witness, a point on two parts of the body made without the refusal (`on_parts`, rays on each part alone) or, for the one shell, `touches_itself_at`. The agreement sum stays at 11582 and the digest at `3f00bc8ced79a654` (the bodies made without the refusal are the bodies made before); the outright agreement goes from 10717 to 10559 and the zero-thickness count from 865 to 1023, so `MATCHED_FLOOR` and `ZERO_THICK_CEILING` move with them (an expectation change, logged in the dev log). The curves come from `geom::intersect::meet_surfaces` (since 2026-10-13; the stand-in of the first version is gone): exact where there is a closed form, splines within the tolerance otherwise. What it cannot give (surfaces tangent or nearly coincident over an area, a touch along a curve without closed form) is refused with its reason. Curve/surface hits use geometry's closed forms for lines, circles and ellipses against planes and cylinders, and for every other analytic pair the bounded root isolation in `hits.rs`, which takes a piece as given (a hit at its end is a touch) and never samples. `BooleanResult::merged` lists, per face, every face of the operands it has a piece of; `BooleanOptions::trust_operands` skips the validity check of operands the kernel itself returned.

**Tolerances.** Points within half the linear tolerance are one vertex; between that and twice the tolerance the operation refuses. The same holds for faces: two faces on one surface, or on surfaces that never cross, are the same surface within half the tolerance of each other where one is over the other, apart at twice the tolerance or more, and refused in between. Joined faces are not moved onto each other: the volume of such a result is right to the area in contact times the gap. The counterpart for faces of different kinds (a ball that dips into a plate, a torus lying a hair too deep in a block): beside every curve in which two faces cross, `section::passage` walks one surface away from the curve both ways until it is twice the tolerance from the other. Where that happens on opposite sides of the other surface the faces cross, exactly as given. Where it happens on the same side, the surface went through the other and came back: a lens thinner than twice the tolerance, whose thickness is measured; up to half the tolerance the faces touch and nothing is imprinted, above it the operation refuses. A body ten tolerances thick is a body. No decision is taken on the sign of a number that could be rounding: membership is a winding number, a root is isolated by a bound and not by sampling, and what cannot be told is refused.

*Method.* The crate's `BoxTree` (made for profile detection, now for boxes in the plane or in space, `TreeBox`) over each operand's face boxes, made the first time it is asked, and per face over its edges' boxes (`outline::piece_box`: the ends, a whole circle or ellipse for a piece of one, a spline's control points). A search hands each node's hull to the same test the loop applied to each face, goes below only where it passes, and returns the faces (edges) that pass **in ascending order**; the loop then runs exactly as before over them. Every test asked is monotone in the box (a box against a box, a point or a line, each grown by a margin; floating point's rounding is monotone, and a hull is minima and maxima, exact), so the faces found are those testing every face finds, in the same order, and nothing after it can tell. Faces whose box is not finite (empty, infinite or NaN corners) are in no node and always handed on; fewer than 16 boxes get no tree. **Freeform faces (2026-10-13, `boolean/freeform.rs`).** A Bézier or B-spline face is indexed by a `PatchTree`: the surface as Bézier pieces (`geom`: `NurbsSurface::bezier_spans` by knot insertion, `RationalBezierPatch` with de Casteljau splitting), each quartered five times (since 2026-10-23 halved only across the way it is long where it is short the other way: *Patch trees of thin strips, and a budget for the trees of one operation* below), with the box of every piece's control net (the piece lies in it). The tree answers what the booleans ask of a surface: a bound below the distance from a point to the surface that never overestimates (the distance to the nearest box, split further on the spot to a quarter of the tolerance or of the distance), which is what calls a stretch of a curve provably clear of the surface in the root isolation of `hits.rs`; the nearest point, by Newton's iteration (second derivatives from differences of the first, steps held to the domain's sides) from the middle of the nearest piece and from every piece whose box is nearer than the best point found; the signed distance; and a bound on how fast the parameters change per length travelled on the surface (the smallest singular value of the Jacobian, from the Bézier nets of the derivatives and of the normal field), which the chart of the face needs: a freeform face's chart is its own parameter rectangle, reached through the nearest point. A `Sheet` (a surface with its index) is what the helpers of `outline`, `hits`, `section`, `classify`, `regions` and `chart` take; an `Outline` builds the tree for a freeform face once and shares it with the pieces of the face. The hull of a freeform face includes its control net. Where a freeform surface meets another, the surfaces count as crossing (no lens is looked for). A NURBS surface or curve evaluated beyond its domain continues the end span's polynomial, as a Bézier does, because the marcher of the surface intersections needs something to correct onto past a border. Since the evening of 2026-10-13 the root isolation (`hits.rs`, `numeric`) judges a whole stretch of a curve against a freeform face at once wherever the curve's bend is bounded (lines, circles, ellipses and splines, rational ones included, whose second derivative is bounded through the nets of their Bézier pieces: `NurbsCurve::derivative_bounds`): a stretch on the surface is proven to stay within the tolerance from the distances at its ends and a bound on the surface's second derivatives over its domain (from the Bézier nets: the difference between curve and surface along the parameter path between the feet stays within an eighth of the bend, `PatchTree::stretch_within`); a stretch beside it is proven clear by a lower bound on the distance from its chord to the surface (`segment_lower_bound`; the distance from a segment to a box is exact, one convex quadratic per side crossing) less how far the curve can leave the chord. The tree bounds its pieces by boxes in the piece's own frame (as thick as the piece bends, not as it tilts: a tilted piece's axis-aligned box is as thick as the piece is wide, which left a curve at 1.06 tolerances from a face unprovable), takes a threshold above which nothing matters, and the point bound is only asked where it can prove something. The slowest corpus case (a contact of two spline-roofed blocks, 200 s per boolean) takes 0.19 s.

**Rational faces (2026-10-13, late).** A face on a rational Bézier or NURBS surface (weights that are not all one) is an operand like a polynomial one. A rational patch lies in the hull of its control points just the same, so the boxes of the tree, the bound below the distance, the nearest point and the signed distance needed nothing. What was polynomial-only were the three bounds that came from derivative nets, and each now has a proven counterpart in `geom` (`nurbs.rs`; the arguments are in the doc comments):

- *First and second derivatives* (`RationalBezierPatch::derivative_bounds`, `NurbsCurve::derivative_bounds`). With `S = A / w` (weighted points over weight, both in Bernstein form), `S_u = (A_u − S w_u) / w` and `S_uu = (A_uu − 2 w_u S_u − w_uu S) / w`. `A_u − S w_u` is a Bernstein form whose coefficients are `m (w₁ (P₁ − P₀) + (w₁ − w₀) (P₀ − S))` over neighbours, and `S` lies in the hull, so `|P₀ − S|` is at most the reach of `P₀` across the box of the net; `w` is at least the lightest weight; the second derivatives likewise from second differences of `w (P − S)`. For weights that are all one these are the hull bounds of a polynomial patch, to the last bit. They loosen as the weights of a piece differ and tighten as the piece shrinks, so they are taken from small pieces: the leaves of the tree for the bound that `stretch_within` uses, the pieces of a span quartered twice for the marcher's field (which used estimates from samples for a rational surface before), a span halved three times for a curve.
- *The normal* (`RationalBezierPatch::weighted_normal_net`). `w³ (S_u × S_v) = w (A_u × A_v) + w_v (A × A_u) + w_u (A_v × A)` is a polynomial where the normal is not; its Bézier net (degrees `3m − 1` by `3n − 1`, products of Bernstein forms) holds it in its hull, so the normal is at least as long as the distance from the origin to the box of that net over the cube of the heaviest weight. With the bound on the first derivatives that gives the smallest singular value of the Jacobian, and so the parameter speed the chart needs.
- *The bend of a rational edge* (`hits.rs`, `turn_bound`) comes from the curve bound above, so a rational spline is judged a whole stretch at once like any other curve.

Measured on a NURBS cylinder the parameter speed bound is within 20 % of the true value and the second-derivative bound within 50 %; on a bicubic roof with the weights 1, 2, 0.5 and 3 within 36 % and 24 %. The parameter speed is now taken in the surface's own parameters, with the width of the piece: before, it was the piece's own, which is right only for a span one wide, and a B-spline face over a wider knot span got a bound that was too small by that width (found here, fixed for polynomial faces too; `the_parameter_speed_is_in_the_parameters_of_the_surface`).

The oracle (`crates/kernel/tests/boolean_rational.rs`): a rational surface can *be* a piece of a quadric or a torus, so each test solid exists twice, with its curved faces on the analytic surfaces and on the rational patches that equal them (a quarter rod, a quarter ball zone, a quarter frustum, a quarter slice of a ring with an outer and an inner face; patches that end on the trims and patches a fifth larger; circular edges as circles and as rational arcs). The twins pass `check`, measure as the closed forms to 1e-9 and mesh watertight; their three booleans against a block, a cylinder, a ball, a cone and a torus through the rational face agree with the same booleans of the analytic solid within 5.1e-8 in volume and area (360 booleans; held to 1e-6) at the scales 1e-3, 1 and 1e6, in the world's frame and tilted; tangent contact with a plane and a ball and an edge of a block on the face come out as for the analytic twin. A roof that is no quadric (the weights above) is held to a quadrature of its own and to the identities of sets, against a block, a ball, a block over a corner (which cuts the rational roof edges) and a cylinder.

*Weights far apart (2026-10-15).* A patch whose weights are a hundred apart moves a hundred times faster with its parameters near its light corners than near its heavy ones, and the winding numbers, which halved the loops by one bound on the chart's speed over the whole face, gave up ("too close to a face boundary"). On a surface drawn in its own parameters a stretch that the bound for the whole face would split is now judged by the bound near it, on every such surface (2026-10-15; the first version did so only where the pieces' bounds were more than sixteen apart): `PatchTree::sigma_near`, the least bound of the pieces that come within twice the stretch's length plus the distance of its start from the surface, which is where the feet of its points are; a piece wholly within reach gives its own bound, one partly within reach is looked at closer. It is never looser than the bound for the whole face. Held in `boolean.rs` › `what_is_not_supported_says_so`: the plate with its top as a bilinear patch with the weights 1, 100, 100, 1 against a ball at a corner of its bottom and at a corner of its top (which cuts the weighted face), union, difference and intersection, each the same body as the same boolean of the plain block (400 seeded points classified by ray casting, `boolean::contains_points`, against the block's result and against the exact membership of block and ball; the same face counts). *Measured since 2026-10-17* (`topo::mass_properties`; the method is in [`topology.md`](topology.md) › *Rational weights far apart*): the plate with weights 1, h, h, 1 to 1e-12 for h up to a thousand, and the analytic twins with weights up to ten thousand apart per span to 1e-9, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6; the booleans of the plate with weights a hundred apart above are now held to the volume, area and centroid of the plain block's too. Two causes, two general methods. (1) Where weights have a product form (`w_ij = a_i b_j`: every surface of revolution, and the twins skewed by a Möbius reparametrisation of their spans), a reparametrisation takes the unevenness out: each inner integral is summed in the parameter of the Möbius map that makes the weights at its stretch's ends equal (exact; a change of variable). Without it a cylinder skewed a thousandfold along its axis *settled 7e-6 short*, because the inner panels stopped doubling at 64 a stretch while the boundary's went on to 128, so the last two sums had the same inner error (fixed: they double to the last). (2) Where no reparametrisation helps (the plate: `w₀₀ w₁₁ / (w₀₁ w₁₀) = 1 / h²` in every parametrisation), the boundary integrand gathers whole strips of the face on a stretch `1 / h²` of an edge long next to a heavy corner, and doubling every panel ran out of doublings (the earlier diagnosis is right, the corner is the heavy one); the panels are now halved only where their own halves disagree, twenty times there for h = 1000 and not at all elsewhere. The plate at h = 1e4 (a stretch 1e-8 long) is refused: its sums scatter at the rounding of the boundary's parameters; weights further apart than 1e4 after evening (what no reparametrisation removes) are refused before anything is summed, since from 1e7 the sums settled wrong. *Refused until 2026-10-17* (see the next paragraph): weights a thousand apart in the booleans, by the winding numbers ("too close", where the face's boundary passes near a heavy corner) and by the surface intersection ("tangent or nearly coincident over too large an area": a ball through the weighted face at a light corner, at a heavy corner already with the weights a hundred apart, and the twins skewed a thousandfold), and more than a factor of a thousand apart within a span at the tree ("rational … weights"). Both came from bounds taken over too much of the surface at once: the tree's bound on the Jacobian's least singular value pairs the shortest normal of a piece with its longest derivative, and next to a heavy corner the Jacobian changes by a factor of 150 within one leaf (h = 1000: bound 9.4e-6 against a true 3e-3), and the marcher's cells were classified with the derivative bounds of the whole surface.

*A thousand apart in booleans (2026-10-17, `geom/src/intersect/field.rs` › `Pieces`, `march.rs` › `rescaled`, `between`, `geom/src/nurbs.rs` › `NurbsSurface::evened`, `boolean/freeform.rs` › `settled_sigma`, `finer`, `evened`, `topo/src/mass.rs` › `net_change`).* The two causes above were confirmed, and behind them three more; each has a general method, and each applies to every freeform surface (a polynomial one included), where it changes nothing but cost unless the old bound was the one in the way.

- *The marcher's cells take the bounds of the pieces under them.* A freeform chart keeps its derivative bounds piece by piece (`Pieces`): from each Bézier span, a piece is split where a split tightens a bound on a first derivative by more than half again (`PIECE_GAIN` 1.5, the gain of halves along u, along v, or of quarters per halving), the pieces that gain most first (a heap, so that the budget of 8192 pieces goes next to the light end of a span rather than evenly; breadth first it ran out on the rod skewed ten thousandfold along its axis, and halves alone missed the plate's heavy corner, where only quarters gain). A node holds the larger of its leaves' bounds where that is tighter than its own. `Chart::bounds_in` takes the largest bounds of the pieces that meet a cell, and the bounds of the whole surface where those are tighter or the cell reaches past the domain. The grid, the floor cell and the borders keep the bounds of the whole surface. Every piece's bound is a proof over it, so the rule for splitting (its gain, its budget) decides only what a cell costs, never whether a bound holds. The pieces of a surface are kept on a shelf of 32 surfaces, as the projector is: on the benchmark the turbofan's 1312 charts took 0.93 s of pieces made anew and take 0.08 s, the impeller's 714 0.58 s and 0.06 s.
- *A step across a jump in the parametrisation's speed* (`Marcher::rescaled`). Where two spans of a spline meet and are drawn at paces a hundred apart (the twins skewed along u: each span is reparametrised on its own), the step predicted from the derivatives on one side goes a hundred times too far or too short however short it is made, and the march stalled at the knot. Where halving has run out, the parameter step is scaled to the length it went and, once one went too short and one too far, halved between the two (48 times at most), until a step lands within half to twice its length, ahead, turning less than the march allows, on the field and in the chart. A rescaled step is taken only where the middle of its parameters corrects onto the curve near its chord (the parameters may have moved by orders of magnitude, and Newton could land on a branch close by), and a loop that closes within it closes there, by the same test as after any other step (review, 2026-10-17: without it a loop could be marched around twice; `meet_fixes.rs` › `a_circle_across_a_knot_where_the_speed_jumps_is_marched_once`). A point between two of a branch (`between`) whose middle in parameters is next to one of them, or from which Newton does not settle, starts from the foot of the middle of the chord instead, and is taken only within half the chord of its middle. All of these apply only where the old rule had given up, so every curve marched before is marched as before.
- *The winding numbers' bound is settled where it is slack, and asked locally.* A leaf whose bound on the least singular value is more than four times (`SIGMA_SLACK`) below the least of the values measured at its corners and its middle is halved, the way that raises the lower half more, until it is not (256 pieces a leaf at most, 16 halvings each way), when the tree is built (`settled_sigma`): with the weights ten thousand apart along a span, a leaf next to the light end had no bound at all (the normal is bounded through the cube of the heaviest weight, a factor of 3e7 within the leaf) and the surface no parameter speed ("stalls or folds"). And `sigma_near` asks a leaf's pieces (`finer`), lowest bound first: the lowest is split, and the split kept, where its box is more than four times the reach across (a narrow fan of iso-curves from a heavy corner has a wide box: next to the plate's light corner every leaf's box reached a boundary point it is nowhere near, and the bound there was 2000 times below the truth), or where its bound is four times below the largest value measured on it (the least, for a piece wholly within reach); the first that cannot be split is the answer. A split kept from an earlier question is looked into only where this question would make it, and the store (16,384 pieces a surface, about 11 MB for a bicubic one) is emptied when full, so the answer is a function of the question alone, whatever was asked before (rule 5: `freeform.rs` › `the_bound_near_a_point_does_not_depend_on_what_was_asked_before`). As with the marcher's pieces, `SIGMA_SLACK` and `FINE_REACH` decide how much is split, not whether the bound holds. The plate a thousand apart with a ball at its light corner: 9 s and 240,000 steps with a slack rule alone, 80 ms and 4000 steps.
- *A chart drawn evenly.* Winding numbers are of points in space, so any parametrisation of the same point set is a chart of it. A NURBS surface's chart is now that of `NurbsSurface::evened` (where it differs; its tree on the shelf of trees like any other, so that its bytes are counted): split into Bézier spans, each span of u and of v reparametrised by the Möbius map that brings the weights at its ends together (the geometric mean over every row of the net across it, so that the map is one of the parameters alone), a change of weights only; held in `nurbs.rs` › `an_evened_surface_is_the_same_surface_drawn_by_moebius_maps_span_by_span`. Where the weights have a product form (every surface of revolution; the twins skewed span by span) that is as even as the weights allow; the plate (`w₀₀ w₁₁ / (w₀₁ w₁₀) = 1 / h²`) is left as it is. Without it a boundary running along a knot between spans drawn a thousand times apart took the bound of the fast side: the frustum skewed a thousandfold both ways took 20 to 40 s a boolean (25,000 steps a stretch), now 0.1 to 1.5 s.
- *The cap of a span* (`MAX_WEIGHT_RATIO`) is two million, not a thousand: what is held, 1.4e6 (the twins a thousandfold both ways); refused by the cap: 1.4e8. The booleans and the measure part here: a boolean takes weights up to that cap within a span, `topo::mass_properties` refuses weights further apart than 1e4 after its Möbius evening (its sums settled wrong from 1e7, [`topology.md`](topology.md) › *Rational weights far apart*), so a boolean of a face skewed beyond what evening removes may be built and its result not measured.
- *The measure* (`topo::mass_properties`), for the plate's quarter disc cut at a heavy corner a thousand apart: the plan decided from the coarsest quadrature of `∮ dv` that the boundary "goes around in v" (its parameters move so unevenly there that the sum was off by more than the domain); how far the loops go around is now taken from where the samples are (`net_change`), unless they jump across a parameter whose closure is not known on a surface whose two borders there are not proven further apart, as point sets, than twice the distance a boundary point may be off its surface (`certify::curves_apart`: a seam the closure test may have missed; derived, and erring only towards the old rate sum or a refusal; a quick excursion over an unseen seam and back between two sampled places is the known limit): then the sum of the rates, as before, and a refusal where the inner integrals run along that parameter. See [`topology.md`](topology.md). And the face needed a little more than the budget (`BUDGET` 2^27, was 2^26; it settles in 0.1 s).

Measured (release, the whole set of `boolean_rational.rs`'s twins against the five tools of each, three ops, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6, weights skewed along u, along v and both): ratio 10, 100 and 1000, 1620 of 1620 booleans agree with the analytic solid's within 1e-6 in volume and area (worst 9.1e-8; held in the tests with the centroid to 1e-5 of the scale, the shells and `check`); a boolean takes 0.02 to 1.5 s, a skewed group of fifteen 2 to 11 s. Ten thousand along u: all built; along v (a degree-one profile) 9 of 15 refused for the rod and 6 of 15 for the frustum at each scale ("tangent or nearly coincident over too large an area": more than a million cells), both ways refused by the cap. Held in `boolean_rational.rs` › `a_*_with_weights_far_apart_against_tools` (two tools per ratio and direction in turn, not meshed: the tessellator's grid is even in the parameters and it does not mesh every face drawn this unevenly, open in `tess`) and `boolean.rs` › `what_is_not_supported_says_so` (h = 100 and 1000, four balls). Meshed only at ten apart in the tests: from a hundred the tessellator does not mesh every such face. *Open:* the meshes of unevenly drawn faces (in `tess`), ten thousand apart along a degree-one direction (the cells), the plate at ten thousand. (A rational face on the surface it equals, a patch or an analytic one, wholly over it or not: built since 2026-10-14 and 2026-10-15, see the table above.) (A patch with a collapsed border was refused here until 2026-10-14: see the next section.) *Cost* (the optimized test profile, four at a time on a quiet machine): a boolean with a rational quadric face takes 35 to 420 ms (medians per pair of solids 45 to 250 ms, a block the slowest tool) where the analytic twin takes 0.4 to 14 ms (closed forms against marched splines and nearest-point searches, as for any freeform face); the weighted roof against a block takes 1.5 s where the polynomial roof takes 0.2 s, because its parameters really move four times as fast and its second derivatives really are twelve times as large, and the chart (one speed for the whole face) and the proofs pay for both. The polynomial roof itself got 15 to 30 % faster by the parameter speed taken in the surface's parameters (0.28 s before).

**Freeform faces with a pole (2026-10-14, `geom/src/pole.rs`, `boolean/freeform.rs`, `chart.rs`).** A border of a patch that is one point is where the parameters stall: the derivative along the border is zero, `∂u × ∂v` has no length, and the parameter rectangle is no chart (a loop through the pole is not closed in it, and nothing bounds how fast the parameter along the border changes nearby). The surface itself is a surface there: smooth when it closes up smoothly (a dome), a cone-like point when it does not (a drop's tip). Every place that read the surface through its parameters is listed here with what it does now; a face without a pole takes the code it took before in each of them.

- *The chart* (`PatchTree::chart`, `chart_speed`). The parameter rectangle is drawn as the surface is. With `s` and `t` the shares of the domain along and across the poles' borders, the point goes to `(ω(t) (s − ½), t)`, with the width `ω = t` for a pole at the start, `1 − t` at the end and `t (1 − t)` for both: a wedge, or a lens between two points. A pole is one ordinary point of it, the map from the surface is one to one and continuous, and it turns the way the parameters do, so the winding number of the loops is what it is on the surface. Its speed is bounded, not sampled: a step `(ds, dt)` moves the chart by the shear `[1 k; 0 1]` of `(ω ds, dt)` with `|k| = |ω′ (s − ½)| ≤ ½` (norm at most 1.2808; 1.29 is used) and the surface by `E ω ds + F dt` with `E = S_s / ω`, `F = S_t`, so the speed is at most 1.29 over the least singular value of `[E F]`, which comes from an upper bound on `|E|² + |F|²` and a lower bound on `|E × F|` as before. `E` and `E × F` are bounded piece by piece of the tree: away from a pole by the piece's nets over the least and the greatest `ω` on it (`ω` is concave); next to a pole, where `S_s` and the normal vanish as `ω` does, by the Bézier nets of `w² S_σ` and `w³ (S_σ × S_τ)` *divided by the piece's own parameter* (`B_j^n(τ) / τ = (n / j) B_{j−1}^{n−1}(τ)`: a net whose border row is zero is the net, one degree lower, of the quotient; `geom`: `RationalBezierPatch::weighted_derivative_nets`, new, and `weighted_normal_net`). A border row counts as zero below 1e-9 of the net's longest vector (a border collapsed up to rounding is collapsed, as the measure and the detection take it). Measured, the bound is 1.4 to 2.5 times the fastest the chart is seen to move (an octant, a lune, a cone, a drop, either way round; `the_chart_of_a_surface_with_poles_moves_no_faster_than_its_speed`, which also steps to within 1e-9 of the poles).
- *The side of the surface a point is on* (`PatchTree::signed_distance`, and the marcher's field for a freeform surface, `field.rs`). The sign is that of `(p − foot) · normal`, and at a pole the formula has no normal. For a point whose nearest point is the pole the normal is taken along the meridian that leaves the pole nearest to the direction of the point (`Pole::normal_towards`): a point a little further on, whose foot is an ordinary point near the pole, has that foot on that meridian and is on the side its normal says, and the sign cannot change on the way, since it would have to pass through zero, which takes a point straight behind that meridian, for which it is the farthest in direction and not the nearest. Where the surface closes up smoothly every meridian gives the same normal. This is an argument by continuity, not a bound; it is held against the distance to a ball (straight above and below the pole of an octant, to 1e-9) and against a cone's two sides behind its apex.
- *The directions around a pole* (`outline::outward_normal`, which `regions` sorts the curves at a vertex by, `sew` and `section`). At a pole it gives the direction the surface closes around (`freeform::pole_axes`): the mean of the unit normals the surface reaches the pole with, which is its normal there when it closes up smoothly and stands in for the axis, as at a cone's apex, when it comes to a point. Seen along it the meridians leave the pole in the order they have on the surface as long as every limit normal is on the axis's side (the direction of a meridian dropped onto the plane across the axis turns at a rate with the sign of axis · normal). That is proven, not sampled: the limit normals are, up to a positive factor, the divided normal net's border row, whose vectors hold them in their hull, and each must make an angle with the axis whose cosine is above 0.05.
- *The marcher* (`geom/src/intersect/field.rs`, `short_of_poles`). The chart of a freeform surface stops a quarter of the tolerance short of a pole, as a cone's does of its apex: the border is moved in by `tolerance / (4 L)` with `L` the proven bound on the speed across it, so everything left out is within a quarter of the tolerance of the pole and a curve that runs into the pole ends within that of it, where the vertex rule joins it to the pole. The bounds the cells are judged by are upper bounds on derivatives and hold at a pole as anywhere. The grid normals of a freeform field are the limit normals at a pole.
- *Unchanged, and why:* the boxes of the tree, the bound below the distance and the nearest point need no regular parameters; `stretch_within` compares along any path in the parameters, so its proof holds (it is only less often conclusive next to a pole).

*Handled:* a pole where the surface closes up smoothly and one where it comes to a cone-like point; one pole or one at each end of the same parameter; cuts away from the pole, around it (the pole inside the tool) and through it (the pole on a face, an edge or the curved side of the tool). *Refused by name:* poles on more than two borders across both parameters (`POLES_BOTH_WAYS`; two adjacent ones are a corner since 2026-10-15, below), a point the limit normals do not all lean one way around, or folds back over (`POLE_SHAPE`, or `IRREGULAR` when the divided nets give no bound). *Held to* (`crates/kernel/tests/boolean_poles.rs`): the octant of a ball as a rational patch against the same booleans of the analytic octant, with a block, a cylinder, a ball, a cone and a torus away from the pole, around it and through it (the pole on a block's face, on a block's edge, on the side of the others), in the world's frame and tilted, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6: 288 booleans, within 3.7e-7 in volume and area (held to 1e-6), none refused since 2026-10-15 (the torus through the pole, tilted, at 1e6 was refused "imprint lost": see *The helmet's findings* below); the dome and the drop of `kernel::revolve` against the same kinds of tools by the identities of sets (1e-6) and, for planes across the axis and through it (through both poles, through the drop's point), by closed forms from the profile (1e-7). Every result passes `check` and is measured; every result is meshed, except eight kinds of cut through a pole that the tessellator declines ("segments per side") or leaves with a gap, pinned in the test's `UNMESHED` with a reproducer for `crates/tess` (meshed since the same night, §6 *Poles of spline surfaces*; the list is empty). *Cost:* 75 to 280 ms per boolean with the octant's patch (the test profile), as for any rational face.

*Cusps (2026-10-15).* A spline that leaves the axis along it sweeps a needle: its width grows as the square of the distance from the point, not in proportion, so the meridians leave the point all along one line and not in a fan. `S_s` vanishes there to the second order (the net of `w² S_σ` divides by the parameter across twice: `pole_order`), and the wedge is drawn with that width, `ω = tᵐ (1 − t)ⁿ` to the orders of its poles: `E = S_s / ω` is bounded again (the nets divided `m` times), the shear is that of `|ω′| ≤ max(m, n)` (1.634 for a cusp, 1.618 with a hundredth of margin), and a surface that closes on itself is a disc of radius `tᵐ` (speed `√(m² + 4π²)` times the tree's). The direction the surface closes around at a cusp is the line its meridians leave along, on the side its normals lean to as they leave (the next row of the divided normal's net: at a cusp the limit normals are square to the line, all around, as at the apex of a cone of no angle). Where every piece that leaves a vertex leaves it along the normal of its tangent plane (at a cusp, all of them), the regions put them in order a little further along (`untie`, all of them at once). A cusp at either end of a surface with a pole at each (an egg whose chart is a sphere) is refused (`POLE_SHAPE`). Measured: the speed bound of the wedge and of the disc against the fastest seen (`the_chart_of_a_surface_with_poles_moves_no_faster_than_its_speed`: 2.29 against 0.57; the closed charts' test: 6.58 against 0.74); `boolean_poles.rs` › `a_cusp_is_an_operand`: a needle against a block around its point, a slab away from it, a ball, a leaning block and a half-space across the axis, at three scales: closed forms from the profile where the tool cuts across the axis (1e-7), the identities of sets, 400 seeded points against the exact membership, every result meshed. (A face that holds the axis through the point was refused until 2026-10-17: next paragraph.)

*A cusp against a face that holds its axis (2026-10-17, `geom/src/pole.rs` › `narrowing`, `geom/src/intersect/field.rs` › `short_of_poles`, `Chart::bounds_in`, `march.rs` › `on_into_cusp`).* A plane through the needle's axis, the two faces of a block whose edge is the axis, or a cylinder with the axis on its side were refused "surfaces that touch along a curve": the needle is walked in its parameters, and next to the point the distance to such a face is `f ≈ ρ(t) g(θ)` with `ρ ~ c t²`, so `f` and its gradient vanish to the second order all along the border the chart stopped at (`tol / (4 L)` from the point, where the needle is `1e-14` wide), the cells there are neither empty nor simple, and the critical point found is flat. Three rules, each for any freeform surface with a pole of order two or more (`pole::narrowing`: the order is how many times the net of `w² S_σ` of every span at the pole divides by the parameter across, as `freeform::pole_order` counts it; the divided net `N` bounds `|S_σ| ≤ β^k |N| / w²`) and any other surface, and only where the other surface passes within the tolerance of the point *and holds the strip that would be left out* (`holds_the_strip`; elsewhere the chart stops as at any pole, which keeps a plane across the axis near the point cut where it is): (0) *The other surface holds the strip.* Every point of the iso-curve at `β` is within `r(β) = ½ width β^k` of the meridian `A` through the middle of the border, and the other surface's distance `F` is 1-Lipschitz; the strip is left out only where `|F(A(β))| ≤ r(β) + tol` from the tolerance around the point all the way to the border (walked by halving stretches across, each settled by its middle and the speed across, down to an eighth of the tolerance; at most 100 000 samples). And the other surface must cross every iso-curve of the strip the same number of times, from the border down to the tolerance around the point (`same_crossings`: iso-curves a tolerance apart in space across, each sampled half a tolerance apart by the bound on its length, the sign read where it is above its rounding; at most 2 000 000 samples): no piece of the intersection then begins or ends inside the strip, and every piece in it crosses the border, where the marcher finds it. (Found in the second review: within the strip's width of the meridian a cylinder or a ball of radius 1 through the point that crosses the axis again at 4e-3 meets the needle in a closed loop from 2.7e-3 to 8e-3 wholly inside the strip; the needle less it came back whole, the tip through the tool by 2 tolerances. A piece thinner than the sampling, a tolerance across, is still missed.) (Found in review: without it a ball of radius `c` about the axis through the point, swallowing the tip up to `2 c`, met the needle in nothing, and the needle less the ball came back whole with its tip through the ball, valid by `check`. The other way, `|F| > r + tol` all along, cannot hold for a surface through the point.) (1) *The chart stops where the surface is wide.* The border moves in to where every iso-curve along it is at most `CUSP_WIDTH` long by the bound: `ρ > TOUCH tol / sin φ` is where the marcher tells a crossing at the angle `φ` from a touch (`touches`: `|∇f| ≤ TOUCH tol |H|`, `TOUCH` = 4, with `|H| ≈ 1 / ρ` across a cusp), a whole turn of that is `2π TOUCH tol`, and the bound is at most four times the iso-curve (`WIDTH_SLACK`, held in `pole.rs`), so `CUSP_WIDTH = 4 · 2π · 4 tol ≈ 100 tol` for crossings at a right angle; a crossing at the border shallower than the slack allows is refused as a touch (on the needle at scale 1, `t = 1.4e-3`). With `s` the share of the way around and `t` of the way across, the way around is an angle `θ = 2π s`: closed one way, the annulus `(1 + t) e^{iθ}`; with a pole at one end, the disc `t e^{iθ}` (or `(1 − t)`), the pole its centre; with a pole at each end (a ball), `z = t e^{iθ} / (1 − t)` puts one pole at the origin and the other at infinity and `w = 1 / (z − z₀)` sends a point `z₀` clear of the loops to infinity instead (the poles and twelve places between them are tried, the one furthest from the loops taken; refused within 1e-3 of the surface's size), so both poles are ordinary points and the face may contain the point left out, as on a sphere; closed both ways (a torus), cut open along a line of constant `t` (or `s`) that the loops do not cross, found as for the analytic torus by following each boundary piece through the nearest point, and `(1 + (t − cut mod 1)) e^{iθ}`. The direction between two images is never computed through a far point: for the ball, `(a_q b_p − a_p b_q) conj(A_p) conj(A_q)` with `a = t e^{iθ}`, `b = 1 − t`, `A = a b₀ − a₀ b`, bounded and continuous through the pole at infinity of `z`. The speed is bounded from the tree's bound on the Jacobian: in the shares, `4π` times it for the annulus and the torus (`r ≤ 2`), `√(1 + 4π²)` and `√(4 + 4π²)` times the wedge's bound (`wedge_sigma`, `ω` the width that vanishes at a pole) for the disc and the ball, where the derivatives of the direction along `s` vanish as `ω` does. Measured, the bounds are 4 to 13 times the fastest the chart is seen to move, across the seam too (`chart.rs` › `the_chart_of_a_closed_surface_moves_no_faster_than_its_speed`). Refused by name: a surface that closes into a point along the same parameter it closes in, or closes both ways and into a point (`CLOSED_POLE`); a torus-like face whose boundary crosses every line both ways (`NO_SEAM_CUT`); a ball-like face whose boundary comes near every point tried (`NO_DROP`).
- *The surface intersections* (`intersect/field.rs`, `march.rs`). Such a surface is a periodic chart, as a cylinder is: the quadtree wraps its cells, and a branch is marched over the seam with its parameters taken around into the domain where the surface is evaluated (`Chart::at`, and the start of the projector's search). A curve around the surface closes, and one across the seam is one curve. A border across the way around that lies on the other surface (a rim on a plate) is reported as a closed curve; one that is a pole gives none.

Besides: a face without loops on a surface closed both ways, or one way with a pole at each end, is the whole surface, as on a sphere or a torus (merge may join pieces into one, `freeform::borderless`); the fixed points looked at inside such a face are taken in the shares of the domain. *Held to* (`crates/kernel/tests/boolean_closed_twins.rs`): a NURBS can (with and without a seam edge, the seam in two places, the turn in u or v), ball, cup and torus, each one face on a rational surface that closes, against the same solid on the analytic cylinder, sphere and torus, with blocks, cylinders, balls, cones and tori that cross the seam, cut through both poles, around the axis and across the tube; the three booleans at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6, in the world's frame and tilted: 594 booleans, all built and valid, within 7.9e-8 of the analytic twin in volume and area (held to 1e-6), the identities of sets to 2.4e-8 (held to 1e-7), and `(A − B) ∪ (A ∩ B)` built and measured as A. Six results through both poles of a ball cut along its seam are not measured by `topo` (named there) and are held to their twins by 4000 sampled points each; 77 results are declined by the tessellator (listed by the test). The vase of `boolean_closed_freeform.rs` (a Bézier profile turned, the seam inside the half, on the cut and outside it) is cut in half at three scales, within 9.1e-9 of half its volume. *Cost:* 0.13 to 0.4 s per boolean (the test profile), as for other rational faces. *Not done:* knots that are not clamped (a closure the net does not show).

**The helmet's findings (2026-10-15, `geom/src/intersect/field.rs`, `march.rs`, `geom/src/pole.rs`, `boolean/section.rs`).** A shell turned from two splines and cut by lofted frustums (the Voronoi helmet; `crates/kernel/tests/boolean_helmet.rs`) found six mistakes, all where two freeform faces meet. Five are in how a freeform surface on the *field* side of the marcher (the surface measured against, not the one walked) was read: a lofted side whose corners are rounded is a bilinear patch, not a plane, so the shell is the field (with a planar tool the shell is walked and none of these happen). The sixth is in the bound below the distance to a freeform face:

- *Curvature at a pole.* The projector estimates the curvature of a freeform surface from how fast the normal turns between neighbouring nodes of its 25 × 25 grid. Nodes on one pole are one point, apart by rounding (1e-14 at a size of 100), each with the limit normal of its own meridian; their difference over that distance read as a curvature of 1e7 per unit length, every cell within two grid steps of the pole was too bent to be simple, and the boolean refused "tangent or nearly coincident over too large an area". Nodes on one pole are no longer compared.
- *The nearest point next to a pole.* Newton's step for the nearest point cannot turn from one meridian to another at a pole (the derivative along its border is zero), and next to it the step along the border is ill-conditioned: from the pole node, or by a step that clamps onto the border, it kept to the meridian it started on and stopped at the pole or on that meridian, for a point a millimetre off the pole a millimetre from the surface. A search that ends within one grid row of a pole is done again from the meridian that leaves the pole towards the point (`Pole::meridian_towards`), first along that meridian, then in both parameters, and the nearer foot is kept.
- *Pieces between the field's borders.* Past a border of a freeform surface the field goes on (the distance to the nearest point, on the border), and the quadtree sees only that: a piece of curve between two borders of the field (a frustum's side cutting a corner off a half of the shell, between the half's meridian and the vent's rim) lay in simple cells whose every edge seed was past the field's edge, and was lost ("imprint lost"). The marcher now also seeds where a border of a freeform field that is an edge (not a seam, not a pole) crosses the walked surface: along each such border, bisection on its parameter wherever the distance to the walked surface could be zero (it changes no faster than the border moves; the finest piece is sixteen tolerances, and at most 4096 pieces per border), and a root that is on the walked surface gives a seed.
- *A pole border is no curve.* A border of the walked patch that lies on the other surface is reported as a curve; a border that is one point (the pole of an octant on an open patch) was reported as a curve from the pole to itself, a few tolerances long in the chart's parameters, which on the other face hid the arc that divides it. It was already left out where the border runs around a closed surface; now everywhere.
- *A loop through a cut place.* An end of a curve next to a place where an edge meets a surface is that place, and the place is found on the curve once. A curve that begins and ends there (the figure eight in which a torus meets the plane it touches at its inner equator, through the octant's pole) had the place at its start only, and the lobe back into the face was dropped. An end whose place was found at the other end of the curve is now that place itself.

- *A bound below the distance that is one* (`boolean/freeform.rs`, `PatchTree::bound_by` and `refine`). Asked up to a threshold (does the distance exceed it?), the bound stopped as soon as the nearest piece looked at so far was under the threshold and returned that piece's distance; a nearer piece not yet looked at could undercut it, so between the threshold (1.001 times what is asked) and what is asked the value was no bound. A ray through a lofted frustum was proven clear of the side it leaves by (its distance there 0, the value returned 1.0006 tolerances), a face of another frustum, apart from it, was taken for inside and left out, and the union of the two had edges used once (the check caught it: "produced an invalid body"; the helmet's frustums from 60 mm, united). The pieces left (the heap's nearest, the boxes of those still to be split) now count. The union of radial frustums that come within a few millimetres of each other towards the centre is no tolerance case: the same mistake.

With the fourth and fifth, the octant against a torus through its pole, tilted, at 1e6 (pinned as refused in `boolean_poles.rs`) is built and agrees with the analytic octant. *Held to:* four cells of the helmet against the shell with a pole and with a vent, the three booleans at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6, valid, the identities of sets to 1e-7, and the common part against the same boolean with the cell's planar twin (its far corners the near ones scaled exactly, so that its sides are planes and the shell is walked instead) to 2e-5 (the twin's sides are 1e-5 mm off the cell's); two frustums apart, whose union is both (to 1e-12), whose intersection is empty and whose difference is the first; through the CLI, each of 70 cells cut from the shell with a pole, 71 from 60 mm and 71 from 100 mm from the shell with a vent, all built (before: 8, 1 and 1 refused), and the helmet's 71 frustums from 60 mm united and taken from the shell, valid (before: an invalid body). Unit tests in `field.rs` for the curvature and the nearest point next to a pole.

**Not done:** freeform faces whose parametrisation stalls anywhere but at a border that is one point; a lens between a freeform surface and another; speed (a boolean of a domed block and a block takes about 0.1 s, most of it nearest-point searches); a spatial index over the faces of a body (every face pair is still tested by box and by side); an untouched operand does not come back with its entities in their old order.

### The boolean's own check (2026-10-17)

The third oracle the developer asked for on 2026-10-17 (roadmap › *Oracles owed*): a cheap check after every boolean, beside `topo::check`, that the result is the boolean of its operands and not just a valid solid. `kernel::boolean::verify(a, b, op, result, tolerance, options)`; every rule holds for any two solids and any surface, and each allowance comes from the tolerance or from the measurement, none is tuned.

| Rule | Allowance, and why it is general |
|---|---|
| **Volumes.** Union: `max(V(A), V(B)) ≤ V(R) ≤ V(A) + V(B)`. Difference: `V(A) − V(B) ≤ V(R) ≤ V(A)`. Intersection: `V(A) + V(B) − V(H) ≤ V(R) ≤ min(V(A), V(B))`, `H` the box around both. `V(R) ≥ 0`. | The error bounds of the three volumes, which `topo::mass_bound` reports (new: the sum of what the integrator held each of its sums to, `1e-12` of their size plus their rounding, per face, part, panel group and whole surface, and the rounding of adding the faces), plus `2 t (S(A) + S(B))`: what the boolean decides within the tolerance `t` (coincident, touching, one vertex) lies within `t` of the boundary of an operand, and the band of half-width `t` around a surface of area `S` has volume `2 t S` to first order. The next terms (`π t²` times the edge length, `t³` per vertex) are smaller by the ratio of `t` to a face's width and are left out. A body that cannot be measured leaves the rule unchecked (`unmeasured`), never a violation. |
| **Reach.** Every vertex of R and the middle of every edge lies within `t` of the box of A or of B (union), of A (difference), of A and of B (intersection). | `t`: vertices are merged within half of it, and the boxes are conservative (`Body::face_bbox`). |
| **Out of the tool's reach the target is unchanged.** A face of A whose box is more than `t` from B's box is in R (union, difference), and so is a face of B far from A (union): a face of R on the same surface (equal, or within `t` of it at the face's boundary points with the same outward normal), on the same side, with as many loops and edges, each edge's ends and middle within `t`. For an intersection such a face is outside R, which the reach rule checks. | `t` for "far" and for each matched point. The face's box holds the face, so the face is outside the other operand; the boolean never splits it (no curve can meet it) and merges only pieces of one face or coincident faces of both, and keeps the vertices an operand had. Found through the result's edges by their middles (sorted along x), so it costs a box per operand face and no box per result face. |
| **Membership.** At 16 seeded points (SplitMix64, a fixed seed; half in the box around both, half where the boxes overlap), in R exactly when the set formula of in A and in B says so. | Points within `2 t` of a boundary are passed over (`contains_points` says nothing there); a body the ray casting does not take is reported (`unclassified`), never a violation. |

**Where it runs.** (a) Inside every boolean, rules *reach* and *far faces* (`VerifyOptions::ALWAYS`); a violation is `KernelError::WrongResult` with the first violation and their count. (b) In full in the tests: `boolean_common::solve`, `solve_parts` and `verified` (most boolean test files), `boolean_random`, and every result of the corpus (a violation is an `INVALID` outcome). (c) In full in `document.check` deep, for every boolean feature in the history (its body taken by a later feature or not), problems named by feature and, for a far face, by the operand face's persistent name; `booleansChecked` counts them. (d) In full in every boolean of a process with `CRAFT3D_BOOLEAN_CHECK=full` (a sweep of a whole test suite, fillets and shells included; for test runs only: a boolean may then be refused in one process and not in another, so no document may depend on it).

**Cost, and why only part of it always** (release, the five bench models, 1196 booleans, 46.6 s of booleans; CPU time beside the boolean's own): reach and far faces 3.9 % (face boxes 2.6 %, the matching 0.9 %, reach 0.3 %); with the 16 points 56 %; with the volumes 690 % (three mass integrations of bodies of hundreds of faces against a boolean that looks only where the tool is). Below the 5 % the developer set, the first two run always; the points and volumes are left to (b)–(d). The first matching cost 27 % on the plate with 500 holes: a far face's candidates include the plate's top face with all its loops, whose edges were evaluated for every hole; loops and edges are now counted before anything is evaluated (2.8 %).

**Found.** Nothing, over: the 1196 booleans of the bench models (every rule, before the decision); the kernel's test suite (546 tests) with the full check in the helpers and the always-on part in every boolean, and again in release with `CRAFT3D_BOOLEAN_CHECK=full` (every boolean of every operation, fillets, chamfers, shells and face operations included); the corpus, every one of its 11,988 outcomes held to the full check (digest `3f00bc8ced79a654` unchanged, 11,582 agree, 240 refused, none invalid); and 1338 boolean features of 131 showcase files of `target/work` (the weld table and the laser box among them) and the four bench scripts through `document.check` deep (the three lamp files took longer than the sweep's 15 minutes each and are not in it).

**Held to** (`crates/kernel/tests/boolean_check.rs`, at three scales): correct results of five pairs (plate and rod, cavity, overlapping blocks, ball and ring, rod and cone) pass every rule with an allowance below 1e-4 of the target's volume; seeded mistakes are caught: a far face of the target dropped (also by the always-on part), the union passed off as the difference (subtraction adds volume, out of reach, a point), the difference as the intersection and as the union, a far face's surface moved by ten tolerances, one of its vertices moved, the face turned inside out, an edge cut short, a cavity filled (the target returned as the target less a ball inside it: only a point tells), and a target grown by two allowances (subtraction adds volume). `topo`'s `the_mass_bound_covers_the_error_and_is_small`: the bound covers the error against the closed forms of the reference and trimmed solids and stays below 1e-9 of the volume.

**Open.** The far-face rule matches by box distance only: faces near the tool but outside it are not checked; the volume allowance leaves out the second-order terms of the band; a rule for the area (`S(R) ≤ S(A) + S(B)` holds, a lower bound does not) is not used.

### A shell that touches itself (fuzzer seed 5279, 2026-10-17)

**Found** (the random-design fuzzer, `wrong-self-intersection/5279`): the union of a disc (the rectangle x 0..10, y 5..10 turned once about y) and a quarter turn about z of an L on the yz plane was accepted; `verify` and the volume identities passed, and `document.check` deep found the body touching itself at (0, 5, 7.5), at every scale. **Which side was wrong: the boolean.** In closed form: the L's step is the cylinder x² + y² = 25 for z in 5..7.5, which reaches y = 5 only along its edge with the L's start face, the line x = 0, y = 5, and lies below it elsewhere (y = 5 − x²/10 to second order); that edge lies inside the disc's bottom face y = 5, the L's material below it and the disc's above. The L's lower part runs into the disc, so the union is one shell, and that shell touches itself along the line: zero thickness under the decision of 2026-10-15. `zero_thickness` only looked for touches between two shells.

**Fixed** (`within_a_shell` and `walls_apart` in `boolean/mod.rs`), with three rules:

1. *Two faces of one shell at a place where the operands meet (within twice the tolerance, not outside either boundary, their nearest points within twice the tolerance of each other) touch, unless they share a vertex or an edge within four times the tolerance of the place.* General because in a manifold every point of a shell lies inside one face, on one edge (two faces) or at one vertex (the faces round it): two faces at one place with nothing in common there are two walls of the shell. The margin for "joined" is twice that for "at"; that covers faces meeting at an edge at 29° or more (their gap at a distance d from the edge is 2d·sin(θ/2)), and for a sharper wedge or groove the margin of a shared edge grows to the tolerance over sin(θ/2), θ read from the outward normals at the edge's nearest point (`wedged`; review, 2026-10-18), capped at a tenth of the smaller face. 2. *Two such walls touch as parts of the solid only when each stays on the outer side of the other a little way off the place* (eight directions in the tangent plane at a tenth of the smaller face's box, onto the surface, inside the face; a point more than twice the tolerance inside the other's material says no, counted only where its foot on the other surface lies inside the other face: past its boundary a surface says nothing about material, and a bar turned 45° lying on a plate, its side faces under the plate's plane beyond their shared edge, was taken for a thinning wall; review, 2026-10-18). General because two tangent walls with opposite outward normals are one of two configurations that agree to first order at the place: materials on their far sides, the gap between them closing up (two parts touch: refused), or material between them thinning to nothing (a hole whose wall touches the plate's side along a line: a wall of no thickness). ### Tangent contact along an edge (2026-10-18, checklist item 2: the contact a fillet's boolean refused)

A fillet made as a boolean with its tool (the solid the fillet takes away, whose round face is tangent to the two faces it rounds along curves) was refused wherever that contact has no closed form. The cases, and what they were (`plan/fillet-contact.md`, local; `crates/kernel/tests/fillet_contact.rs`): a block's edge less the corner section extruded, with its cylinder on the rational patch that is that quarter of it and a fifth larger, so that the lines of contact run through the middle of the patch; a quarter of a rod's rim tool with its torus and side strip on such patches; the blade root's edge between two spline walls less its exact tool (the two Bézier pieces and the ball's arc, extruded flush and past both ends, the arc also on a patch); and the tools a rolling-ball fillet takes away (the body less the fillet) along a straight edge between a plane and a cone and along the rim of a lofted blade's tip. All were "boolean produced an invalid body" (edges used once); the blade root at 1e-3 also "tangent or nearly coincident over too large an area".

*Why they fell outside.* An edge of one body on a face of the other is imprinted only through a curve in which that face meets one of the edge's two faces. Here one of them is the tool's strip, which lies on the face it rounds (coincident: no curve), and the other is the round face, tangent to it along the edge: the numeric intersection settles such a contact as a string of one-sided touches (it keeps to one side of the face: nothing is divided there), not as a curve. So the edge was in neither, the face was not divided where the tool's strip ends, and the regions came out wrong. The closed forms (a cylinder on a plane: the line) and a contact along a border of the walked patch (a lying border, reported as a curve) had worked; a contact in the middle of the walked surface, or along a border of the other one, had not. At 1e-3 the contact was not even settled as touches: geometry's *A contact along a curve judged on its valley*.

*Method* (`section::lying_edges`). After every pair of faces has been met, each pair whose surfaces only touch (geometry gave points and no curve) looks for edges of either face that come within the tolerance of the other face's surface at one of nine places. Where there are any, the edges of both faces are cut where they meet the other surface (`edge_cuts`, exactly as for a pair that meets in a curve), each such edge is cut at those places, and every piece that lies on the other surface is a stretch of contact along that edge: proven, not sampled (`geom::certify`'s bound on how far a curve strays from a surface, pieces halved where it is over the tolerance, at most 4096; five places first only to turn away cheaply the edges that are not; a proof not reached is no imprint. Five samples alone took a rod's top circle for lying on a lid's underside that touches the rod's top along two lines through its centre: at 45° the circle is 0.05 off, and it was imprinted; the review's probe, `fillet_contact.rs` › `an_edge_that_touches_a_surface_at_places_does_not_lie_on_it`): its own curve, clipped against both faces by `clip` like any meeting curve (tangent, so it divides nothing it runs over the boundary of without a cut there). A piece is cut where a stretch already on the face it lies on ends on it, and each part whose middle is on such a stretch is left out; the rest is imprinted. The pass adds no stretch where an edge of neither face is in the contact: on both sides of a one-sided touch the face is on the same side of the other body, so such a contact needs no imprint for classification (zero thickness there is looked for at the touch points, as before). Only the pairs that only touch and have an edge on the other surface do any new work, so the corpus (no such pair) has the same digest `3f00bc8ced79a654` and counts (10717 + 865 = 11582, 0 mismatch, 0 invalid), and the benchmark's counts are unchanged.

*Held to* (`fillet_contact.rs`, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6): the blend's fillet and the boolean with the tool are the same body (volume within the tolerance times the area, area within a band of the tolerance along every edge, the same faces, shells, edges and vertices, the checker and the boolean's own check in full); closed forms (the block 1 − (1 − π/4) r², the rod by Pappus, a whole ring and a quarter); the rational tools against the analytic ones (tilted too for the block); the identities (the common part and the cut make up the body; the fillet and its tool share nothing; put back, the body). B less the tool a rolling fillet takes away gives the fillet; put back, the body.

*Still owed.* Where an edge touches a face tangentially (the body's edge across the end of a fillet, against the round face of its tool) the touch is placed within the length over which the edge stays within the tolerance of the face, `sqrt(2 tol r)` (the reach the test allows), not at the vertex of the other operand that is there: B less the tool a rolling fillet takes away has one short extra edge per such end (27 tolerances at scale 1 on the frustum, 48 on the lofted tip), and the test allows exactly those vertices within that reach. ### A sheet's rim that enters a face and leaves it (2026-10-21, review of the above)

**Finding.** The imprint of a boundary edge on a coincident face (`Lying::Boundary`) covered only rims wholly inside the face (a disc) or wholly outside it (a wide sheet), and rims whose ends land on the face's edges. A rim that enters the face from outside and leaves it again was not cut where it crosses the face's edges: `edge_cuts` cuts an edge where it meets the other face's surface, and the rim lies on that surface everywhere (its only cuts were its own ends), while the face's edges lie on the sheet's surface too, so no surface crossing sees the place. `clip` then found the whole rim partly in and partly out of the face and, the curve being a touch, dropped it; the face stayed whole and one sample point (`classify::on_coincident`) decided for all of it. Probes (block 4 × 3 × 1, sheets in its top's plane): a sheet over x ≤ 2 cut by luck to the right answer, its mirror image (x ≥ 2) was refused as passing through nothing, a strip from inside as nearly coincident, a block with a boss broke (`edge used 1 times`) or was refused as a tangent cut. Trims were right throughout: the sheet is divided by the solid's other faces, which cross its surface. Second, `topo::check` let a closed shell with no edges pass as a sheet (`Some(0) if edges > 0`): a whole sphere marked as a sheet checked clean, and a sphere sheet trimmed by a ball came back whole.

**Method.** `section::edge_crossings`, for every boundary edge of either face of a coincident pair against every edge of the other face near it: where on the boundary edge it comes within the tolerance of the other edge, by branch and bound on the distance from a point of the edge to the other edge's piece (`outline::nearest`), which moves along the edge by at most its speed bound (`hits::speed_bound`) times the change of parameter. A piece whose middle is further than the tolerance plus that bound times its half width is dropped, the rest halved to a quarter of the tolerance; a run of pieces that follow one another is one place: a vertex of either edge within twice the tolerance of both (as `edge_cuts` takes a hit near an end to the end), else the point of the run nearest the other edge. The places are cuts like any other (`Cut` with both edges), so `clip` divides the rim there and judges each stretch by its middle, and the face's edges are split at the new vertices as at any crossing. It works on curves alone (lines, circles, ellipses, NURBS) and on any surface the two lie on: nothing in it reads the surface's kind. New conditions, each general: *dropped* when `d > tol + L·h` (a proof for exact curves, an estimate for NURBS through their speed bound, as in the curve–surface hits); *pieces of a quarter tolerance* (the resolution of the curve–surface hits); *a shared stretch* (`ALONG` = 64 tolerances): a piece at least that long within the tolerance of the other edge at nine places lies along it, is not halved and gives no place (the two share a stretch, which their vertices bound; without it two edges along each other would be halved down to the tolerance over their whole length); crossings flatter than about two degrees (within the tolerance of each other over 64 tolerances) read as such a stretch, and their stretches are refused by `clip` as before if they straddle the face; *at most `CROSSING_PIECES` pieces* per pair of edges (2^16 since the second review below), refused beyond with the reason. ### Rims beside an edge (2026-10-21, second review)

**Finding.** `edge_crossings` dropped a piece of the rim only once its half width was under (d − tol) / speed, d its distance from the other edge: a rim running beside an edge of the face at a distance d visits about L / d pieces, and the cap (then 2^14) was reached on ordinary inputs: a rim 0.1 inside the long edge of a block 4000 long, 0.01 inside one 400 long, 300 tolerances inside the 4 × 3 block's edge, a spline arch rising 10 or 20 tolerances beyond an edge; all refused, with a reason ("within the tolerance … in more places than can be told apart") that was not true there.

**Method.** A second lower bound, from the chords: a piece of a curve lies within `turn · h² / 2` of its chord (`hits::turn_bound` bounds the second derivative; h is half the parameter range), so the distance between the chords of a piece of the rim and a piece of the other edge (exact, `section::segments_apart`: the clamped closest points of the two segments and the four end-to-segment distances), less both strays, bounds the distance between the pieces from below. The search now runs over pairs of pieces, halving the longer; two lines side by side are told apart at the first pair, two arcs as soon as their chords hug them. The middle-distance bound stays (to the other edge's piece), and is all there is where a curve has no bend bound. Cap 2^16 pairs, refused with a reason that says what happened: the crossing could not be told within the work allowed. The bounds are proofs for lines, circles and ellipses; for a rational spline the speed and bend bounds come from its Bézier nets and are estimates, so a crossing of a heavily weighted spline edge (as a STEP file may bring) could in principle be missed.

**Noted.** A disc on the top of a block whose rim lies 0.5 tolerance inside the top's edge counts as reaching that edge (one place) and cuts; 2 tolerances or more inside, the top beyond the rim is a part the sheet does not cross and the cut is refused (`NOT_CROSSED`). **Method.** Three rules, each general by construction. (1) Between its cuts a stretch crosses no edge of either face (a crossing is a cut), so it is either along the boundary, within the tolerance of it everywhere, or inside the face: a sample within the tolerance of the boundary says nothing by itself, one clear of it says inside. But a cut can be lost in that band: an edge fitted within the tolerance of its curve meets the surface at a shallow angle more than a tolerance from the meeting curve, or a hit is taken to the edge's end (`hugs` in `edge_cuts`). So `clip` reads a stretch whose three samples are `In` and `On` as inside only when, at seven places along it, every place in the band is inside by the sign of its offset from the nearest edge (`Outline::inside_by_sign`: the sign `left_of` reads a witness by, with the foot away from the piece's ends and no other piece as near), and refuses it otherwise; a stretch with a sample `Out` and another not is refused as before, one `On` at all three is along the boundary as before, and a touching curve with mixed samples is dropped as before. (2) Which side of its own loop a point is on is a question of the loop's pieces as they are, so a loop with no room at the margin gets its witness as close as a sixteenth of the margin (a quarter tolerance at the four the regions use: `outline::left_of` continues with the same passes at `margin / 16`); the candidate still steps in from a piece's stations, so with vertices two tolerances apart it is a tolerance from the piece's ends at the middle station (half that at the others), nearer to its piece than to anything else, and a winding about a point within rounding of another loop is refused (`TOO_CLOSE`); nothing else reads that point. (3) A region with no point at the margin from its boundary is classified at its boundary, which is exact where a sample is not (`classify::at_boundary`, the neighbourhood classification of the textbooks): at the middle `p` of each arc of the region along which the other body passes through the face, the direction into the region is `n × t` (the region lies to the left of its loop seen from outside); the faces of the other body that hold the arc (within the tolerance of `p` and of the arc's quarter points; a face that holds `p` and not the arc would make `p` a vertex, and is refused) are either one face through `p`, or the two along an edge of the other body that runs along the arc, with any face on the region's own surface among them. A face on the region's surface that reaches to the inward side (the direction into it from its boundary piece along the arc, `n_i × t_i`, has a positive dot with the inward direction) has the region on it, `OnSame` or `OnOpposite` by the normals as for a sampled point; otherwise the region is inside the other body when the inward direction points behind the one face, or into the wedge of the two (behind both when the edge is convex, which it is when each face lies behind the other's surface; behind either when it is concave; behind the one when they continue each other smoothly). A dot within `ANGLE_GUARD` (1e-6, the guard `regions` puts in order by) of zero is faces tangent there, not read; a wall with `p` on its boundary whose other face was not found (a fitted edge whose second face is further than the tolerance at the arc's middle) is not read either, since a half-space would read a convex edge as inside; the arcs of a region must agree (at most 64 are read), and a region with no such arc keeps the sliver refusal. This reading's "on" is a direction (a dot within `ANGLE_GUARD` of zero), where the sampled point's is a distance (within twice the tolerance of a face: `ON_A_FACE`): the reading is the limit at the arc, exact to first order, so a face at a small angle is read by its sign where a sample next to it could not be used; what it decides is a region whose every point is within the margin of its boundary, so a result read differently from what a sample would have said, had there been room for one, differs from the exact result by at most that region, inside the band the boundary already has. The sampled point stays the first method, so every result that was decided is decided as it was: of the corpus only outcomes that were refused move (below).

**Why the rules are general.** None names a configuration: (1) follows from what a cut is, with the band checked by sign because a cut in the band may be lost; (2) from what a winding number is; (3) is the exact classification of a point of the boundary, valid for any surface with a normal and any region, which the sampled point approximates. The only numbers are the ones the kernel already had (the tolerance for "holds", `ANGLE_GUARD` for "tangent", the margin), the seven places of (1), and the arc-holding test, which keeps the reading local. What a sliver *is* in the result is unchanged: the sliver of a union is merged back into the face it was cut from, the sliver of a difference stays as the tiny face the exact geometry has (the corner cut at three tolerances leaves a triangle whose vertices are 4.2 tolerances apart, which `craft3d_topo::touches` accepts).

**Held to** (`crates/kernel/tests/boolean_truss_joint.rs`, at 1e-3, 1, 1e3 and 1e6): the corner cut at 3 and 6 tolerances, the cube's edge beside a convex edge (a turned block) and a concave edge (a notched prism) of the other body at 3 and 5 tolerances (the review's probe: two walls read, both ways round, closed-form volumes), union in both orders (12 faces), difference (7 faces: the block's six and the tiny triangle) and intersection (the corner tetrahedron, 4 vertices and faces, volume δ³/6 within the integrator's bound); every pair of bars in both orders and the three bars in all six orders to the union's volume by inclusion–exclusion of the legs' strips (convex polygon clipping, closed form), the third bar cut from and kept in common with the union of the other two; every result through `topo::check` and the boolean's own check. ### Tangent contact of higher order at the boundary (2026-10-21, checklist item 2: the Garabit pier's union 127, the Perseverance's drill tip)

The pier: union 127 of 307, a stair tread (a block) whose end touches the inside of the helical stringer (a sweep along a helix) near its middle, its corners going into the stringer.

*Cause, measured.* Tilted, the positions are given to a thousandth, so the cone's axis is 3.4e-6 rad off the drill's and its base centre 3.9e-4 mm off it: the base circle is up to 3.9e-4 mm (390 tolerances) off the wall, not on it, and the curve of cone and wall crosses the base circle at two points at about `2 e h / R²` = 2.6e-5 rad. One crossing lay 1.9e-4 mm from the cone's seam vertex, so on the cone's side face the boundary piece from the crossing to the seam was 1.9e-4 long, and `untie` reads the order of rays that leave together within a quarter of the shortest of them: there the curve and the base circle are 1e-9 apart, under the tolerance, and it refused. On the pier the stringer's curve on the tread's top and bottom faces leaves the end edge along it where the end touches the stringer, and the pieces at that vertex end 0.015 and 0.0025 m further (other vertices of the imprint), where they are still within a few tolerances of the edge. In both the tie is between a piece of the face's boundary and a curve imprinted on the face, and the distance to tell them apart by geometry is longer than the pieces.

*Method* (`regions::order_run`). An imprinted arc lies in its face: it is clipped to the face and arcs do not cross between vertices (the invariant `untie` already rests on). The face lies to the left of its boundary seen from outside: counter-clockwise from the boundary use that leaves the vertex, clockwise from the one that arrives. So in a run of rays that leave a vertex together, with exactly one boundary ray, the imprinted rays go right after that ray when its use leaves the vertex and right before it when its use arrives; only the imprinted rays among themselves are measured (`untie`, as before, when there are two or more). This is topology, the same for any surfaces and placements, at any angle however shallow and any distance to the next vertex. New conditions, each with why it is general: (1) the rule applies only to a run with exactly one boundary ray: with two (a corner of no angle) the face may lie between them or around them, which topology does not say, so the run is measured as before; (2) the boundary ray's arc must be used once by the face's boundary (`Sides::once`): a seam or a slit is used twice, with the face on both sides, and says nothing of the side; (3) the cusp branch (every ray along the normal) is unchanged. **Method 1, covers** (`boolean/cover.rs`). A face's cover is a tree over its surface's parameters: each node is the box of a patch, the box of its four corners grown by `(hu² max|S_uu| + hv² max|S_vv|) / 8`, the error bound of bilinear interpolation along any direction (so every point of the patch is in it; the bounds of the second derivatives are closed forms per analytic kind), cut to the face's box grown by the tolerance (nothing of the face lies outside that box, nor does its boundary). A node is left out only when its patch lies wholly outside the face: no piece of the boundary comes within its box (each piece's distance proven, a line or a circle in closed form, any other curve by halving with its speed bound, 4,096 stretches at most and "may come near" beyond), so the boundary does not cross the patch, which hangs together and is all on one side of it, and its middle is outside the face (`Outline::side`). A node is halved along the direction in which it is longer in space, down to a 64th of the face box's diagonal or 16 levels. The first nodes (a quarter of the diagonal; a sixteenth once a face has been asked about 64 times) are made at once and kept in a tree of their boxes; below them nodes are made as questions need them. Every node is a function of the face alone. How far a question goes down depends on what was asked before (a face asked 64 times starts from finer first nodes, a pair gives up after 4,096 pairs of nodes, a tree makes at most 8,192 nodes below its first ones, which bounds a cover's memory to about a megabyte for the boolean that made it), but only towards "may meet" or "may hold", the answer without a cover: results are the same bit for bit in any order and on any thread. Freeform faces have no cover (their box stands for them, as before). Covers are used in three places, each only to skip what would have come out the same: a pair of faces whose covers are further apart than `APART` = 16 tolerances is not intersected (beyond every distance a rule of the section reads: two tolerances for faces over or touching each other, the outline's margin of four, with room to spare; a pair whose cover question takes more than 4,096 pairs of nodes is intersected); a point further than four tolerances from a face's cover is outside it, so the ray casting and the clipping of a stretch's middle do not ask the outline (`Side::Out` is what the outline says of a point further than the tolerance from the face); and an edge further than 16 tolerances from the other face's cover is not cut against the other surface (the stretches between the cuts that are left cross no boundary of the other face and hold a point outside it, so they are outside it whole and are dropped as before). New conditions, each with why it is general: the bilinear bound is a theorem for any C² parametric patch; the cut to the face's box holds because the face and its boundary lie in it; "no boundary within, middle outside" is the connectedness of a patch; the margins are tolerances beyond those the section reads. No decision reads a cover except to skip.

**Method 2, closed forms against balls and cones** (`hits::turning_points`, `levels`, `monotone`). Along a line or a circle the squared distance to a ball's centre is a quadratic, or `h + a cos t + b sin t`, which turns at its vertex, or at `atan2(b, a)` and the angle opposite; along a line the distance from a cone (`cos α ρ(t) − sin α z(t)`, less a constant) turns where `cos² α (A t + B)² = sin² α z'² (A t² + 2 B t + C)` and has no derivative where the line meets the axis. Between those points the signed distance is monotone, so each stretch is clear of the surface on one side, near it, then clear on the other side, at most: the runs come from where the distance is plus or minus the tolerance, in closed form for balls (the roots of `(R ± tol)²`, measured from the curve's point nearest the centre so that nothing cancels when the line's origin is far away) and by bisection for cones, which a monotone function needs no bound for; the cone's turning points likewise from the line's point nearest the axis. A root in closed form is used only where the distance there is the level to within twice the rounding below; anywhere else bisection finds it, so a closed form that loses digits costs time, never a crossing (review of 2026-10-22: from a line origin 1e5 radii away the root of "a tolerance inside" lay where the line was outside, and both crossings of a line dipping three tolerances into the ball were lost; `a_line_from_far_away_crosses_a_ball_or_a_cone_it_dips_into`, origins 1e3 to 1e7 radii away at three scales, failed before). The runs are then read by the rules the numeric isolation reads its own by (`hits_of_runs`, split out of it unchanged): a near run between clear runs on opposite sides is a crossing, on one side a touch, at an end of the piece a crossing only when it crosses on the way. ### Quartic turning points, covers of freeform faces, covers against the exact surface (2026-10-22, bool-speed-3)

**Quartic turning points** (`hits::quartic_turns`, `by_quartic`, `boolean/poly.rs`). Along an ellipse the squared distance to a ball's centre turns where `(b² − a²) sin t cos t − a p sin t + b q cos t` is zero, a quartic in `u = tan(t/2)` (taken on `|t| ≤ π/2` and on the other half turn, so that `|u| ≤ 1`); along a line the distance from a torus, `sqrt(D) − r`, turns where `D' = 0` (and has no derivative where the line meets the axis), whose roots are roots of `(k s + n)² (A s² + 2 B s + C) − R² (A s + B)²`, measured from the line's point nearest the axis or, where that lies beyond the piece (a line nearly along the axis), from the piece's end nearest to it. No root formula: `Poly::changes` finds where the quartic may change sign from the roots of its derivatives down (between two roots of the derivative it is monotone and changes sign at most once, found by Newton's steps kept in the bracket), and gives as well every place it looks at where the value is within its rounding of zero (each coefficient carries the size of what it was made of; 256 units in the last place of that, about six times the roundings counted). Extra places only divide the curve where nothing turns. The factor that is no turning point (`(k s + n) ρ + R (A s + B)`) only adds places; where both factors vanish (the point nearest the axis, and `z = 0` as the line turns towards the axis) a place is given. Then the same discipline as the closed forms of balls and cones: every stretch between the places is held to the distance itself (monotone at its quarters, to twice the rounding) or the pair goes to the root isolation; the places where the distance passes `±(tol + rounding)` and zero are found by false position (Illinois) and held to the distance like a closed-form root, bisection where they are not near enough; a piece whose stretches are proven clear by their middles and the speed bound (beyond two tolerances and 64 ulp of the sizes; eight halvings at most) is answered at once. A run near the surface that is no plain crossing and in which the distance changes sign (a dip of half to one tolerance through the surface) is a touch whose place is either zero, whichever a search happens on; such pieces go to the root isolation, so that they are placed as before (*Corpus regression and review*, below). *Held to:* the root isolation for 2,000+ random pairs (ellipses and balls, lines and two tori, three scales, turned; same count, kind and place within the tolerance; without the turning points the path is wrong or sent back in 100+ of them); grazing at ±0.6 and 0 tolerances touches once (and 2,400 random grazing lines and ellipses at ±3 tolerances, some ending at the place they graze, answer as the root isolation does), 3 outside misses, 3 inside crosses twice (an ellipse over a ball's top, a line over a tube's top, a line along the axis past the tube's inner equator); a line from 1e3 to 1e7 radii away dipping 3 tolerances into the tube, and an ellipse of that size dipping into a ball, cross twice, by this path (not sent back). *Speed* (release, 4,000 random pieces each, root isolation → this path): ellipses missing a ball 0.95 → 0.88 ms, meeting it 12.2 → 6.9 ms; lines missing a torus 2.5 → 3.9 ms, meeting it 44.6 → 17.0 ms; lines grazing a tube's top within 3 tolerances 154 → 8 ms (missing) and 563 → 73 ms (touching or crossing). Not bit for bit (roots by false position): the corpus digest changes; its per-pair table must not.

**Covers of freeform faces** (`cover.rs`). A freeform face is covered from the Bézier pieces of its surface, one first node per knot span: a piece lies in the convex hull of its control points (weights positive), so their box, grown by `1e-12` of the largest coordinate times the spread of its weights (heaviest over lightest) for the rounding of knot insertion and halving, holds it (some `1e-14` times the spread is what the de Casteljau steps can round; the box only grows by it); a node's halves are its piece split by de Casteljau's algorithm, cut out of the span's piece when the node is halved (a node keeps the index of its span, not a piece; the cover holds the pieces once). How long a node is comes from the longest row of its control polygon (a plane meets a Bézier curve no more often than its polygon, so by Crofton's formula the curve is no longer). A surface of more than 4,096 spans or a weight that is not positive keeps its box; a front holds at most 4,096 nodes. Everything else is the analytic cover's: nodes cut to the face's box, left out only when no piece of the boundary comes within and the middle is outside, used only to skip. *Held to:* every point of a domed block's roof (Bézier and NURBS), of the roof with a round hole through it and of the roof with a ball sunk in it, inside and along the edges, is in its cover at three scales, turned; a point of the roof's surface over the hole's middle is not (shrinking the box to the piece's corner points fails at once); unions, differences and intersections of the roof with a block under it, a ball sunk in it and a second roof crossing it are the same bit for bit with covers and without, and the block under the roof is no longer intersected (1 pair → 0).

### A spline edge that meets a surface at a shallow angle (2026-10-22, the tube clusters)

Found by bool-speed-3 (`kernel/tests/boolean_tube_clusters.rs`): four patterns of five thin tubes (radius 10, wall 0.3125) united one after the other; the third union was refused, "an imprinted face could not be divided into regions; this is a bug". Measured: the face was the bore of a tube of pattern 1, with a hole whose edge is a spline (where a tube of pattern 0 crossed it, fitted within the tolerance of the curve of the two cylinders). The tube of pattern 2 parallel to it has its axis 0.46 off the bore's, so its outer wall meets the bore at about 2.5° along two straight lines, which cross that spline. The spline met the outer wall 1.2e-6 and 8.1e-6 from those lines (a spline a fraction of the tolerance off its surfaces meets a surface at angle `a` up to that fraction over `sin a` from where the three surfaces meet); `section::clip` drops a cut further than the tolerance from its curve, so each line ran on through the hole from one side of it to the other, and the loops of the bore could not be followed (a use reached twice at the vertex where the line entered the hole). The same placement of three tubes reproduces it, and of 48 placements of a tube, a tube across its end and a tube beside the first a third of a unit off, 30 were refused before: as this bug, as a lost imprint, or as a curve partly on a boundary.

The fix (`boolean/corner.rs`, called from `section::edge_cuts`): a hit of a spline edge on a surface is taken to the point where the surfaces of the edge's two faces and that surface meet (Newton's method on the three tangent planes, from the hit, at most 32 steps), when that point is more than a quarter of the tolerance from the hit and within the tolerance of the edge; otherwise the hit stands. Every face pair that asks where that edge meets that surface gets the same point, so the vertex is one, on the meeting curve of each pair, within the tolerance of the edge. Exact edges (lines, circles, ellipses) are not touched: they lie on their surfaces, and their hit is that point already. Hardened after review (same day):
- *How far.* The point may be as far from the hit as the angle explains: four times the hit's distance from the edge's own surfaces over the sine of the angle at which the edge crosses the surface, at least 256 tolerances, at most 65536; a hit that would have to slide further is refused as too shallow an angle ("an edge on a spline meets a surface at an angle so shallow …", never an invalid body). A hit where the edge only touches the surface stands as found: there the three surfaces are tangent and meet in no point of their own, and Newton creeps along the band where all three are near to wherever its stopping rule halts (48.0 or 48.7 tolerances from the hit at a rolling fillet's end on a cone, `fillet_contact.rs`, which the first hardening broke: two points closer than the tolerance can tell apart).
- *On the surfaces.* Newton stops when the guess is within a millionth of the tolerance of the three tangent planes (what is left is rounding, which a step only magnifies where two surfaces are close to tangent: at 5.7e-4 the steps never fell below a millionth of the tolerance) or a step is that small, and the point is then measured within a quarter of the tolerance of each surface (a nearest point clamped at a patch border, or a local one, makes the tangent planes agree where the surfaces do not). The normals are made unit, so the bound on their determinant (1e-10) is free of scale.
- *In order.* A moved hit must stay on the edge strictly between the halfway points to the edge's hits on that surface on either side, so two hits never become one place or swap.
- *A crossing taken for a touch.* Two cylinders on parallel axes, the outer wall of one 1e-6 off the bore of the other (offset 0.312501 against a wall of 0.3125), cross along two lines 0.043 apart, but geometry gives one line along which they touch; imprinted as a touch, it ended at a point where the bore's hole touched its rim, and the regions were lost ("this is a bug"). A curve geometry calls a touch is now held to the rule for lenses too (`section::clip`): where the surfaces go through each other beside it by more than half the tolerance, the boolean is refused as faces at the edge of tolerance.

**Open.** No test reaches the refusal of a slide beyond 65536 tolerances: the tubes closest to touching inside are refused by the lens rule first. The tube beside a tube is held at ten offsets from 0.3124999 to 0.3126 at two places of the crossing tube: all unite and pass the full check but 0.312501 and 0.312502, refused at the edge of tolerance.

Held to: the four patterns and the 48 placements unite, each step under the full check and the boolean's own check (`verify`, volumes and seeded points), against an independent reference volume: horizontal slices of tubes whose axes lie in one plane are unions of rectangles, whose area is measured exactly (Green's theorem over the pieces of edges no other rectangle covers), integrated over the height by adaptive Simpson's rule in angles that take the square roots out of the widths; the reference holds a tube, two apart and two overlapping on one axis to their closed forms to 1e-9, and the kernel's volumes agree with it to 1e-8 (4682.08999 against 4682.08995 for the four patterns). Also 600 seeded points near the walls against exact membership, a watertight mesh, three scales and two runs bit for bit. Whether the corpus digest changes: to be run (a spline edge's hit that moved by more than a quarter of the tolerance changes the bits of its vertex, and a touch whose surfaces cross by more than half the tolerance is now refused).

### Patch trees of thin strips, and a budget for the trees of one operation (2026-10-23, the spur gear's memory)

**Finding.** `craft3d-cli check-range spur-gear.3dcraftjig --samples 16 --deep` (the Jig of an involute spur gear drawn from formulas, `sketch.curve`, patterned twenty to a hundred times) took 21.5 GB on the developer's laptop and was killed by the kernel's OOM killer, twice, taking the machine down. Reduced: one parameter, the hub's diameter. At the default (12 mm, inside the root circle) the gear regenerates in 0.1 s and 60 MB; from about 20 mm, where the hub's bottom covers the face the teeth end in, the union of the hub reads every flank, tip and root face of the teeth, and from 28 mm (wider than the tips) it took 2.3 GB and 18 s at twenty teeth (`hubD` 20 / 24 / 28 mm: 375 MB / 1.4 GB / 2.3 GB). Measured with a print in `PatchTree::shelved`: the union built 160 trees, 80 of 30 Bézier spans (40,950 nodes, 21.6 MB each) and 80 of 35 (11,935 nodes, 6.3 MB), 2.23 GB, all held at once: a boolean holds the tree of every freeform face it looks at (each `Outline` keeps its tree) until it ends, the shelf's 768 MB caps only what is kept afterwards, and each tree is capped (`MAX_LEAVES`, 32,768 leaves, about 22 MB) but their number is not. A hundred teeth make five times the faces.

**Cause.** A flank is a cubic strip along a fitted formula curve (`regen::curve_fit`: 32 spans to start with, more where the fit is refined), extruded the gear's width. A span is a hundredth of a millimetre to a tenth along the curve and the face's width (6 to 30 mm) across. `subdivide` quartered each piece until its box was a 32nd of the largest span's: the width needed five halvings, and so did the length, which needed none, so each span became 1024 leaves.

**Fix.** A piece is halved only across the direction it is long in when it is short the other way (`long_ways`: the longest row of its control net each way against half the leaf size; a node keeps two children then, `Kids`). A box holds the net and any two points of the net are joined by a row each way, so a piece whose box is above the leaf size is long at least one way: square pieces are quartered as before, and every leaf ends as small in space as before. Only in trees whose spans times `4^depth` exceed 4,096 (`HALVED_FROM`; five spans or more at the full depth, see below: at 16,384 the gear's 35-span faces, quartered four times, kept 6.3 MB trees and 300 MB on the shelf): halving every thin piece of every tree moved what smaller trees answer, and a fillet's boolean at 1e-3 (`fillet_contact.rs` › `a_straight_edge_rounded_by_its_tool`) was refused at the edge of tolerance; smaller trees are quartered as before and answer the same to the bit. The flank's trees: 30 spans, 960 leaves, 1,890 nodes, 0.9 MB. The gear with the hub over its teeth (twenty teeth, hub 30 mm): peak 57 MB and 0.4 s in release, from 2.3 GB and 18 s; eighty teeth, hub 110 mm, 30 mm wide, with `document.check --deep`: 509 MB and 7 s, where the build before was killed at a 3 GB cap; the volume assertion holds. The Jig's `check-range --samples 16 --deep` (81 points, the Jig as it was that evening, measured while every tree still halved): all pass, peak 656 MB, 5 min.

**A budget for one operation's trees** (`freeform::holding`, `HELD_BYTES`): `boolean_with`, `interference`, `verify`, `contains_points`, `classify_points` and `parts_touch` hold the trees they ask for (`PatchTree::shared`) to 2 GiB together (512 MiB on WebAssembly), each tree counted once by its nodes and leaves (`base_bytes`, a function of its surface alone, whether built then or found on the shelf, so the refusal is the same on every run). Past it the operation is refused ("the freeform faces it meets need more memory for their indexes than one operation may take") and no further tree is built. The refusal is the operation's, whatever its work made of it: some callers take a face they cannot outline as one that holds nothing (`contact.rs` › `faces_at`, `parts_touch`, `corner.rs`, the covers of `section.rs`), which would have turned it into fewer contacts or a wrong side, so `holding` marks the operation once a tree is refused and returns the refusal at the end even where its work returned `Ok` (`an_interference_past_the_budget_is_refused_not_answered_short`). What a tree remembers of the surface is remembered only where the surface decides it: an evened tree (`PatchTree::evened`, kept with the tree for every later operation) is not remembered as missing when the budget refused it, and one remembered counts against the budget of each operation that asks (`a_refused_evened_tree_is_not_remembered`); before, a refusal would have left later operations of the process with the uneven chart.

*What the budget bounds and what it does not.* It counts each tree's nodes and leaves, not what the tree remembers: its three memos (16,384 answers each) and its fine pieces (16,384 at most) are capped per tree, about 13 MB at their caps for a bicubic surface, and grow with what was asked (so they cannot be counted the same on every run). The trees of one operation can then take the budget and one tree, plus up to about 13 MB for every tree it holds where its questions fill those stores; the gear's runs stayed far below that (68 MB on the shelf for 80 trees). The shelf (768 MB) holds trees between operations on top. The budget is per thread: `document.interference` checks pairs of bodies on up to eight threads (`interference_cmds.rs` › `in_order`), each pair an operation of its own, so in the worst case eight pairs can each hold up to the budget. It is not divided by the number of threads, because a refusal would then depend on the machine's thread count; trees of the same surface are shared between the threads through the shelf. A browser (WebAssembly, one thread) refuses earlier than a native build: the same operation can be answered natively and refused in the browser between 512 MiB and 2 GiB of trees. The impeller of the showcase, the largest freeform body measured, holds about 450 MB: the browser's budget was first 384 MiB, which could have refused it there, and is 512 MiB so that it still works (the developer's decision, 2026-10-23).

*Expectations.* The boolean corpus on the commit that brought the halving and the budget (3f152e35, merged on `work` as 53a03b95): every case succeeds, 11,591 results agreeing (the floor), and the digest of all results is 119e9ff6efa87ed3, the same as before. The random digests and the benchmark's counts were not measured apart; the gate's benchmark passed.

*When trees are halved.* `HALVED_FROM` compares the spans times `4^depth`, the most leaves quartering could make, not the leaves it makes (a piece stops when it is small): at the full depth that is five spans or more, so a surface flips from quartered to halved between four and five spans, whatever its shape.

Held to: `boolean/freeform.rs` › `a_thin_strip_along_a_fitted_curve_is_indexed_by_few_leaves` (30,720 leaves before, at most 1,920 now) and `the_trees_of_one_operation_are_held_to_a_budget` (refused at the sixth tree of a budget of five and a half, a tree asked for again counted once, nothing held after); `crates/engine/tests/gear_tree_memory.rs` (the Jig's build with a hub 30 mm across: both assertions pass, the shelf at most 128 MB; measured on the Strix, 80 trees built and 68 MB on the shelf, and on the code before, 160 trees built and the shelf full, 57 trees of 804 MB).

**Open.** A body of hundreds of genuinely two-way curved freeform faces (each tree at its cap of 22 MB) is refused past 2 GiB rather than handled; the trees could be built lazily, leaf by leaf, as questions need them.

### The sea kayak's tips: a ray proven clear of a face it crosses (2026-10-27)

The sea-kayak model's `TipHalfD` (the tips' stock, an intersection of extruded formula curves, cut by the deck's cylinder: each operand a ruled spline face of degree 3 × 1 beside flat ends and sides, 5.3 m long and 3 m thick) was built, and `document.check` deep called it wrong: the point (−1864.42, −1469.54, −1233.82) outside the target, inside the tool and inside the result. Reduced to its two operands (`TipHalf3`, `CylD`), the result was right: a mesh's winding number agrees with it at every seeded point, as with the three booleans before it. The ray casting was wrong, and wrong about the tool alone (a body of six faces): the ray from that point crosses the deck's spline face 2432 mm on, and the root isolation (`hits::numeric`) proved the whole ray clear of the face at once by `PatchTree::segment_lower_bound`, which read the distance from the ray to a flat leaf it passes through as 1.5e-5 mm instead of zero. `segment_box_distance` evaluated each piece's squared distance as the expanded quadratic `qa t² + qb t + qc`; where a coordinate's offset `α + β t` is zero (the ray at the side of a box) that is `α²` less `α²` again, and with `α` the 2600 mm from the ray's start to the box the rounding is `√(ε · 2600²) ≈ 4e-5` mm, above the tolerance of 1e-6 it was compared with. A leaf of a planar piece has a box of no thickness, so the piece of the ray inside it is a point, and only that rounding stood between the ray and the face. Five per cent of the points seeded in the box of the deck's cylinder were classified wrongly; a boolean that had classified a region by such a ray would have been wrong, not refused.

The fix (general: no case, no tolerance): the squared distance on each piece is the sum of the squared offsets, each `α + β t` evaluated as it stands, at the piece's ends and at the vertex `−Σαβ / Σβ²`; the rounding is then that of the coordinates (5e-13 of the segment's length at most, measured). Held to: `boolean/freeform.rs` › `a_segment_through_a_flat_box_far_from_its_start_meets_it` (flat and nearly flat boxes 1000 units across, segments up to 5200 long through them, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6: at most 1e-11 of the scale; up to 4e-5 before), and `crates/engine/tests/boolean_findings.rs` › `the_sea_kayak_tips_against_the_deck` (the four features and the deep check; the found point outside the tool; at 1e-3, 1 and 10 times the size, intersection and difference, their volumes summing to the target's, the full verify with 2000 seeded points each, and 400 points against the ray casting and against the winding numbers of the operands' and results' meshes; at a million times the size the hull would be 5000 km against 1e-6, beyond what any geometry holds). The point bound (`box_distance`) has no such expansion.
