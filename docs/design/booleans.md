# 3DCraft design: Booleans

> Moved verbatim from `docs/architecture.md` on 2026-10-15. Section numbers (§4.1, §5.3, …) in the text are those of that file before the split; [README.md](README.md) says where each one is now.

#### Booleans as built (M7, generalised 2026-10-13)

`kernel::boolean::boolean(a, b, op, tolerance)` returns a `BooleanResult`: the body and, per face, edge and vertex of it, a source (`FaceSource`, `EdgeSource`, `VertexSource`). `boolean_with` takes `BooleanOptions` (`merge`, on by default). `boolean::contains_points` classifies points against a solid with the same ray casting the booleans use; it does not go through `mass_properties`, so a count of sampled points measures a body independently of it.

**Method** (imprint, classify, select, sew, merge; one file per step in `crates/kernel/src/boolean/`):

1. *Section* (`section.rs`). For each face of A and face of B that can meet (their boxes meet and neither lies wholly to one side of the other's surface), the curves in which their surfaces meet are asked of `meet_surfaces`, with the box where both bodies are (so what else a body holds decides where two surfaces are looked at; the box of the two faces is owed: §5.3 › *Showcase findings (2026-10-15)*). A curve is cut where an edge of either face meets the other face's surface (`hits.rs`: the closed forms of `geom::intersect`, and where there is none the roots of the signed distance along the curve, isolated by the fact that the distance changes no faster than the curve moves), and where the curve itself ends. A stretch between two cuts is kept when its middle is in or on both faces. The boolean does not know which pair of surfaces a curve came from, nor whether it is exact: a vertex is always where an *edge's own curve* meets a surface, and is then found on the stretch within the tolerance.
2. *Imprint.* Edges are split at the vertices found on them. Edge pieces and stretches are registered as arcs; two arcs between the same vertices along the same curve are one arc. An edge of B that lies on a face of A, or along an edge of A, is shared from then on, and nothing is matched by position afterwards.
3. *Regions* (`regions.rs`). A face with arcs across it falls into regions: around each vertex the arcs are sorted by direction in the tangent plane (at the apex of a cone: seen along the axis), arcs that leave in one direction are told apart a little further along, and loops are followed keeping the region on the left. An arc with the same region on both sides divides nothing and is dropped. Loops are grouped in a chart of the surface: a loop that goes around a point just to its left is an outside, any other a hole of the innermost outside around it.
4. *Classify* (`classify.rs`, `chart.rs`). One point well inside a region decides for the region: on a face of the other solid with a coincident surface, or inside or outside the other solid by ray casting (a ray that meets an edge or grazes a face is abandoned for another, twelve at most; a point within twice the tolerance of the other body is no use, and the next of up to four is tried). **Inside or outside a face is a winding number in a chart of its surface**, computed from points in space, so no seam or pole is a special place: a plane is its own chart; a cylinder is mapped conformally (angle and `exp(height / radius)` as polar coordinates, evaluated without the exponential); a cone is dropped along its axis onto a plane, which makes the apex an ordinary point; a sphere is projected stereographically from a point chosen clear of the boundary; a torus is cut open along a circle that the boundary does not cross; a freeform face has its own parameters, drawn as a wedge or a lens where a border of its patch is one point. A face on a sphere or a torus may contain the place its chart leaves out; the winding of its loops about a point just inside it says which. A face without loops is its whole surface.
5. *Select.* See the table below.
6. *Sew* (`sew.rs`). Faces connected through edges form a shell. Where more than two faces meet along an arc, faces and material alternate around it: each face that runs against the arc is paired with the next face counter-clockwise (faces tangent there are put in order a little way off the arc) and every pair gets its own copy of the edge. Where several fans of faces meet at a vertex, every fan gets its own copy of the vertex. The copies are in one place and share nothing, so every result is a manifold. A bounded face on a sphere gets a frame whose poles are clear of its boundary (a sphere is the same surface whichever way its poles point; what reads a face through its parameters is at its best that way).
7. *Merge* (`merge.rs`). Faces that share an edge and lie on one surface facing one way are one face when they are pieces of one face of an operand, or pieces of a face of A and a face of B that are coincident; every edge inside such a group goes and the loops are spliced where it was. A vertex left with two edges that continue each other along one curve goes too, unless its operand had it like that. The face that remains has the source of the lowest-numbered face of A in the group, else of B; a joined edge the lesser of its sources (an edge of A before an edge of B before an intersection). The result passes `topo::check` or the operation fails.

|---|---|---|---|---|

**Contact without overlap.** Bodies that only touch (face against face along a line, a circle or at a point; an edge against a face or an edge; a vertex against anything) have nothing in common: the union is both bodies as shells that share no entity, the intersection is `EmptyResult`, the difference is the first body with the entities it had. A curve along which two faces touch is imprinted like any other; where the result does not need it (it ends inside a face, or has one face on both sides) it leaves no trace, and where it does (a cylinder that touches all four sides of its block cuts it into four parts) it is an edge. A result whose parts hang together only by an edge or a vertex has that edge or vertex once per part.

The curves come from `geom::intersect::meet_surfaces` (since 2026-10-13; the stand-in of the first version is gone): exact where there is a closed form, splines within the tolerance otherwise. What it cannot give (surfaces tangent or nearly coincident over an area, a touch along a curve without closed form) is refused with its reason. Curve/surface hits use geometry's closed forms for lines, circles and ellipses against planes and cylinders, and for every other analytic pair the bounded root isolation in `hits.rs`, which takes a piece as given (a hit at its end is a touch) and never samples. `BooleanResult::merged` lists, per face, every face of the operands it has a piece of; `BooleanOptions::trust_operands` skips the validity check of operands the kernel itself returned.

**Tolerances.** Points within half the linear tolerance are one vertex; between that and twice the tolerance the operation refuses. The same holds for faces: two faces on one surface, or on surfaces that never cross, are the same surface within half the tolerance of each other where one is over the other, apart at twice the tolerance or more, and refused in between. Joined faces are not moved onto each other: the volume of such a result is right to the area in contact times the gap. The counterpart for faces of different kinds (a ball that dips into a plate, a torus lying a hair too deep in a block): beside every curve in which two faces cross, `section::passage` walks one surface away from the curve both ways until it is twice the tolerance from the other. Where that happens on opposite sides of the other surface the faces cross, exactly as given. Where it happens on the same side, the surface went through the other and came back: a lens thinner than twice the tolerance, whose thickness is measured; up to half the tolerance the faces touch and nothing is imprinted, above it the operation refuses. A body ten tolerances thick is a body. No decision is taken on the sign of a number that could be rounding: membership is a winding number, a root is isolated by a bound and not by sampling, and what cannot be told is refused.

**Speed (2026-10-13).** Measured by stage on a cylinder through a plate with 1024 holes (1030 faces): classification 73 %, section 24 %, both from asking every boundary piece of a face with a thousand loops about every point. Now each outline keeps a box per boundary piece, sorted along x (`outline::Boxes`), and "is anything nearer than r" looks at the few pieces near the point; in a plane a loop is not followed around a point outside its box; edges are split only near a vertex; a region is classified by one point, the others being looked for only when it does not decide; a whole face away from the other solid's box is outside it. Bit-identical results (a digest of every result of the corpus and the random runs is printed by the tests). 100 holes one by one 371 → 134 ms, 1024 holes 82 → 9.9 s, the cylinder through the 1030-face plate 269 → 47 ms, two plates of 1030 faces each 0.64 to 0.85 s per operation.

**Speed (2026-10-14): what is asked again is answered from memory.** Measured by phase on an aircraft built by commands (not in the repository: a revolved spline fuselage, lofted cubic wings mirrored, a tailplane and a fin, five unions, every face a rational or polynomial NURBS face but the end caps): building the `PatchTree`s of the operands' freeform faces about a fifth of each union (a fuselage half 7848 nodes, about 10 µs a node for its speed bound), classification and the clipping of the curves nearly half, nearly all of it nearest-point searches (a winding number about a point of a freeform face projects every end and every bisection point of the face's boundary into its chart, and the next winding number about another point does it again), `meet_surfaces` a quarter to a third, the operand check and the final check a few percent. Five memos, each a pure function remembered, so that nothing changes but the time:
- *the nearest point per tree* (`PatchTree::nearest`), by the bits of the point asked about: nearly three quarters of the searches of a union were for a point asked about before (up to 16 384 points per tree, then forgotten all at once);
- *the trees*, shared between booleans (`PatchTree::shared`, least recently used out): in a row of unions each operand's faces are on the surfaces the last one had, and the fuselage is indexed once instead of five times. *Since 2026-10-15* up to 1024 trees and 768 MiB (`PatchTree::bytes`: the nodes and the pieces the leaves keep, about 520 bytes a node); it was 32 trees and 131 072 nodes, about ten trees of a lofted blade (12 000 to 16 000 nodes each). The impeller of the showcase (a hub with six blades and six splitters, lofted, filleted and chamfered: 62 freeform faces, some 860 000 nodes) then took its trees round the shelf in the order its booleans asked for them, each pushing out the one asked for next, and every boolean on the finished body built all of them again: 124 trees for a chamfer of the two bore circles (12 s of its 12, CPU), 62 for the bore's subtraction (8 s), 68 for the union with the splitters. A boolean holds the trees of every freeform face of both operands while it works, so the shelf keeps no more than such a body takes anyway (the peak memory of the impeller's run is the same, 0.7 GB). Measured (release, CPU time, a machine other agents loaded; `craft3d-cli run` of `impeller.steps.json`): the chamfer 8 to 17 s before, 1.1 to 1.7 s after; the bore 3.7 to 8 s, 0.9 to 1.1 s; the union with the splitters 18 to 31 s, 12 to 16 s; the fan blades of the turbofan 12 to 16 s, 5 to 9 s. Every output line of both runs is the same text. `the_trees_of_a_body_of_many_freeform_faces_are_built_once` counts the trees built (`trees_built`), not time;
- *the checked bodies* (`boolean::checked`, up to 8 bodies and 32 MB written out): a union's result is checked as a result and again as the first operand of the next; written out, a body is compared in about a twentieth of what checking it costs. Since 2026-10-15 written out as its bits rather than as text (`craft3d_math::Bits`, implemented in `math`, `geom` and `topo` by destructuring every field, so a field added later does not compile until it is written out too; a list starts with its length, an enum with its variant): formatting every number of a plate with a thousand holes was a quarter of a long row of cuts;
- *the projectors* of `geom::intersect` (`Projector::shared`, 32), the sampled grid a freeform field starts its nearest-point search from, made once per surface instead of once or twice per pair of faces;
- *the numeric intersections* (`meet_surfaces`, after the closed forms: 64 of them, 2¹⁸ curve points at most): the union, difference and intersection of the same bodies ask the same questions (the three booleans of a corpus case: its slowest case, a cylinder lying on a spline roof, spent 1.1 s of each of its three booleans marching the same 700 000 cells), and so does a boolean done again on operands an edit did not touch.

A key is the thing written out in full (`{:?}`, which writes every `f64` so that it reads back to the same bits, -0.0 included, or for the checked bodies the bits themselves), never a hash, so two things are one entry only when every number of them is the same. Each memo is a `Mutex`, poisoning ignored, and nothing is computed under a lock. Besides the memos, two scans that looked at every node of a projector's 25 × 25 grid per sample (the nearest node, and the curvature near a point) look only at the nodes in a window along x, from a list of the nodes in order of x: a node whose x alone is further than the best so far is further whatever rounding does, so the answer is the same node, ties to the first in the grid's order included (held to the full scan at three scales, at a pole where nodes coincide, and halfway between nodes); and the leaf of a tree gives its own box to a bound query instead of one made again. On the aircraft the five unions went from 3.8 to 1.2 s (CPU time per union, the least of three runs each, on a loaded machine; the two ran in turn); what is left is `meet_surfaces` (about half: the quadtree that finds every branch, some 27 000 samples of the fuselage's field for the two curves of a wing root), classification (a fifth) and the checks. Bit for bit the same: the verbose corpus output of every one of its 12 097 outcomes, its digest, the long random runs, and `craft3d-cli info` and the STL and OBJ meshes of every showcase document and of the aircraft. Not taken, because they would give other (equally valid) curves: asking `meet_surfaces` about the box where the two faces are rather than where the two bodies are; tighter derivative bounds or a cell budget from curvature in the marcher's quadtree; a step from curvature where it marches; a box tree of the field surface that settles empty cells without a nearest-point search (a third of the samples are empty cells, but nearly all within four times the cell's reach of the surface, where a box bound proves little).

**Freeform faces (2026-10-13, `boolean/freeform.rs`).** A Bézier or B-spline face is indexed by a `PatchTree`: the surface as Bézier pieces (`geom`: `NurbsSurface::bezier_spans` by knot insertion, `RationalBezierPatch` with de Casteljau splitting), each quartered five times, with the box of every piece's control net (the piece lies in it). The tree answers what the booleans ask of a surface: a bound below the distance from a point to the surface that never overestimates (the distance to the nearest box, split further on the spot to a quarter of the tolerance or of the distance), which is what calls a stretch of a curve provably clear of the surface in the root isolation of `hits.rs`; the nearest point, by Newton's iteration (second derivatives from differences of the first, steps held to the domain's sides) from the middle of the nearest piece and from every piece whose box is nearer than the best point found; the signed distance; and a bound on how fast the parameters change per length travelled on the surface (the smallest singular value of the Jacobian, from the Bézier nets of the derivatives and of the normal field), which the chart of the face needs: a freeform face's chart is its own parameter rectangle, reached through the nearest point. A `Sheet` (a surface with its index) is what the helpers of `outline`, `hits`, `section`, `classify`, `regions` and `chart` take; an `Outline` builds the tree for a freeform face once and shares it with the pieces of the face. The hull of a freeform face includes its control net. Where a freeform surface meets another, the surfaces count as crossing (no lens is looked for). A NURBS surface or curve evaluated beyond its domain continues the end span's polynomial, as a Bézier does, because the marcher of the surface intersections needs something to correct onto past a border. Since the evening of 2026-10-13 the root isolation (`hits.rs`, `numeric`) judges a whole stretch of a curve against a freeform face at once wherever the curve's bend is bounded (lines, circles, ellipses and splines, rational ones included, whose second derivative is bounded through the nets of their Bézier pieces: `NurbsCurve::derivative_bounds`): a stretch on the surface is proven to stay within the tolerance from the distances at its ends and a bound on the surface's second derivatives over its domain (from the Bézier nets: the difference between curve and surface along the parameter path between the feet stays within an eighth of the bend, `PatchTree::stretch_within`); a stretch beside it is proven clear by a lower bound on the distance from its chord to the surface (`segment_lower_bound`; the distance from a segment to a box is exact, one convex quadratic per side crossing) less how far the curve can leave the chord. The tree bounds its pieces by boxes in the piece's own frame (as thick as the piece bends, not as it tilts: a tilted piece's axis-aligned box is as thick as the piece is wide, which left a curve at 1.06 tolerances from a face unprovable), takes a threshold above which nothing matters, and the point bound is only asked where it can prove something. The slowest corpus case (a contact of two spline-roofed blocks, 200 s per boolean) takes 0.19 s.

**Rational faces (2026-10-13, late).** A face on a rational Bézier or NURBS surface (weights that are not all one) is an operand like a polynomial one. A rational patch lies in the hull of its control points just the same, so the boxes of the tree, the bound below the distance, the nearest point and the signed distance needed nothing. What was polynomial-only were the three bounds that came from derivative nets, and each now has a proven counterpart in `geom` (`nurbs.rs`; the arguments are in the doc comments):

- *First and second derivatives* (`RationalBezierPatch::derivative_bounds`, `NurbsCurve::derivative_bounds`). With `S = A / w` (weighted points over weight, both in Bernstein form), `S_u = (A_u − S w_u) / w` and `S_uu = (A_uu − 2 w_u S_u − w_uu S) / w`. `A_u − S w_u` is a Bernstein form whose coefficients are `m (w₁ (P₁ − P₀) + (w₁ − w₀) (P₀ − S))` over neighbours, and `S` lies in the hull, so `|P₀ − S|` is at most the reach of `P₀` across the box of the net; `w` is at least the lightest weight; the second derivatives likewise from second differences of `w (P − S)`. For weights that are all one these are the hull bounds of a polynomial patch, to the last bit. They loosen as the weights of a piece differ and tighten as the piece shrinks, so they are taken from small pieces: the leaves of the tree for the bound that `stretch_within` uses, the pieces of a span quartered twice for the marcher's field (which used estimates from samples for a rational surface before), a span halved three times for a curve.
- *The normal* (`RationalBezierPatch::weighted_normal_net`). `w³ (S_u × S_v) = w (A_u × A_v) + w_v (A × A_u) + w_u (A_v × A)` is a polynomial where the normal is not; its Bézier net (degrees `3m − 1` by `3n − 1`, products of Bernstein forms) holds it in its hull, so the normal is at least as long as the distance from the origin to the box of that net over the cube of the heaviest weight. With the bound on the first derivatives that gives the smallest singular value of the Jacobian, and so the parameter speed the chart needs.
- *The bend of a rational edge* (`hits.rs`, `turn_bound`) comes from the curve bound above, so a rational spline is judged a whole stretch at once like any other curve.

Measured on a NURBS cylinder the parameter speed bound is within 20 % of the true value and the second-derivative bound within 50 %; on a bicubic roof with the weights 1, 2, 0.5 and 3 within 36 % and 24 %. The parameter speed is now taken in the surface's own parameters, with the width of the piece: before, it was the piece's own, which is right only for a span one wide, and a B-spline face over a wider knot span got a bound that was too small by that width (found here, fixed for polynomial faces too; `the_parameter_speed_is_in_the_parameters_of_the_surface`).

The oracle (`crates/kernel/tests/boolean_rational.rs`): a rational surface can *be* a piece of a quadric or a torus, so each test solid exists twice, with its curved faces on the analytic surfaces and on the rational patches that equal them (a quarter rod, a quarter ball zone, a quarter frustum, a quarter slice of a ring with an outer and an inner face; patches that end on the trims and patches a fifth larger; circular edges as circles and as rational arcs). The twins pass `check`, measure as the closed forms to 1e-9 and mesh watertight; their three booleans against a block, a cylinder, a ball, a cone and a torus through the rational face agree with the same booleans of the analytic solid within 5.1e-8 in volume and area (360 booleans; held to 1e-6) at the scales 1e-3, 1 and 1e6, in the world's frame and tilted; tangent contact with a plane and a ball and an edge of a block on the face come out as for the analytic twin. A roof that is no quadric (the weights above) is held to a quadrature of its own and to the identities of sets, against a block, a ball, a block over a corner (which cuts the rational roof edges) and a cylinder.

*Weights far apart (2026-10-15).* A patch whose weights are a hundred apart moves a hundred times faster with its parameters near its light corners than near its heavy ones, and the winding numbers, which halved the loops by one bound on the chart's speed over the whole face, gave up ("too close to a face boundary"). On a surface drawn in its own parameters a stretch that the bound for the whole face would split is now judged by the bound near it, on every such surface (2026-10-15; the first version did so only where the pieces' bounds were more than sixteen apart): `PatchTree::sigma_near`, the least bound of the pieces that come within twice the stretch's length plus the distance of its start from the surface, which is where the feet of its points are; a piece wholly within reach gives its own bound, one partly within reach is looked at closer. It is never looser than the bound for the whole face. Held in `boolean.rs` › `what_is_not_supported_says_so`: the plate with its top as a bilinear patch with the weights 1, 100, 100, 1 against a ball at a corner of its bottom and at a corner of its top (which cuts the weighted face), union, difference and intersection, each the same body as the same boolean of the plain block (400 seeded points classified by ray casting, `boolean::contains_points`, against the block's result and against the exact membership of block and ball; the same face counts). *Not yet:* `topo::mass_properties` does not settle on such a face (on the weighted plate itself either): its sums creep towards the answer and agree to 1e-12 only after more doublings than it allows. **Freeform faces with a pole (2026-10-14, `geom/src/pole.rs`, `boolean/freeform.rs`, `chart.rs`).** A border of a patch that is one point is where the parameters stall: the derivative along the border is zero, `∂u × ∂v` has no length, and the parameter rectangle is no chart (a loop through the pole is not closed in it, and nothing bounds how fast the parameter along the border changes nearby). The surface itself is a surface there: smooth when it closes up smoothly (a dome), a cone-like point when it does not (a drop's tip). Every place that read the surface through its parameters is listed here with what it does now; a face without a pole takes the code it took before in each of them.

- *The chart* (`PatchTree::chart`, `chart_speed`). The parameter rectangle is drawn as the surface is. With `s` and `t` the shares of the domain along and across the poles' borders, the point goes to `(ω(t) (s − ½), t)`, with the width `ω = t` for a pole at the start, `1 − t` at the end and `t (1 − t)` for both: a wedge, or a lens between two points. A pole is one ordinary point of it, the map from the surface is one to one and continuous, and it turns the way the parameters do, so the winding number of the loops is what it is on the surface. Its speed is bounded, not sampled: a step `(ds, dt)` moves the chart by the shear `[1 k; 0 1]` of `(ω ds, dt)` with `|k| = |ω′ (s − ½)| ≤ ½` (norm at most 1.2808; 1.29 is used) and the surface by `E ω ds + F dt` with `E = S_s / ω`, `F = S_t`, so the speed is at most 1.29 over the least singular value of `[E F]`, which comes from an upper bound on `|E|² + |F|²` and a lower bound on `|E × F|` as before. `E` and `E × F` are bounded piece by piece of the tree: away from a pole by the piece's nets over the least and the greatest `ω` on it (`ω` is concave); next to a pole, where `S_s` and the normal vanish as `ω` does, by the Bézier nets of `w² S_σ` and `w³ (S_σ × S_τ)` *divided by the piece's own parameter* (`B_j^n(τ) / τ = (n / j) B_{j−1}^{n−1}(τ)`: a net whose border row is zero is the net, one degree lower, of the quotient; `geom`: `RationalBezierPatch::weighted_derivative_nets`, new, and `weighted_normal_net`). A border row counts as zero below 1e-9 of the net's longest vector (a border collapsed up to rounding is collapsed, as the measure and the detection take it). Measured, the bound is 1.4 to 2.5 times the fastest the chart is seen to move (an octant, a lune, a cone, a drop, either way round; `the_chart_of_a_surface_with_poles_moves_no_faster_than_its_speed`, which also steps to within 1e-9 of the poles).
- *The side of the surface a point is on* (`PatchTree::signed_distance`, and the marcher's field for a freeform surface, `field.rs`). The sign is that of `(p − foot) · normal`, and at a pole the formula has no normal. For a point whose nearest point is the pole the normal is taken along the meridian that leaves the pole nearest to the direction of the point (`Pole::normal_towards`): a point a little further on, whose foot is an ordinary point near the pole, has that foot on that meridian and is on the side its normal says, and the sign cannot change on the way, since it would have to pass through zero, which takes a point straight behind that meridian, for which it is the farthest in direction and not the nearest. Where the surface closes up smoothly every meridian gives the same normal. This is an argument by continuity, not a bound; it is held against the distance to a ball (straight above and below the pole of an octant, to 1e-9) and against a cone's two sides behind its apex.
- *The directions around a pole* (`outline::outward_normal`, which `regions` sorts the curves at a vertex by, `sew` and `section`). At a pole it gives the direction the surface closes around (`freeform::pole_axes`): the mean of the unit normals the surface reaches the pole with, which is its normal there when it closes up smoothly and stands in for the axis, as at a cone's apex, when it comes to a point. Seen along it the meridians leave the pole in the order they have on the surface as long as every limit normal is on the axis's side (the direction of a meridian dropped onto the plane across the axis turns at a rate with the sign of axis · normal). That is proven, not sampled: the limit normals are, up to a positive factor, the divided normal net's border row, whose vectors hold them in their hull, and each must make an angle with the axis whose cosine is above 0.05.
- *The marcher* (`geom/src/intersect/field.rs`, `short_of_poles`). The chart of a freeform surface stops a quarter of the tolerance short of a pole, as a cone's does of its apex: the border is moved in by `tolerance / (4 L)` with `L` the proven bound on the speed across it, so everything left out is within a quarter of the tolerance of the pole and a curve that runs into the pole ends within that of it, where the vertex rule joins it to the pole. The bounds the cells are judged by are upper bounds on derivatives and hold at a pole as anywhere. The grid normals of a freeform field are the limit normals at a pole.
- *Unchanged, and why:* the boxes of the tree, the bound below the distance and the nearest point need no regular parameters; `stretch_within` compares along any path in the parameters, so its proof holds (it is only less often conclusive next to a pole).

*Handled:* a pole where the surface closes up smoothly and one where it comes to a cone-like point; one pole or one at each end of the same parameter; cuts away from the pole, around it (the pole inside the tool) and through it (the pole on a face, an edge or the curved side of the tool). *Refused by name:* poles on more than two borders across both parameters (`POLES_BOTH_WAYS`; two adjacent ones are a corner since 2026-10-15, below), a point the limit normals do not all lean one way around, or folds back over (`POLE_SHAPE`, or `IRREGULAR` when the divided nets give no bound). *Held to* (`crates/kernel/tests/boolean_poles.rs`): the octant of a ball as a rational patch against the same booleans of the analytic octant, with a block, a cylinder, a ball, a cone and a torus away from the pole, around it and through it (the pole on a block's face, on a block's edge, on the side of the others), in the world's frame and tilted, at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6: 288 booleans, within 3.7e-7 in volume and area (held to 1e-6), none refused since 2026-10-15 (the torus through the pole, tilted, at 1e6 was refused "imprint lost": see *The helmet's findings* below); the dome and the drop of `kernel::revolve` against the same kinds of tools by the identities of sets (1e-6) and, for planes across the axis and through it (through both poles, through the drop's point), by closed forms from the profile (1e-7). Every result passes `check` and is measured; every result is meshed, except eight kinds of cut through a pole that the tessellator declines ("segments per side") or leaves with a gap, pinned in the test's `UNMESHED` with a reproducer for `crates/tess` (meshed since the same night, §6 *Poles of spline surfaces*; the list is empty). *Cost:* 75 to 280 ms per boolean with the octant's patch (the test profile), as for any rational face.

*Cusps (2026-10-15).* A spline that leaves the axis along it sweeps a needle: its width grows as the square of the distance from the point, not in proportion, so the meridians leave the point all along one line and not in a fan. `S_s` vanishes there to the second order (the net of `w² S_σ` divides by the parameter across twice: `pole_order`), and the wedge is drawn with that width, `ω = tᵐ (1 − t)ⁿ` to the orders of its poles: `E = S_s / ω` is bounded again (the nets divided `m` times), the shear is that of `|ω′| ≤ max(m, n)` (1.634 for a cusp, 1.618 with a hundredth of margin), and a surface that closes on itself is a disc of radius `tᵐ` (speed `√(m² + 4π²)` times the tree's). The direction the surface closes around at a cusp is the line its meridians leave along, on the side its normals lean to as they leave (the next row of the divided normal's net: at a cusp the limit normals are square to the line, all around, as at the apex of a cone of no angle). Where every piece that leaves a vertex leaves it along the normal of its tangent plane (at a cusp, all of them), the regions put them in order a little further along (`untie`, all of them at once). A cusp at either end of a surface with a pole at each (an egg whose chart is a sphere) is refused (`POLE_SHAPE`). Measured: the speed bound of the wedge and of the disc against the fastest seen (`the_chart_of_a_surface_with_poles_moves_no_faster_than_its_speed`: 2.29 against 0.57; the closed charts' test: 6.58 against 0.74); `boolean_poles.rs` › `a_cusp_is_an_operand`: a needle against a block around its point, a slab away from it, a ball, a leaning block and a half-space across the axis, at three scales: closed forms from the profile where the tool cuts across the axis (1e-7), the identities of sets, 400 seeded points against the exact membership, every result meshed. *Refused still:* a face that holds the axis through the point (a plane through it along the axis): on the needle's chart the distance to such a face vanishes to the second order all along the border the point is, and the marcher takes it for a contact along a curve (pinned in the same test). What it needs: the marcher's chart stopped short of a cusp where its width is a quarter of the tolerance (at `√(tol / c)`, not `tol / speed`), and the curves that end there drawn on to the point.

**Poles at a corner of the parameters (2026-10-15, `boolean/freeform.rs` › `Corner`, `geom/src/pole.rs`, `geom/src/intersect`, `tess/src/face.rs` › `CornerPole`).** A patch whose borders `u = 0` and `v = 0` (or any border across u and one across v) are both one point closes into that point along an L of its parameter rectangle; the two other borders meet at an ordinary corner. Until now it was refused (`POLES_BOTH_WAYS`). With `s` and `t` the shares of the domain from the pole's borders:

- *The chart* (`Corner`). The point goes to `(s t (s − t), s t)` (its first coordinate negated when one of the two shares is measured backwards, so that it turns the way the parameters do): the L is the origin, `(s − t, s t)` is one to one on the square, and near the origin the chart is a wedge `|x| ≤ y` in which the meridians leave the pole in their order along the L. - *The direction the surface closes around* (`corner_axis`): the mean of the limit normals along the whole L, the rows of the doubly divided normal net on both borders and, at the corner, of the two halves `over_sum` gives.
- *The side of the surface at the pole* (`Pole::normal_towards`, `normal_along`): the meridians leave the pole from both borders, and the nearest of all is taken; a meridian near the end of a border that is the other pole's border is looked at as far from that end as from its own border (nearer, both derivatives are rounding). - *The tessellator* (`CornerPole`): a boundary that comes into the pole goes along the L from the meridian of the point before (its smaller share dropped: near the border `t = 0` the line of constant `s` leaves the pole in the direction `R(s, 0)` of `S = P + s t R`) to that of the point after, round the corner when they are on different borders; the pole is also recognised by position (its projection may come back off the L). - *The chart* (`chart.rs`, `Layout`). With `s` the share of the way around and `t` of the way across, the way around is an angle `θ = 2π s`: closed one way, the annulus `(1 + t) e^{iθ}`; with a pole at one end, the disc `t e^{iθ}` (or `(1 − t)`), the pole its centre; with a pole at each end (a ball), `z = t e^{iθ} / (1 − t)` puts one pole at the origin and the other at infinity and `w = 1 / (z − z₀)` sends a point `z₀` clear of the loops to infinity instead (the poles and twelve places between them are tried, the one furthest from the loops taken; refused within 1e-3 of the surface's size), so both poles are ordinary points and the face may contain the point left out, as on a sphere; closed both ways (a torus), cut open along a line of constant `t` (or `s`) that the loops do not cross, found as for the analytic torus by following each boundary piece through the nearest point, and `(1 + (t − cut mod 1)) e^{iθ}`. The direction between two images is never computed through a far point: for the ball, `(a_q b_p − a_p b_q) conj(A_p) conj(A_q)` with `a = t e^{iθ}`, `b = 1 − t`, `A = a b₀ − a₀ b`, bounded and continuous through the pole at infinity of `z`. The speed is bounded from the tree's bound on the Jacobian: in the shares, `4π` times it for the annulus and the torus (`r ≤ 2`), `√(1 + 4π²)` and `√(4 + 4π²)` times the wedge's bound (`wedge_sigma`, `ω` the width that vanishes at a pole) for the disc and the ball, where the derivatives of the direction along `s` vanish as `ω` does. Measured, the bounds are 4 to 13 times the fastest the chart is seen to move, across the seam too (`chart.rs` › `the_chart_of_a_closed_surface_moves_no_faster_than_its_speed`). Refused by name: a surface that closes into a point along the same parameter it closes in, or closes both ways and into a point (`CLOSED_POLE`); a torus-like face whose boundary crosses every line both ways (`NO_SEAM_CUT`); a ball-like face whose boundary comes near every point tried (`NO_DROP`).
- *The surface intersections* (`intersect/field.rs`, `march.rs`). Such a surface is a periodic chart, as a cylinder is: the quadtree wraps its cells, and a branch is marched over the seam with its parameters taken around into the domain where the surface is evaluated (`Chart::at`, and the start of the projector's search). A curve around the surface closes, and one across the seam is one curve. A border across the way around that lies on the other surface (a rim on a plate) is reported as a closed curve; one that is a pole gives none.

Besides: a face without loops on a surface closed both ways, or one way with a pole at each end, is the whole surface, as on a sphere or a torus (merge may join pieces into one, `freeform::borderless`); the fixed points looked at inside such a face are taken in the shares of the domain. *Held to* (`crates/kernel/tests/boolean_closed_twins.rs`): a NURBS can (with and without a seam edge, the seam in two places, the turn in u or v), ball, cup and torus, each one face on a rational surface that closes, against the same solid on the analytic cylinder, sphere and torus, with blocks, cylinders, balls, cones and tori that cross the seam, cut through both poles, around the axis and across the tube; the three booleans at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6, in the world's frame and tilted: 594 booleans, all built and valid, within 7.9e-8 of the analytic twin in volume and area (held to 1e-6), the identities of sets to 2.4e-8 (held to 1e-7), and `(A − B) ∪ (A ∩ B)` built and measured as A. Six results through both poles of a ball cut along its seam are not measured by `topo` (named there) and are held to their twins by 4000 sampled points each; 77 results are declined by the tessellator (listed by the test). The vase of `boolean_closed_freeform.rs` (a Bézier profile turned, the seam inside the half, on the cut and outside it) is cut in half at three scales, within 9.1e-9 of half its volume. *Cost:* 0.13 to 0.4 s per boolean (the test profile), as for other rational faces. *Not done:* knots that are not clamped (a closure the net does not show).

**The helmet's findings (2026-10-15, `geom/src/intersect/field.rs`, `march.rs`, `geom/src/pole.rs`, `boolean/section.rs`).** A shell turned from two splines and cut by lofted frustums (the Voronoi helmet; `crates/kernel/tests/boolean_helmet.rs`) found six mistakes, all where two freeform faces meet. Five are in how a freeform surface on the *field* side of the marcher (the surface measured against, not the one walked) was read: a lofted side whose corners are rounded is a bilinear patch, not a plane, so the shell is the field (with a planar tool the shell is walked and none of these happen). The sixth is in the bound below the distance to a freeform face:

- *Curvature at a pole.* The projector estimates the curvature of a freeform surface from how fast the normal turns between neighbouring nodes of its 25 × 25 grid. Nodes on one pole are one point, apart by rounding (1e-14 at a size of 100), each with the limit normal of its own meridian; their difference over that distance read as a curvature of 1e7 per unit length, every cell within two grid steps of the pole was too bent to be simple, and the boolean refused "tangent or nearly coincident over too large an area". Nodes on one pole are no longer compared.
- *The nearest point next to a pole.* Newton's step for the nearest point cannot turn from one meridian to another at a pole (the derivative along its border is zero), and next to it the step along the border is ill-conditioned: from the pole node, or by a step that clamps onto the border, it kept to the meridian it started on and stopped at the pole or on that meridian, for a point a millimetre off the pole a millimetre from the surface. A search that ends within one grid row of a pole is done again from the meridian that leaves the pole towards the point (`Pole::meridian_towards`), first along that meridian, then in both parameters, and the nearer foot is kept.
- *Pieces between the field's borders.* Past a border of a freeform surface the field goes on (the distance to the nearest point, on the border), and the quadtree sees only that: a piece of curve between two borders of the field (a frustum's side cutting a corner off a half of the shell, between the half's meridian and the vent's rim) lay in simple cells whose every edge seed was past the field's edge, and was lost ("imprint lost"). The marcher now also seeds where a border of a freeform field that is an edge (not a seam, not a pole) crosses the walked surface: along each such border, bisection on its parameter wherever the distance to the walked surface could be zero (it changes no faster than the border moves; the finest piece is sixteen tolerances, and at most 4096 pieces per border), and a root that is on the walked surface gives a seed.
- *A pole border is no curve.* A border of the walked patch that lies on the other surface is reported as a curve; a border that is one point (the pole of an octant on an open patch) was reported as a curve from the pole to itself, a few tolerances long in the chart's parameters, which on the other face hid the arc that divides it. It was already left out where the border runs around a closed surface; now everywhere.
- *A loop through a cut place.* An end of a curve next to a place where an edge meets a surface is that place, and the place is found on the curve once. A curve that begins and ends there (the figure eight in which a torus meets the plane it touches at its inner equator, through the octant's pole) had the place at its start only, and the lobe back into the face was dropped. An end whose place was found at the other end of the curve is now that place itself.

- *A bound below the distance that is one* (`boolean/freeform.rs`, `PatchTree::bound_by` and `refine`). Asked up to a threshold (does the distance exceed it?), the bound stopped as soon as the nearest piece looked at so far was under the threshold and returned that piece's distance; a nearer piece not yet looked at could undercut it, so between the threshold (1.001 times what is asked) and what is asked the value was no bound. A ray through a lofted frustum was proven clear of the side it leaves by (its distance there 0, the value returned 1.0006 tolerances), a face of another frustum, apart from it, was taken for inside and left out, and the union of the two had edges used once (the check caught it: "produced an invalid body"; the helmet's frustums from 60 mm, united). The pieces left (the heap's nearest, the boxes of those still to be split) now count. The union of radial frustums that come within a few millimetres of each other towards the centre is no tolerance case: the same mistake.

With the fourth and fifth, the octant against a torus through its pole, tilted, at 1e6 (pinned as refused in `boolean_poles.rs`) is built and agrees with the analytic octant. *Held to:* four cells of the helmet against the shell with a pole and with a vent, the three booleans at 1e-3, 1 and 1e6, valid, the identities of sets to 1e-7, and the common part against the same boolean with the cell's planar twin (its far corners the near ones scaled exactly, so that its sides are planes and the shell is walked instead) to 2e-5 (the twin's sides are 1e-5 mm off the cell's); two frustums apart, whose union is both (to 1e-12), whose intersection is empty and whose difference is the first; through the CLI, each of 70 cells cut from the shell with a pole, 71 from 60 mm and 71 from 100 mm from the shell with a vent, all built (before: 8, 1 and 1 refused), and the helmet's 71 frustums from 60 mm united and taken from the shell, valid (before: an invalid body). Unit tests in `field.rs` for the curvature and the nearest point next to a pole.
