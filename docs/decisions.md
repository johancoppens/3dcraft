### 2026-10-14: The tessellator's seams and its promise of a closed mesh (decided by an agent overnight, for the developer to overturn)

Taken while repairing three findings about the tessellator ([`architecture.md`](architecture.md) §6); nobody was awake to ask.

- **A face on a spline surface that closes on itself is meshed, not refused.** The seam machinery of the analytic surfaces carried over whole once the surface had a period, so refusing would have been the larger change to explain. To overturn: return no period from `Closure::of` in `crates/tess/src/face.rs`; such a face is then refused by the jump check below.
- **The period is found in the tessellator, from the control net,** not reported by `craft3d-geom`: `geom` was not this task's to change. First and last rows of control points the same to 10⁻⁹ of the net's size, weights in one proportion, clamped knots. A surface that closes on knots that are not clamped is therefore not recognised and a face across its seam is refused. The better home is `Surface::period_u` and `period_v` in `geom`, where the exact measure and the booleans can use it too; `Closure::of` then shrinks to a call.
- **A loop that comes back from a pole the way it went there turns all the way around the pole.** A whole sphere (or closed spline surface, or a cone to its apex) with its seam as an edge, as a STEP file has it, is thereby meshed. It was refused before, and asserted to be (`crates/tess/tests/invalid.rs`); on a spline surface it was refused at coarse tolerances and meshed at fine ones, by the rounding of the projection near the pole, which is what forced a rule.
- **The promise of a closed mesh is checked per face, on its border, and not by welding the mesh.** Every side of a triangle on the boundary of the face's region is held against the chords of the face's edges. It costs the sorting of the boundary, where a weld sorts every triangle; it names the edge and both faces; and it works for a sheet, whose border is open by right. What it does not look at is the inside of a face, which is closed by the making of the triangulation. The error is a `TessError::Face`, not a new variant: `TessError` is matched in other crates, which this task did not touch.
- **A boundary that jumps is an error:** a chord whose straight segment in parameter space strays from the chord by more than the chord's own length and more than twice the tolerance. The second limit is the one `chords_to_refine` asks finer chords for; the first is what tells a jump across the surface (as far as the surface is wide, however short the chord) from a chord the parameters merely distort (a part of its length, and less with every cut). It is tested after the chords were cut, at the moment the face is triangulated, so nothing that could be cured is refused.
- **A boundary narrower than 10⁻⁹ of a spline surface's parameter range bounds no region.** Parameters on a spline surface are found by a search and carry its rounding; a loop that runs there and back along one line enclosed a sliver of that rounding and was triangulated or not by chance.
- **The seed grid of a spline face is sized by proven bounds, and each parameter gets a step of its own.** The bounds are `RationalBezierPatch::derivative_bounds` on the part of each knot span the face covers. Two things were decided beyond putting them in place of the sampled estimate. *(a) The patches are cut in halves four times before they are bounded,* each time across the parameter that gains most: uncut, the bounds are 2.7 to 4.7 times the second derivatives they bound on surfaces of revolution (1.2 times on polynomial surfaces), cut four times 1.5 to 2.1 times, and six cuts gain little more for four times the work. Rational faces still get up to a third more triangles than the sampled estimate gave them (it multiplied what it saw by 1.5, and promised nothing); to trade the other way, change `HALVINGS` in `face.rs`. A part is never cut narrower than a sixteenth of its span, where the differences the bounds are made of would lose their digits. *(b) The grid has a step per parameter* (`spline_cells`): `(Muu + Muv) hu² = (Mvv + Muv) hv² = 4 × tolerance`, which keeps the same bound as one step for both and never has more cells. Nobody asked for this; it is here because the proven bounds made the one-step grid finer on the faces that could least afford it, and because it is what a wall straight one way needs: an extruded spline's wall went from 109,468 triangles to 1,160 at a tolerance of 10⁻⁴ of its size, measured within the tolerance like the rest. Bézier patches (`Surface::Bezier`) keep their one step, so that their meshes stay what they were, bit for bit; they would gain the same way, which is a decision of its own. To overturn (b): pass the larger of the two sums for both in `spline_cells`.
- **A face that needs more cells than the limit one way is held at the limit there and made finer the other way,** as far as the same bound allows (a factor √2). Without it, three bodies of the comparison set that were meshed at 10⁻⁵ of their size were refused as too fine; with it, one still is (a three-quarter turn of a cubic spline, 1.8 million triangles in one face before): the proven grid is finer than the sampled one was, and the limit of 1024 cells a side is where it always was.
- **Two faces that share a rung are parted by a point in the curved one,** not refused and not left. Left, the mesh is closed and no manifold, which is what the promise is about; refused, a valid body a user can build (a ball's patch cut by a ring around its pole) has no mesh at a tolerance of a four-hundredth of its size. The point takes the mesh off the chord by less than the tolerance; the two meshes may then pass through each other by as little. Two plane faces that share a rung are left alone: they overlap in one plane, and the self-intersection test needs their mesh to say so.
- **A chord that two edges share is cut on the curved edge,** in the rounds in which faces ask for finer chords; two straight edges with the same chord are the same edge twice, the body's to answer for. Found on a sweep of a sliver (`crates/kernel/tests/sweep.rs`, `the_mesh_of_a_sliver_is_closed_at_a_coarse_chord`); the closedness check could not see it, every face being right along its own edges.
- **Near a pole of a spline surface the boundary is projected by descent from its neighbour, and triangles whose area is rounding are left as they are.** Both are for spline surfaces only: at a sphere's pole and a cone's apex the parameters are exact, and there such a triangle is mended by cutting it (asserted in `crates/tess/tests/winding_seam.rs`, which failed when the rule was general). The thresholds are ratios: a thousandth of the surface's fastest movement along the loop, and 10⁻¹² of the triangle's coordinates times its longest side.
- **The kernel goes on making two halves** of a full turn of a spline. The tessellator is no longer a reason for that; the exact measure (wrong without an error on a surface that closes in v, see the roadmap, M10) and the booleans still are.
### 2026-10-14: Freeform faces with a pole, decided by an agent overnight, for the developer to overturn

Taken while making a face with a collapsed border measurable and a boolean operand (`architecture.md` §5.3 › *Freeform faces with a pole*); nobody was awake to ask.

- **A border is a pole when its control points agree to 1e-9 of the size of the control net** (`geom::pole`), for the measure, the booleans and the marcher alike. Tighter (exact equality) would refuse a patch whose quarter turn's cosine left its border 6e-17 wide; looser would call a real, narrow border a point.
- **The chart of a face with poles is a wedge or a lens of its parameter rectangle**, not a chart taken from space as the analytic surfaces have (a sphere's stereographic projection). It keeps the nearest point as the one way from space to the chart and needs one new bound (the divided nets). Poles both ways across a patch are refused by name rather than given a chart of their own: no sweep of this kernel makes one.
- **A cut through a cone-like point is taken, not refused.** The side of the surface at such a point is decided by the meridian nearest to the point's direction, which is argued by continuity and tested, where every other decision of the booleans is a bound. To overturn: refuse a pole whose limit normals are not all one (within the angular tolerance) in `PatchTree::chart_speed`.
- **The marcher stops a quarter of the tolerance short of a pole and the vertex rule joins the curve to the pole**, as at a cone's apex, rather than marching into the pole and ending the curve on it exactly. One case in 288 loses a piece of the curve that way (a torus through the pole, tilted, at 1e6: refused, pinned in `boolean_poles.rs`). Ending the branch on the pole itself is a change in `march.rs`, which was not this work's file.
- **A boundary that only comes near a pole is measured as before** (from a point of the boundary), and so settles or is declined; only a boundary *through* a pole (within 1e-6 of its reach) is integrated from it. The apex rule of a cone (nearer than half its reach) would measure more near misses, and would change the last digits of faces that are measured today.
- **`feature.revolve` no longer refuses anything about a spline's ends.** A cusp (a spline that leaves the axis along it) is built and measured; as a boolean operand it is refused by name.
- **Results the tessellator does not mesh are pinned, not hidden**: eight kinds of cut through a pole (a refusal "segments per side", or a mesh with a gap) are listed in `boolean_poles.rs` with an ignored reproducer; `crates/tess` was another agent's that night. (That agent took them the same night: all eight are meshed, the list is empty and the reproducer runs with the rest; `architecture.md` §6, *Poles of spline surfaces*.)

### 2026-10-14: Decided by an agent overnight, for the developer to overturn

Taken while lifting tangent contact in `geom::intersect` (branch `tangent-contact`, architecture §5.1); nobody was awake to ask.

- **A contact along a curve gets a closed form per pair, not a general method in the marcher.** A cylinder that goes on in a torus and cones along a shared ruling are recognised by their position and answered from their equations. The alternative, a marcher that follows a curve of tangency and the branch that crosses it (dividing the distance by the square of the distance to the curve of contact), would also cover tori that continue each other and freeform blends, and is the larger piece of work; the closed forms are exact where they apply and leave everything else as it was. *Overturn by:* building that method; the closed forms can stay as its oracle.
- **The position must fit within half a tolerance for the cylinder and the torus** (radii, the height of the axis over the middle circle's plane, its distance from the torus' axis, summed), and within the angular tolerance for the cones (the line through the apexes against either half-angle; the two tangent planes). Outside that the pair goes to the marcher as before, which refuses it or draws what is there. The second curve of the cylinder and the torus is a fitted spline; its stated deviation is the fit (a quarter of a tolerance at most) plus that misfit.
- **Apexes within the linear tolerance are one apex.** Two cones whose apexes are that close are answered as cones with one apex (rulings through it).
- **A marched branch ends on a crossing point it comes near, from further out than before** (two steps, or the radius at which the arms are seeded). Every curve that reaches such a point is changed in its last digits by it (sixteen corpus outcomes moved in the eighth or ninth digit of their volume, with the same verdicts). The alternative was to keep the step rule and detect a branch drawn twice afterwards.
- **The ignored reproducer in `crates/kernel/tests/fillet.rs` was rewritten, not just switched on.** It asserted the union's and the difference's volume at one scale, from the operands' measured volumes, and its comment said the bodies lie end to end. They do not only: half a turn on, the tool's other end lies flat against the bar's end. The test now runs at three scales, holds bar and tool to closed forms, and asks for one shell, the area less that end twice, the face count, the difference both ways and an empty intersection. Stricter in every respect; for the developer to review all the same (testing.md › *Changing an expectation*).

Taken while building revolved and closed splines (`architecture.md` §4.1 › *Revolved splines, closed splines and corners*); nobody was awake to ask.

- **No face on a spline surface that closes on itself.** A full turn of a spline is two faces of half a turn, a closed spline is two pieces. The alternatives were one face with a seam edge walked there and back, or a period for spline surfaces in `geom`: either needs the tessellator, the exact measure and the booleans to follow a seam they cannot see today, and the first try (a seam edge) gave a mesh that was silently not watertight. With ordinary patches all three worked unchanged, booleans included. The price is two faces where a cylinder has one, and an edge between them that is smooth.
- **The second and later entities a curve sweeps are numbered pieces:** `F2:piece(1)/F2:side(e7)`, a new role written like a copy, the first keeping the plain name. The alternative was to let both halves answer to `F2:side(e7)` (a name already may resolve to several faces after a boolean), which reads better and would let "the wall" mean both halves, but leaves a vertex or an edge of a sweep without a name of its own. To overturn: drop `piece` in `regen::extrude::name_of`.
- **Analytic faces next to a revolved spline stay whole** (their loop is two arcs); only the spline's faces are in halves.
- **A spline that ends on the axis was refused by the feature for a few hours** (superseded the same night: see *Freeform faces with a pole* below). The measure and the booleans take the face now, and `feature.revolve` builds a dome, a drop and a round bottom like any other profile.
- **A polyline's legs become lines** (planes, cylinders and cones when swept), not degree-1 spline faces.
- **A point on a spline is one equation with the curve's parameter eliminated** (the distance at the foot of the point), not two equations with the parameter as an extra unknown: nothing new for the solver to store, to guess a start for or to count, and a solution stays a solution when solved again. Beyond its ends the spline counts as going on straight (a point can slide off the end onto the line of the last leg), as a point on a line is on the whole line; holding it between the ends needs a constraint that does not exist for lines either.

### 2026-10-13: Decided by the developer

- **The boolean kernel is the first priority:** it is what makes 3DCraft a professional tool. A case the kernel refuses is work owed, not an end state; the checklist is in [`roadmap.md`](roadmap.md).
- **The kernel's tests may use the tessellator** (a dev-dependency inside one layer, listed as `SAME_LAYER_DEV` in `xtask/src/layers.rs`): every body an operation makes is also meshed as an independent check. The kernel's own code still does not depend on `tess`.
- **Where FreeCAD and the kernel disagree in the boolean corpus:** eleven cases stay in `REFERENCE_OFF` (the reference is off, with a closed form, a direct integration or an identity as evidence); `near/cylinder-torus/009` is `UNDECIDED`, because nothing independent measures our result there. Building that measurement is not worth the time now.
- **The gate in two steps:** a quick check per commit (fmt, clippy and tests of the touched crates), the full `cargo xtask ci` only before `main` moves. The full run is the only automatic safety net and stays; it is too slow to pay per commit. Making it faster (the heavy random tests in release, test files side by side) is open.

### 2026-10-13: Waiting for the developer (presented, not yet decided)

Each stands as described until the developer says otherwise.

- **Extrude ends (2026-10-13, built by an agent on the M8.5 row).** Five choices, each easy to turn round: (1) *through all* is measured against the boxes of the bodies that exist at that point of the history and lie in the profile's way, and ends a hundredth of the way (at least a thousand tolerances) past the farthest, rather than flush with it or "infinitely" far; (2) *up to a face* ends on the face's **surface**: a profile that hangs over the face's edge ends on the plane, cylinder, sphere or torus continued, and only a profile that misses the face altogether is refused (the strict reading, "the whole end must lie within the face", is the alternative); (3) the direction of an extrusion up to a face is never guessed: a face behind the sketch plane is an error that names `reversed`, so an upstream edit that moves the face to the other side fails the feature instead of flipping the body; (4) the `.3dcraft` version stays 6: `end` and `reversed` are optional fields, written only when they are not the default, so an older build of version 6 reads every file that does not use them and refuses one that does as an unknown field, not as "a newer 3DCraft is needed"; (5) `feature.extrude` got parameters (`throughAll`, `upTo`, `reversed`) rather than a second command, and `reversed` also flips a `distance`.

- **A ball that dips half a tolerance to two tolerances into a block is refused.** "Touches" is also defensible; the refusal costs 18 corpus cases.
- **Meshes are bit-identical under scaling by powers of two, not by powers of ten** (Delaunay ties on cocircular points fall differently; the triangle count stays within 10%). A wider tie band in `in_circle` would remove most of it but changes the meshes at scale 1.
- **Pinched solids pass the check:** parts of one body that meet only along an edge or at a vertex.
- **Two tests that pinned a refusal were replaced by stricter tests of the value** when the refusal was lifted (the developer agreed to the layer exception that goes with it; the replacement itself was explained, not explicitly approved).
- **The viewport renders with the wgpu renderer by default (`--renderer auto`, `CRAFT3D_RENDERER`), the CPU renderer as the fallback** (2026-10-13, built by an agent on the developer's question whether the GPU is used). It matches the CPU renderer within the §7.2 tolerances, but the picture is read back from the GPU into the same frame the CPU renderer makes, and on this laptop's Intel driver that readback is slow (a few hundred MB/s): for a light model the GPU viewport is slower than the CPU one (20 against 7 ms per frame while orbiting at 1280 × 800; equal for a sharp frame). The way to a fast GPU viewport is no readback (the colour as a texture on eframe's own device, the overlay's depth test on the GPU), not built. Whether `auto` should prefer the GPU until then is the developer's call; `--renderer cpu` gives the old behaviour. *Superseded the same day by the next bullet.*
- **The viewport draws on the window's own wgpu device, multisampled, with the lines drawn and depth-tested on the GPU; nothing is read back for display** (2026-10-13, built by an agent after the developer compared the two windows: the CPU viewport felt a little smoother than the GPU one, and in both the lines were not smooth, least of all while turning the model). What it does now: `craft3d_gpu::Onscreen` on eframe's `RenderState` draws the bodies with 4 samples per pixel and the overlay as screen-space quads inked by the CPU's own line formula and hidden by the CPU viewport's rule in the fragment shader, into a texture registered with egui; every frame is at the viewport's full size and sharp, also while moving, and a frame is drawn only when something changed. The trade-offs: (1) the CPU viewport, the readback path and `ui.screenshot` are unchanged, so the oracle stays, at the price of a second implementation of the line test (its decisions are checked against the CPU's at 1812 of 1812 sample points); (2) the GPU tests against the true depth and the CPU against its screen-interpolated distance, so the GPU allows a line the depth spread of its 3 × 3 neighbourhood as well, which keeps the edge of a hollow (a post on a plate) visible as the CPU's interpolation does by accident; (3) multisampling changes the colour of edge pixels by design, so the parity test does not compare colours inside the band and allows a pixel of gradient on the seams between triangles of one face (documented in §7.2); (4) on wgpu's OpenGL backend the picture has one sample, because that backend cannot make the depth buffer the lines need, multisampled and read by a shader (investigated 2026-10-13 in wgpu-hal 30.0.1, `src/gles`): a multisampled texture that is more than a render target is given the target `GL_TEXTURE_2D` (`mod.rs`, `Texture::get_info_from_desc`; `GL_TEXTURE_2D_MULTISAMPLE` occurs nowhere in the backend) and its storage with `glTexStorage2DMultisample(GL_TEXTURE_2D, …)` (`device.rs`, `create_texture`), which OpenGL refuses, so the texture has no storage, the framebuffer it is attached to is incomplete, and every clear, draw and resolve into it fails silently. Mesa says so with `MESA_DEBUG=1` (`GL_INVALID_ENUM in glTexStorage2DMultisample(target=GL_TEXTURE_2D)`, then `GL_INVALID_FRAMEBUFFER_OPERATION` in `glClearBufferfv`, `glClear` and `glBlitFramebuffer`), and `crates/gpu/tests/gl_multisample.rs` pins it on every adapter: four samples resolve on OpenGL without a depth buffer and with one that is only an attachment (a multisampled renderbuffer), and not with one a shader reads; on Vulkan all three do. It is a limitation of wgpu-hal, not of the driver or of how the resolve is set up, and nothing in wgpu's downlevel flags or format features announces it. Multisampling on OpenGL would take a depth buffer for the lines made at one sample in a pass of its own (the bodies drawn a second time, depth only) and a lines shader that reads one sample: not built, OpenGL being the window's last resort. The test fails the day wgpu's backend learns it; (5) `RenderStats::milliseconds` on the direct path is the drawing thread's time, not the GPU's: the window never waits for the device. The developer can overturn any of it; `--renderer cpu` gives the CPU viewport.
- **See-through bodies are drawn by the GPU with two depth layers; five choices the developer may overturn** (2026-10-14, built by an agent while the developer slept; the method and the measurements are in [`architecture.md`](architecture.md) §7.2, *See-through on the GPU*). (1) *The comparison between the layers is strict and has no bias.* Both passes rasterise the same triangles on the same device, so a surface is never its own "behind" without one, and a bias would hide a second surface that is really there; the price is that two surfaces closer together than a 32-bit depth buffer resolves (about 10⁻⁷ of the frame's depth range) count as one on the GPU where the CPU renderer still separates them. (2) *In the multisampled viewport the front is told from what is behind it by a key per sample (the bits of the front triangle's depth at the pixel centre), not by depth*, because the per-sample depth a shader gets is not the depth buffer's on Intel's Vulkan driver (measured). It costs one more `Rgba8Unorm` target with the viewport's samples, and it relies on the same triangle giving the same bits in two passes, which the tests check on every adapter of the development machine but which Metal, DX12 and a browser's WebGPU have not been seen to do; if one of them does not, a body would look opaque through itself there, and the remedy is a triangle id in place of the depth bits. (3) *The blend is a 256 × 256 table of the CPU renderer's own expression*, so that the two pictures agree to the level also where the blend lies half-way between two levels; the expression is written a second time in `crates/gpu/src/peel.rs`, pinned by the parity tests. (4) *Over a see-through picture the viewport's lines are drawn on the resolved picture at one sample*, not into the multisampled target as over an opaque one (the blend of the layers is itself the resolve); they are inked by distance, so they look the same. (5) *A device that cannot draw the layers falls back to the CPU renderer for see-through frames only* (`GpuError::SeeThrough`), as every device did before; none of the three adapters here needs it. Also decided: the extra targets are kept once made, until the viewport changes size (three colour layers and a depth buffer at four samples: about 50 MB at 1020 × 754), rather than freed when the bodies are opaque again. A finding left for the developer: the CPU renderer draws a surface opaque where a sample lies exactly on an edge shared by two of its triangles (§7.2); the oracle was not changed.
- **The section plane is a view with a counted cap; seven choices the developer may overturn** (2026-10-14, built by an agent while the developer slept; the method and the measurements are in [`architecture.md`](architecture.md) *Current state* › *Section plane* and §7.2 › *Section on the GPU*). (1) *The normal points into the half that is cut away,* and `flip` keeps that half: on a face (whose normal points out of its body) offset 0 cuts nothing and a negative offset cuts into the body; on `xy` the top is taken off. The other convention ("the normal points to what is kept") is as defensible. (2) *The cap is one flat colour, unshaded* (the theme's `section_cap`, an orange): a cut then reads as a cut from any side, and the two renderers have the same bytes there. *No hatching:* it would have to be the same pattern to the pixel in both renderers and would swim over the cap when the camera moves if it were made in screen space; left for when it is asked for. (3) *The cap is found by counting, over all bodies together:* where two bodies overlap in space the plane through both shows no cap (a count per body would need a pass per body on the GPU). (4) *A face that lies in the plane goes with its body* (kept when it faces the cut half), decided per triangle by its own normal within a third of a degree and 2⁻¹⁴ of the mesh's size: without that rule a plane laid on a face shows speckles of rounding. (5) *In the multisampled viewport the cut is decided per pixel,* not per sample (the silhouettes keep their samples); the outline of the cut is drawn as an edge over that line. Per-sample shading would cost four times the fragments and Intel's driver does not give a per-sample fragment the depth test's number. (6) *Sketches and the world axes are not cut,* only bodies, their edges and the marks of what is pointed at. (7) *The section travels with the marks* (`viewport::Marks::cut`) to both drawing paths of the window, and its controls sit in a row of their own beside the view cube: a toggle, X/Y/Z, a slider and Flip; a face or a datum plane as the plane is set through `ui.set` only. A plane that is lost switches the section off and keeps the name (`ui.get` › `section.lost`); a plane that means nothing when it is set is an error of `ui.set`.
- **The CPU renderer's shared-edge rule is "one surface at a sample on a shared edge", not the top-left rule; and one expectation changed with it** (2026-10-14, by an agent while the developer slept; [`architecture.md`](architecture.md) §7.2, *A finding about the oracle*). The task was to stop a surface from being its own "behind" in a see-through picture without changing any opaque picture except on shared-edge samples. The top-left rule in the rasteriser does more than that by definition (it also drops a sample exactly on the right or bottom outline of a mesh, in opaque pictures), although no existing test or hashed picture changed when it was tried; so the rule that was built leaves the rasteriser alone and only says that triangles sharing an edge or a vertex are one surface at a sample on it. Opaque pictures: unchanged, bit for bit, by construction (the code that picks the front is untouched) and by measurement (60 frames hashed before and after). See-through pictures change on such samples only. *The expectation that changed* (`docs/testing.md` › *Changing an expectation*): `crates/gpu/tests/see_through.rs`, `a_surface_is_never_behind_itself`, printed the CPU's count of self-showing pixels for the view on the diagonal (8) and did not compare that view; it now asserts 0 on the CPU and compares the view with the GPU like the other three. (1) The reason is a fact: a sheet with nothing behind it over black at opacity ½ is half as bright as the opaque sheet at every pixel; 8 pixels were as bright as the opaque sheet. (2) It is not weaker: an assertion was added and a comparison that was skipped is made. (3) It stays pinned independently of the code: by the blend formula on the front picture, in `render`'s own test `a_surface_is_not_behind_itself_on_an_edge_its_triangles_share` (which fails without the rule, at pixel (177, 32): 149 where 75 is expected), and by the GPU's picture. (4) This entry is the record. If the developer prefers the top-left rule (one line in `render`, and the CPU then equals the GPU on outlines too), it is a decision about the oracle's opaque pictures and theirs to take.
- **The viewport's mesh is finer than the export's: a display quality of its own, as UI state** (2026-10-13, made by the agent after the developer saw the bolt holes of the flange as polygons; the developer can overturn). The viewport asks the engine for `SceneQuality::Display { relative, max_triangles }` (architecture.md §6): a chordal tolerance of `relative` times the model's size, coarsened until the scene has at most `max_triangles` triangles and every body is within the tessellator's reach, but never beyond the automatic tolerance `view.render`, previews and `file.export` keep (1e-3 of the size), which is unchanged. The defaults, `ui.set {"quality": {"chordal": 3e-5, "maxTriangles": 1000000}}`: 3e-5 is a chord error of a thirtieth of a pixel on a model that fills a 1020-pixel viewport (a thirteenth at 2560), so a circle stays round at ten times that zoom; on the flange it means 4328 triangles instead of 820 and a chord error of 0.02 px instead of 0.66 (1.4 at 2560 × 1400), at no measurable cost on the GPU (0.2 to 0.5 ms per frame on the drawing thread either way) and 18 ms more on the CPU viewport's sharp image (74 to 92 ms at 1020 × 754). A million triangles is about 1.5 s of tessellation at worst on the development machine (1.5 µs per triangle beyond the cost per face) and about 25 ms per GPU frame; the task's suggestion was two million, halved for those two numbers. The CPU viewport follows the same rule rather than a coarser one: its cost is per sample, and the flange's extra triangles cost it a quarter of a sharp image, nothing while orbiting (the preview is 7 ms either way); at scale (`viewport_cost`, 2026-10-13) a scene of 665,104 triangles costs it 176 ms at rest and 30 ms while moving at 1020 × 777 against 123 and 8 ms for 12,796 triangles, 0.08 µs per triangle at rest and 0.03 µs while moving, so the budget of a million adds at most about 80 ms to a sharp image and 30 ms to a preview; the GPU draws that scene in 16 to 18 ms and uploads it once in 60 ms. `ui.screenshot` renders the scene the viewport shows (not the automatic one) because it is the picture of the window and it costs nothing (the scene is cached); the oracle property is the CPU renderer's, not the mesh's, and `view.render` still gives the automatic mesh. The budget stops a body inside the tessellator since 2026-10-13 (`TessParams::max_triangles`, architecture.md §6): a body that alone exceeds it is no longer tessellated in full before the tolerance is coarsened. Known limit: the granularity is a face, so one face that alone exceeds the budget still is.

### 2026-10-12: Persistent names, first version (M6)

### 2026-10-12: Sketches (start of M5)

Decided by the developer, from options the agent put forward:

- **A sketch lies on a world plane with an offset.** `xy`, `yz` or `zx`, moved along the normal by an expression. The plane is stored as a tagged enum, so datum planes and planar faces of bodies can join it in M6 without breaking files (a face of an extrusion did, the same day). On `xy` the sketch axes are world X and Y, on `yz` they are Y and Z, on `zx` they are Z and X: always right-handed, with the normal as the third axis.
- **The full constraint list from the first version:** coincident, point on curve, horizontal, vertical, parallel, perpendicular, tangent, equal, concentric, symmetric, midpoint, fixed, distance (also horizontal and vertical), angle, radius, diameter. One format version (3), no later migration for the missing ones.
- **Profile detection moves to M6,** next to extrude, its first user. M5 ends at a solved sketch with degrees of freedom and diagnosis.

- **The sketch constraint solver is our own** (`craft3d-solve`), like the kernel and the tessellator: no SolveSpace, PlaneGCS or other solver library, and its linear algebra is written in the crate (the default of D-open-5). The brainstorm named only the kernel and the tessellator; the developer confirmed on 2026-10-12 that the solver belongs in the same list.

Made by the agent while building it; the developer can overturn any of them:

- **Coordinates in a sketch are plain numbers,** in the document's length unit, not expressions: they say where something was drawn and are overwritten by the solver. Only dimensions (and the plane offset) are expressions.
- **The stored coordinates are the last solution.** Every edit ends by solving all sketches and storing the result in the same undo step, so a changed variable moves the sketches that use it, a saved file holds the solved shape, and the next solve starts from where the sketch is (no flip to a mirror solution).
- **A constraint that conflicts is stored, not refused,** like a feature whose parameter cannot be evaluated: the sketch's status is `failed` and names the constraint; the rest of the sketch stays solved. A redundant constraint is reported but is not an error. While a sketch has a conflict its stored coordinates stay as they last were without one, so it comes back the way it was (same side, same branch) when the conflict is removed.
- **Horizontal and vertical distances are not signed:** the points keep the side of each other they are on.
- **Lines, circles and arcs are built on point entities** (a shared corner is one point), so "coincident" is rarely needed for a closed profile and a point cannot be deleted while something is built on it.
- **Unconstrained parts move as little as possible** (minimum-norm step), so adding a constraint to a rough drawing disturbs only what it has to.

### 2026-10-12: Decisions made while building M2

Made by the agent; the developer can overturn any of them.

- **A bare number in a length parameter is in the document's unit.** `{"size": [40, 20, 5]}` in a millimetre document means millimetres, as typing a dimension does in any CAD system. Inside a formula the expression language stays strict: `10 mm + 5` is still a dimension error.
- **A feature whose parameters cannot be evaluated is stored, not refused.** `feature.block` with an unknown variable succeeds and returns `"status": "failed"` with the reason; the recipe is the user's, and fixing the variable fixes the feature. A command is refused only when the request itself is malformed.
- **Undo history is snapshots,** not reversible operations (see `crates/ops`). Chosen because the document is a small recipe, and because an edit applied to a copy cannot leave the document half-changed. Two known limits, both on the [roadmap](roadmap.md): each step copies the whole document, so large imported bodies must be shared between snapshots before M10; and snapshots cannot merge concurrent edits, which would need reversible operations (M12+ candidate). The developer agreed to this on 2026-10-12.
- **Saving validates.** `.3dcraft` files are only written from documents that pass `Document::validate()`, so everything written can be read back. A document made invalid by a bug can therefore not be saved; that is preferred over writing a file that will not open.
- **`craft3d-zipstore`** is a new standalone crate: the ZIP code is shared by 3MF export (`meshio`) and the native format. `meshio` → `zipstore` is the one listed exception to "standalone crates depend on no workspace crate".
- **`serde` and `serde_json`** are workspace dependencies (document model, file format, command parameters).
- **Default export and render tolerance** is a thousandth of the side of a square with the bodies' total area, so it follows the size of the model; `tolerance` overrides it.
- **The roadmap's M2 and the start of M3 were built together** (primitives as features, regeneration, B-rep tessellation), because a document without bodies could not be checked by any oracle.

### 2026-10-14: Decisions made while building sweep along a path

The developer was not there to ask; each of these is the smallest decision that let the work go on, and each can be overturned.

- **Format version 10 for the `sweep` feature kind**, by the rule in `crates/format/src/migrate.rs`: nothing a version-9 file holds changes meaning, and the version is raised all the same so that an older build reports the version and not an unknown kind. One step that changes nothing (`v9_to_v10`), a fixture of a version-9 file (a fillet and a loft), and the tests that pin the number moved on by one. (The sweep was built as version 9 while the fillet, built the same night, took 9 as well; the fillet reached `work` first, so the sweep became 10 when the branches met. Nothing was ever written by a released build as a version-9 file with a sweep in it.)
- **The profile has to be square to the path where the path passes through its plane**, and the path has to pass through that plane: at an end of an open path (within the linear tolerance), anywhere along a closed one. A profile that leans is refused with the angle; one within 1e-6 rad is laid into the square plane. Nothing is moved along the path to meet a profile that was drawn elsewhere: a path that starts 3 mm from the profile's plane is an error that says so, not a sweep from somewhere the user did not draw. The profile need not be *on* the path: its place beside the path is kept all the way, which is how a rail off a centre line or a moulding inside a frame is made.
- **Tangent means within 1e-6 rad** (`sweep::SMOOTH`), not within the document's angular tolerance of 1e-9: a path that a solver made tangent is tangent to 1e-9 or so, and at 1e-6 the pieces still fit to second order (a millionth of a millionth of the profile's size). Above it two lines are a corner and get a mitre, and a line and an arc are an error.
- **A corner is mitred, and only between two lines.** Both sides end on the bisecting plane, so the joint is flat, the edges in it are lines and ellipses, and nothing is approximated. A corner at an arc or a spline would need a joint that is not flat; it is refused with the two curves and the angle. A mitre is too sharp when, on some line of the path, the mitres at its two ends cross within the profile (the line is shorter than what they take of it on the inside): an error that names the corner, what the profile reaches there and what is left of the line. A single mitre between two long enough lines is never too sharp, up to a turn of 179.9°, beyond which the path counts as turning back on itself.
- **One face for a profile line that is parallel to the path's plane**, along the whole path: it sweeps one plane whatever the path does, and seams across it would be seams nobody drew. It is named after the profile's line alone (`F9:side(e3)`), which also keeps its name when the path gains or loses a bend. Other faces that happen to continue each other (two collinear lines of a path) are not merged. The boolean merges coplanar faces too, so the union of an extrusion, a revolution and an extrusion has as many faces as the sweep, not more.
- **A circle that is the whole path is two half turns, a closed spline two pieces**, as a full revolution of a spline is two halves and a closed spline profile two segments: no face goes all the way round and meets itself in a seam. Their faces are told apart by a number (`F9:piece(1)/…`).
- **Along a spline: half the document's tolerance, by raising the degree before the number of stations.** The faces are polynomials of degree 5 to 9 along the path, one per part of a span, through Chebyshev points. Cubics through evenly spaced stations, tried first, gave faces with a knot line per station, on some of which `topo::mass_properties` gave up ("a boundary integral that does not settle"); how many stations they need for the same tolerance was not measured. The deviation is measured, not derived, at three places between stations; the bound a caller may rely on is half the tolerance where it was measured. A tolerance no plan reaches (the most is sixteen parts of degree 9 per span) is an error that says what was reached.
- **A fold is refused when the radius of curvature of a spline comes within 2 % of the profile's reach** on the inside, at 64 samples per span: a margin, since the samples need not hit the tightest place. An arc is refused only when the profile reaches its axis to within the tolerance (that is exact).
- **`FeatureResult` has a field for what a sweep says of itself** (`sweep: Option<SweepReport>`), as it has one for where a datum lies; `document.inspect` shows it under `path`.
- **The regeneration check for self-intersection is shared with the loft** (`regen/src/selfcheck.rs`); `regen/src/loft.rs` now calls it and says what it said before.
- **Curve ids in the kernel's sweep errors are written as the sketch writes them** (`e7`), in the text of `KernelError::Sweep`: the ids a kernel operation gets are documented as sketch entity ids, and a message that has to be rewritten by every caller is one that will be shown raw somewhere.
- **A path that is not in one plane is not built**, and needs the developer: how does the profile turn about the path? What I would do: rotation-minimising frames by the double reflection method (Wang, Jüttler, Zheng and Liu, *Computation of Rotation Minimizing Frames*, 2008), which for a path in a plane gives exactly today's frame, for lines and arcs in different planes is exact per piece (a line carries the frame unchanged, an arc turns it about its axis), and for a closed path leaves an angle between the last frame and the first that has to be spread along the path or refused. The path would be two or more sketches joined end to end, or a 3D polyline through datum points.

### 2026-10-14: Decisions made while building loft

Made by the agent, overnight; the developer can overturn any of them.

- **Format version 8 for the `loft` feature kind.** Nothing a version-7 file holds changes meaning, and AGENTS.md §8 asks for a new version only then; `crates/format/src/migrate.rs` asks for one whenever an older build could not read what a new kind writes, so that it reports the version and not an unknown kind (as versions 2, 3, 5, 6 and, for the chamfer the same night, 7 did). The second rule was followed: a build that knows version 7 knows chamfers and not lofts. The four features of 2026-10-13 that went in as optional fields without a new version are untouched. **For the developer to confirm**, with the question of which rule stands (and whether two kinds added in one night before any release should share a version).
- **How sections are matched.** Loops are walked the same way round by geometry (counter-clockwise looking back along the loft), not by how their sketch planes face, so there is no `reverse` parameter. The first section starts at its smallest curve id; later sections are turned to lie nearest to the one before, and `start` overrides that per section. The alternative, every section from its smallest id, twists a loft by default whenever a sketch plane faces the other way or a profile was drawn from another corner; the price of the rule chosen is that the matching, and with it the names of the side faces, follows the geometry: an edit that turns a section past the next corner changes them (references then fail; they never point at another face). Giving `start` on every section pins the matching.
- **Sections with different numbers of curves are cut by length,** at the fractions of the loop where the other sections have their corners, rather than refused. Corners within a thousandth of the loop of each other count as one, so that a sliver of a face is not made for a difference nobody drew.
- **A whole circle is four faces** in a loft with B-spline sides (four quarter arcs), and one face without a seam only where the side is a cylinder or a cone. A B-spline face that closes on itself would need a seam edge, which the tessellator and the measure handle on analytic surfaces only.
- **The parts a curve was cut in share its name** (`F9:side(e2,e2)` four times for a quartered circle), as the pieces of a face a boolean cut do. A name role with a part number would tell them apart; it was not added, to leave `doc/src/name.rs` alone while other features are being built on it.
- **`regen` depends on `tess`** (a lower layer), for the self-intersection check of every loft: the kernel cannot use the tessellator (same layer), and a loft that passes through itself must never be handed on. The check is the one of `document.check {"deep": true}`, with its parameters; it costs a mesh per rebuilt loft (the showcase hull with its hollowing: 0.6 s for the whole script).
- **Smooth means interpolation in homogeneous coordinates** for sections with arcs. Where the weights of the surface between the sections would not stay positive the loft fails and says so (make it ruled, or draw the sections with splines); none of the tested shapes does.
