# Decisions

> **Last updated:** 2026-10-14

Decisions that shape the project, newest first, each with its reason. An agent that needs something listed under *Open decisions* asks the developer; it does not pick silently. When one is settled, move it up with the date.

## Taken

### 2026-10-13: Decided by the developer

- **The boolean kernel is the first priority:** it is what makes 3DCraft a professional tool. A case the kernel refuses is work owed, not an end state; the checklist is in [`roadmap.md`](roadmap.md).
- **The kernel's tests may use the tessellator** (a dev-dependency inside one layer, listed as `SAME_LAYER_DEV` in `xtask/src/layers.rs`): every body an operation makes is also meshed as an independent check. The kernel's own code still does not depend on `tess`.
- **Where FreeCAD and the kernel disagree in the boolean corpus:** eleven cases stay in `REFERENCE_OFF` (the reference is off, with a closed form, a direct integration or an identity as evidence); `near/cylinder-torus/009` is `UNDECIDED`, because nothing independent measures our result there. Building that measurement is not worth the time now.
- **The gate in two steps:** a quick check per commit (fmt, clippy and tests of the touched crates), the full `cargo xtask ci` only before `main` moves. The full run is the only automatic safety net and stays; it is too slow to pay per commit. Making it faster (the heavy random tests in release, test files side by side) is open.

### 2026-10-13: Waiting for the developer (presented, not yet decided)

Each stands as described until the developer says otherwise.

- **Extrude ends (2026-10-13, built by an agent on the M8.5 row).** Five choices, each easy to turn round: (1) *through all* is measured against the boxes of the bodies that exist at that point of the history and lie in the profile's way, and ends a hundredth of the way (at least a thousand tolerances) past the farthest, rather than flush with it or "infinitely" far; (2) *up to a face* ends on the face's **surface**: a profile that hangs over the face's edge ends on the plane, cylinder, sphere or torus continued, and only a profile that misses the face altogether is refused (the strict reading, "the whole end must lie within the face", is the alternative); (3) the direction of an extrusion up to a face is never guessed: a face behind the sketch plane is an error that names `reversed`, so an upstream edit that moves the face to the other side fails the feature instead of flipping the body; (4) the `.3dcraft` version stays 6: `end` and `reversed` are optional fields, written only when they are not the default, so an older build of version 6 reads every file that does not use them and refuses one that does as an unknown field, not as "a newer 3DCraft is needed"; (5) `feature.extrude` got parameters (`throughAll`, `upTo`, `reversed`) rather than a second command, and `reversed` also flips a `distance`.

- **A ball that dips half a tolerance to two tolerances into a block is refused.** "Touches" is also defensible; the refusal costs 18 corpus cases.
- **Meshes are bit-identical under scaling by powers of two, not by powers of ten** (Delaunay ties on cocircular points fall differently; the triangle count stays within 10%). A wider tie band in `in_circle` would remove most of it but changes the meshes at scale 1.
- **Pinched solids pass the check:** parts of one body that meet only along an edge or at a vertex.
- **Two tests that pinned a refusal were replaced by stricter tests of the value** when the refusal was lifted (the developer agreed to the layer exception that goes with it; the replacement itself was explained, not explicitly approved).
- **The viewport renders with the wgpu renderer by default (`--renderer auto`, `CRAFT3D_RENDERER`), the CPU renderer as the fallback** (2026-10-13, built by an agent on the developer's question whether the GPU is used). It matches the CPU renderer within the §7.2 tolerances, but the picture is read back from the GPU into the same frame the CPU renderer makes, and on this laptop's Intel driver that readback is slow (a few hundred MB/s): for a light model the GPU viewport is slower than the CPU one (20 against 7 ms per frame while orbiting at 1280 × 800; equal for a sharp frame). The way to a fast GPU viewport is no readback (the colour as a texture on eframe's own device, the overlay's depth test on the GPU), not built. Whether `auto` should prefer the GPU until then is the developer's call; `--renderer cpu` gives the old behaviour. *Superseded the same day by the next bullet.*
- **The viewport draws on the window's own wgpu device, multisampled, with the lines drawn and depth-tested on the GPU; nothing is read back for display** (2026-10-13, built by an agent after the developer compared the two windows: the CPU viewport felt a little smoother than the GPU one, and in both the lines were not smooth, least of all while turning the model). What it does now: `craft3d_gpu::Onscreen` on eframe's `RenderState` draws the bodies with 4 samples per pixel and the overlay as screen-space quads inked by the CPU's own line formula and hidden by the CPU viewport's rule in the fragment shader, into a texture registered with egui; every frame is at the viewport's full size and sharp, also while moving, and a frame is drawn only when something changed. The trade-offs: (1) the CPU viewport, the readback path and `ui.screenshot` are unchanged, so the oracle stays, at the price of a second implementation of the line test (its decisions are checked against the CPU's at 1812 of 1812 sample points); (2) the GPU tests against the true depth and the CPU against its screen-interpolated distance, so the GPU allows a line the depth spread of its 3 × 3 neighbourhood as well, which keeps the edge of a hollow (a post on a plate) visible as the CPU's interpolation does by accident; (3) multisampling changes the colour of edge pixels by design, so the parity test does not compare colours inside the band and allows a pixel of gradient on the seams between triangles of one face (documented in §7.2); (4) on wgpu's OpenGL backend the picture has one sample, because that backend cannot make the depth buffer the lines need, multisampled and read by a shader (investigated 2026-10-13 in wgpu-hal 30.0.1, `src/gles`): a multisampled texture that is more than a render target is given the target `GL_TEXTURE_2D` (`mod.rs`, `Texture::get_info_from_desc`; `GL_TEXTURE_2D_MULTISAMPLE` occurs nowhere in the backend) and its storage with `glTexStorage2DMultisample(GL_TEXTURE_2D, …)` (`device.rs`, `create_texture`), which OpenGL refuses, so the texture has no storage, the framebuffer it is attached to is incomplete, and every clear, draw and resolve into it fails silently. Mesa says so with `MESA_DEBUG=1` (`GL_INVALID_ENUM in glTexStorage2DMultisample(target=GL_TEXTURE_2D)`, then `GL_INVALID_FRAMEBUFFER_OPERATION` in `glClearBufferfv`, `glClear` and `glBlitFramebuffer`), and `crates/gpu/tests/gl_multisample.rs` pins it on every adapter: four samples resolve on OpenGL without a depth buffer and with one that is only an attachment (a multisampled renderbuffer), and not with one a shader reads; on Vulkan all three do. It is a limitation of wgpu-hal, not of the driver or of how the resolve is set up, and nothing in wgpu's downlevel flags or format features announces it. Multisampling on OpenGL would take a depth buffer for the lines made at one sample in a pass of its own (the bodies drawn a second time, depth only) and a lines shader that reads one sample: not built, OpenGL being the window's last resort. The test fails the day wgpu's backend learns it; (5) `RenderStats::milliseconds` on the direct path is the drawing thread's time, not the GPU's: the window never waits for the device. The developer can overturn any of it; `--renderer cpu` gives the CPU viewport.
- **See-through bodies are drawn by the GPU with two depth layers; five choices the developer may overturn** (2026-10-14, built by an agent while the developer slept; the method and the measurements are in [`architecture.md`](architecture.md) §7.2, *See-through on the GPU*). (1) *The comparison between the layers is strict and has no bias.* Both passes rasterise the same triangles on the same device, so a surface is never its own "behind" without one, and a bias would hide a second surface that is really there; the price is that two surfaces closer together than a 32-bit depth buffer resolves (about 10⁻⁷ of the frame's depth range) count as one on the GPU where the CPU renderer still separates them. (2) *In the multisampled viewport the front is told from what is behind it by a key per sample (the bits of the front triangle's depth at the pixel centre), not by depth*, because the per-sample depth a shader gets is not the depth buffer's on Intel's Vulkan driver (measured). It costs one more `Rgba8Unorm` target with the viewport's samples, and it relies on the same triangle giving the same bits in two passes, which the tests check on every adapter of the development machine but which Metal, DX12 and a browser's WebGPU have not been seen to do; if one of them does not, a body would look opaque through itself there, and the remedy is a triangle id in place of the depth bits. (3) *The blend is a 256 × 256 table of the CPU renderer's own expression*, so that the two pictures agree to the level also where the blend lies half-way between two levels; the expression is written a second time in `crates/gpu/src/peel.rs`, pinned by the parity tests. (4) *Over a see-through picture the viewport's lines are drawn on the resolved picture at one sample*, not into the multisampled target as over an opaque one (the blend of the layers is itself the resolve); they are inked by distance, so they look the same. (5) *A device that cannot draw the layers falls back to the CPU renderer for see-through frames only* (`GpuError::SeeThrough`), as every device did before; none of the three adapters here needs it. Also decided: the extra targets are kept once made, until the viewport changes size (three colour layers and a depth buffer at four samples: about 50 MB at 1020 × 754), rather than freed when the bodies are opaque again. A finding left for the developer: the CPU renderer draws a surface opaque where a sample lies exactly on an edge shared by two of its triangles (§7.2); the oracle was not changed.
- **The viewport's mesh is finer than the export's: a display quality of its own, as UI state** (2026-10-13, made by the agent after the developer saw the bolt holes of the flange as polygons; the developer can overturn). The viewport asks the engine for `SceneQuality::Display { relative, max_triangles }` (architecture.md §6): a chordal tolerance of `relative` times the model's size, coarsened until the scene has at most `max_triangles` triangles and every body is within the tessellator's reach, but never beyond the automatic tolerance `view.render`, previews and `file.export` keep (1e-3 of the size), which is unchanged. The defaults, `ui.set {"quality": {"chordal": 3e-5, "maxTriangles": 1000000}}`: 3e-5 is a chord error of a thirtieth of a pixel on a model that fills a 1020-pixel viewport (a thirteenth at 2560), so a circle stays round at ten times that zoom; on the flange it means 4328 triangles instead of 820 and a chord error of 0.02 px instead of 0.66 (1.4 at 2560 × 1400), at no measurable cost on the GPU (0.2 to 0.5 ms per frame on the drawing thread either way) and 18 ms more on the CPU viewport's sharp image (74 to 92 ms at 1020 × 754). A million triangles is about 1.5 s of tessellation at worst on the development machine (1.5 µs per triangle beyond the cost per face) and about 25 ms per GPU frame; the task's suggestion was two million, halved for those two numbers. The CPU viewport follows the same rule rather than a coarser one: its cost is per sample, and the flange's extra triangles cost it a quarter of a sharp image, nothing while orbiting (the preview is 7 ms either way); at scale (`viewport_cost`, 2026-10-13) a scene of 665,104 triangles costs it 176 ms at rest and 30 ms while moving at 1020 × 777 against 123 and 8 ms for 12,796 triangles, 0.08 µs per triangle at rest and 0.03 µs while moving, so the budget of a million adds at most about 80 ms to a sharp image and 30 ms to a preview; the GPU draws that scene in 16 to 18 ms and uploads it once in 60 ms. `ui.screenshot` renders the scene the viewport shows (not the automatic one) because it is the picture of the window and it costs nothing (the scene is cached); the oracle property is the CPU renderer's, not the mesh's, and `view.render` still gives the automatic mesh. The budget stops a body inside the tessellator since 2026-10-13 (`TessParams::max_triangles`, architecture.md §6): a body that alone exceeds it is no longer tessellated in full before the tolerance is coarsened. Known limit: the granularity is a face, so one face that alone exceeds the budget still is.

### 2026-10-14: STEP export and its reader (made by the agent during the night; the developer can overturn any of them)

- **STEP writes the knots of every B-spline curve on the interval from 0 to 1**, whatever they are in the body. It is a courtesy to a reader, not something the standard asks: FreeCAD called the bearing housing invalid at the scale 1e6, and the experiments ([`file-formats.md`](file-formats.md) › *What a reader made of the knots*) showed its answer follows the knots of the closed spline edges, which the kernel parametrises by length. The curve is the same point set, nothing else in a file refers to a curve's parameter, and a curve already on [0, 1] is written bit for bit. To overturn: remove the call of `unit_interval` in `crates/io/src/step.rs`; the housing and four sleeves with an oil hole in `crates/engine/tests/step_freecad/features.rs` then fail at 1e6. An alternative not taken: have the kernel parametrise its intersection curves on [0, 1]; that is the kernel's to decide, and the file should not depend on it either way.
- **Parameter-space curves are still not written.** They were the suspected cause and are not it: written by hand for the edge that trips FreeCAD, they change nothing, because it makes its own on import whether the file has them or not. They remain what AP214 offers for readers that do use them (`SURFACE_CURVE`, `SEAM_CURVE`, `PCURVE` in a `DEFINITIONAL_REPRESENTATION`); with FreeCAD as the only outside reader on this machine there is nothing to measure them against, and a 2D spline of ours that a reader trusts is one more thing that can be wrong. Worth taking up when a second reader is at hand (one not built on OpenCascade), or with STEP import, which needs the same mapping the other way.
- **Seam edges are still not written.** One measured reason to write them exists: FreeCAD's own seam fails on a cylinder of a radius above some 4.5e8 mm, and the same file with a seam written by hand is read right. That is 450 km in a document in millimetres, far outside the sizes 3DCraft is tested at (1e-3 to 1e6), and a seam of ours means the writer cutting faces and edges the kernel does not have (the counts in the file would no longer be the body's). Left as it is; the limit is recorded in [`testing.md`](testing.md).

### 2026-10-13: Made during the night of 13 to 14 October (the developer asked for autonomous work)

Made by the agent; the developer can overturn any of them.

- **Chamfers are made with a tool solid per edge and the boolean, not with a local operation** (2026-10-14, M9; the task left the route open). The reasons and the price are in architecture §5.3 › *Chamfers as built*: the boolean kernel is measured, checks or refuses its result and names every entity, and it handled every contact the tools produce (faces flush with the neighbours, tools that overlap or end against each other, a corner plane through three vertices) without one refusal; a local operation is still owed for fillets, and for chamfers the tool route cannot do (below). To overturn: the kernel's `chamfer` keeps its signature, so a local operation can replace the inside.
- **A chamfer holds an edge as the two faces it lies between, in order, not as the edge's own name** (2026-10-14). The order says which face gets the first distance; the pair survives a later cut of the edge (both pieces stay chamfered) and never holds a numbered name. The price: one of several edges between the same two faces cannot be picked alone. The body chamfered is the one the first face belongs to; there is no `source` parameter.
- **Where three chamfers meet the corner is a flat triangle through the points where the cut lines cross** (2026-10-14), for unequal distances too; two chamfers meet in a mitre. It is what OpenCascade gives (measured through FreeCAD on a block: same volume, area and counts) and what the task named as usual. The pointed corner where the three chamfer planes meet is not offered; it would be a parameter (`corner: mitre`).
- **The angle of a distance-and-angle chamfer is between the first face and the chamfer face** (2026-10-14), and the first face is the first of the pair. Other modellers ask the user to pick the face; here the pair's order is that pick.
- **Chamfer faces are named by their place in the chamfer's own list of edges** (`F9:chamfer(0)`), and by a count in the order of where they lie in space when one reference gave several edges (`F9:chamfer(0,2)`) (2026-10-14). A name by the two faces was not possible: edges and vertices list their faces by name, and a listed name lists none itself. Removing an edge from the list renumbers the ones after it; the numbered form has the limit of every numbered name (architecture §4.1).
- **Format version 7 for the chamfer** (2026-10-14): a new feature kind and two new name roles, which a version-6 build cannot read; AGENTS.md §8 and `migrate.rs` ask for a version then. Five pinned version numbers in `crates/format/tests/format.rs` went from 6 to 7 with it, and a version-6 fixture was added. *For the developer to confirm*, together with the open question whether the datums, the extrude ends and the names by faces of 13 and 14 October should each have been a version (they were not, and this does not settle it).
- **`feature.edit` changes the form of a chamfer by what is given** (2026-10-14): `distance` alone keeps the form, with `second` it becomes two distances, with `angle` a distance and an angle. There is no way back to "one distance" other than giving `second` the same expression, which is the same solid.

- **`feature.move` never refuses an order; a feature that comes before something it is made from fails** (2026-10-14, M8.5; the roadmap left the choice open). The reason names the later feature and the remedy (`… comes after this feature in the history; move it before (feature.move)`), what is made from the failed feature fails naming it, everything else is built, and undo restores exactly. Refusing was the alternative. It was not taken because moving several features one after the other passes through orders that do not build (a sketch and its extrusion moved up past the body they lie on), because `feature.delete` and `feature.suppress` do not refuse to break a reference either, and because the tests of the extrude ends and of the regeneration cache already pin that such a move is accepted and fails the dependant.
- **A circle of references is reported as a circle** (2026-10-14). A feature that needs a later feature which is itself made from it (a datum plane put on a face of the body sketched on that plane) cannot be built in any order; its reason says so instead of advising `feature.move`. A reference to the feature itself says `is this feature itself`. Three pinned wordings changed for it (`crates/regen/tests/datums.rs`, `crates/engine/tests/datums.rs`), each with the reason in a comment.
- **Deleting the feature the rollback marker is on leaves the marker on the feature before it** (2026-10-14). Until then the marker was cleared, and everything that had been rolled back came back at once. Only for the first feature of the history is it still cleared: the marker has no way to say "before the first feature".
- **`causedBy`** (2026-10-14): a feature that fails because something it is made from failed carries, in `document.inspect`, `document.check` and the results of feature commands, the id of the feature at the start of that chain. The reasons themselves were left as they are (each names the feature it directly waits for); naming the root in the text would have changed a dozen pinned wordings for no gain over the field.

- **A meeting curve through a point where the surfaces are tangent is built, also with a freeform face** (it replaces the refusal of the same day). The case is common with freeform faces: the tangent plane of a roof at a point where the roof is a saddle cuts the roof in two branches that cross at the touch, and a ball, a cone or a torus set against a roof does the same. The refusal was taken because this kernel and FreeCAD disagreed on six such cases by a fifth of the lens. The common volume integrated directly (lines along the dome's axis: the dome is the stretch from its floor to its roof, a Bernstein polynomial; the other solid the stretch between the roots of its faces' equations) settles it for ours: `tangent/sphere-dome/007` common 0.0064231926 integrated, ours 0.0064231919, FreeCAD 0.0053844056 (it loses one of the two lumps); `tangent/torus-dome/001` 0.0536391766, ours 0.0536390870, FreeCAD 0 (no common volume at all); `tangent/cone-dome/005` 0.1764729109, ours 0.1764729716, FreeCAD 0.1655206124.
- **The corpus test judges a volume disagreement by that integral** where the pair is a domed block and a primitive: when our three results are the ones that follow from the integrated common volume (within the corpus's own bound) and the reference's is off by more than that bound, or ten times further off than any of ours, the outcome is unscored ("reference volume") and listed in the report with the numbers. A disagreement the integral does not decide stays a mismatch. This is the third independent judge next to the sampled volume and the area identity; unlike `REFERENCE_OFF` it is computed on every run, not a list.
- **A touch between surfaces is recorded only where a floor cell of the marcher would have reached it**: within the tolerance and within the reach of the finest cell (half to one tolerance). A saddle of the distance exactly one tolerance off the other surface is no crossing: its two branches pass thousands of tolerances apart.
- **The intersection of surfaces settles a disc around a point of tangency by the second-order model there**, checked on two rings of samples (architecture §5.2, *The numeric method*). It is an estimate of the same standing as the sampled curvature of a freeform field, not a proof.

### 2026-10-13: Made during the night of 12 to 13 October (the developer was asleep)

Made by the agent on the developer's instruction to decide by recommendation; the developer can overturn any of them.

- **A sketch on a cap survives a change of the cap's outline** (field test, finding 1). All caps of one role of one extrusion lie in one plane with one frame, so for the plane of a sketch a cap name resolves to that plane as long as the feature has a cap of that role, also when the outline in the name is gone. `F2:endCap()` is accepted as "where F2 ends". This is not a retarget: the sketch lands exactly where it was. It replaces "a cap is named after all curves of its outer loop, and a reference then fails" below, for sketch planes only; a later consumer that needs the face itself (not its plane) still gets the exact match or nothing. The stored name is not rewritten.
- **A sketch on a side face is measured from the origin of the extruded sketch, dropped onto the face** (field test, finding 2), not from the start of the line that made the face. Files written on 12 October with a sketch on a side face whose line does not start at that point place the sketch differently now; format 4 was never released.
- **STEP export declares AP214** (was D-open-8, the default taken): `AUTOMOTIVE_DESIGN` with the common B-rep subset (`ADVANCED_BREP_SHAPE_REPRESENTATION`). Units and the uncertainty come from the document; angles are written in the document's angle unit; the file carries no time stamp, so the same document gives the same bytes; no parameter-space curves, colours, assemblies or entity names yet. FreeCAD reads it; AP242 was not tried.
- **A revolution turns about a line in its sketch's plane:** the sketch's x or y axis, or one of its lines by entity id (usually a construction line), whose direction from start to end sets which way a positive angle goes (counter-clockwise seen from its tip). "Start" is the cap in the sketch plane, as for an extrusion; a full turn has no caps.
- **Robust predicates (was D-open-6): tolerant floating point only, for now.** The boolean kernel for planes and cylinders takes no decision on the sign of a number that could be rounding: membership is a winding number or a parity, points merge at half the tolerance, and input within a band of the tolerance (up to twice) or within 1e-6 rad of tangent or parallel is refused as unsupported. Measured: 0 wrong or invalid results in 13,500 seeded random pairs at scales 1e-3, 1 and 1e6. The default in the table below was "adaptive exact for orientation predicates"; revisit when general polyhedra or freeform faces enter booleans.
- **Booleans: one feature kind with an operation** (`feature.boolean` with `op`: union, subtract, intersect), a target and a tool. Both bodies go into the result; `keepTool` leaves the tool its body. `feature.extrude` and `feature.revolve` take `cut`, `join` or `intersect` with a target and then add the sweep and the boolean as two features in one undo step.
- **`document.check` takes `deep`:** it then also looks for bodies that pass through themselves (`tess::body_self_intersections`, on a mesh, independent of the kernel). A finding is reported only when it is deeper than the mesh and the tolerance could explain; the rest is counted as marginal. Two shells of one body that lie face on face count as a finding.
- **An edge is referred to by the two faces it lies between,** not by a name of its own: that works for every body (primitives, sweeps, copies, booleans) with the one naming scheme faces already have, and gives a list, because two faces can meet in several edges. `document.edges` answers it today; fillets and chamfers will take the same pair.
- **Names through a boolean are copies** like those of a transform: instance 0 the target, 1 the tool. Pieces of one face share one name, and a sketch accepts that (one plane). Edges and vertices of a boolean are named by their faces (below).
- **A merged face answers to the name of every face it was merged from** (2026-10-13, made by the agent; the developer can overturn). When the kernel merges faces of both operands into one (the flush tops of two united blocks; the caps of touching instances a pattern joins), the face is named after the first contributor in the kernel's order (the target's lowest-numbered face, else the tool's) and carries the names of the others as aliases, derived at regeneration and listed by `document.inspect` as `faceAliases`. Any of them resolves to the face (a sketch by the tool's cap name lands on the merged top, in the frame of the tool's cap), and `document.edges` takes either. Nothing is stored in the file and no existing name changes: a document from before resolves as it did. The alternatives were a name of its own for a merged face (`F3:merged(…)`, which would change on every edit that changes which faces merge) and a name that only the first contributor's name resolves (what we had: a reference by the tool's cap failed although the face was there). When the faces stop being flush the tool's name means the tool's own face again, which is there; it is `Missing` only when that face is gone.
- **Rational freeform faces are operands in booleans** (2026-10-13, late; made by the agent on the developer's order, it supersedes the refusal in the next bullet). The bounds come from the nets of the rational pieces (architecture §5.3, *Rational faces*): proven, not sampled, and taken from small pieces because they loosen with the spread of the weights. Three things stay refused, each with its reason: a rational face that lies on the surface of a face of the other body (coincidence goes by the kind of the surfaces; recognising a freeform surface as the quadric it is, or matching two freeform surfaces, is work owed), a patch with a collapsed border, and weights more than a factor of a thousand apart within one span (the developer can move that number: it is where the bounds stop being of use, not a law).
- *Superseded for rational faces on 2026-10-13 (late) by the bullet above:* **A freeform face in a boolean: polynomial Bézier and B-spline faces are operands like any other, rational ones are refused** (2026-10-13, made by the agent; the developer can overturn). The kernel indexes such a face with a tree of boxes around pieces of its surface (architecture §5.3, *Freeform faces*), which gives a bound below the distance that never overestimates, the nearest point and the signed distance; the chart of the face is its own parameter rectangle, which needs a bound on how fast the parameters move, and that bound comes from the Bézier nets of a polynomial surface and does not exist for a rational one yet. Where a freeform face meets another surface the curve is a spline within the tolerance (as for a cylinder and a torus), and the surfaces count as crossing: no lens is looked for.
- **Edges and vertices of a boolean are named by their faces, with the name they had as an alias** (2026-10-14, made by the agent; the developer can overturn). In a body a boolean made, or a pattern that joined its instances, an edge is `F7:between[a,b]` and a vertex `F7:corner[a,b,c]`, the faces by their names in that body; one the kernel says an operand already had also answers to the name first given to it (`F7:copy(0)/F1:vertex(6)`). When several lie on the same faces each also lists the faces near it after `|`, and when no face tells them apart a number by where they lie in space, the one part of a name an edit can move to another entity without a failure (architecture §4.1 has the scheme, the limits and the minimal example). Choices made that can be overturned: (1) the own name is the one by faces and the origin is the alias, not the other way round, because the faces are there for every entity and for a joined pattern too, and an alias is ambiguous as soon as an edge is cut in two; (2) the bare name is kept as long as it is unique, at the cost that a second entity on the same faces takes it away from the first; (3) the number is by x, y, z in the world, not by the kernel's order (which the task ruled out) and not by anything intrinsic, because for two concentric circles nothing intrinsic exists; (4) primitives, sweeps and copies keep the names they had: no name that existed changed; (5) the file format is still version 6: `faces` is an optional field of a name and `between` and `corner` two more roles, as the datums and the ends of an extrusion were added the same day, so a build from before reads such a file as damaged, not as "a newer 3DCraft is needed"; a bump to 7 is one line and a migration that does nothing.
- **A freeform face in a boolean: polynomial Bézier and B-spline faces are operands like any other, rational ones are refused** (2026-10-13, made by the agent; the developer can overturn). The kernel indexes such a face with a tree of boxes around pieces of its surface (architecture §5.3, *Freeform faces*), which gives a bound below the distance that never overestimates, the nearest point and the signed distance; the chart of the face is its own parameter rectangle, which needs a bound on how fast the parameters move, and that bound comes from the Bézier nets of a polynomial surface and does not exist for a rational one yet. Where a freeform face meets another surface the curve is a spline within the tolerance (as for a cylinder and a torus), and the surfaces count as crossing: no lens is looked for.
- *Superseded on 2026-10-13 (night) by the bullet under "Made during the night of 13 to 14 October":* **An intersection curve of a freeform face that passes through a point where the surfaces are tangent is refused** ("a crossing through a touch"; a ball, a cone or a torus set against a dome's roof, touching at one point and cutting in beside it). The six such cases of the corpus gave volumes a fifth of the lens away from FreeCAD's; refusing costs 216 outcomes of the tangent category. Tangent contact with a freeform face is work owed (checklist item 2).
- **The corpus test judges an area-only disagreement by the area identity** `area(A ∪ B) + area(A ∩ B) = area A + area B`, which holds where nothing coincides: when our three results satisfy it to 1e-7 and the reference's to ten times less well, the reference's area is off (FreeCAD integrates the area of a spline face numerically and does not take it to convergence) and the outcome is unscored, not a mismatch. Twelve `near` cases with a dome are judged so; their volumes and centroids agree.
- **What the boolean kernel cannot do is an error, never a body:** cones, spheres, tori, freeform faces, cylinders that cross at an angle, tangent contact, contact only along an edge or at a vertex, and input at the edge of the tolerance. Faces are never merged after a boolean (a union of flush boxes keeps the seam).
- **The entities of a primitive are named by their number** (`F3:face(1)`): a primitive is built in a fixed order, so the number says where the entity came from. The order is part of the kernel's contract.
- **A feature made from another feature's body takes that body** (transform, mirror, pattern; later booleans). The source feature stays in the history and its names still resolve, but its body is no longer listed, rendered or exported; `keep` makes a copy instead. A body can be taken once: the second taker fails and says who has it. The alternative, features that edit a shared body in place, would have made the order of independent features matter.
- **A pattern counts the source as instance 0** and gives one body with all instances. A circular pattern takes the angle between neighbours (`360 deg / n` goes evenly round), a linear one the step between neighbours; both are expressions, and so is the count. Instances that touch or overlap are joined by the kernel's union, one after the other (2026-10-13; before, they were put side by side unchecked): side by side they would leave faces inside the material. When the union cannot be made the pattern fails with the kernel's reason. Instances that are apart are merged as they are and keep their edge and vertex names.
- **Names of copied entities are the original name with a prefix** (`F5:copy(2)/F2:side(e7)`), so one scheme serves transforms, mirrors, patterns and, later, booleans. File format 5.
- **`file.list` and `file.activate`** (field test, finding 5): a document is referred to by its number in the list of open documents, which is what `file.new` and `file.open` already returned, or by its path. Numbers shift when a document before it is closed; a stable document id was not introduced.

### 2026-10-12: Persistent names, first version (M6)

Made by the agent while building the first consumer (a sketch on a face); the developer can overturn any of them. The scheme itself is described in [`architecture.md`](architecture.md) §4.1.

- **A name is made of the feature, the role and the input entities** (`F2:side(e7)`), never of an index or a position. It resolves to exactly the face with that origin or to nothing.
- **A reference that cannot be resolved is stored and fails,** with the reason and the names that do exist, like every other feature whose input is not there. It is never repaired by guessing.
- **Start and end of an extrusion are about the sketch plane,** not about which side is lower: the start cap lies in the sketch plane, the end cap at the far side. A symmetric extrusion starts on the side the sketch normal points away from.
- **A sketch on a face looks at the face from outside,** with the frame described in the architecture document. The price: turning an extrusion round mirrors a sketch on its end cap. Keeping the position instead would mean a sketch whose normal points into the body.
- **A sketch on a side face is measured from the plane of the extruded sketch,** not from where the extrusion starts, so it does not move when the extrusion gets deeper or symmetric.
- **A cap is named after all curves of its profile's outer loop.** Adding or replacing a side of that loop renames the cap, and a reference to it then fails; a hole drawn inside does not. Whether a cap should be found by a looser match is left until a real model needs it.

### 2026-10-12: Sketches (start of M5)

Decided by the developer, from options the agent put forward:

- **A sketch lies on a world plane with an offset.** `xy`, `yz` or `zx`, moved along the normal by an expression. The plane is stored as a tagged enum, so datum planes and planar faces of bodies can join it in M6 without breaking files (a face of an extrusion did, the same day). On `xy` the sketch axes are world X and Y, on `yz` they are Y and Z, on `zx` they are Z and X: always right-handed, with the normal as the third axis.
- **The full constraint list from the first version:** coincident, point on curve, horizontal, vertical, parallel, perpendicular, tangent, equal, concentric, symmetric, midpoint, fixed, distance (also horizontal and vertical), angle, radius, diameter. One format version (3), no later migration for the missing ones.
- **Profile detection moves to M6,** next to extrude, its first user. M5 ends at a solved sketch with degrees of freedom and diagnosis.

- **The sketch constraint solver is our own** (`craft3d-solve`), like the kernel and the tessellator: no SolveSpace, PlaneGCS or other solver library, and its linear algebra is written in the crate (the default of D-open-5). The brainstorm named only the kernel and the tessellator; the developer confirmed on 2026-10-12 that the solver belongs in the same list.

Made by the agent while building it; the developer can overturn any of them:

- **Coordinates in a sketch are plain numbers,** in the document's length unit, not expressions: they say where something was drawn and are overwritten by the solver. Only dimensions (and the plane offset) are expressions.
- **The stored coordinates are the last solution.** Every edit ends by solving all sketches and storing the result in the same undo step, so a changed variable moves the sketches that use it, a saved file holds the solved shape, and the next solve starts from where the sketch is (no flip to a mirror solution).
- **A constraint that conflicts is stored, not refused,** like a feature whose parameter cannot be evaluated: the sketch's status is `failed` and names the constraint; the rest of the sketch stays solved. A redundant constraint is reported but is not an error. While a sketch has a conflict its stored coordinates stay as they last were without one, so it comes back the way it was (same side, same branch) when the conflict is removed.
- **Horizontal and vertical distances are not signed:** the points keep the side of each other they are on.
- **Lines, circles and arcs are built on point entities** (a shared corner is one point), so "coincident" is rarely needed for a closed profile and a point cannot be deleted while something is built on it.
- **Unconstrained parts move as little as possible** (minimum-norm step), so adding a constraint to a rough drawing disturbs only what it has to.

### 2026-10-12: Decisions made while building M2

Made by the agent; the developer can overturn any of them.

- **A bare number in a length parameter is in the document's unit.** `{"size": [40, 20, 5]}` in a millimetre document means millimetres, as typing a dimension does in any CAD system. Inside a formula the expression language stays strict: `10 mm + 5` is still a dimension error.
- **A feature whose parameters cannot be evaluated is stored, not refused.** `feature.block` with an unknown variable succeeds and returns `"status": "failed"` with the reason; the recipe is the user's, and fixing the variable fixes the feature. A command is refused only when the request itself is malformed.
- **Undo history is snapshots,** not reversible operations (see `crates/ops`). Chosen because the document is a small recipe, and because an edit applied to a copy cannot leave the document half-changed. Two known limits, both on the [roadmap](roadmap.md): each step copies the whole document, so large imported bodies must be shared between snapshots before M10; and snapshots cannot merge concurrent edits, which would need reversible operations (M12+ candidate). The developer agreed to this on 2026-10-12.
- **Saving validates.** `.3dcraft` files are only written from documents that pass `Document::validate()`, so everything written can be read back. A document made invalid by a bug can therefore not be saved; that is preferred over writing a file that will not open.
- **`craft3d-zipstore`** is a new standalone crate: the ZIP code is shared by 3MF export (`meshio`) and the native format. `meshio` → `zipstore` is the one listed exception to "standalone crates depend on no workspace crate".
- **`serde` and `serde_json`** are workspace dependencies (document model, file format, command parameters).
- **Default export and render tolerance** is a thousandth of the side of a square with the bodies' total area, so it follows the size of the model; `tolerance` overrides it.
- **The roadmap's M2 and the start of M3 were built together** (primitives as features, regeneration, B-rep tessellation), because a document without bodies could not be checked by any oracle.

### 2026-10-12: Interpretations made while writing the docs

Proposed by the agent that wrote the first docs; the developer can overturn any of them.

- **"Two compositors" becomes "two renderers".** The brainstorm's compositor, copy-on-write tiles and multithreaded filters come from PhotoCraft's description. For a CAD modeller the equivalents are: a CPU reference renderer and a wgpu renderer for *display*; persistent, `Arc`-shared documents and bodies; parallel tessellation and regeneration. Kernel and tessellator have a single CPU implementation, and geometric correctness is tested with mathematical oracles rather than pictures ([`testing.md`](testing.md)).
- **Machine-name prefix `craft3d`.** A Rust crate name cannot start with a digit, so `3dcraft-*` is impossible. Product name stays **3DCraft**; crates, binaries and environment variables use `craft3d`. File extension `.3dcraft`.
- **Persistent topological names from the first feature** ([`architecture.md`](architecture.md) §1.1, §4.1).
- **Tolerance and units are document data,** kernel maths is `f64`, NURBS are in the geometry enums from M1.
- **UI toolkit: egui/eframe on wgpu,** as in PhotoCraft, for the phase-1 viewer. The engine seam keeps it replaceable.
- **Not adopted from the storytold apps for now:** the private `craftrules` standards (not available next to this checkout), `craft-libs`, `craft-fonts`, the contributor-credits machinery, release packaging and the parity/scorecard documents.

### 2026-10-14: Decisions made while building loft

Made by the agent, overnight; the developer can overturn any of them.

- **Format version 8 for the `loft` feature kind.** Nothing a version-7 file holds changes meaning, and AGENTS.md §8 asks for a new version only then; `crates/format/src/migrate.rs` asks for one whenever an older build could not read what a new kind writes, so that it reports the version and not an unknown kind (as versions 2, 3, 5, 6 and, for the chamfer the same night, 7 did). The second rule was followed: a build that knows version 7 knows chamfers and not lofts. The four features of 2026-10-13 that went in as optional fields without a new version are untouched. **For the developer to confirm**, with the question of which rule stands (and whether two kinds added in one night before any release should share a version).
- **How sections are matched.** Loops are walked the same way round by geometry (counter-clockwise looking back along the loft), not by how their sketch planes face, so there is no `reverse` parameter. The first section starts at its smallest curve id; later sections are turned to lie nearest to the one before, and `start` overrides that per section. The alternative, every section from its smallest id, twists a loft by default whenever a sketch plane faces the other way or a profile was drawn from another corner; the price of the rule chosen is that the matching, and with it the names of the side faces, follows the geometry: an edit that turns a section past the next corner changes them (references then fail; they never point at another face). Giving `start` on every section pins the matching.
- **Sections with different numbers of curves are cut by length,** at the fractions of the loop where the other sections have their corners, rather than refused. Corners within a thousandth of the loop of each other count as one, so that a sliver of a face is not made for a difference nobody drew.
- **A whole circle is four faces** in a loft with B-spline sides (four quarter arcs), and one face without a seam only where the side is a cylinder or a cone. A B-spline face that closes on itself would need a seam edge, which the tessellator and the measure handle on analytic surfaces only.
- **The parts a curve was cut in share its name** (`F9:side(e2,e2)` four times for a quartered circle), as the pieces of a face a boolean cut do. A name role with a part number would tell them apart; it was not added, to leave `doc/src/name.rs` alone while other features are being built on it.
- **`regen` depends on `tess`** (a lower layer), for the self-intersection check of every loft: the kernel cannot use the tessellator (same layer), and a loft that passes through itself must never be handed on. The check is the one of `document.check {"deep": true}`, with its parameters; it costs a mesh per rebuilt loft (the showcase hull with its hollowing: 0.6 s for the whole script).
- **Smooth means interpolation in homogeneous coordinates** for sections with arcs. Where the weights of the surface between the sections would not stay positive the loft fails and says so (make it ruled, or draw the sections with splines); none of the tested shapes does.
